# Neurovasculature of the lower limbs

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Surgery > Vascular surgery > Neurovasculature of the lower limbs*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MJ0MuS)

---

## Summary

The <u>arteries</u> of the lower limb arise from the <u>external iliac artery</u>, a branch of the common iliac <u>branch of the abdominal aorta</u>. Behind the <u>inguinal ligament</u>, the <u>external iliac artery</u> continues as the <u>femoral artery</u>, which is the main <u>artery</u> of the thigh. The <u>femoral artery</u> continues as the <u>popliteal artery</u>, which divides into the <u>anterior</u> tibial, <u>posterior</u> tibial, and <u>fibular arteries</u>, which supply the leg and foot. The venous system of the lower limbs consists of a <u>superficial</u> and a <u>deep venous system</u>. The <u>long saphenous vein</u> and the <u>short saphenous vein</u> comprise the <u>superficial venous system</u>, which drains into the <u>deep venous system</u> via <u>perforator veins</u>. The <u>deep veins</u> accompany the <u>arteries</u> of the lower limbs and drain into the <u>external iliac veins</u>, which drain into the <u>inferior vena cava</u>. The lower limbs are innervated by the nerves of the <u>lumbosacral plexus</u>, which is composed of the <u>lumbar plexus</u> (T12–L4) and the <u>sacral plexus</u> (L4–<u>S4</u>). The major motor nerves of the thigh are the <u>femoral nerve</u>, the <u>obturator nerve</u>, and the <u>sciatic nerve</u>. The <u>tibial nerve</u> and the <u>common peroneal nerve</u> are the major motor nerves of the leg. The lower limb receives cutaneous innervation from pure sensory nerves (e.g., <u>saphenous nerve</u>, <u>sural nerve</u>) as well as from mixed sensorimotor nerves (e.g., <u>femoral nerve</u>, <u>tibial nerve</u>, <u>deep peroneal nerve</u>).

## Veins

### Overview [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* The lower limbs have <u>superficial</u> and deep venous systems, both of which drain into the <u>inferior vena cava</u>.

* The <u>superficial veins</u> lie within the subcutaneous tissues and drain into the <u>deep venous system</u>.

* The <u>deep veins</u> lie deep to the deep <u>fascia</u> and drain into the <u>inferior vena cava</u> via the iliac <u>veins</u>.

* Perforator veins are short horizontal or oblique <u>veins</u> that connect the <u>superficial veins</u> to the <u>deep veins</u> by piercing through the deep <u>fascia</u>.

* Venous valves ensure the unidirectional flow of blood from the <u>superficial</u> to the <u>deep venous system</u> towards the <u>heart</u>.

#### <u>Veins of the leg</u>




* <u>Superficial veins</u>: long and <u>short saphenous veins</u> (see the table “Overview of <u>veins</u> of the lower limbs” above)

* <u>Deep veins</u>

* Anterior tibial vein

* Arises from <u>venae comitantes</u> of the <u>dorsalis pedis artery</u>

* Accompanies the <u>anterior tibial artery</u>

* Drains the <u>anterior</u> aspect of the leg and foot

* Posterior tibial vein

* Arises from the <u>medial</u> and <u>lateral</u> <u>plantar</u> <u>veins</u>

* Accompanies the <u>posterior tibial artery</u>

* Drains the <u>posterior</u> aspect of the leg and foot

* Fibular veins (<u>peroneal veins</u>)

* Arise from the <u>medial</u> and <u>lateral</u> <u>plantar</u> <u>veins</u>

* Accompany the <u>fibular artery</u>

* Drain the <u>lateral</u> aspect of the leg





#### <u>Veins of the foot</u>




* <u>Superficial veins</u>

* Dorsal venous arch of the foot

* Lies on the <u>dorsal</u> aspect of the heads of the <u>lateral</u> four <u>metatarsal bones</u>

* Tributaries: <u>Dorsal</u> digital <u>veins</u> unite to form <u>dorsal</u> <u>metatarsal</u> <u>veins</u> that drain into the <u>dorsal</u> venous arch.

* Marginal <u>veins</u>

* <u>Lateral</u> marginal <u>vein</u> continues as the <u>short saphenous vein</u>

* <u>Deep veins</u>

* Plantar venous arch of the foot

* Lies on the <u>plantar</u> aspect of the <u>lateral</u> four <u>metatarsal bones</u>

* Tributaries: <u>Plantar</u> digital <u>veins</u> unite to form <u>metatarsal</u> <u>veins</u> that drain into the deep <u>plantar</u> venous arch.

* Termination: continues as the <u>medial</u> and <u>lateral</u> <u>plantar</u> <u>veins</u>

* <u>Medial</u> and <u>lateral</u> <u>plantar</u> <u>veins</u>: drain into the <u>posterior tibial vein</u>

## Nerves

### Overview




* The lower limb is innervated by branches of the <u>lumbosacral plexus</u>.

* Lumbosacral plexus: a network of nerves composed of two distinct plexuses

* The <u>lumbar plexus</u>: formed by the <u>subcostal nerve</u> (T12) and first four lumbar nerves (L1–L4)

* The <u>sacral plexus</u>: formed by the last two lumbar nerves (L4–L5) and the first four sacral nerves (<u>S1</u>–<u>S4</u>)

* Lumbosacral trunk

* Formed by the fusion of lumbar nerves L4 and L5

* Contributes to the <u>sacral plexus</u>

### Lumbar plexus




* Definition: a neural network formed by the subcostal and first four lumbar nerves

* <u>Nerve roots</u>: <u>anterior</u> (<u>ventral</u>) divisions of T12–L4

* Anatomical location: : <u>anterior</u> to the <u>transverse processes</u> of the <u>lumbar vertebrae</u>, within the <u>psoas major muscle</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0pYeLJ)

* Supply

* Motor innervation

* Lower abdominal muscles: <u>external oblique</u>, <u>internal oblique</u>, and <u>transversus abdominis</u>

* <u>Pelvic</u> muscles: <u>Iliacus</u>, <u>pectineus</u>, cremaster

* Muscles of the <u>anterior</u> and <u>medial</u> compartments of the thigh

* Sensory innervation

* Lower abdomen

* <u>Lateral</u> <u>gluteal region</u>

* External genitalia

* Thigh

* <u>Medial</u> aspect of the leg (via the <u>saphenous nerve</u>)

### Sacral plexus




* Definition: a neural network formed by the last two lumbar nerves and the first four sacral nerves

* <u>Nerve roots</u>: <u>anterior</u> (<u>ventral</u>) divisions of L4–<u>S4</u>

* Anatomical location: lies within the <u>pelvis</u>, on the surface of the <u>piriformis</u>, <u>posterior</u> to the iliac vessels and the <u>ureter</u>

* Supply: lower limbs and <u>pelvis</u>

#### <u>Branches of the sciatic nerve</u>




* The <u>sciatic nerve</u> terminates at the apex of the <u>popliteal fossa</u> by dividing into the <u>tibial nerve</u> and the <u>common peroneal nerve</u>.

* A cutaneous branch from both the tibial and <u>common peroneal nerve</u> unites to form the <u>sural nerve</u>.
