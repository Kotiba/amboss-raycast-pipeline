## Summary

<u>Intoeing</u> is a condition characterized by <u>internal rotation</u> of the foot relative to the <u>longitudinal axis</u> of the leg. It is typically caused by one or more benign, <u>self-limited</u> conditions (i.e., <u>metatarsus adductus</u>, <u>internal tibial torsion</u>, and/or <u>femoral anteversion</u>). These conditions typically manifest at different ages, with <u>metatarsus adductus</u> appearing in the first year of life, <u>internal tibial torsion</u> between 1–3 years of age, and <u>femoral anteversion</u> between 3–6 years of age. A <u>pigeon-toed</u> stance is characteristic; additional clinical features vary by cause and may include a <u>curved foot</u> or <u>W-sitting</u> posture. Diagnosis is clinical and based on a focused history and <u>physical examination</u> that includes <u>clinical measurements for intoeing</u>. Imaging and/or referral to pediatric orthopedics is indicated if there are <u>red flags for pathological causes of intoeing</u> (e.g., <u>cerebral palsy</u> or <u>DDH</u>) or if <u>common causes of intoeing</u> persist past the expected age range. Management for most children consists of observation and reassurance, as benign rotational <u>intoeing</u> typically resolves spontaneously with age.
