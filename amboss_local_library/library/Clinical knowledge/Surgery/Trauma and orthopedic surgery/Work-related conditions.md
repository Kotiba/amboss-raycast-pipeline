# Work-related conditions

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Surgery > Trauma and orthopedic surgery > Work-related conditions*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-Q0DAf)

---

## Summary

Work-related conditions are injuries or illnesses caused, aggravated, or exacerbated by workplace events or hazards. Conditions include musculoskeletal injuries, respiratory diseases, infectious diseases, <u>poisoning</u>, <u>hearing loss</u>, and <u>burnout syndrome</u>. High-risk occupations include manufacturing, cleaning, construction, animal handling, agriculture, and health care. Patients with new or worsening health conditions should be screened for possible occupational causes. If screening is positive, a detailed occupational history is required. Management involves removing patients at risk of severe injury from their work environment, modifying activities for those with less severe injuries, initiating specific treatment, and education on <u>PPE</u> use. Consultation with occupational health specialists may be necessary. Always follow local protocols for reporting work-related injuries or illnesses.




For <u>skin</u> diseases contracted from exposure in the workplace, see “<u>Occupational skin diseases</u>.”

## Work-related musculoskeletal conditions

### Etiology [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BQdzyp0)




* Extremely forceful motions

* Repetitive tasks

* Poor <u>ergonomics</u> (e.g., sustained and/or awkward positions)

* Vibration exposure

* Contact <u>stress</u> from localized pressure on a body part (e.g., resting forearms on a desk, kneeling on hard surfaces)

* Cold working environment





### Overview of occupational musculoskeletal injuries




#### Neck and shoulder injuries [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7114Rf0)




* Tension neck syndrome [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BQdzyp0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zQdrAp0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bjdH_p0)

* Nonradiating <u>pain</u> and/or discomfort in the neck and shoulder muscles

* <u>Risk factors</u>

* Prolonged sitting

* Holding the neck in a forward and downward position (e.g., working on a computer or mobile device)

* Poor <u>ergonomics</u> (e.g., inappropriately placed keyboard)

* Sedentary work

* Prevention

* Stretching and strengthening exercises

* Improved <u>ergonomics</u> (e.g., more functional keyboard placement, forearm support)

* Breaks from positions that aggravate the injury

* Differential diagnoses: cervical <u>osteoarthritis</u>, cervical <u>nerve root</u> compression

* Shoulder injuries most commonly occur with heavy <u>overhead</u> lifting or sports-related repetitive <u>overhead</u> movements, e.g., swimming and baseball.

* <u>Subacromial impingement syndrome</u>

* <u>Rotator cuff tendinopathy</u>

* <u>Rotator cuff tear</u>

* <u>Biceps tendon tear</u>

* <u>Biceps tendinopathy</u>

* <u>Thoracic outlet syndrome</u>





#### <u>Elbow</u> injuries [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PQ0Wwf)




<u>Elbow</u> injuries are most commonly caused by low-demand repetitive wrist movements and/or forearm <u>supination</u>-<u>pronation</u>.




* <u>Lateral epicondylitis</u>

* Mechanism: wrist extension and/or repetitive forearm <u>supination</u>-<u>pronation</u> with the <u>elbow</u> extended

* Common inciting activities: office work, sewing, gardening, racket sports

* <u>Medial epicondylitis</u>

* Mechanism: repeated wrist <u>flexion</u> and forearm <u>pronation</u>

* Common inciting activities: golf, baseball, woodwork





#### Hand and wrist injuries




* <u>Carpal tunnel syndrome</u> [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Mj0MaT)

* Mechanisms

* Prolonged exposure to hand-transmitted vibration (e.g., construction)

* Awkward and/or repetitive wrist movements (e.g., data entry)

* Forceful handgrip and/or pinching motion (e.g., auto repair, agricultural work)

* See “<u>Carpal tunnel syndrome</u>” for details.

* Hand-arm vibration syndrome [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Xqc9CW0)

* Mechanism: prolonged exposure to vibration (e.g., vibrating power tools)

* Clinical features: <u>paresthesias</u>, sensory loss, decreased grip strength, and/or peripheral vasospasm

* Complications: <u>osteoporosis</u>, <u>joint</u> damage, muscle <u>necrosis</u>

* Management

* Early identification and exposure avoidance

* <u>Alpha blockers</u>, <u>calcium channel blockers</u>, and <u>prostaglandins</u> are occasionally used, but their <u>efficacy</u> for this condition is uncertain.

* Hypothenar hammer syndrome [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gjdF-p0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sjdy-p0)

* A rare condition caused by injury to the <u>ulnar artery</u> as it exits the <u>Guyon canal</u>

* Mechanism: repetitive trauma to <u>hypothenar eminence</u> (e.g., from vibrating tools, mountain biking, volleyball, baseball)

* Clinical features: <u>pain</u> over the <u>hypothenar eminence</u>, <u>ulnar nerve</u> <u>paresthesia</u>, cold <u>sensitivity</u>, <u>pallor</u> of fingers

* Treatment: <u>thrombolysis</u>, embolectomy, and/or <u>revascularization</u>





#### Back injuries




* <u>Degenerative disc disease</u>

* Displacement of disc fragments causing nerve or spinal cord impingement

* Mechanisms: prolonged sitting (e.g., office work, long-haul driving), heavy lifting, and/or repetitive bending and twisting

* <u>Clay-shoveler fracture</u> [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6EWj9M0)

* <u>Avulsion fracture</u> of a <u>spinous process</u> in the lower cervical or upper <u>thoracic spine</u>

* Mechanism: forceful <u>flexion</u> or rotation of the <u>cervical spine</u>





#### Knee injuries




Knee injuries are common in occupations requiring prolonged kneeling, repetitive stair climbing, and/or use of ladders.




* <u>Meniscus tear</u>

* <u>Anterior cruciate ligament injury</u>

* <u>Posterior cruciate ligament injury</u>

* <u>Osteoarthritis of the knee</u>





#### Additional work-related injuries




* <u>Tendinitis</u> and <u>tenosynovitis</u>

* <u>Osteoarthritis</u>

* <u>Inguinal hernia</u>





### Clinical evaluation




* Obtain a <u>focused history for work-related conditions</u>.

* Perform a focused <u>musculoskeletal examination</u>.





### Diagnostics




Diagnostic testing is condition-specific.




* <u>X-rays</u>

* CT or <u>MRI</u>

* <u>Electromyography</u>





### Management




* Begin initial treatment of work-related conditions.

* Initiate <u>acute pain management</u>.

* Use the <u>POLICE principle</u> for acute musculoskeletal injury.

* Provide injury-specific treatment.





> [!TIP]
> Before prescribing <u>opiates</u> for <u>chronic pain</u>, assess side effects, work status, driving ability, and whether the patient operates machinery. [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/INdYcq0)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rNdfcq0)

## Work-related hearing conditions

<u>Work-related hearing conditions</u> can be caused by hazardous noise (≥ 85 dB) and/or <u>ototoxic</u> chemicals (e.g., solvents, metals, <u>asphyxiants</u>). In the US, approximately 22 million workers are exposed to hazardous noise annually, and about 10 million workers are exposed to <u>ototoxic agents</u> (e.g., from the manufacture of paint, pharmaceuticals, rubber, footwear). Occupational noise is a major cause of <u>hearing loss</u> in the US (approximately 24% of all cases). [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YqcnCW0)




### Noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL)




* Definition: <u>hearing loss</u> due to continuous exposure to sounds > 85 dB (e.g., during construction work, mining, welding) or a single exposure to sounds > 120 dB (e.g., gunshots, jet engines, fireworks)

* Pathophysiology

* Repeated or constant exposure to noise > 85 dB → cumulative microtrauma and irreversible damage to the <u>stereocilia</u> of <u>hair</u> cells of the <u>organ of Corti</u> → <u>sensorineural hearing loss</u>

* Sudden noise > 120 dB → <u>tympanic membrane rupture</u> → <u>conductive hearing loss</u>

* Clinical features

* Slowly progressive <u>hearing loss</u>, beginning with loss of high-frequency <u>hearing</u>

* Difficulty <u>hearing</u> in noisy, crowded environments

* Difficulty <u>hearing</u> high-pitched voices (e.g., children's) occurs as the condition progresses.

* Treatment

* There is no definitive treatment.

* <u>Hearing aids</u> or <u>cochlear implants</u>

* Prevention: <u>hearing</u> protection (i.e., earplugs or earmuffs)





### <u>Tinnitus</u>




See “<u>Tinnitus</u>” for more information.

## Work-related respiratory conditions

<u>Lung</u> diseases are considered work-related if they are caused, exacerbated, or permanently worsened by substances in the workplace. They may be obstructive, restrictive, and/or <u>neoplastic</u>. [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JNdsXq0)




### Overview of occupational respiratory diseases [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fOYk76)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PjdW0J0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aYcQna0)




* Acute

* <u>Inhalational injury</u>

* <u>Hypersensitivity pneumonitis</u>

* <u>Pneumonia</u>

* <u>Inhalational anthrax</u>

* <u>Irritant and asphyxiant</u> exposure

* <u>Radiation pneumonitis</u>

* Chronic or acute-on-chronic

* <u>COPD</u> and <u>acute exacerbation of COPD</u>

* <u>Occupational asthma</u>

* <u>Allergic asthma</u>

* <u>Pneumoconioses</u> (e.g., <u>coal workers' pneumoconiosis</u>, <u>silicosis</u>, <u>berylliosis</u>)

* <u>Asbestos-related diseases</u> (e.g., <u>asbestosis</u>, <u>mesothelioma</u>)

* <u>Lung cancer</u>

* Granulomatosis disease (e.g., <u>tuberculosis</u>)

* Bronchiolitis obliterans: a chronic disease of the small <u>airways</u>, usually caused by repeated cycles of inflammation and scarring

* Chronic scarring causes <u>obstructive lung disease</u>.

* Exposure to toxic fumes (e.g., <u>nitrogen oxides</u>) is the most common workplace-related cause; other causes include severe <u>adenovirus</u> <u>pneumonia</u>.

* Occupational rhinitis: <u>rhinitis</u> caused or exacerbated by exposure to irritants (e.g., fur, paint, pesticides, <u>detergents</u>) and/or <u>allergens</u> (e.g., latex, guar <u>gum</u>, <u>psyllium</u>) [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WlXPDy)





### Clinical evaluation [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fOYk76)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PjdW0J0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aYcQna0)




A thorough occupational history is crucial for identifying work-related respiratory disease, as diagnostic tests and physical examinations often cannot distinguish between occupational and nonoccupational causes.




* Obtain a <u>focused history for work-related conditions</u>.

* Perform a detailed inhalational exposure history (often specific to the type of occupation).

* <u>Irritants and asphyxiants</u>: industrial workers, cleaning workers, painters, agricultural workers, military personnel

* <u>Allergens</u>: e.g., pollen (agricultural workers), animal dander (animal handlers), grain dust (bakers), exposure to humidifiers

* Microbes: e.g., agricultural pathogens , <u>mold</u>, spores

* <u>Minerals</u>

* Silica: miners, workers who mill or grind countertops, boiler workers

* <u>Beryllium</u>: individuals working with electronics, ceramics, metal alloys; aerospace workers

* <u>Asbestos</u>: auto mechanics; miners; and workers installing insulation, roofing, or pipes

* Lead: painters, construction workers (especially if working on older structures), battery manufacturers

* Inquire about respiratory <u>PPE</u> usage.

* Document <u>past medical history</u> and <u>family history</u> of respiratory conditions.





> [!TIP]
> The latency between exposure and symptom appearance is highly variable; it can be as long as 25 years for some <u>interstitial lung diseases</u>. [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PjdW0J0)




### Diagnostics [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PjdW0J0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pNdLXq0)




* <u>CXR</u>

* <u>PFTs</u>: Consider testing before and after the work shift in patients with <u>obstructive lung disease</u>.

* CT chest: especially for <u>interstitial lung disease</u>, granulomatosis

* Disease-specific tests: e.g., <u>allergy testing</u>, <u>serology</u>, <u>bronchoalveolar lavage</u>





### Management [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aYcQna0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pNdLXq0)




* Begin <u>initial treatment of work-related conditions</u>.

* Initiate treatment for <u>dyspnea</u> in patients with <u>respiratory distress</u>.

* Obtain initial diagnostic testing.

* Begin disease-specific treatment (e.g., <u>antibiotics</u>, treatment of <u>acute asthma exacerbation</u>).

* Include exposure reduction and <u>PPE</u> recommendations in activity modifications.

* Refer for expert consultation (e.g., pulmonology, infectious diseases, oncology).

* Counsel the patient on prevention (e.g., adherence to respiratory <u>PPE</u>).

## Work-related infectious diseases

<u>Work-related infectious diseases</u> are caused or exacerbated by exposure to <u>biological agents</u>, e.g., bacteria, <u>viruses</u>, <u>parasites</u>, or fungi. [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pjdLYJ0)[[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CJYqwJ)




### Etiology [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pjdLYJ0)[[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6jdjYJ0)




* Disease factors

* High transmission risk (e.g., <u>influenza</u>)

* Novel pathogens  (e.g., <u>ebola virus</u>)

* Workplace factors

* Environmental (e.g., exposure to fungus, ticks)

* Engineering limitations (e.g., poor air quality)

* Individual factors

* <u>Immunocompromised</u> status

* <u>Socioeconomic status</u> (e.g., limited access to <u>vaccinations</u>)





### High-risk occupations [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pjdLYJ0)[[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6jdjYJ0)




* Health care workers

* Military personnel

* Livestock and dairy workers, veterinarians

* Slaughterhouse workers

* Agricultural, garden, and forestry workers

## Burnout syndrome

### Definition




<u>Burnout</u> is a psychological syndrome caused by chronic work-related <u>stress</u> and characterized by three dimensions.   [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NNc-Y10)[[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Djd1cJ0)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IjdYbJ0)




* Emotional exhaustion

* Cynicism, <u>negativism</u>, <u>depersonalization</u>

* Reduced feelings of personal achievement, job satisfaction, and/or productivity





### <u>Epidemiology</u>




Work-related conditions are more common in female individuals than males. [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2CcTre0)




* Less decision-making authority

* More work-family conflicts





### Etiology [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Djd1cJ0)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IjdYbJ0)




* Organizational factors

* Mismatched work demands and resources

* Lack of worker <u>autonomy</u> and influence

* Lack of community or social support

* Perceived injustice (e.g., unfair treatment, moral conflict)

* Individual factors

* Inadequate <u>coping mechanisms</u>

* External <u>locus of control</u>





### Clinical features [[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IjdYbJ0)




* Mild

* Physical symptoms (e.g., <u>headache</u>, appetite change)

* Reduced productivity

* Moderate

* Concentration deficits, progressive loss of motivation

* Exhaustion, fatigue, <u>insomnia</u>

* <u>Apathy</u>, frustration, personal negativity

* Severe

* Absenteeism, desire to leave the occupation

* <u>Alcohol</u>, <u>psychotropic drug</u> misuse

* Extreme

* <u>Depression</u>, <u>suicidal ideation</u>

* Extreme behaviors (e.g., isolation, aggression)





### Diagnosis




* <u>Mental health screening for adults</u>

* Standardized tools for <u>burnout</u> (e.g., Maslach <u>burnout</u> inventory manual) [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vjdAXJ0)





### Differential diagnoses




* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>Anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Trauma- and stressor-related disorders</u>





### Complications




* Worker: impaired physical and mental well-being, substance misuse, <u>depression</u>, loss of employment

* Employer: reduced productivity and quality of work, increased employee turnover and absenteeism





### Management [[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IjdYbJ0)




* Organization-focused strategies

* Reduction of work stressors and increase in organizational resources (e.g., reduced workload)

* Often more effective than employee-focused strategies [[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aVWQG40)

* Employee-focused strategies: interventions that enhance employees' personal resources

* Organization-initiated interventions: <u>mindfulness</u>, <u>stress</u> management, and/or communication training

* Individual-initiated interventions: exercise, <u>cognitive therapy</u>, lifestyle changes
