## Summary

A <u>mediastinal</u> mass is an abnormal growth or lesion located within the <u>mediastinum</u> that can manifest at any age. Causes may be benign or malignant and include <u>thymomas</u>, <u>lymphomas</u>, neurogenic tumors, <u>germ cell tumors</u>, <u>thyroid</u> masses, benign cysts, and <u>metastatic</u> cancer. Clinical features depend on the location and size of the mass, ranging from asymptomatic to compressive effects such as <u>dyspnea</u>, <u>chest pain</u>, and <u>superior vena cava syndrome</u>. Diagnosis involves imaging, particularly <u>contrast-enhanced CT</u> or <u>MRI</u>, and, in some cases, cause-specific <u>laboratory studies</u> (e.g., <u>tumor markers</u>) and/or <u>biopsy</u>. Management varies by cause and may include observation for benign lesions; surgical resection and systemic treatment such as <u>chemotherapy</u> or <u>radiation therapy</u> for malignancies; and symptomatic treatment. Early multidisciplinary involvement is critical to optimize outcomes.
