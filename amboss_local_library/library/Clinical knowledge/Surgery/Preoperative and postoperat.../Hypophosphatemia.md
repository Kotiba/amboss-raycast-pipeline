## Summary

Hypophosphatemia is defined as a serum <u>phosphate</u> of < 2.5 mg/dL. It is more common in patients with <u>alcohol use disorder</u> and in critically unwell patients, who have high <u>phosphate</u> demands. Particularly in mild deficiency, symptoms can be nonspecific or absent entirely. However, severe hypophosphatemia can cause serious complications such as <u>seizures</u>, <u>respiratory failure</u>, and <u>arrhythmias</u>. Treatment may be oral or intravenous, depending on the severity of the deficiency, and should occur in conjunction with investigation of underlying causes. Supplementation regimens are covered in <u>electrolyte repletion</u>.

## Clinical features

Although mild hypophosphatemia rarely causes symptoms, moderate to severe hypophosphatemia is associated with various cardiac, musculoskeletal, neurological, and hematological abnormalities. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GwYB4r)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/twYXkr)




* Cardiac 

* <u>Arrhythmias</u>

* <u>Cardiomyopathy</u>

* <u>Cardiac arrest</u>

* Musculoskeletal

* Generalized <u>muscle weakness</u>

* Respiratory muscle dysfunction, which can lead to:

* <u>Respiratory failure</u>

* Ventilator dependence and/or difficult <u>ventilator weaning</u>

* Muscle <u>pain</u> (secondary to <u>rhabdomyolysis</u>)

* Loss of <u>bone mass</u>

* <u>Osteomalacia</u> in adults, <u>rickets</u> in children

* Bone <u>pain</u>

* Neurological

* Fatigue

* <u>Anorexia</u>

* <u>Dysarthria</u>

* <u>Paresthesia</u>, numbness

* <u>Altered mental state</u> (e.g., <u>disorientation</u>)

* <u>Seizures</u>

* <u>Coma</u>

* Hematological

* <u>Hemolysis</u>

* <u>Anemia</u>, <u>RBC</u> dysfunction

* <u>Thrombocytopenia</u>, <u>thrombocytopathy</u>

* <u>WBC</u> dysfunction, leading to impaired <u>immune response</u>

* Metabolic: reduced <u>insulin sensitivity</u> [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C9bqpD)

## Treatment

* Identify and treat the underlying cause (see <u>etiology of hypophosphatemia</u>).

* Consider discontinuation or dose reduction of any contributing medications.

* <u>Phosphate</u> supplementation (see <u>repletion regimens for hypophosphatemia</u>)
