# Insulinoma

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Surgery > Abdominal surgery > Pancreas, liver, and bile ducts > Insulinoma*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iS0Jz2)

---

## Summary

Insulinomas are <u>insulin</u>-secreting <u>pancreatic beta-cell</u> tumors. They are the most common cause of <u>endogenous hyperinsulinism</u> and are benign in 90% of patients. Most insulinomas occur sporadically but they can also occur in patients with <u>multiple endocrine neoplasias</u> (e.g., parathyroid tumors, <u>pituitary adenomas</u>, <u>gastrinomas</u>). Typical clinical features include recurrent attacks of symptomatic <u>hypoglycemia</u> in individuals without <u>diabetes</u>. The diagnosis is established if serum <u>insulin</u> and <u>C-peptide</u> are elevated despite <u>hypoglycemia</u>, either during a spontaneous episode or during a <u>hypoglycemic</u> episode provoked by a <u>72-hour fasting test</u>. The treatment of choice is surgical enucleation of the insulinoma. In inoperable cases and patients with persistent <u>hypoglycemic</u> attacks, pharmacotherapy (e.g., <u>diazoxide</u>, <u>somatostatin analogues</u>) can be used to decrease <u>insulin</u> secretion.

---

## Definitions

* Insulinoma: : the most common type of <u>pancreatic</u> <u>neuroendocrine tumor</u> (arising from <u>beta cells</u>); causes <u>endogenous hyperinsulinism</u>

* <u>Hyperinsulinism</u>: a condition of elevated blood <u>insulin</u> levels

* <u>Endogenous hyperinsulinism</u>: a condition characterized by inappropriately high serum <u>insulin</u> levels relative to blood sugar levels; manifests as <u>hypoglycemic</u> symptoms

---

## Pathophysiology

* Insulinomas are <u>neuroendocrine tumors</u> that arise from <u>beta cells</u> of the <u>pancreas</u>.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xxYEAr)

* < 1% occur at <u>ectopic</u> sites (e.g., <u>spleen</u>).

* ∼ 90% of insulinomas occur as solitary tumors.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xxYEAr)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1ZX209)

* Most insulinomas occur sporadically.

* Over 90% of insulinomas are benign. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xxYEAr)

* ∼ 5% of insulinomas are associated with <u>multiple endocrine neoplasia</u> type 1 (<u>MEN 1</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)





References:[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U30b3f)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p30Ljf)

## Clinical features

* Clinical features of <u>hypoglycemia</u>, e.g., <u>lethargy</u>, <u>syncope</u>, and <u>double vision</u>

* <u>Hypoglycemic</u> symptoms usually occur several hours after a meal and may be precipitated by exercise and/or <u>alcohol</u> consumption.

* <u>Neuroglycopenic symptoms</u>  may be preceded by symptoms of <u>sympathetic</u> overactivity.

* Relief of <u>symptoms of hypoglycemia</u> after administering <u>glucose</u> (see also <u>Whipple triad</u>)

* Weight gain

* Symptoms characteristic of other endocrine neoplasias may occur (see “<u>Multiple endocrine neoplasias</u>” for more information).

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)




* Consider insulinoma in patients with both:

* <u>Whipple triad</u>

* No other known <u>cause of hypoglycemia</u> (e.g., <u>diabetes mellitus</u>, <u>drugs that cause hypoglycemia</u>, critical illness)

* Order <u>laboratory studies</u> to confirm <u>endogenous hyperinsulinism</u>.

* Obtain imaging to confirm insulinoma.

* Evaluate patients with confirmed insulinoma for clinical features of <u>MEN 1</u> and <u>neurocutaneous syndromes</u>.

* If insulinoma is ruled out or there is clinical suspicion for other causes of <u>endogenous hyperinsulinism</u>, consider additional workup, e.g.:

* <u>Hepatic vein</u> <u>insulin</u> sampling to evaluate for <u>NIPHS</u>

* <u>IGF-1</u>/<u>IGF</u>-2 to evaluate for <u>NICTH</u>

* <u>Endocrine tests for adrenal insufficiency</u>





### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)




#### Initial studies




* Obtain during a symptomatic episode of <u>hypoglycemia</u> in patients with suspected insulinoma.

* Findings consistent with endogenous hyperinsulinism

* ↓ <u>Glucose</u>: < 55 mg/dL

* ↑ <u>Insulin</u>: > 3.0 mIU/L

* ↑ <u>C-peptide</u>: > 0.6 ng/mL

* ↑ <u>Proinsulin</u>: > 5.0 pmol/L

* ↓ <u>β-hydroxybutyrate</u>: < 28 mg/dL

* Negative screening for oral <u>hypoglycemic</u> agents (e.g., negative <u>sulfonylurea</u> levels)

* Negative <u>insulin</u> <u>antibodies</u>





> [!TIP]
> Factitious <u>hypoglycemia</u> secondary to <u>exogenous</u> <u>insulin</u> use will not raise <u>C-peptide</u> levels, whereas factitious <u>hypoglycemia</u> secondary to the use of <u>insulin</u> secretagogues (e.g., <u>sulfonylureas</u>, <u>meglitinides</u>) can elevate both <u>C-peptide</u> and <u>proinsulin</u> levels.




#### 72-hour fasting test




Obtain in consultation with a specialist, e.g., <u>endocrinology</u>.




* Indication: patients with no spontaneous <u>hypoglycemic</u> episodes during the evaluation to provoke <u>hypoglycemia</u>

* Procedure

* The patient fasts for 72 hours, only drinking noncaloric beverages, and all nonessential medications are discontinued.

* Measure <u>insulin</u>, <u>C-peptide</u>, and <u>glucose</u> every 4–6 hours.

* Once blood <u>glucose</u> < 45 mg/dL or < 55 mg/dL with documented <u>Whipple triad</u>, obtain initial studies for <u>hyperinsulinism</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)

* Supportive findings: : <u>findings consistent with endogenous hyperinsulinism</u>, e.g., low <u>glucose</u> and high <u>insulin</u> levels

* Limitations [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VsYGFq)

* Results may be inaccurate if the physiological <u>glucose</u> level is low.

* Rarely, insulinomas may suppress <u>insulin</u> release in response to <u>hypoglycemia</u>.

* <u>Insulin</u> levels can be artificially elevated in the presence of circulating anti-<u>insulin</u> <u>antibodies</u>.





#### Glucagon stimulation test




<u>Glucagon stimulation test</u> may be obtained in conjunction with a <u>fasting</u> test or alone.




* Procedure

* Inject <u>glucagon</u>. DOSAGE

* Measure serum <u>glucose</u> and <u>insulin</u> at baseline, and again at 10, 20, and 30 minutes after injection.

* Supportive finding: an increase in serum <u>glucose</u> of â¥ 25 mg/dL within 30 minutes of <u>glucagon</u> injection   [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)

* Limitations [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VsYGFq)

* Unreliable in patients with <u>malnutrition</u>, hepatic disease, and <u>cirrhosis</u> with <u>portocaval anastomosis</u>

* Medication interference: <u>diazoxide</u>, <u>hydrochlorothiazide</u>, diphenylhydantoin, <u>sulfonylureas</u>, aminophylline

* Patients with nonislet cell tumors can have insulinoma-like responses.

* The <u>glucagon tolerance test</u> can induce <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, and <u>hypoglycemia</u>.

* In 8% of insulinomas, the expected peak is not observed.

### Imaging studies [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)




Imaging is usually guided by a specialist (e.g., endocrine <u>surgery</u>).




* Indication: confirmed <u>endogenous hyperinsulinism</u> to localize insulinoma for surgical planning   [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)

* Modalities

* Noninvasive, e.g.:

* <u>Ultrasound</u>, CT, and/or <u>MRI</u> abdomen  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)

* Triphasic CT of the <u>pancreas</u>

* <u>Somatostatin</u> <u>receptor</u> <u>scintigraphy</u>

* Invasive, e.g., <u>endoscopic ultrasound</u> (<u>EUS</u>) with or without <u>hepatic vein</u> <u>insulin</u> sampling  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)





> [!TIP]
> Insulinomas are commonly < 1.0 cm in diameter and difficult to detect with noninvasive imaging. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)

## Treatment

Provide oral <u>glucose</u>, IV dextrose, or IM <u>glucagon</u> to patients with acute <u>hypoglycemia</u>. See also “<u>Treatment of hypoglycemia</u>.”




### Surgical therapy [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WZXP09)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gaWFPP0)




* <u>Tumor</u> resection is the first-line treatment for localized insulinoma.

* Options

* Enucleation of the <u>tumor</u>: preferred

* <u>Partial pancreatic resection</u>: if the <u>tumor</u> is within 3 mm of the <u>pancreatic duct</u>





### Pharmacological treatment [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WsYPFq)




* Consider agents that inhibit <u>insulin</u> secretion for:

* Short-term <u>bridge therapy</u> until <u>tumor</u> resection is feasible

* Long-term management of inoperable tumors or recurrent symptoms after excision

* Options 

* Diazoxide DOSAGE

* <u>Somatostatin analogs</u>: e.g., <u>octreotide</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Tumor</u> resection has a good prognosis and a low risk of recurrence.
