## Summary

Bowel obstruction refers to the interruption of the normal passage of bowel contents through the bowel, either due to a functional or mechanical obstruction. <u>Functional bowel obstruction</u>, or <u>paralytic ileus</u>, is a temporary disturbance of <u>peristalsis</u> in the absence of mechanical obstruction (see “<u>Paralytic ileus</u>”). <u>Mechanical bowel obstruction</u> is the interruption of normal passage through the bowel due to a structural barrier. <u>Mechanical bowel obstruction</u> can be classified as either a <u>small bowel obstruction</u> (<u>SBO</u>) or <u>large bowel obstruction</u> (<u>LBO</u>) according to its location, and, depending on the extent of the obstruction, as either partial or complete. Postoperative <u>bowel adhesion</u> is the most common cause of <u>SBO</u> and <u>malignancy</u> is the most common cause of <u>LBO</u>. Regardless of the cause, bowel obstruction typically manifests with <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>, abdominal distention, and <u>constipation</u> or <u>obstipation</u>. <u>Bowel sounds</u> are increased and high-pitched in the early phases of bowel obstruction and decreased or absent in the later stages. Bowel distention leads to <u>third-space volume loss</u>, resulting in <u>dehydration</u> and <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities. The <u>symptoms of bowel obstruction</u> are typically less severe in <u>partial bowel obstruction</u> than in total bowel obstruction. A <u>diagnosis of bowel obstruction</u> should be confirmed on imaging (e.g., CT abdomen and <u>pelvis</u>). Typical findings include dilated bowel loops <u>proximal</u> to the obstruction, collapse of bowel loops <u>distal</u> to the obstruction, and multiple <u>air-fluid levels</u>. Laboratory tests are needed to assess severity (e.g., <u>metabolic acidosis</u> and elevated serum <u>lactate</u> suggest <u>bowel ischemia</u>) and identify acid-base and <u>electrolyte</u> imbalances. A trial of nonoperative management (NOM), which includes bowel rest and <u>supportive care</u> (such as <u>IV fluids</u>, <u>analgesics</u>, <u>antiemetics</u>, and, if needed, <u>antibiotics</u>) can be considered in patients with <u>simple bowel obstruction</u> with no evidence of complications. <u>Surgery</u> is indicated for <u>complicated bowel obstruction</u>, <u>closed-loop bowel obstruction</u>, or if there is no clinical improvement following NOM for <u>simple bowel obstruction</u>. The underlying cause of bowel obstruction should be sought for and managed appropriately.

## Definitions

* Bowel obstruction: the interruption of normal passage through the bowel

* Mechanical bowel obstruction: the interruption of normal passage through the bowel due to a structural barrier (e.g., a <u>tumor</u>, adhesions)

* <u>Paralytic ileus</u> (<u>functional bowel obstruction</u>): temporary functional impairment of <u>peristalsis</u> in the absence of a mechanical obstruction (see “<u>Mechanical bowel obstruction vs. paralytic ileus</u>”)

* According to the site of obstruction, <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u> can be classified as:

* Large bowel obstruction (<u>LBO</u>): obstruction at the level of the <u>cecum</u>, <u>colon</u>, or <u>rectum</u>

* Small bowel obstruction (<u>SBO</u>): obstruction at the level of the <u>duodenum</u>, <u>jejunum</u>, or <u>ileum</u>

* <u>Gastric outlet obstruction</u> (GOO): obstruction at the level of the <u>pyloric</u> channel or <u>duodenum</u>

## Pathophysiology

* Bowel obstruction → stasis of luminal contents and gas <u>proximal</u> to the obstruction → ↑ intraluminal pressure, which leads to the following: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PLYWBp)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)

* Gaseous abdominal distention → sequestration of fluids within the distended bowel loops (<u>third spacing</u>) → <u>dehydration and hypovolemia</u>

* <u>Vomiting</u> → loss of fluid and Na<sup>+</sup>, <u>K<sup>+</sup></u>, H<sup>+</sup>, and <u>Cl<sup>-</sup></u> → <u>hypokalemia</u>, <u>metabolic alkalosis</u>, and <u>hypovolemia</u>

* Compression of intestinal <u>veins</u> and <u>lymphatics</u> → bowel wall <u>edema</u> → compression of intestinal <u>arterioles</u> and <u>capillaries</u> → <u>bowel ischemia</u>, which leads to:

* ↑ Bowel wall permeability → translocation of intestinal microbes to the <u>peritoneal cavity</u> → <u>sepsis</u>

* <u>Necrosis</u> and perforation of the bowel wall → <u>peritonitis</u>

* <u>Anaerobic metabolism</u> and lysis of <u>ischemic</u> cells → accumulation of <u>lactic acid</u> and release of intracellular <u>K<sup>+</sup></u> → <u>metabolic acidosis</u> and <u>hyperkalemia</u>

## Clinical features

### Cardinal signs




The cardinal signs of <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u> are abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>constipation</u>, abdominal distention, and decreased <u>bowel sounds</u>, regardless of the underlying etiology. The severity and progression of clinical features differ according to the site and severity of the obstruction.




* Complete bowel obstruction [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)

* Total obstruction of the intestinal lumen, preventing the passage of air and fluid

* Rapid progression of clinical features

* Can be associated with obstipation (complete inability to pass stool or gas)

* Partial bowel obstruction [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)

* Partial obstruction of the intestinal lumen, allowing a small amount of air and fluid to pass through

* Clinical features may be less severe than in <u>complete bowel obstruction</u>

* Can be associated with the intermittent passage of flatus and <u>overflow diarrhea</u>





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Partial bowel obstruction</u> causes gradually progressive symptoms that are typically milder than those caused by complete obstruction. <u>Obstipation</u> is only present in <u>complete bowel obstruction</u>.

### Progression




Depending on the onset and progression of clinical features, <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u> can be classified as simple or complicated and acute or subacute. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)




* Simple bowel obstruction: bowel obstruction with no evidence of complications (i.e., no features of <u>bowel ischemia</u>, <u>bowel perforation</u>, or <u>red flags for complicated bowel obstruction</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PLYWBp)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C_Yqq7)

* Complicated bowel obstruction: bowel obstruction associated with strangulation, <u>ischemic necrosis</u>, or perforation [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PLYWBp)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C_Yqq7)

* Red flags for complicated bowel obstruction [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PLYWBp)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C_Yqq7)

* <u>Pain out of proportion</u>

* <u>Peritoneal signs</u>

* Signs of systemic toxicity, e.g., <u>SIRS</u>

* <u>Hemodynamic instability</u>

* Laboratory abnormalities: e.g., significant <u>leukocytosis</u>, <u>metabolic acidosis</u>, ↑ <u>lactate</u>

## Management

Bowel obstruction is an emergency and should be detected and managed early to minimize the risk of <u>bowel perforation</u> and strangulation, and the subsequent development of <u>sepsis</u>. The <u>initial management of bowel obstruction</u> is similar to that of undifferentiated <u>acute abdomen</u>.




![Initial management of bowel obstruction](../../../../../media/rbg66cvatf41fl1amzrzcsta.jpg)

Initial management of bowel obstruction




* <u>ABCDE approach</u>: Evaluate <u>vital signs</u>, <u>volume status</u>, and the need for invasive monitoring.

* Urgent consults: general <u>surgery</u>, gastroenterology (also contact an intensivist as needed)

* Initial management

* <u>NPO</u> status

* <u>Obtain IV access</u> with two large-bore <u>peripheral IVs</u>; simultaneously draw blood for urgent <u>laboratory studies</u>.

* <u>IV fluid resuscitation</u>

* <u>Electrolyte repletion</u> as needed

* Insert a <u>nasogastric tube</u> in patients with recurrent <u>vomiting</u> and/or significant abdominal distention.

* <u>Supplemental oxygen</u> as needed

* Administer <u>supportive care</u> as needed.

* <u>Parenteral analgesics</u>

* Parenteral <u>antiemetics</u>

* <u>Empiric antibiotics for intraabdominal infections</u> (not routinely recommended for <u>simple bowel obstruction</u>)  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)

* If <u>fever</u> and/or <u>leukocytosis</u> are present [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)

* For strangulated or perforated bowel obstruction

* As prophylaxis for operative malignant bowel obstruction  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)

* Obtain imaging: See “Diagnostics.”

* Disposition: Admit to the surgical service or transfer to the operating room (based on <u>surgery</u> consult).  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XzY9r7)

* Definitive management: See “Treatment.”





> [!TIP]
> Admit patients with a bowel obstruction to a surgical service, even if <u>conservative treatment</u> is planned. Patients managed on a surgical service have better overall outcomes and lower health care expenditures than those managed on a medical service. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XzY9r7)

## Diagnosis

### General principles




* Imaging is required to:

* Confirm <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u>

* Identify the site and assess the severity of the obstruction

* Identify complications and the underlying etiology of the obstruction

* Guide treatment planning

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> provide supportive evidence to help assess the severity of the obstruction.





> [!WARNING]
> Do not wait for imaging before initiating definitive management if there is an emergent critical finding (e.g., <u>peritonitis</u>). [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)




> [!TIP]
> Bowel obstruction requires a swift diagnostic workup to establish if emergency <u>surgery</u> is required.

#### <u>Abdominal series x-ray</u>




* Indication: most appropriate initial test in <u>hemodynamically unstable</u> patients or in resource-poor centers  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9_YNq7)

* Findings

* <u>Proximal</u> bowel dilatation

* Minimal or no intraluminal air <u>distal</u> to the obstruction

* Stepladder sign (best seen on an upright view): multiple <u>air-fluid levels</u> and stacked dilated loops of <u>small bowel</u>

* <u>Chest x-ray</u> : Air under the <u>diaphragm</u> is an indicator of <u>bowel perforation</u>.

* See also “<u>Radiological signs of mechanical bowel obstruction</u>.”

* Important considerations : <u>X-rays</u> have a number of limitations. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p8XLn-)

* Variable <u>sensitivity</u> (50–65%)  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9_YNq7)

* Cannot reliably identify the site of obstruction, underlying etiology, or extent of complications

* Do not influence the management of <u>acute bowel obstruction</u> to the same extent as CT abdomen

> To detect <u>pneumoperitoneum</u> on a <u>chest x-ray</u>, patients must be sitting upright for at least 10 minutes to allow free air to move upward and collect under the <u>diaphragm</u>. Patients with severe abdominal <u>pain</u> often require preemptive <u>analgesia</u> to tolerate the procedure! [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0VceGY0)




#### CT abdomen and <u>pelvis</u> (<u>gold standard</u>)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D_Y1q7)




* Indications

* With IV contrast: most appropriate initial test in hemodynamically stable patients with <u>acute bowel obstruction</u>  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/doYoaJ)

* With water-soluble oral contrast: Consider in patients with <u>subacute bowel obstruction</u> and no evidence of complications. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p8XLn-)

* Without contrast: for patients with a contrast <u>allergy</u>

* Findings

* Similar to those seen on <u>abdominal x-ray</u>; see “<u>Radiological signs of mechanical bowel obstruction</u>.”

* Transition point: sudden narrowing of the bowel lumen at the site of obstruction

* Closed-loop bowel obstruction: a type of <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u> in which the <u>proximal</u> and <u>distal</u> ends of the obstructed loop are closed

* Single site of obstruction: e.g., in <u>volvulus</u> or <u>incarcerated hernia</u>

* Multiple sites of obstruction: e.g., obstructing <u>colorectal cancer</u> with a competent <u>ileocecal valve</u>

* Progresses rapidly and is associated with an increased risk of strangulation

* Important consideration: In <u>acute bowel obstruction</u>, a <u>CT scan</u> is more accurate than an <u>x-ray</u> in the identification of the site of obstruction, complications, and underlying etiology, and, therefore, influences patient management to a greater extent. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p8XLn-)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Closed-loop bowel obstructions</u> are associated with an increased risk of bowel strangulation and perforation.

<u>POCUS</u> or <u>formal ultrasound</u> can be performed.




* Indication: <u>Hemodynamically unstable</u> patients (may be preferred over <u>abdominal x-ray</u>)  [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8zYOu7)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6Tcj7b0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pTcL7b0)

* Altered <u>peristalsis</u>

* Increased (early finding) or decreased/absent (late finding)

* Pendular peristalsis: dysfunctional so-called “to-and-fro” <u>peristalsis</u>

* <u>Intraperitoneal</u> fluid accumulation may be present.

#### <u>Barium enema</u> or water-soluble contrast enema [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9_YNq7)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vzYAu7)




* Indication: suspected <u>distal</u> <u>LBO</u> if CT is unavailable [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)

* Findings [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9_YNq7)

* Tapering of the bowel lumen at the site of obstruction

* <u>Complete bowel obstruction</u>: contrast not visible beyond the obstruction

* <u>Partial bowel obstruction</u>: small amount of contrast visible beyond the obstruction

* <u>Bird beak sign</u>: in <u>volvulus</u>

* <u>Apple core sign</u>: in <u>colonic</u> <u>malignancy</u> [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vzYAu7)

> <u>Contrast enema</u> helps differentiate <u>complete bowel obstruction</u> from <u>partial bowel obstruction</u>.




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Barium enema</u> is contraindicated if <u>bowel perforation</u> is suspected (water-soluble <u>contrast enema</u> can be used instead).

#### Water-soluble contrast challenge (<u>WSCc</u>) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/t_YXJ7)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)




* Indication: <u>SBO</u>, to differentiate partial <u>SBO</u> from complete <u>SBO</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p8XLn-)

* Procedure: A water-soluble contrast medium is administered orally or via an <u>enteric tube</u>, followed by <u>abdominal x-ray</u> 8 and 24 hours after ingestion. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)

* Findings [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p8XLn-)

* Normal <u>WSCc</u>: contrast reaches the <u>colon</u> within 24 hours of administration

* Indicates <u>partial bowel obstruction</u>

* The patient may be a candidate for nonoperative management.

* Intraluminal and intramural <u>causes of bowel obstruction</u> may be identified (see “Etiology”). [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)

* Additional considerations

* <u>WSCc</u> is also used to evaluate response to nonoperative management.

* See “<u>Nonoperative management of mechanical bowel obstruction</u>.” [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)





### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)




* Routine studies: <u>CBC</u>, <u>BMP</u>, serum <u>lactate</u>, and <u>CRP</u>

* Additional studies: See “<u>Approach to acute abdomen</u>.”

* Supportive findings

* In patients who are <u>dehydrated</u> 

* ↑ <u>BUN</u> and <u>creatinine</u> (<u>prerenal acute kidney injury</u>)

* ↑ <u>Hematocrit</u> (due to <u>hemoconcentration</u>)

* In patients with recurrent <u>vomiting</u>

* <u>Hypochloremic</u> <u>hypokalemic</u> <u>metabolic alkalosis</u>

* <u>Hyponatremia</u>

* Suggestive of <u>complicated bowel obstruction</u> ;  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)

* <u>Hyperkalemia</u> , elevated serum <u>lactate</u>, and <u>metabolic acidosis</u>: suggestive of <u>bowel ischemia</u>  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)

* <u>Leukocytosis</u> (> 16,000/mmÂ³) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)

* Elevated nonspecific <u>inflammatory markers</u> (↑ <u>CRP</u> and serum <u>creatine kinase</u>) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)

* ↑ <u>Amylase</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PLYWBp)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKYXRJ)

* Altered <u>coagulation panel</u> (e.g., elevated <u>INR</u> in <u>sepsis</u>)





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Leukocytosis</u>, <u>metabolic acidosis</u>, and elevated serum <u>lactate</u> in a patient with suspected bowel obstruction are suggestive of <u>bowel ischemia</u>.

## Treatment

* See “<u>Initial management of bowel obstruction</u>” for first steps and <u>supportive care</u>.

* Definitive management: depends on the severity and etiology of the obstruction and clinical presentation of the patient

* Interventional management

* <u>Surgery</u>: Transfer the patient to the operating room or admit to a surgical ward depending on the urgency of surgical intervention.

* Endoscopic intervention: e.g., for the removal or fragmentation of foreign objects that are within reach of an endoscope

* Stool evacuation

* Nonoperative management: <u>simple bowel obstruction</u> with no evidence of complications (e.g., <u>partial bowel obstruction</u> or <u>postoperative ileus</u>)

* Identify and treat the underlying cause (see “Etiology”).

## Interventional management

### <u>Surgery</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)[[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/G_YBp7)




* Indications

* <u>Complicated bowel obstruction</u> (i.e., signs of <u>ischemia</u>, perforation, or clinical deterioration)

* <u>Closed-loop bowel obstruction</u>

* Suspected bowel obstruction in patients presenting with <u>hemodynamic instability</u> refractory to initial <u>fluid resuscitation</u>

* Failure of nonoperative management (i.e., no improvement after 3 days of NOM; clinical deterioration/development of complications during NOM) [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UacbPa0)

* Underlying etiology necessitates surgical intervention (e.g., <u>surgery for inguinal hernia</u>; <u>enterolithotomy</u> for <u>gallstone ileus</u>)

* Procedure: <u>exploratory laparotomy</u> 

* Management of the obstruction (e.g., adhesiolysis, <u>hernia</u> reduction, <u>cecopexy</u>, <u>tumor</u> resection)

* Resection of gangrenous bowel with restoration of intestinal transit or creation of a <u>stoma</u>

### Endoscopic intervention [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A_YRI7)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mzYVt7)




Endoscopic interventions can be considered for bowel obstruction with no <u>signs of strangulation</u> or perforation. Rigid or <u>flexible sigmoidoscopy</u>, <u>upper GI endoscopy</u>, or <u>colonoscopy</u> under <u>procedural sedation</u> can be used for endoscopic investigation for the following indications.




* <u>Sigmoid volvulus</u>: Attempt <u>endoscopic decompression, detorsion, and reduction</u>.

* <u>Intraluminal bowel obstruction</u>  that is within reach of an endoscope: fragmentation or removal

* Inoperable malignant bowel obstruction: Consider placement of <u>stents</u> and decompression tubes.





### Stool evacuation [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O9XInZ0)




* Indication: <u>simple bowel obstruction</u> caused by <u>fecal impaction</u>

* Procedures

* The specific procedure is chosen based on the site of <u>fecal impaction</u>, only after <u>bowel perforation</u> has been definitively ruled out.

* <u>Manual disimpaction</u>

* <u>Distal</u> softening or washout with enemas or suppositories

* <u>Proximal</u> softening or washout with oral solutions such as <u>polyethylene glycol</u> or <u>sodium</u> <u>phosphate</u>

* See “<u>Treatment of fecal impaction</u>” for details and dosages.

* Important consideration: Identify and manage the underlying cause of <u>constipation</u> that led to <u>fecal impaction</u>.

## Nonoperative management

### Indications [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B_Yzq7)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)




* Early postoperative bowel obstruction (i.e., within 6 weeks of abdominal <u>surgery</u>)  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)

* <u>Partial bowel obstruction</u> with no evidence of complications

* Consider in patients with complete <u>SBO</u> and no evidence of complications.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F_YgJ7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)





### Contraindications [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)




* <u>Complicated bowel obstruction</u> (e.g, <u>peritoneal signs</u>, <u>signs of strangulation</u>)

* Refractory <u>metabolic acidosis</u>

* Significant <u>leukocytosis</u> (> 18,000/mmÂ³)

* Significant cecal dilation





### Initial measures




* Bowel rest (<u>NPO</u>)

* <u>Supportive care</u>

* <u>IV fluid therapy</u> (initial <u>fluid resuscitation</u> followed by <u>maintenance fluid therapy</u>)

* <u>Electrolyte repletion</u>

* <u>Parenteral analgesics</u> (<u>nonopioid analgesics</u> are preferred)  [[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ypYdHJ)[[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QzYus7)

* Parenteral <u>antiemetics</u> as needed





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Peristalsis</u>-inducing medications (i.e., <u>prokinetic agents</u> such as <u>metoclopramide</u>) are contraindicated in complete <u>mechanical bowel obstruction</u>.




> [!TIP]
> <u>Prophylactic antibiotic therapy</u> is not routinely indicated for <u>simple bowel obstruction</u> that is being managed nonoperatively.  [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3acS4a0)




#### <u>Nasogastric tube</u> insertion (bowel decompression)




* Indications: not routinely required but should be considered in the following situations  [[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y_YdI7)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/macVOa0)

* Persistent <u>vomiting</u>

* Significant upper GI distention

* <u>Complete bowel obstruction</u>

* <u>Volvulus</u>

* Procedure: See “<u>Nasogastric tube placement</u>.”

### Serial monitoring




* Symptom severity

* Bowel movements

* <u>Serial abdominal examination</u> every 4–8 hours [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x_YEq7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)

### Duration of nonoperative management trial [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E_Y8J7)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s_Ytp7)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UacbPa0)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hacc4a0)




* No longer than 72 hours

* Some authors suggest trialing NOM for a maximum of 5 days.

* Continuing nonoperative management for > 72 hours does not decrease the need for <u>surgery</u> but does increase surgical <u>morbidity</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v_YAJ7)
