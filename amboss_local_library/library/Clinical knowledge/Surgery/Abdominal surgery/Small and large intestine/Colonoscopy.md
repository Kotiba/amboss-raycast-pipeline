## Summary

Colonoscopy is a procedure in which a flexible fiberoptic endoscope is passed through the <u>anus</u> to visualize the <u>mucosa</u> of the <u>rectum</u>, <u>colon</u>, and, sometimes, the <u>terminal ileum</u>. It is commonly used to diagnose and/or manage lower gastrointestinal (GI) disorders, such as <u>GI bleeding</u>, and for <u>colorectal cancer screening</u>. <u>Bowel preparation</u> involves a combination of dietary restrictions (e.g., a <u>low-residue diet</u> for 1 day) and pharmacological bowel-cleansing agents (e.g., <u>polyethylene glycol</u>-based solutions). Contraindications include known or suspected <u>GI perforation</u> and conditions with a risk of perforation (e.g., <u>toxic megacolon</u> and acute <u>diverticulitis</u>). Complications are rare but include <u>colonic</u> perforation and <u>GI bleeding</u>. For patients unable to undergo colonoscopy, alternatives include <u>video capsule endoscopy</u> and <u>radiography</u>.
