## Summary

<u>Acute abdominal pain</u> is nontraumatic abdominal <u>pain</u> lasting from hours to days (usually < 7 days) and is a common symptom in children. The underlying pathology may be intra-abdominal, thoracic, or systemic. <u>Red flags for acute abdominal pain in children</u> (e.g., <u>bilious</u> <u>vomiting</u>, blood in stool, <u>peritoneal signs</u>) warrant urgent evaluation for <u>urgent and life-threatening causes of pediatric acute abdominal pain</u>. Diagnosis may be clinical or involve targeted evaluation. Abdominal <u>ultrasound</u> is the preferred initial imaging modality for most causes; <u>abdominal x-ray</u> is obtained for suspected <u>bowel obstruction</u>, <u>bowel perforation</u>, or <u>foreign body ingestion</u>. Treatment is based on the underlying etiology.




For <u>acute abdomen</u> in <u>neonates</u>, see “<u>Differential diagnoses of neonatal intestinal obstruction</u>.”
