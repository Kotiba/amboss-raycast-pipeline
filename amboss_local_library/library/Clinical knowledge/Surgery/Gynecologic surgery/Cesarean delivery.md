## Summary

Cesarean delivery refers to the delivery of <u>newborns</u> via a surgical <u>incision</u> through the abdominal wall and <u>uterus</u>. It is typically performed in situations where maternal and/or fetal health is at risk or compromised, but can also be performed as an alternative to vaginal delivery in routine <u>pregnancies</u>. Cesarean delivery can be planned based on known maternal and/or fetal <u>risk factors</u>, or performed as life-saving emergency procedures for unexpected <u>labor</u> and delivery complications. There are two common types of surgical <u>incision</u>: the <u>classical cesarean incision</u>, which is vertical, and the <u>low segment transverse incision</u>, which is horizontal. Fetal complications are rare. Maternal recovery is longer than with vaginal delivery and complications can include common surgical complications (e.g., infection, hemorrhage, <u>venous thromboembolism</u>) as well as an increased risk of specific mechanical complications in subsequent <u>pregnancies</u>. Patients who have had a cesarean delivery often undergo <u>planned repeat cesarean births</u> for subsequent <u>pregnancies</u>, however, vaginal <u>birth</u> after cesarean delivery is possible in select patients.




See also “<u>Normal labor and delivery</u>,” and “<u>Abnormal labor and delivery</u>.”

## Definitions

* The delivery of a <u>newborn</u> through a vertical or horizontal <u>incision</u> in the lower abdominal and uterine wall

## Indications

> [!TIP]
> There are guidelines detailing <u>indications for cesarean delivery</u> that are based on scientific findings. However, each hospital can individually determine how these indications are interpreted. The well-being of the mother and child should be of the utmost priority.




### Maternal indications




* Primary cesarean delivery

* <u>Placenta praevia</u> totalis

* Refractory <u>HELLP syndrome</u> (<u>hemolysis</u>, <u>elevated liver enzymes</u>, and <u>low platelet count</u>), <u>severe preeclampsia</u>

* Severe uterine abnormalities (e.g., myoma) of the mother

* Maternal skeletal deformities

* Relative:

* Severe maternal disease (e.g., cardiopulmonary disorders, uncontrolled <u>diabetes mellitus</u>)

* Maternal <u>HIV infection</u>

* Severe <u>stress</u> reactions

* Elective cesarean delivery

* Possible indications :

* Fetal head is disproportionately large compared to the maternal <u>pelvis</u>.

* <u>Breech presentation</u> in a <u>nullipara</u> or <u>multiple pregnancy</u>

* Suspected absolute <u>fetal macrosomia</u>

* Secondary cesarean delivery (after <u>PROM</u> and/or onset of phase 1)
* <u>Prolonged labor</u> in:

* <u>Premature birth</u>

* <u>Intraamniotic infection</u>

* Abnormal <u>fetal position</u> (e.g., <u>breech presentation</u>, longitudinal position)

* Maternal exhaustion; ineffective contractions

* Emergency cesarean delivery

* Immediate threat to life of mother

* Severe <u>vaginal bleeding</u> of unknown etiology (suspected <u>placental</u> separation)

* Suspected <u>uterine rupture</u>

* See also “<u>Perimortem cesarean delivery</u>.”





### Fetal indications




* Primary cesarean delivery

* <u>Fetal growth retardation</u> with circulatory <u>depression</u>

* <u>Premature birth</u>, if further <u>risk factors</u> are present, e.g., infection

* Fetal <u>malformations</u> that hinder a natural <u>birth</u> (e.g., severe <u>hydrocephalus</u>)

* <u>Multiple pregnancy</u> with a significant difference in fetal weight

* Emergency cesarean delivery

* Immediate threat to life of fetus

* Pathological <u>CTG</u> (particularly persistent, severe fetal <u>bradycardia</u>)

* Fetal <u>acidosis</u>

* See also “<u>Perimortem cesarean delivery</u>.”





### Cesarean delivery on maternal request (on-demand)




* Overview

* Primary cesarean delivery that is performed on the mother's request in the absence of medical indications

* Medically and ethically acceptable if the patient is well-informed

* Possible reasons include:

* Fear of the <u>pain</u> of <u>labor</u>

* Prior negative <u>labor</u> experience

* Possibility of scheduled delivery

* Concerns about fetal harm during vaginal <u>birth</u>

* Physicians are not obliged to perform a non-medically indicated cesarean delivery and may refer the patient to another <u>obstetrician</u> willing to perform the procedure.

* Approach

* Explore the reasons behind the request

* Address concerns about <u>labor</u> and provide information about prenatal <u>childbirth</u> education, <u>obstetric analgesia</u>, and emotional support during <u>labor</u>

* Lead a balanced discussion about the risks and benefits of cesarean delivery and vaginal <u>birth</u>

* If the patient insists on having a cesarean delivery, schedule the procedure for after 39 weeks of <u>gestation</u>

## Complications

### Maternal




* Infections (i.e., of the <u>endometrium</u>, <u>pelvis</u>, <u>lungs</u>, <u>urinary tract</u>)

* Hemorrhage

* <u>Iatrogenic</u>: surgical injury (e.g., to the bowel, <u>bladder</u>, <u>ureter</u>)

* Postoperative incisional <u>pain</u>

* Assess and monitor for signs of adequate <u>wound healing</u> and infection (see “<u>Postoperative management</u>” for more information)

* <u>Pain management</u> (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u> and/or <u>NSAIDs</u>)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kG1mzR0)

* Neuropathy (due to ilioinguinal and/or <u>iliohypogastric nerve</u> entrapment)

* <u>Thromboembolic events</u>

* <u>Ileus</u> and <u>acute colonic pseudo-obstruction</u>

* Related to the <u>placenta</u> and <u>uterus</u> 

* Abnormal <u>placental</u> attachment

* <u>Uterine rupture</u>

* Impaired uterine regression





### Fetal




* Postnatal <u>transient tachypnea of the newborn</u>

* <u>Acute respiratory distress syndrome</u>





We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.

## Subsequent deliveries

### Mode of delivery after cesarean delivery




Patients who have undergone a previous cesarean delivery have two options for mode of delivery in a subsequent <u>pregnancy</u>: <u>TOLAC</u> and <u>PRCB</u>.




* <u>Trial of labor after cesarean</u> (TOLAC): A planned or attempted <u>childbirth</u> in a mother who has had a prior cesarean delivery.

* Results in <u>vaginal birth after cesarean</u> (VBAC) if successful or a repeat cesarean delivery if unsuccessful.

* Associated with increased risk of rupture of the cesarean <u>scar</u> on the <u>uterus</u>.

* Contraindicated in patients with a history of > 2 prior low-transverse cesarean deliveries or classic cesarean delivery.

* <u>Planned repeat cesarean birth</u> (PRCB): A planned cesarean delivery in a patient who has had a prior cesarean section.
* Multiple cesarean births increase the risk of <u>uterine rupture</u>.





### Decision-making




* <u>Patient-centered</u> decision-making: The decision for <u>TOLAC</u> or <u>PRCB</u> should be made by the patient in collaboration with their provider.

* Factors to consider include:

* <u>TOLAC</u> can only be provided at facilities with the resources for cesarean <u>birth</u>.

* Potential complications associated with <u>TOLAC</u> or <u>PRCB</u> (e.g., risk of <u>uterine rupture</u> is higher in <u>TOLAC</u>), including patient factors that affect the risks and benefits for each route of delivery (e.g., prior <u>uterine rupture</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PK1Wg30)

* Patient's personal preferences, past birthing experiences, and future <u>pregnancy</u> plans

* <u>Probability</u> of successful <u>VBAC</u>  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4K13g30)





### Examples of indications [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pK1Lh30)




* <u>TOLAC</u>

* ≤ 2 previous low-transverse cesarean deliveries

* A previous cesarean delivery with unknown <u>incision</u>, unless there is clinical suspicion of a previous classic cesarean delivery (e.g., cesarean delivery at <u>gestational age</u> < 28 weeks; large lower segment <u>uterine fibroid</u>)

* <u>PRCB</u> (if <u>TOLAC</u> is contraindicated) 

* Patients with contraindications to <u>labor</u> or vaginal <u>birth</u> (e.g., <u>placenta previa</u>)

* Patients with a prior <u>uterine rupture</u>

* Patients with prior uterine <u>incisions</u> at cesarean <u>birth</u> that are associated with an increased risk of <u>intrapartum</u> <u>uterine rupture</u> during <u>TOLAC</u>:

* <u>Classical incision</u> (<u>TOLAC</u> is contraindicated)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NK1-g30)

* T or J <u>incisions</u>

* Transfundal uterine <u>incision</u>





> [!WARNING]
> <u>TOLAC</u> is contraindicated in patients with previous classical cesarean delivery.
