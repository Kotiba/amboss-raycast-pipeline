# Chronic noncancer pain management

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Anesthesiology > Chronic noncancer pain management*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wu0ht3)

---

## Summary

<u>Chronic pain</u> is <u>pain</u> that persists or recurs for more than 3 months. Unlike <u>acute pain</u>, which usually signals an injury or illness and dissipates as the body heals, chronic noncancer <u>pain</u> may not have a clear cause and persists long after an initial injury or illness has resolved. Clinical evaluation of <u>chronic pain</u> includes a comprehensive history, <u>physical examination</u>, <u>medication review</u>, and assessment of psychosocial factors. Diagnostic studies are not routinely recommended but should be considered if a serious underlying pathology or condition requiring specific management is suspected. Patient education and optimization of <u>condition-specific analgesia</u> are core components of <u>chronic pain</u> management. The <u>WHO analgesic ladder</u> can help clinicians select an appropriate <u>pain management</u> strategy based on <u>pain</u> severity and response to existing management. Multimodal <u>nonpharmacological analgesia</u> and/or <u>nonopioid analgesics</u> are the preferred initial management for chronic noncancer <u>pain</u>. <u>Interventional pain management</u> strategies may be beneficial for certain types of <u>chronic pain</u> (e.g., inflammatory or degenerative <u>arthritis</u>, <u>complex regional pain syndrome</u>). For <u>chronic pain</u> refractory to nonopioid management, <u>opioid therapy</u> may be considered only if benefits outweigh risks and additional precautions are taken (i.e., <u>risk mitigation for opioid prescribing</u>). Patients who are prescribed <u>opioid therapy</u> for <u>chronic pain</u> should receive close follow-up, and the risks vs. benefits of treatment should be regularly reassessed; <u>opioids</u> should be tapered or discontinued if the benefits no longer outweigh the risks.




<u>Pain management in palliative care</u> and <u>management of acute exacerbation of chronic pain</u> are detailed separately. For information on psychogenic <u>pain</u>, see “<u>Somatic symptom and related disorders</u>.”

## Management approach

Effective management of chronic noncancer <u>pain</u> often requires a multimodal approach, integrating various strategies tailored to the individual's needs and the type of <u>pain</u>. For <u>pain</u> related to cancer, <u>sickle cell disease</u>, or other high-<u>morbidity</u> illnesses, see “<u>Palliative pain management</u>.” [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-eWDYk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6mWjgN0)




* Optimize the following as needed:

* Management of <u>comorbid mental health conditions</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1jW2zO0)

* <u>Condition-specific pain management</u>

* Provide <u>patient education on chronic pain management</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JYWsJP0)

* Consider enrolling patients into a <u>pain management</u> program (<u>PMP</u>).  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JYWsJP0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kPWmUl0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)

* According to the <u>WHO analgesic ladder</u> approach, initiate nonopioid management of <u>chronic pain</u>.

* Periodically reevaluate treatment <u>efficacy</u> using a validated tool to <u>assess pain</u> and functioning (e.g, the <u>PEG pain scale</u>).  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-eWDYk0)

* For severe or refractory <u>pain</u>, <u>interventional pain management</u> strategies may be appropriate; consult a <u>pain management</u> specialist.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fMWkLN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TMW6LN0)

* For <u>pain</u> refractory to the above measures, consider initiation of <u>opioid therapy</u> only if benefits outweigh risks.

* Establish goals of treatment and an <u>opioid exit strategy</u> before initiation; use shared-decision making.

* Continue nonopioid therapies in conjunction with <u>opioid therapy</u>.

* Do not rapidly taper or abruptly discontinue <u>opioid therapy</u> once started.





> [!TIP]
> A <u>biopsychosocial model</u> of medical care is integral to chronic noncancer <u>pain management</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6mWjgN0)




> [!TIP]
> Maximize the use of nonpharmacologic <u>analgesia</u> and <u>nonopioid analgesics</u> as appropriate for the condition. <u>Opioids</u> should only be initiated if the improvement in <u>pain</u> and function is anticipated to outweigh the risks to the patient. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




> [!WARNING]
> Avoid perpetuating existing racial and ethnic disparities in <u>chronic pain</u> management. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

## Opioid management

### Considerations before initiating <u>opioids</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w3WhkO0)




* Perform a thorough evaluation of chronic noncancer <u>pain</u>.

* Ensure <u>nonopioid therapy for chronic noncancer pain</u> has been maximized and alternative treatment options have been trialed.

* Check institutional and state guidelines for indications for <u>opioid</u> prescribing.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)

* Determine if the patient has <u>contraindications to opioids</u> or <u>risk factors for opioid-related harm</u>.

* Discuss benefits and risks of opioid therapy with patient; ensure benefits outweigh risks, given type of <u>pain</u> and goals of treatment.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Benefits: possible minor reduction in <u>pain</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Risks

* <u>Adverse effects of opioids</u>, including the development of <u>opioid use disorder</u>

* <u>Pain</u> may worsen because of <u>opioid-induced hyperalgesia</u>.

* May impact employment opportunities, especially in safety-critical jobs  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* If an <u>opioid</u> prescription is deemed appropriate, follow risk mitigation practices.





> [!WARNING]
> Ensure patients are aware that chronic use of <u>opioids</u> can interfere with employment opportunities, especially in safety-critical jobs. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




> [!TIP]
> Before initiating <u>opioid therapy</u>, take a full <u>patient history</u>, screen for <u>comorbid mental health conditions</u>, and perform <u>medication reconciliation</u> to determine patient suitability for <u>opioid therapy</u> and to reduce <u>modifiable risk factors</u>.




### Risk mitigation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)




Consider risk mitigation for opioid prescribing prior to starting <u>opioids</u> and at each follow-up appointment.




* Assess for the development of <u>contraindications to opioids</u> or <u>risk factors for opioid-related harm</u>.

* Review:

* State and federal laws relevant to prescribing controlled substances

* The state's <u>prescription drug monitoring program</u> (<u>PDMP</u>)

* <u>Principles of prescribing for older adults</u>, if relevant

* Prescribe <u>naloxone</u> and provide education on its use if any of the following are present: [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zVWrC40)

* State requires coprescription

* <u>Risk factors for opioid overdose</u> in patient or household members

* <u>Sleep-disordered breathing</u>

* Provide <u>counseling on the use of prescription opioids</u>.

* Consider referral to a <u>pain</u> specialist and/or the use of <u>buprenorphine</u> for <u>pain management</u> in patients who: [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iL1Jxh0)

* Require long-term daily <u>opioids</u>

* Are unable to taper or discontinue <u>opioids</u>

* Are at risk of <u>opioid use disorder</u> or have a history of <u>opioid use disorder</u>

* Avoid concurrent use of <u>opioids</u> and <u>sedative-hypnotic</u> medications (e.g., <u>benzodiazepines</u>, sleeping aids, <u>muscle relaxants</u>) or prescribe with extreme care.





> [!TIP]
> Ensure patients with <u>risk factors for opioid overdose</u> have been provided with <u>naloxone</u> and educated on how to use it. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




> [!WARNING]
> The prescription of more than a 90-day supply of <u>opioids</u> is associated with a dose-dependent increase in the risk of <u>adverse effects of opioid use</u>, including <u>opioid use disorder</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




#### Aberrant drug-related behaviors




<u>Aberrant drug-related behaviors</u> may suggest abuse, misuse, or diversion of <u>opioids</u>.




* Concerning behaviors include:  [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_mW5RN0)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3PWSel0)

* Obtaining medication from nonmedical sources

* Requesting prescriptions early

* Frequently reporting loss of prescriptions

* Missing or canceling appointments at which no <u>opioid</u> refill is anticipated

* Obtaining prescriptions from multiple providers

* Presenting to the emergency department for medications

* Managing patients with <u>aberrant drug-related behaviors</u>

* Do not discharge patients from care unless they are violent or threatening.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPWuel0)

* Evaluate for <u>opioid use disorder</u> and/or other <u>substance use disorders</u>; if present, provide treatment with a multidisciplinary team.





#### Urine drug monitoring for opioid therapy [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D3W1kO0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2MWTLN0)




* Consider urine toxicology prior to starting <u>opioid therapy</u> and at least annually thereafter.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D3W1kO0)

* Communicate clearly with patients to reduce misunderstandings.

* Explain urine drug monitoring procedures and their purpose in maintaining <u>patient safety</u>.

* Ask nonjudgmentally about the nature and timing of all recent substance use before ordering a test.

* Interpret results with care.

* Screening immunoassays have limitations; ask patients nonjudgmentally about any unexpected results.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D3W1kO0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2MWTLN0)

* If results are unexplained and/or will affect management, obtain <u>confirmatory testing</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D3W1kO0)

* Do not use results punitively (e.g., discontinuation of <u>opioids</u>, discharge from practice).





### Initiating <u>opioids</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-eWDYk0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)




* Establish the following, using <u>shared decision-making</u>.

* How and when treatment <u>efficacy</u> will be assessed: Patients should experience functional gains as measured, e.g., with the <u>PEG pain scale</u>.  [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w3WhkO0)

* An <u>opioid exit strategy</u>: how <u>opioids</u> will be discontinued or how to transition to <u>medication-assisted treatment</u> if the risks outweigh the benefits

* Create a <u>controlled substance agreement</u> and ensure <u>informed consent</u> has been obtained in the appropriate form.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)

* Provide <u>anticipatory guidance</u> to avoid <u>adverse effects of opioid use</u>.

* Establish dosing and frequency based on the pharmacology of <u>opioids</u> (see “<u>Treatment of pain</u>” for specific dosages). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Initiate therapy with immediate-release <u>short-acting opioids</u>.

* Start with a low dose and titrate to the lowest effective dose.

* In patients with hepatic or renal impairment, consider longer dosing intervals.

* Prescribe as needed rather than scheduling doses.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Create a plan for events requiring <u>acute-on-chronic pain management</u>.

* Follow-up within 1–4 weeks after initiating therapy to evaluate for improved functioning and <u>pain control</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)





### Ongoing therapy [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)




* Follow-up:

* At least every 3 months to evaluate <u>efficacy</u> and safety [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* More frequently for patients with a high risk of overdose or misuse  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Continue to:

* Maximize <u>nonopioid management of chronic noncancer pain</u>.

* Follow <u>risk mitigation for opioid prescribing</u> at every visit, including consideration of <u>urine drug monitoring for opioid therapy</u> at least annually.

* Maintain accurate records at every visit, including:

* Calculated daily <u>MME</u>

* Average daily <u>pain</u> level

* Functional assessment (e.g., ability to perform <u>ADLs</u>, score on <u>PEG pain scale</u>)

* Adverse effects

* <u>Aberrant drug-related behaviors</u>

* Confirmation of review of the state's <u>PDMP</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* Convert from immediate-release to extended-release <u>opioids</u> only if:

* <u>Pain</u> is ongoing, severe, and constant

* Patient has <u>opioid tolerance</u> (when relevant)  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XMW9MN0)

* If transitioning from one formulation to another, reduce the <u>MME</u> of the new formulation initially.





> [!WARNING]
> There is a paucity of evidence supporting > 12 months of <u>opioid therapy</u> for <u>chronic pain</u> management. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w3WhkO0)




> [!WARNING]
> Prescriptions ≥ 50 <u>MME</u>/day are unlikely to improve <u>pain</u> and also increase the risk of <u>adverse effects of opioids</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




### Tapering




The decision to taper chronic opioid therapy (and, possibly, to discontinue therapy) should be made on an individual basis using <u>shared decision-making</u>, weighing up the <u>benefits and risks of opioid therapy</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)




> [!TIP]
> The goal of tapering may be complete discontinuation or dose reduction to improve the risk versus benefit profile. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




#### Indications [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w3WhkO0)




Consider tapering in the following scenarios:




* Patient request

* Resolution of <u>pain</u>

* Lack of response to therapy, e.g.:

* Inadequate improvement in quality of life, function, or <u>pain</u> scores

* Escalation of dosages without improvement

* <u>Adverse effects of opioids</u> impacting quality of life

* Evidence of <u>diversion of prescription drugs</u> or misuse (<u>aberrant drug-related behaviors</u>)

* Overdose event or concern for impending overdose  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* <u>Risk factors for opioid-related harm</u>





> [!WARNING]
> Consult a <u>pain</u> specialist if considering tapering <u>opioids</u> in pregnant individuals. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




#### Process [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jPW_el0)




* Use a multidisciplinary approach.

* Avoid rapid tapering and sudden discontinuation.

* Advise patients that <u>pain</u> may worsen initially.

* First, reduce the dose per administration.

* Maximize <u>nonopioid therapy for chronic noncancer pain</u>.

* Determine tapering speed based on duration of <u>opioid</u> use:  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)

* < 1 year: Taper at ≤ 10% of the original dose per week.

* ≥ 1 year: Taper at ≤ 10% of the original dose per month.

* Slow the taper if signs of withdrawal develop.

* Once the lowest dose per administration has been achieved:

* Gradually increase the dosing interval.

* Discontinue <u>opioids</u> when the interval is less than once daily.

* Follow-up monthly during tapering

* Consider the patient's wishes to slow or pause the taper.

* Evaluate for and manage complications of <u>opioid</u> tapering





> [!WARNING]
> Do not rapidly taper or discontinue <u>opioids</u> unless the patient is at imminent risk for life-threatening complications such as overdose. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




> [!WARNING]
> Inform patients that they are at increased risk for overdose during and shortly after tapering because of decreased tolerance. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




> [!TIP]
> Most patients on <u>long-term opioid therapy</u> who agree to taper or discontinue <u>opioids</u> experience overall satisfaction, with improved quality of life and no increase in <u>pain</u>, but may experience short-term effects of <u>hyperalgesia</u>, <u>insomnia</u>, and <u>agitation</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




#### Management of complications [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FC1gFQ0)




* Development of <u>opioid withdrawal symptoms</u>

* Slow or pause the taper.

* Consider <u>medications for opioid withdrawal</u>.

* Inability to taper or discontinue because of ongoing <u>pain</u>

* Maximize nonopioid therapies for chronic noncancer <u>pain</u>

* Consider referral to a <u>pain</u> specialist and/or the use of <u>buprenorphine</u> for <u>pain management</u>.

* Unmasking of <u>opioid use disorder</u>: See “<u>Treatment of opioid use disorder</u>.”

* Development or unmasking of <u>depression</u> and/or <u>anxiety</u>

* See “<u>Major depressive disorder</u>” and “<u>Anxiety disorders</u>.”

* Involve mental health professionals in care as needed.

* Overdose due to decreased tolerance: Prescribe <u>naloxone</u>. [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zVWrC40)

* Development of <u>aberrant drug-related behaviors</u>: Engage <u>multidisciplinary team care</u> and address any underlying <u>substance use disorders</u>.
