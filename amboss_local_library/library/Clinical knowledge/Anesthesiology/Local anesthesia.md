# Local anesthesia

## Summary

Local anesthesia refers to the reversible numbing of <u>pain</u> conduction by blocking nerve endings at and around the site of administration. It is commonly used in both adults and children for minor procedures and <u>wound repair</u> and is further subdivided into <u>topical anesthesia</u> and <u>infiltration anesthesia</u>, which is the loss of sensation in a localized <u>superficial</u> area via injection of a <u>local anesthetic agent</u> into subcutaneous or <u>submucosal</u> tissue. Contraindications include <u>allergy</u> to the class of <u>anesthetic agent</u>. The preparation consists of performing a baseline <u>neurovascular examination</u> and selecting the appropriate <u>local anesthetic</u>. Complications are rare.




See also “<u>Regional anesthesia</u>” and “<u>Local anesthetic agents</u>.”

---

## Indications

Local anesthesia is used to decrease the <u>pain</u> associated with minor procedures. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3wYSir)




* Single point infiltration : localized painful procedures (e.g., <u>lumbar puncture</u>, <u>incision and drainage</u>)

* <u>Local anesthesia for wound repair</u> : larger <u>lacerations</u> (i.e., requiring more than one <u>anesthetic</u> injection)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Topical anesthesia</u> may help reduce <u>pain</u> and <u>anxiety</u> in children before minor invasive procedures (e.g., IV placement, <u>wound repair</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0o1e030)

## Topical anesthesia

* Definition: the loss of sensation to <u>skin</u> or <u>mucosa</u> by direct application of a <u>local anesthetic agent</u> (e.g., in the form of a gel, ointment, spray, or patch) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3wYSir)

* Indications: typically used for <u>superficial</u> and localized procedures, particularly in children

* Good for anesthetizing small <u>lacerations</u>, intact <u>skin</u>, or mucus membranes

* Common applications include minor <u>wound repair</u>, <u>peripheral IV</u> placement, and <u>foley catheter placement</u>

* Choice of agent is based on the intended location, e.g.: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3wYSir)

* <u>Skin</u>: <u>eutectic mixture of local anesthetic</u> (<u>EMLA</u>) cream, <u>lidocaine/epinephrine/tetracaine</u> (LET) gel

* Ophthalmic: <u>tetracaine</u>, proparacaine

* Oropharyngeal: <u>benzocaine</u> spray, viscous <u>lidocaine</u>

* Genitourinary: viscous <u>lidocaine</u>
