# Preoperative management

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Anesthesiology > Preoperative management*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aj0Q_f)

---

## Summary

The purpose of a <u>preoperative evaluation</u> is to assess whether a patient is medically optimized for the <u>stress</u> of <u>surgery</u> and to identify reversible factors that may increase the perioperative risk for the patient. Important components of the <u>preoperative evaluation</u> are a thorough history and <u>physical examination</u>, identification of patients at greatest risk of a perioperative <u>adverse event</u>, and appropriate diagnostic testing. <u>Preoperative preparation</u> enhances surgical outcomes by improving the patient's functional status, mitigating identified <u>risk factors</u>, and optimizing perioperative medication regimens. This includes optimization of medical treatment, specialty consultation for advanced disease management, patient education on lifestyle modifications, and instruction on day-of-<u>surgery</u> oral intake and medication use. The extent of patient evaluation and preparation varies according to both the proposed <u>surgery</u> and the patient's functional status. Low-risk patients having low-risk <u>surgery</u> seldom need testing or intervention, while patients with a high <u>disease burden</u> having high-risk <u>surgery</u> often require extensive testing, consultation, and possible postponement of <u>surgery</u>. Patients having emergent <u>surgery</u> still require evaluation even though opportunities for management beyond resuscitation are typically limited.




See also “<u>Postoperative management</u>.”

## Overview

A <u>preoperative evaluation</u> is performed to determine whether a patient is in optimal health for a planned surgical procedure and its extent depends on the urgency of the <u>surgery</u>.




### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JNcsX10)




The following applies primarily to patients having elective <u>surgery</u>. See “<u>Emergency preoperative evaluation</u>” for patients needing emergency <u>surgery</u>.




* Confirm the surgical procedure and indication for <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>Evaluate surgical procedural risk</u> based on <u>surgery</u> type and urgency.

* Evaluate the patient's health and individual risk of perioperative complications. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)

* Perform a <u>preoperative clinical evaluation</u> with a complete history and <u>physical examination</u>.

* <u>Assess preoperative functional capacity</u>.

* Use validated <u>preoperative risk stratification tools</u>, e.g., <u>ASA-PS</u>, <u>RCRI</u>, to improve perioperative risk estimation.

* Order <u>preoperative diagnostic testing</u> based on combined estimated perioperative risk.

* Consider more focused evaluation and management of high-risk systems (e.g., see “<u>Preoperative cardiac assessment</u>”).

* Make recommendations to the surgical team, for example:

* Proceed with <u>surgery</u> if the patient's comorbidities are optimally treated.

* Optimize treatment of comorbidities if necessary.

* Order advanced testing or specialty consultation if underlying health concerns are unclear or complex.

* Consider delaying <u>surgery</u> if diagnostics and/or treatment are expected to influence perioperative risk.

* Coordinate <u>perioperative preparation</u> steps (e.g., <u>perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis</u>, <u>fasting</u>, <u>preoperative medication management</u>).





> [!TIP]
> Evaluate the procedural risk and the patient's health and functional capacity in order to: decide whether to proceed with <u>surgery</u> without delay, obtain further testing and/or specialist consultation, or consider delaying or canceling the procedure. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)




### Multidisciplinary team roles




* Medical practitioner [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)

* Determines if the patient is medically optimized for the surgical procedure

* Initiates measures to optimize the patient's health if necessary

* Alerts the perioperative team to special health concerns and abnormal test results

* <u>Surgeon</u>

* Obtains consent

* <u>Informed consent</u> is required prior to all elective procedures.

* <u>Surgical consent</u> requires assessment and communication of perioperative risk to the patient (i.e., <u>shared decision-making</u>).  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RMclo10)

* Determines if <u>surgery</u> can be delayed for further optimization  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Pb1WF20)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4b13F20)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kb1mF20)

* Anesthesiologist: chooses the appropriate <u>anesthetic</u> technique based on patient health and procedural requirements





### Emergency preoperative evaluation [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qmcCg10)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/85cON10)




Patients who need emergency <u>surgery</u> often require simultaneous resuscitation and assessment (e.g., via an <u>ABCDE approach</u>).




* Focus the clinical evaluation on conditions associated with high <u>morbidity</u>.

* Order diagnostic testing that reflects the dual roles of assessment and resuscitation, including: 

* <u>CBC</u> and <u>CMP</u>

* <u>ABG</u> and <u>lactate</u>

* <u>ECG</u> if <u>ASCVD risk factors</u> are present

* <u>Coagulation panel</u>, <u>type and screen</u>, and <u>crossmatch</u> if active or anticipated bleeding or suspected <u>coagulopathy</u>.

* Notify members of the perioperative team (e.g., <u>surgery</u>, anesthesiology, <u>ICU</u>) of urgent findings.





> [!TIP]
> <u>Preoperative evaluation</u> of a patient having emergency <u>surgery</u> includes assessing the adequacy of ongoing resuscitation. Continuous reassessment of critical organ function (e.g., tissue <u>perfusion</u>, oxygenation, coagulation) may be necessary.

## Evaluating patient health

### Goals [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Db11D20)




* Assess whether current acute and chronic illnesses are being optimally managed.

* Begin treatment of untreated or undertreated medical conditions.

* Identify high-risk patients who may benefit from:

* Advanced diagnostic testing

* Medical specialist referral

* Delay in <u>surgery</u>

* Higher levels of postoperative care, e.g., <u>ICU</u> admission

### Preoperative functional capacity assessment [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Db11D20)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ENc8110)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8b1Ov20)




Limited functional capacity is associated with an increased risk of perioperative <u>morbidity</u> and mortality. Several techniques are available to estimate functional capacity. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mb1V820)




* Subjective patient reporting of exercise capacity  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mb1V820)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vNcA110)

* Technique: The patient's subjective report of attainable activity is reported in the <u>metabolic equivalents</u> (<u>METs</u>) associated historically with that activity.

* Activities associated with ≥ 4 <u>METs</u>

* Climbing a flight of stairs

* Walking up a hill

* Performing strenuous household tasks

* Activities associated with < 4 <u>METs</u>

* Walking at 2–3 mph

* Golfing with a cart

* Slow ballroom dancing

* Findings: Exercise capacity of < 4 <u>METs</u> is associated with increased risk of perioperative complication

* Duke Activity Status Index (DASI) [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ENc8110)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vNcA110)

* Description: a validated, self-administered questionnaire that assesses the patient's functional capacity [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zb1rw20)

* Technique: A 16 item questionnaire completed by the patient results in a score of 0–58

* Findings: A DASI score ≤ 34 predicts an increased risk of major perioperative <u>adverse events</u>.

* <u>Cardiopulmonary exercise testing</u> (<u>CPET</u>) [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_b15w20)

* Technique: Laboratory measurement of the patient's maximum <u>VO<sub>2</sub></u> during exercise

* Findings: No clear threshold for increased perioperative risk has been established using <u>CPET</u> data.





> [!WARNING]
> A patient who cannot achieve and maintain 4 <u>METs</u> is at an increased risk for perioperative complications. Additional evaluation may be warranted. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)




> [!WARNING]
> A DASI score ≤ 34 consistently predicts an increased risk of major perioperative <u>adverse events</u>. [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vNcA110)

## Risk stratification tools

Validated risk <u>stratification</u> tools identify which patients may benefit from medical intervention, <u>preoperative diagnostic testing</u>, and/or consultation prior to intermediate or high-risk <u>surgery</u>. The derived scores from these tools also allow quick communication about patient risk between perioperative team members.




### The American Society of Anesthesiologists (<u>ASA</u>) classification [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Db11D20)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GNcBc10)




* Description: classification based on subjective reporting of the severity of the patient's comorbid disease

* Clinical application: all perioperative patients

* Key finding: An <u>ASA status</u> ≥ 3 is associated with ≥ 1% perioperative risk of mortality; the patient may be considered at high risk.

### Revised cardiac risk index (<u>RCRI</u>) [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HmcKS10)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7mc4S10)




* Description: a simple calculation using 6 easily obtained factors to predict the risk of <u>major adverse cardiac events</u> (<u>MACE</u>) during hospitalization following noncardiac <u>surgery</u>

* Clinical applications

* Screening for all patients undergoing major <u>surgery</u> to determine the risk of <u>MACE</u>

* Determining the need and priority of cardiac testing in patients with known <u>CAD</u> or <u>ASCVD risk factors</u> (See “<u>Preoperative cardiac assessment</u>.")

* Key finding: An <u>RCRI</u> ≥ 2 is associated with > 1% chance of <u>MACE</u>; the patient may be considered at high risk.

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qmcCg10)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




* Low-risk <u>surgery</u>

* Low-risk patients: Generally, no routine diagnostic testing is required.

* High-risk patients: Consider testing as required for the routine <u>management of chronic conditions</u>.

* High- or intermediate-risk <u>surgery</u>

* All patients: See “<u>Preoperative testing for intermediate- or high-risk surgery</u>” for specific indications.

* High-risk patients: Consider additional specialist consultation and advanced testing based on individual factors.





> [!TIP]
> Order diagnostic testing based on the combined risk derived from <u>surgical procedural risk evaluation</u>, <u>preoperative clinical evaluation</u>, and <u>preoperative risk stratification tools</u>. Avoid broad, nondirected testing. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)




### Investigations for intermediate- or high-risk <u>surgery</u>




* Selective testing based on individual indications is preferred.  [[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nb17820)

* More specialized testing may be indicated depending on the presence and severity of conditions identified during the <u>preoperative clinical evaluation</u> and risk <u>stratification</u>; see, e.g., “<u>Preoperative cardiac assessment</u>” and “<u>Preoperative pulmonary assessment</u>.”

## Cardiac evaluation and management

### <u>Coronary artery disease</u> (<u>CAD</u>)




#### Management approach for known or suspected <u>CAD</u>   [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)




* If <u>surgery</u> is emergent: Proceed directly to <u>surgery</u> but identify <u>risk factors</u> that affect <u>perioperative management</u>.

* If <u>clinical features of acute coronary syndrome</u> are present: Consult cardiology, begin <u>ACS management</u>, and discuss surgical timing with the multidisciplinary team.

* For all other patients: Calculate the risk of a <u>major adverse cardiac event</u>.  

* Risk of <u>MACE</u> < 1% (e.g., <u>RCRI</u> ≤ 1): Proceed to <u>surgery</u> without further testing.

* Risk of <u>MACE</u> ≥ 1% (e.g., <u>RCRI</u> ≥ 2): Assess functional capacity.

* Functional capacity ≥ 4 <u>METs</u>: Proceed to <u>surgery</u> without further testing.

* Functional capacity < 4 <u>METs</u> or unknown: Consider <u>stress</u> testing.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WNcPZ10)





#### Advanced testing for <u>CAD</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7mc4S10)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WNcPZ10)




Preoperative <u>stress</u> testing, <u>CT coronary angiography</u>, and <u>coronary angiography</u> in asymptomatic individuals remain controversial.  [[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WNcPZ10)[[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dNcoZ10)




* <u>Cardiac stress testing</u>: reasonable in patients with an elevated risk of <u>MACE</u> and reduced functional capacity

* Patient is unable to exercise: <u>pharmacological stress test</u>  (e.g., <u>dobutamine</u> <u>stress echocardiography</u>, <u>radionuclide myocardial perfusion imaging</u> with <u>dipyridamole</u>, <u>adenosine</u>, or <u>regadenoson</u>)

* Patient is able to exercise: <u>exercise stress test</u> (e.g., exercise <u>stress echocardiography</u>)

* <u>Angiography</u>: not routinely recommended in patients with <u>stable CAD</u> (See “<u>Revascularization for stable CAD</u>.”)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)

* Biomarkers: <u>BNP</u> and <u>NT-proBNP</u> screening are increasingly used to assess perioperative risk and guide the management of patients with known cardiovascular disease. > 300 pg/mL [[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dNcoZ10)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uncpE10)





> [!TIP]
> In asymptomatic patients, perform advanced cardiac testing only when results will affect the decision to operate or the <u>perioperative management</u>.




#### Elective <u>surgery</u> after <u>coronary angioplasty</u> [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d5coQ10)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/F7cgnd0)




Discontinuation of <u>P2Y12 receptor blockers</u> (and sometimes <u>aspirin</u>) may be required before <u>surgery</u>. In patients with a cardiac <u>stent</u>, the risks of <u>stent thrombosis</u> must be balanced against the risk of bleeding or delaying <u>surgery</u>.




* <u>Balloon angioplasty</u> (no <u>stent</u>): Delay elective <u>surgery</u> for two weeks.

* <u>Bare metal stent</u>: Delay elective <u>surgery</u> for 30 days.

* <u>Drug-eluting stent</u>: Delay elective <u>surgery</u> for six months postplacement if possible.





> [!TIP]
> In patients with a coronary <u>stent</u>, discuss holding <u>aspirin</u> perioperatively with the <u>surgeon</u>; discontinuation is often not required.




### Other conditions




* <u>Congestive heart failure</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qNcCX10)

* Obtain an <u>echocardiogram</u> if there are new or progressive symptoms (e.g., worsening <u>dyspnea</u>, <u>chest pain</u>, <u>syncope</u>).

* Consider an <u>echocardiogram</u> in stable patients if it has been > 12 months since the last one.

* Consider measuring <u>BNP</u> or <u>NT-proBNP</u> if cardiac dysfunction is known or suspected.

* Consider delaying intermediate- or high-risk <u>surgery</u> if there is newly diagnosed <u>heart failure</u> to allow improvement in ventricular function.

* Presence of a <u>cardiac implantable electronic device</u> (<u>CIED</u>) [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U5cbj10)[[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Enc8E10)

* Communicate with the <u>CIED</u> team to determine the type and functional status of the device.

* Document whether the patient is pacemaker dependent.  [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U5cbj10)

* Document the default <u>CIED</u> function if a magnet is applied.

* Valvular disease: Obtain an <u>echocardiogram</u> if symptoms are progressive or it has been > 12 months since the last <u>echocardiogram</u>.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vX1A-20)

* <u>Severe aortic stenosis</u>: Consider <u>aortic valve replacement</u> (open or <u>TAVI</u>) prior to noncardiac <u>surgery</u>.

* Severe <u>mitral stenosis</u>: Consider percutaneous mitral <u>commissurotomy</u> if the patient is symptomatic or <u>pulmonary artery</u> pressure is > 50 mm Hg.

* Aortic or <u>mitral regurgitation</u>: Treatment is generally not required prior to <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>Pulmonary hypertension</u>: Consult a <u>pulmonary hypertension</u> specialist for possible optimization prior to intermediate- or high-risk <u>surgery</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tB0Xci)

## Pulmonary evaluation and management

### General principles




* The <u>preoperative evaluation</u> of pulmonary disease is primarily clinical.

* Assess for <u>risk factors for postoperative pulmonary complications</u>.

* <u>Pulmonary function testing</u> is only necessary in select cases.





### Disease-specific interventions




* Acute respiratory tract infection [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/f5ckj10)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T5c6j10)

* An acute infection increases the risk of perioperative pulmonary and <u>airway</u> complications.

* Consider delaying <u>surgery</u> to treat the infection.

* <u>Obstructive sleep apnea</u> (<u>OSA</u>) [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Srcygd0)[[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eNcxZ10)

* Screen all patients for <u>OSA</u> using a validated tool, e.g., <u>STOP-BANG</u>.

* Evaluate and document <u>CPAP</u> and BIPAP use.

* Optimize treatment of moderate to severe <u>OSA</u> that is untreated or undertreated.  [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eNcxZ10)

* Chronic pulmonary disease (e.g., <u>asthma</u>, <u>COPD</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eNcxZ10)

* Stable pulmonary disease and adequate oxygenation: Further testing is typically not necessary.

* Unstable pulmonary disease, impaired oxygenation or ventilation: Consider delaying <u>surgery</u> to allow for further assessment and treatment.

* Advanced <u>COPD</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sB0tXi)

* Routine <u>pulmonary function tests</u> are obtained prior to <u>lung</u> reduction <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>Cardiopulmonary exercise testing</u> may be considered if <u>pulmonary function testing</u> is equivocal.

## Evaluation and management of other systems

### <u>Malnourishment</u> [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K5cUO10)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/65cjO10)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tzc6HU0)




* Preoperative nutrition screening is recommended for all patients undergoing major <u>surgery</u>.

* Consult a dietitian for formal dietary assessment if any of the following are present:

* <u>BMI</u> < 18.5 kg/m<sup>2</sup>

* Unplanned weight loss > 10% of body weight in the last 6 months

* Decrease in dietary intake of > 50% in the last week

* Serum <u>albumin</u> < 3 g/dL

* Obvious muscle wasting, loss of <u>subcutaneous fat</u>, or reduced ability to perform <u>activities of daily living</u> [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tzc6HU0)

* Provide <u>specialized nutritional support</u> (preferably <u>enteral nutrition</u>) to at-risk patients prior to <u>surgery</u> to optimize their <u>nutritional status</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Malnutrition</u> is associated with a higher risk of postoperative mortality and <u>morbidity</u>, e.g., infectious complications and <u>ICU</u> admission. Arrange formal dietary assessment for all patients with <u>risk factors</u> or signs of <u>malnutrition</u> on screening. [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K5cUO10)[[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v7cAnd0)[[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/D7c1Ld0)




### Hepatic disorders [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[50]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M5cMk10)[[51]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hMcco10)




* Acute <u>hepatitis</u>: Delay elective <u>surgery</u> until there is documented improvement in <u>LFTs</u>.

* <u>Chronic liver disease</u>: Calculate the <u>MELD score</u> or Child class for the <u>liver</u> disease. 

* <u>MELD score</u> < 15 or <u>Child class A</u> and B: Optimize <u>encephalopathy</u>, <u>coagulopathy</u>, and <u>ascites</u> prior to <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>MELD score</u> ≥ 15: May require <u>liver transplantation</u> prior to elective <u>surgery</u> for optimal outcomes

* <u>MELD score</u> > 20 or <u>Child class C</u>: Delay nonurgent <u>surgery</u> until the Child or <u>MELD score</u> improves.





### Hematologic disorders [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)




* <u>Venous thromboembolism</u> (<u>VTE</u>)

* Assess all patients for <u>risk factors for VTE</u>.  [[52]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/j5c_P10)

* Recommend appropriate <u>VTE prophylaxis</u> based on bleeding risk and risk of <u>thrombosis</u>.

* <u>Bleeding disorders</u>: Optimize in consultation with the managing hematologist.

* <u>Anemia</u>  [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45c3410)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/u7cpnd0)

* Obtain <u>iron studies</u> in patients with <u>anemia</u> if planned <u>surgery</u> is associated with significant blood loss.

* Consider delaying elective <u>surgery</u> to correct <u>anemia</u> with a treatable cause (e.g., <u>iron therapy for iron deficiency anemia</u>). [[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/u7cpnd0)

* <u>Sickle cell anemia</u>: Optimize in consultation with the managing hematologist.  [[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Q5cuP10)[[56]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vncAE10)





### Endocrine disorders




* <u>Diabetes</u>

* Consider delay if current <u>HbA1c</u> is 8–9 %. > 8% [[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O5cI410)

* Investigate and document associated complications, including <u>gastroparesis</u>, nephropathy, and neuropathy.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)

* Chronic <u>glucocorticoid</u> use

* Document the dosage and duration of <u>glucocorticoid</u> administration.

* Consider optimizing <u>steroid stress dosing</u> if indicated. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NcjX10)





### Neurological disorders




* <u>Stroke</u>: Consider delay if <u>surgery</u> is planned < 9 months after <u>CVA</u>.  [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/o5c0O10)[[59]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E7c8nd0)

* Cognitive impairment [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Nc2Z10)[[60]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/n5c7k10)[[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L5cwk10)

* Cognitive impairment is a <u>risk factor</u> for postoperative <u>delirium</u> and is associated with increased perioperative <u>morbidity</u> and mortality.  [[62]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wnchv10)

* Screen older adults at risk for <u>dementia</u> with a standardized tool, e.g., <u>Mini-Cog</u>.  [[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L5cwk10)

* Recommend consultation or perioperative comanagement with <u>internal medicine</u> or geriatrics specialists for <u>delirium management</u>.





### Renal disorders




#### General principles [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LTcwrb0)




* Patients with <u>chronic kidney disease</u> (<u>CKD</u>) are at increased risk of <u>acute kidney injury</u> (<u>AKI</u>) following <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>Complications of CKD</u> can impact postoperative recovery.





#### <u>Management of CKD</u> [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LTcwrb0)




* Consider consulting nephrology to help optimize pre-operative <u>kidney</u> functioning.

* Check preoperative <u>kidney function</u> with <u>eGFR</u> approx. one month before <u>surgery</u>. [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

* Treat associated <u>hypertension</u> (see “<u>Management of ASCVD risk factors in CKD</u>”).  [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

* Patients on <u>renal replacement therapy</u>

* Patients on <u>hemodialysis</u>: Perform dialysis on the day of <u>surgery</u> if possible.  [[66]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TD16dQ0)

* Patients on <u>peritoneal dialysis</u>

* Additional preoperative dialysis may be recommended.

* Patients having intraabdominal <u>surgery</u> may temporarily require <u>hemodialysis</u>.  [[67]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OA1IPj0)

* Coordinate care with specialists for the following:

* <u>Preoperative cardiac assessment</u>  [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

* <u>Perioperative bleeding risk assessment</u> and anticoagulation management [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

* <u>Anemia of CKD</u> [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

* <u>Indications for pRBC transfusion</u>  [[68]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HkcK6c0)

* Continuation of the patient's <u>renal replacement therapy</u> schedule [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)





#### Prevention of postoperative AKI [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LTcwrb0)




* Consider using a risk-<u>stratification</u> tool to screen patients for risk of postoperative <u>AKI</u>.

* <u>AKI</u> risk after <u>surgery</u> score  [[69]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1_12Mj0)

* Dialysis Risk After Cardiac <u>Surgery</u> Score (Cleveland Clinic Score)

* Optimize <u>kidney</u> <u>perfusion</u> for patients at risk of perioperative <u>hypotension</u> (e.g., <u>shock</u>).  [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[70]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8y1OSj0)

* Perform a <u>clinical evaluation of volume status</u> and blood pressure.

* Provide <u>IV fluid therapy</u> and <u>vasopressors</u>, as needed, to prevent <u>hypotension</u>.  [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[70]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8y1OSj0)

* Avoid <u>nephrotoxic medications</u>, if possible.  [[71]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LAbw4w)[[72]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Fy1gSj0)

* Adjust dosages of <u>renally cleared medications</u>, as needed.

* Optimize glycemic control; see “<u>Inpatient management of hyperglycemia</u>.”  [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>CKD</u> is associated with increased <u>postoperative complications</u> and mortality and is a <u>risk factor</u> for developing postoperative <u>AKI</u>. [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b9bHND)[[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Op1Ip30)

### <u>Connective tissue diseases</u> (<u>CTDs</u>)




#### General principles [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)




* The risk of <u>postoperative complications</u> (e.g., cardiovascular, infection, <u>VTE</u>) is increased in patients with:

* Certain <u>CTDs</u>

* Current use of medications (e.g., <u>biologics</u>) for <u>CTDs</u>

* Delay elective <u>surgery</u> when possible until disease control has been optimized.

* Consider preoperative specialist consultation to assist with assessment and management of perioperative risks, e.g.:

* Rheumatology

* Cardiology

* Pulmonology

* Anesthesia





#### Screening for <u>cervical spine</u> instability




* Indications  [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)

* Severe, uncontrolled, and/or chronic <u>CTDs</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Rheumatoid arthritis of the cervical spine</u>

* <u>Ankylosing spondylitis</u>

* Abnormal <u>neurological examination</u> (e.g., signs of <u>myelopathy</u>)

* Modality

* Normal <u>neurological examination</u>: <u>plain radiography</u> of the <u>cervical spine</u>  [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)

* Abnormal <u>neurological examination</u> or abnormal <u>plain radiography</u> of the <u>cervical spine</u>: <u>MRI</u> [[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kA1mPj0)





#### <u>Perioperative management</u> of <u>immunosuppressive therapy</u> [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)[[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Zp1ZL30)




* Use of <u>immunosuppressive therapies</u> in the perioperative period increases the risk of infection and delays <u>wound healing</u>.

* Discuss with rheumatology, as the decision to stop medications depends on planned <u>surgery</u> and the severity of the underlying <u>CTD</u>.  [[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Zp1ZL30)

* There is a paucity of patient information for many types of <u>surgery</u>; the exception is orthopedic <u>surgery</u>, for which <u>immunosuppressive therapy</u> suggestions include : [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)[[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Zp1ZL30)

* <u>Conventional DMARDs</u>: usually continued

* <u>Biologics</u>: usually held  [[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Zp1ZL30)

* <u>Glucocorticoids</u>: usually continued [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)

* If possible, total daily <u>prednisone</u> is reduced to < 20 mg.

* <u>Steroid stress dosing</u> is not usually indicated but is required for patients with <u>signs of adrenal insufficiency</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Both <u>CTDs</u> and the medications used for their treatment can increase the risk of <u>postoperative complications</u>. [[76]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JI1sdR0)




> [!WARNING]
> Patients with <u>cervical spine</u> instability due to <u>CTDs</u> have an increased risk of injury during <u>endotracheal intubation</u>, e.g., for <u>general anesthesia</u>. [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3G1S_R0)

## Preoperative preparation measures

Instruct all patients on recommended perioperative medication changes, oral intake on the day of <u>surgery</u>, and lifestyle modifications that may reduce perioperative risk.




### Preoperative lifestyle modifications




* <u>Smoking cessation</u>: Patients should be encouraged to stop smoking, ideally 4–8 weeks or longer before <u>surgery</u>.  [[91]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x5cEm10)[[92]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/97cNLd0)

* <u>Alcohol</u> use: Chronic <u>alcohol</u> use is associated with numerous perioperative complications.  [[93]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fMckL10)

* Encourage abstinence or reduction of intake for 4 weeks prior to <u>surgery</u>. [[94]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C7cqLd0)

* Avoid abrupt discontinuation of heavy <u>alcohol</u> intake 1–3 days before <u>surgery</u> as this may precipitate perioperative <u>alcohol withdrawal syndrome</u>. [[95]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TMc6L10)[[96]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gMcFL10)

* Exercise: Preoperative exercise programs (often called prehabilitation) may improve some surgical outcomes.  [[97]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3McSo10)[[98]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DX11Zf0)





### <u>Fasting</u>




* Fasting guidelines for elective surgery [[99]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iMcJo10)

* 2 hours before <u>surgery</u>: Stop all oral intake (<u>nil per os</u>; <u>NPO</u>).

* 4 hours before <u>surgery</u>: Stop ingestion of <u>breast milk</u>.

* 6 hours before <u>surgery</u>: Stop ingestion of nonhuman milk, solid food, and <u>infant formula</u>.

* 8 hours before <u>surgery</u>: Stop ingestion of meat, fried food, and fatty food.

* Protracted <u>NPO</u> status or preoperative <u>hypovolemia</u>: Correct any volume deficit and replace any ongoing fluid loss with <u>IV fluid therapy</u>.





### Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis [[100]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v9YA6r)[[101]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QMcuo10)




* Purpose: reduce the <u>incidence</u> of postoperative <u>surgical site infections</u>

* Timing: ideally 30–60 minutes prior to <u>skin</u> <u>incision</u>

* <u>Antibiotics</u> of choice

* First-line treatment for most procedures: intravenous <u>cefazolin</u>

* Patients with <u>beta-lactam</u> <u>allergy</u>: <u>clindamycin</u> or <u>vancomycin</u>

* Colorectal <u>surgery</u>, <u>appendectomy</u>, or <u>small bowel obstruction</u>: <u>cefazolin</u> PLUS <u>metronidazole</u>
