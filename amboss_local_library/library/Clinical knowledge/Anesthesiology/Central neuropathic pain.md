# Central neuropathic pain

## Summary

Central <u>neuropathic pain</u> (CNP) is a type of <u>chronic pain</u> that arises directly from a <u>central nervous system</u> (<u>CNS</u>) lesion. The most common causes of CNP are <u>stroke</u>, <u>spinal cord injury</u>, and <u>multiple sclerosis</u>. CNP is characterized by <u>spinothalamic tract</u> dysfunction with <u>neuropathic pain</u> in the region of the body affected by the <u>CNS</u> lesion. Symptoms may not manifest for months or even years after the neurological insult. Diagnosis is based on history, characteristic clinical features, and a lesion identified on <u>neuroimaging</u> that aligns with the observed <u>pattern of central neuropathic pain</u>. Although complete symptom resolution is unlikely, multimodal <u>pain management</u> (e.g., both <u>nonpharmacological pain management</u> and pharmacological therapy) can improve functionality and reduce <u>pain</u>.

---

## Etiology

* Common causes

* <u>Stroke</u>

* <u>Spinal cord injury</u>

* <u>Multiple sclerosis</u>

* Other causes

* <u>Malignancy</u> (e.g., <u>brain tumor</u>)

* Neuroinflammatory conditions (e.g., <u>brain abscess</u>, <u>encephalitis</u>)

* <u>Head trauma</u>

## Clinical features

### Description of <u>pain</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyW0TL0)




* A combination of spontaneous (continuous or <u>paroxysmal</u>) and evoked <u>pain</u> (e.g., <u>allodynia</u>).

* <u>Neuropathic pain</u> and <u>dysesthesia</u>

* <u>Pain</u> may be more intense in the <u>distal</u> regions of the affected areas, particularly in <u>stroke</u>-related CNP.





> [!TIP]
> The onset of CNP is generally months or years after a neurological insult. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)




> [!TIP]
> Patients will also have clinical features of the underlying neurological condition. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)




### Patterns of <u>pain</u> distribution [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyW0TL0)




CNP always manifests with <u>spinothalamic tract</u> dysfunction (i.e., loss of <u>pinprick sensation</u> and <u>temperature sensation</u>) in the regions affected by a neurological insult. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)




| Patterns of central neuropathic pain |  |
| --- | --- |
| Underlying neurological insult | Location of spinothalamic dysfunction and <u>pain</u> |
| <u>Stroke</u> and/or <u>brainstem</u> lesion | <br>* <u>Central poststroke pain syndrome</u><br><br>* <u>Ipsilateral</u> side of the face<br><br>* <u>Contralateral</u> side of the body<br><br> |
| Thoracic <u>spinal cord injury</u> | <br>* Starting at and <u>distal</u> to the level of injury<br><br> |
| <u>Multiple sclerosis</u> | <br>* Correlates with the sites of <u>demyelinating</u> lesions<br><br>* Unilateral or bilateral<br><br>* Can affect multiple regions of the body<br><br>* Manifestations include <u>pain</u> in the extremities, <u>Lhermitte sign</u>, and <u>trigeminal neuralgia</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyW0TL0)<br><br> |





> [!TIP]
> <u>Spinothalamic tract</u> dysfunction with <u>neuropathic pain</u> in the regions of the body affected by the neurological insult is the hallmark of CNP. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)




> [!TIP]
> CNP may not be present in the entire neurologically affected region. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LyWwfL0)

## Treatment

A complete resolution of <u>pain</u> is unlikely. Individualized treatment should focus on reducing <u>pain</u> to a manageable level.




* Multidisciplinary involvement, e.g., with <u>neurology</u>, neurosurgery, <u>physical therapy</u>, psychology, and/or <u>pain</u> specialists

* Multimodal <u>pain management</u> (see also “<u>Chronic noncancer pain management</u>”)

* <u>Patient education on chronic pain management</u>, including establishing appropriate expectations

* <u>Nonpharmacological pain management</u>, e.g., 

* <u>CBT</u>, <u>biofeedback training</u>

* Neuromodulation (e.g., with <u>spinal cord stimulation</u>) for refractory symptoms

* Pharmacological <u>analgesia</u> (titrated to symptom response) 

* <u>TCAs</u> (e.g., <u>amitriptyline</u>)

* <u>SNRIs</u> (e.g., <u>duloxetine</u>)

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u> (e.g., <u>pregabalin</u>, <u>gabapentin</u>)

* Management of other causes of <u>pain</u>, e.g., <u>spasticity</u> with <u>multiple sclerosis</u>
