# Infectious gastroenteritis in children

## Summary

Acute <u>gastroenteritis</u> is a common infection in childhood. The majority of cases are caused by <u>viruses</u>, while ∼20% are bacterial, and a small number are parasitic. Most children have a mild presentation where <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, and <u>diarrhea</u> are not severe enough to prevent adequate oral intake or participation in normal activities. Severe disease is characterized by <u>signs of significant dehydration</u>, <u>end-organ damage</u>, <u>fevers</u> â¥ 40.0Â°C, <u>signs of pediatric sepsis</u>, bloody or <u>bilious</u> emesis, and toxic appearance. Children with <u>mild-to-moderate gastroenteritis</u> can be <u>diagnosed clinically</u>. Children with severe illness, atypical presentations, or <u>signs of significant dehydration</u> should undergo <u>laboratory studies</u>. Treatment is usually supportive with <u>fluid replacement</u>, <u>antiemetics</u>, and <u>antipyretics</u>. <u>Antimicrobial therapy</u> (empiric or tailored) may be utilized for children with suspected or confirmed bacterial or parasitic infections. <u>Prevention of infectious gastroenteritis</u> involves <u>vaccination</u> of <u>infants</u> against <u>rotavirus</u>, <u>travel vaccines</u> where appropriate, and patient/caregiver education on <u>hand hygiene</u> and <u>food and water hygiene</u>.

## Diagnosis

* Diagnosis is usually clinical.

* Consider <u>diagnostic studies for infectious gastroenteritis</u> in children with: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)

* <u>Severe gastroenteritis</u>

* <u>Immunocompromise</u>

* Recent international travel or local <u>outbreak</u>

* Symptoms lasting > 1 week

* <u>Clinical features of HUS</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UHdbpr0)

* Assess for <u>signs of significant dehydration</u>; if present, order <u>diagnostic studies for dehydration</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pu1LHi0)

## Treatment

<u>Treatment of infectious gastroenteritis in children</u> is generally supportive.




### All patients [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pu1LHi0)




* Determine the need for admission, e.g., patients with:

* <u>Admission criteria for dehydration</u>

* <u>Severe gastroenteritis</u>

* Inability to manage symptoms at home

* <u>Barriers to follow up</u>

* Provide symptomatic treatment.

* Give <u>antipyretics</u> and <u>antiemetics</u> as needed.

* <u>Antidiarrheal medications</u> are not routinely used.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7814Li0)

* Probiotics are not routinely recommended but may reduce the duration of <u>diarrhea</u> for immunocompetent patients.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)

* If diagnostics identify an <u>STEC</u> infection, initiate <u>management of STEC-positive illness</u> to prevent <u>HUS</u>.

* Consider <u>antimicrobial therapy</u> for select patients.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)

* See “<u>Empiric antibiotics for bacterial gastroenteritis</u>.”

* See “<u>Treatment of giardiasis</u>.”

* Optimize nutrition.

* Maintain adequate oral intake through <u>breastfeeding</u> or age-appropriate foods.   [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Xu19pi0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)

* <u>Zinc</u> supplementation is recommended for patients with <u>malnutrition</u> or in low-income regions. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)

* Educate patients and/or caregivers on <u>preventing onward transmission of gastroenteritis</u>.





### <u>Mild to moderate gastroenteritis</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)




* Encourage fluid intake.

* Breastfed <u>infants</u>: Continue giving <u>breastmilk</u>.

* All other children: Consider apple juice diluted with water in a 50:50 mix followed by preferred fluids.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)

* Give <u>oral rehydration solution</u> (<u>ORS</u>) if <u>signs of moderate dehydration</u> are present. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)

* Advise caregivers to seek medical assistance if <u>signs of significant dehydration</u> develop.





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Breastmilk</u> should not be withheld in order to give <u>ORS</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1u12Ji0)




### <u>Severe gastroenteritis</u> or children with <u>admission criteria for dehydration</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I0cYha0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)




* Admit to hospital and initiate <u>contact precautions</u>.

* Identify patients in <u>shock</u> and start <u>IV fluid resuscitation</u>.

* Initiate <u>treatment of dehydration</u> and <u>electrolyte repletion</u> with either <u>IV fluids</u> or <u>ORS</u>.





> [!TIP]
> When feasible, <u>ORS</u> is preferred over <u>IV fluids</u>; children unable to tolerate oral fluids can receive <u>ORS</u> via a <u>nasogastric tube</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MybMfw)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vI1AeR0)

## Complications

* <u>Dehydration</u>

* <u>Malnutrition</u>

* <u>Electrolyte</u> imbalance

* <u>Reactive arthritis</u>

* <u>Secondary lactase deficiency</u>  [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dVYotL)

* <u>Hemolytic uremic syndrome</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMYEIp)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UHdbpr0)
