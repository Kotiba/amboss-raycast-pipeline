# Costochondritis

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Family medicine > Musculoskeletal disorders > Costochondritis*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_E05y3)

---

## Summary

Costochondritis is characterized by localized <u>chest wall</u> tenderness caused by inflammation of the costochondral and/or <u>sternocostal joints</u>. Costochondritis typically occurs in individuals > 40 years of age and is more common in women than in men. The etiology is unknown. Diagnosis is clinical, based primarily on reproducible <u>pain</u> upon palpation of the affected <u>joints</u>, and is supported with <u>physical examination</u> maneuvers. Diagnostic testing is performed to rule out other <u>causes of chest pain</u>. The condition is usually <u>self-limited</u>, with <u>pain</u> resolution typically occurring within weeks and a low likelihood of recurrence. Management includes <u>analgesics</u> and application of heat.

---

## Etiology

* Unknown

* Associated with excessive or repetitive exercise and with minor <u>chest wall</u> trauma [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DId1Ur0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OpYIpJ)

---

## Clinical features

* Sharp, well-localized <u>chest pain</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dIdobr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qIdCdr0)

* Reproducible on palpation of affected <u>joints</u>

* Typically affects multiple sites (unilateral or bilateral) between 2nd and 5th costochondral and/or <u>sternocostal joints</u>

* Exacerbating movements include: [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OpYIpJ)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qIdCdr0)

* Deep breaths

* <u>Coughing</u>

* Upper body movements

* May be preceded by exercise, <u>chest wall</u> trauma, or <u>coughing</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DId1Ur0)





> [!TIP]
> The absence of local signs of inflammation (e.g., swelling and <u>erythema</u>) can help distinguish costochondritis from <u>Tietze syndrome</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dIdobr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qIdCdr0)

## Treatment

### General principles [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dIdobr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qIdCdr0)




* Treatment is supportive.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dIdobr0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OpYIpJ)

* Reassure patients that costochondritis is benign and <u>self-limited</u>.

* Symptoms usually resolve within weeks, and recurrence rates are low.





### <u>Supportive care</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dIdobr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qIdCdr0)




* Reduction of activities that provoke symptoms [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OpYIpJ)

* <u>Analgesia</u> as needed, e.g.:

* <u>Acetaminophen</u> or <u>NSAIDs</u> (e.g., <u>naproxen</u>, <u>ibuprofen</u>)

* <u>Topical analgesics</u> (e.g., <u>diclofenac</u> gel, <u>lidocaine</u> patch, <u>capsaicin</u> cream)

* Local <u>corticosteroid</u> or <u>lidocaine</u> injections may be considered for refractory <u>pain</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DId1Ur0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OpYIpJ)

* Application of heat or ice packs

* <u>Cough suppressants</u> may be considered for <u>coughing</u> that exacerbates <u>chest pain</u>.

* <u>Physical therapy</u> (e.g., stretching exercises)
