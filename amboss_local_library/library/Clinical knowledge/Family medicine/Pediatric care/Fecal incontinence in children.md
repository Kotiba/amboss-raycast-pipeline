# Fecal incontinence in children

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Family medicine > Pediatric care > Fecal incontinence in children*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xD0EgR)

---

## Summary

<u>Fecal incontinence</u> in childhood is the repeated involuntary or intentional passage of stool in inappropriate places in children with a developmental age ≥ 4 years. <u>Functional fecal incontinence</u> is a <u>gut-brain axis</u> disorder and the most common type of <u>fecal incontinence</u> in children. <u>Functional retentive fecal incontinence</u> is caused by <u>constipation</u> and <u>overflow fecal incontinence</u> and is characterized by passage of small-volume semiliquid stools, a palpable abdominal and/or rectal stool mass, and other <u>clinical features of constipation in children</u>. <u>Functional nonretentive fecal incontinence</u> is less common, has no identifiable cause, and is characterized by normal stool consistency and <u>physical examination</u> findings. <u>Fecal incontinence</u> due to an organic cause (e.g., <u>neurogenic bowel dysfunction</u>, anorectal <u>surgery</u>) occurs in ∼ 5% of children. Diagnosis of <u>functional fecal incontinence</u> is primarily clinical; diagnostic studies may be considered in case of diagnostic uncertainty or to evaluate for an underlying cause. <u>Functional retentive fecal incontinence</u> typically resolves with treatment of the underlying <u>constipation</u>. Management of <u>functional nonretentive fecal incontinence</u> is more challenging and primarily involves behavioral interventions, such as a structured toileting program and rewards. <u>Management of fecal incontinence</u> due to an organic cause is focused on treating the underlying condition.

## Etiology

### <u>Functional fecal incontinence</u> (<u>encopresis</u>)  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rydfgs0)




<u>Functional fecal incontinence</u> is a <u>gut-brain axis disorder</u> and is the most common type of <u>fecal incontinence</u> in children. There are two subtypes: retentive and nonretentive.




* Retentive fecal incontinence (∼ 80% of cases) is caused by <u>constipation</u> and chronic voluntary stool withholding, which results in: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* <u>Overflow fecal incontinence</u> in patients with <u>fecal impaction</u>

* Involuntary leakage of stool in patients with rectal dilation and reduced rectal sensation

* Nonretentive fecal incontinence (∼ 15% of cases) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* No identifiable organic cause

* Often associated with <u>impulse and conduct disorders</u> (e.g., <u>oppositional defiant disorder</u>, <u>conduct disorder</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)





#### Risk factors for functional fecal incontinence [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)




* Psychosocial stressors (e.g., toilet training, transition to solid food, starting school)

* <u>Child sexual abuse</u>

* Low <u>socioeconomic status</u>

* Male sex

* <u>Family history</u> of <u>fecal incontinence</u>





### Organic causes of fecal incontinence




* Accounts for ∼ 5% of <u>fecal incontinence</u> in children [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* Causes include: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H_dKps0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w_dhqs0)

* <u>Hirschsprung disease</u>

* Previous anorectal <u>surgery</u> or injury (e.g., postsurgical <u>Hirschsprung disease</u> or <u>anorectal malformation</u>)

* <u>Neurogenic bowel dysfunction</u> due to, e.g.: 

* <u>Neural tube defects</u>

* <u>Spinal cord injury</u> or <u>tumor</u>

* <u>Cauda equina syndrome</u>

* <u>Cerebral palsy</u>

* <u>Muscular dystrophy</u>, <u>myopathy</u>

* <u>Inflammatory bowel disease</u> (e.g., <u>ulcerative colitis</u>)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rydfgs0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H_dKps0)




* Evaluate for <u>functional fecal incontinence</u> in symptomatic children with a developmental age of ≥ 4 years. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Determine subtype: See "<u>Retentive vs. nonretentive functional fecal incontinence</u>."

* <u>Constipation</u> present

* <u>Retentive fecal incontinence</u> is likely.

* See "<u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for encopresis</u>."

* See also "<u>Diagnostic approach to constipation in children</u>."

* No <u>constipation</u>

* <u>Nonretentive fecal incontinence</u> is likely.

* See "<u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for encopresis</u>" or "<u>Rome IV criteria for functional nonretentive fecal incontinence</u>."

* Refer to pediatric gastroenterology for additional evaluation if:

* <u>Red flag features of fecal incontinence in children</u> are identified

* There is diagnostic uncertainty

* Diagnostic studies typically include <u>colonic transit time</u> testing, <u>anorectal manometry</u>, and imaging.





> [!TIP]
> Diagnostic studies are not routinely required if the diagnostic criteria for functional <u>constipation in children</u> are met. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




> [!TIP]
> Abdominal <u>x-rays</u> have low <u>specificity</u> for <u>constipation</u> and are not routinely indicated to evaluate for <u>fecal incontinence</u> or <u>constipation</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cD0aWR)




### Clinical evaluation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H_dKps0)




* Obtain a comprehensive <u>medical history</u>.

* Characteristics of <u>fecal incontinence</u>, including:

* Stool consistency: Consider using the Bristol stool chart.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H_dKps0)

* Timing and frequency of symptoms

* Complete evacuation, <u>soiling</u> of clothing

* Associated symptoms, e.g.:

* <u>Clinical features of constipation in children</u>

* Urinary symptoms

* Psychosocial stressors or other <u>risk factors for functional fecal incontinence in children</u>

* Diet

* <u>Medication review</u>

* <u>Family history</u>

* Past medical and surgical history

* Perform a <u>physical examination</u>, including:

* <u>Pediatric growth parameters</u>

* <u>Abdominal examination</u>: Assess for palpable fecal mass.

* Lumbosacral examination for features of occult <u>spina bifida</u> (e.g., sacral dimple, <u>hypertrichosis</u>, <u>lipoma</u>)

* <u>Neurological examination</u> of lower extremities

* Perianal inspection for <u>anal fissures</u> and <u>hemorrhoids</u>

* <u>Digital rectal examination</u>: Assess for stool in the <u>rectum</u> and evaluate <u>anal sphincter tone</u> and anorectal sensation.





### Diagnostic criteria for functional fecal incontinence in children




#### <u>DSM-5</u> diagnostic criteria for encopresis




All criteria must be fulfilled to confirm the diagnosis.




* Inappropriate passage of stool (involuntary or intentional) in a child with a developmental age ≥ 4 years

* At least once per month for ≥ 3 months

* Not caused by a medication or a medical condition other than <u>constipation</u>





#### Rome IV criteria for functional nonretentive fecal incontinence [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)




All criteria must be fulfilled to confirm the diagnosis.




* Inappropriate passage of stool for ≥ 1 month in a child with a developmental age ≥ 4 years

* No <u>constipation</u> or fecal retention

* No identifiable organic cause after appropriate clinical evaluation

## Management

* All patients [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* Educate caregivers on the nature and prognosis of the condition and discourage punitive actions.

* Review <u>skin</u> care and use of <u>continence products</u> as needed (e.g., barrier <u>emollients</u>, disposable underwear).

* <u>Organic causes of fecal incontinence</u>: Refer to an appropriate specialist for management.

* <u>Retentive fecal incontinence</u> 

* Treat the underlying <u>constipation</u> and <u>fecal impaction</u>.

* See "<u>Management of constipation in children</u>." [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cD0aWR)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NQ1-wg0)

* <u>Nonretentive fecal incontinence</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rydfgs0)

* Initiate a structured toileting program. 

* Encourage children to use the toilet as soon as they feel the urge to defecate.

* Encourage defecation by asking the child to spend 5–10 minutes on the toilet after every meal and after school.

* Ensure toilet seat and positioning is optimal (e.g., comfortable toilet seat, proper foot support).

* Consider implementing a reward system and a bowel diary to ensure compliance.

* Screen all patients for psychological symptoms (e.g., with a <u>child behavior checklist</u> or <u>strengths and difficulties questionnaire</u>), and refer for management as indicated. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* Schedule regular follow-ups. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IydYgs0)

* Symptom improvement: Continue management.

* No improvement within 6 months of initial management: Refer to pediatric gastroenterology for further evaluation and management.





> [!TIP]
> If <u>child sexual abuse</u> is suspected, refer to a trained provider for further evaluation. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)
