# Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Family medicine > Pediatric care > Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rP0fTT)

---

## Summary

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a <u>neurodevelopmental disorder</u> that manifests in childhood and may persist into adulthood. It is characterized by inattention and/or impulsivity and hyperactivity resulting in functional impairment in social, occupational, and/or academic activities. ADHD is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> made using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ADHD</u>, and evaluation should include an assessment for <u>differential diagnoses of ADHD</u>. Individuals with ADHD frequently have comorbidities such as <u>anxiety disorder</u>, <u>major depressive disorder</u>, and <u>specific learning disorder</u>. <u>Management of ADHD</u> differs based on the individual's age but typically consists of stimulant medications in combination with behavioral interventions. Untreated ADHD is associated with decreased academic and/or occupational success and an increased risk of developing <u>substance use disorders</u>.

## Clinical features

* <u>Symptoms of inattention</u> and/or <u>symptoms of hyperactivity and impulsivity</u> (see “<u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ADHD</u>”)

* Additional neuropsychiatric symptoms are not features of ADHD but may be part of common comorbidities, e.g., <u>anxiety</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Poor school performance in children with ADHD is usually due to inattention and/or impulsivity and hyperactivity rather than level of <u>intelligence</u>, which is not directly affected by the disorder. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYWxLP0)

## Diagnosis

ADHD is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> made using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ADHD</u>.




### Approach [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/we1haT0)




* Evaluate patients using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ADHD</u>.

* Obtain collateral history from caregivers and individuals who interact closely with the patient.

* Validated scales are available to distribute to collateral historians.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/saWtNP0)

* Screen for comorbidities and rule out <u>differential diagnoses of ADHD</u>.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Consider <u>laboratory studies</u> (e.g., <u>thyroid function tests</u>, lead level) depending on suspected diagnoses. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Refer to a specialist for additional diagnostic workup if necessary.





> [!TIP]
> Establishing the <u>diagnosis of ADHD</u> involves identifying comorbid disorders (e.g., <u>learning</u> disability, psychiatric disorders) as well as ruling out <u>differential diagnoses of ADHD</u> (e.g., <u>hearing</u> or visual impairment, <u>thyroid disorders</u>, <u>sleep disorders</u>). [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

## Differential diagnoses

Alternative causes of ADHD symptoms and common comorbidities include: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




* <u>Seizure disorders</u> (e.g., <u>absence seizures</u>)

* <u>Thyroid disorders</u>

* <u>Sleep disorders</u> (e.g., <u>sleep deprivation</u>, <u>obstructive sleep apnea</u>) [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rH1f7R0)

* <u>Hearing loss</u> and/or <u>vision</u> impairment

* Genetic disorders (e.g., <u>fragile X syndrome</u>)

* Medication-induced <u>symptoms of inattention</u> or hyperactivity

* <u>Substance use disorder</u>

* <u>Tic disorders</u>

* <u>Developmental delays</u> (e.g., <u>speech and language disorders</u>, not meeting <u>developmental milestones</u>)

* <u>Reactive attachment disorder</u>

* <u>Specific learning disorder</u>

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* <u>Autism spectrum disorder</u>

* <u>Oppositional defiant disorder</u>

* <u>Conduct disorder</u>

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>Bipolar disorder</u>

* <u>Anxiety disorders</u>

* <u>Posttraumatic stress disorder</u> [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1g128T0)

* <u>Personality disorders</u>





The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.

## Treatment

### Approach [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)




* Consider specialist referral  for individuals with: [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y71n4R0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/a71Q4R0)

* Unclear diagnosis

* Comorbidities that are severe and/or may affect ADHD management (see “<u>Differential diagnosis of ADHD</u>”) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Inadequate response to management  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y71n4R0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/a71Q4R0)

* Suspected ADHD if < 4 years of age or symptoms first manifest at > 12 years of age  [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/a71Q4R0)

* Initiate first-line management based on the individual's age. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Children 4–5 years of age: behavioral interventions alone

* Children ≥ 6 years of age and adults: pharmacotherapy with adjunctive behavioral interventions  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZYWZnP0)

* Educate patients and caregivers on ADHD. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)

* Children may be eligible for accommodations at school; caregivers should contact their school district.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JBcsbU0)

* Ensure patients get <u>regular physical activity</u> and sufficient <u>sleep</u>.

* Manage <u>comorbid mental health conditions</u>, e.g., <u>anxiety</u>, <u>depression</u>, <u>substance use disorder</u>.

* Schedule regular <u>follow-up for patients with ADHD</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Refer individuals with ADHD and a history of <u>substance use disorder</u> to a <u>psychiatrist</u>. Consider treatment with medications that have a low potential for addiction (e.g., nonstimulants). [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)




### Behavioral interventions [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1g128T0)




* Parent training in behavioral management (PTBM)  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Indication: caregivers with children 4–11 years of age with problematic behaviors (e.g., ADHD)  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Goals: reinforce preferred behaviors and reduce problematic behaviors

* Classroom interventions: behavioral intervention plan (organized via the school district) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JBcsbU0)

* <u>Cognitive behavioral therapy</u> (<u>CBT</u>): may be considered as adjunctive therapy for adults with ADHD  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1g128T0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZYWZnP0)





> [!TIP]
> PTBM can also be utilized for children who do not meet the full <u>criteria for ADHD</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)




### Pharmacotherapy [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)




* Options include stimulant and nonstimulant therapy.

* Stimulant therapy is usually first-line treatment for children ≥ 6 years of age and adults.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S71yOR0)

* Nonstimulants are preferred for individuals with: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S71yOR0)

* Contraindications to or potential for serious adverse effects with stimulants  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* A preference for nonstimulants

* Certain comorbidities  [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/se1tZT0)





#### Stimulant therapy [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)




* Options: methylphenidate;  or <u>amphetamine</u> analogues (e.g., <u>lisdexamfetamine</u>, dextroamphetamine)

* Mechanism of action: indirect and central <u>sympathomimetic</u> activity → increased release and blocked reuptake of <u>norepinephrine</u> and <u>dopamine</u> (minor effect on <u>serotonin</u>) → increased concentration of <u>norepinephrine</u> and <u>dopamine</u> in the <u>synaptic cleft</u> → increased mental performance (e.g., improved concentration, <u>cognition</u>; , <u>short-term memory</u>) and fine motor skills [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Od1IJf0)

* Adverse effects [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)

* <u>Sympathomimetic</u> effects

* <u>Anxiety</u>, <u>agitation</u>, restlessness, <u>bruxism</u>, <u>tics</u>  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MS1MZg0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AaWRMP0)

* Difficulty falling asleep (<u>insomnia</u>)

* Reduced appetite,;  <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, weight loss

* Increased arterial blood pressure, <u>tachycardia</u>

* Increased risk of <u>seizure</u>: reduces the <u>seizure</u> threshold [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

* Decreased growth rate (may be reversible if medication is stopped) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)

* <u>Psychosis</u> (e.g., <u>hallucinations</u>) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)

* <u>Priapism</u>  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qI1CdR0)

* Other indications: : also used in patients with <u>narcolepsy</u> or <u>binge eating disorder</u> [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b71H4R0)[[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X7194R0)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Methamphetamine</u> has <u>FDA</u> approval for the <u>treatment of ADHD</u> but is rarely prescribed because of its high potential for misuse. [[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3aWS4P0)




##### Initiation of stimulant therapy




* Review state and federal laws relevant to prescribing controlled substances (stimulants are <u>Schedule II drugs</u>).  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Consider implementing a <u>controlled substance agreement</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1g128T0)

* Obtain an <u>ECG</u> if either of the following are present:  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/II1YVR0)

* Symptoms of a cardiac disorder (e.g., <u>syncope</u>, <u>palpitations</u>)

* A personal or <u>family history</u> of cardiac conduction disorders

* Inform individuals that effects may be noticed within 2–3 days of initiation. [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S71yOR0)

* Start on a low dose and slowly titrate medication as necessary, e.g.:

* Children: every 1–4 weeks [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)

* Adults: every 4–6 weeks [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Arrange regular <u>follow-up for patients with ADHD</u> to monitor the effects of pharmacotherapy.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/se1tZT0)

#### Nonstimulant therapy




Patients and caregivers should be informed that it may take 4–6 weeks for nonstimulants to reach maximum <u>efficacy</u>. [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/se1tZT0)




##### Selective <u>norepinephrine</u> reuptake inhibitors [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)




* Indication: alternative to stimulant medications for ADHD in adults and children ≥ 6 years of age [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Mechanism of action: block <u>norepinephrine</u> reuptake, which increases <u>synaptic cleft</u> levels of <u>norepinephrine</u> and <u>dopamine</u> in the <u>prefrontal cortex</u> [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)

* Options: : atomoxetine DOSAGE or viloxazine DOSAGE [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Adverse effects [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

* GI symptoms (e.g., appetite suppression, <u>nausea</u>, discomfort)

* Sedation

* Rare: <u>liver</u> failure, <u>prolonged QT interval</u>, <u>suicidal ideation</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>SNRIs</u> are not addictive or <u>Schedule II drugs</u> and therefore may be preferred for individuals with a history of <u>substance use disorder</u>. [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iYWJKP0)




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Atomoxetine</u> has a black box warning for increased <u>suicidal ideation</u> in children and <u>adolescents</u>. Monitor individuals closely, especially during the first few weeks of treatment. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)




##### <u>Alpha-2 adrenergic agonists</u> [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)




* Indications: children 6–17 years of age with either of the following.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)

* Contraindications to stimulants [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Preference for nonstimulants

* Inadequate response to stimulants alone (i.e., adjunctive therapy) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Options: <u>guanfacine</u> ; extended-release DOSAGE or <u>clonidine</u> extended-release DOSAGE   [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)





> [!WARNING]
> To avoid rebound <u>hypertension</u>, do not abruptly stop <u>alpha-2 adrenergic agonists</u>; taper gradually. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)




##### <u>Antidepressants</u> [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9e1NaT0)




* Indication: adults in whom stimulants and nonstimulants are not effective or contraindicated (i.e., an <u>off-label</u> alternative to stimulants)  [[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IH1Y7R0)

* Options: <u>bupropion</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE or <u>tricyclic antidepressants</u>, e.g., <u>desipramine</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE





### Follow-up for patients with ADHD




* Educate patients and caregivers on how to manage ADHD; see also “<u>Chronic disease management</u>.”

* For patients on pharmacotherapy:

* Advise patients and/or caregivers that several different medications and dosages may need to be trialed. [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wg1P8T0)

* Arrange regular scheduled follow-up appointments. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

* Monthly until treatment is optimized

* Every 3 months for at least the first year

* Once stable, every 6 months

* At every visit, assess <u>vital signs</u> and inquire about adverse effects.  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> are not routinely required. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

* If the response is insufficient or significant adverse effects occur, consider medication adjustments.

* Periodically reassess if medication is still required.  [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/071e4R0)

* For patients not on pharmacotherapy, arrange follow-up to:  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/He1KZT0)

* Reassess symptoms

* Determine if medication is now indicated





> [!TIP]
> In children, routine <u>laboratory studies</u> to monitor for medication adverse effects are generally not recommended. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eU1xXT0)

## Prognosis

* The persistence of symptoms after treatment predicts prognosis into adulthood.

* In 35–65% of patients, <u>symptoms of ADHD</u> and their associated functional impairment will persist into adulthood. [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S1XyTC)

* Patients with untreated ADHD are at higher risk of injury , <u>substance use disorder</u>, and <u>antisocial personality disorder</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hz1K8j0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)
