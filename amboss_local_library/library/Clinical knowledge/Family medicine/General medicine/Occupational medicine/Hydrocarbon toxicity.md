# Hydrocarbon toxicity

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Family medicine > General medicine > Occupational medicine > Hydrocarbon toxicity*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/050eig)

---

## Summary

Hydrocarbons are a large class of organic compounds composed solely of hydrogen and carbon, all of which can be toxic if inhaled or ingested. Hydrocarbons have a wide range of household and industrial applications, especially as fuels (e.g., gasoline), solvents (e.g., <u>turpentine</u>), and lubricants (e.g., mineral oil). Chlorinated and <u>aromatic hydrocarbons</u> have a particularly severe toxicity and their occurrence is generally restricted to industrial uses. At low doses, exposure to household hydrocarbons may manifest with <u>coughing</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>signs of hypoxia</u>, signs of <u>CNS depression</u>, and other neurological symptoms such as <u>agitation</u>, <u>hallucinations</u>, and <u>tremors</u>. High doses can cause potentially fatal <u>arrhythmias</u>. Chronic exposure may cause rashes, <u>peripheral neuropathy</u>, chronic <u>headaches</u>, and cognitive impairment. Chlorinated and <u>aromatic hydrocarbons</u> may furthermore cause severe symptoms already in acute exposure and at relatively low doses, including <u>chloracne</u> (a hallmark of <u>dioxin</u> toxicity) and other dermatological manifestations (e.g., <u>skin</u> irritation, <u>hirsutism</u>, <u>skin</u> pigmentation), <u>loss of consciousness</u>, numbness, and decreased <u>immune response</u>. In chronic exposure or at high doses, they may have <u>nephrotoxic</u>, <u>hepatotoxic</u>, <u>neurotoxic</u>, <u>carcinogenic</u>, and <u>teratogenic</u> effects. Industrial accidents have led to severe effects in the general population including in Vietnam, via contamination of <u>Agent Orange</u> with <u>TCDD</u> and the contamination of rice bran oil in Kyusho, Japan, with <u>PCB</u> in 1968 (<u>Yusho disease</u>).

## Overview

### Halogenated hydrocarbons




* Overview

* Fluorinated or <u>chlorinated hydrocarbons</u>

* From biomass burning or natural production by fungi/bacteria

* <u>CCl4</u>: formerly widely used in fire extinguishers, refrigerants, dry-cleaning fluids, and propellants

* Examples: chloroform, carbon tetrachloride





#### Polychlorinated dibenzodioxins (<u>dioxins</u>; <u>PCDDs</u>) and polychlorinated dibenzofurans (<u>PCDFs</u>)




* Overview

* Not commercially produced; mostly byproducts in the manufacture of other chemicals (e.g., <u>PCBs</u>, pesticides), combustion processes (e.g., waste incineration, automobile emissions), or organic <u>decomposition</u> (e.g., compostation, sewage)

* Very stable, <u>lipophilic</u> compounds that are absorbed via contaminated foods, mainly of animal origin (e.g., beef, dairy products, and chicken), or via environmental exposure, mainly as an <u>occupational hazard</u> involving inhalation or <u>skin</u> contact

* Accumulate in <u>fatty tissue</u> and, therefore, eventually reach toxic levels with continued exposure to even small amounts

* Almost all <u>PCDDs</u> and <u>PCDFs</u> are classified as probable human <u>carcinogens</u> (group 2A <u>carcinogens</u>)

* Examples

* <u>2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzodioxin</u> (<u>TCDD</u>): best known for being a contaminant in the herbicide Agent Orange used by the US military during the Vietnam War and the release of several kilograms in Seveso, Italy, as a result of an industrial accident.

* <u>2,3,7,8-tetrachlorodibenzofuran</u> (<u>TCDF</u>)

* Clinical features

* Acute toxicity: chloracne

* Mild cases: increased oiliness of the <u>skin</u>, large number of <u>blackheads</u> around the eyes, fluid-filled cysts, <u>hirsutism</u> and <u>hair</u> thickening  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aQcQuX0)

* Severe cases: acneiform eruptions of <u>pustules</u>, cysts, and <u>blackheads</u>, mainly behind the <u>ears</u> and on the cheeks; scarring is possible

* Treatment: symptomatic and exposure avoidance

* Prognosis: symptoms typically resolve within 2 years of exposure cessation.

* Chronic toxicity

* Cancer: especially blood cancers such as <u>leukemia</u>, <u>Hodgkin lymphoma</u>, <u>Non-Hodgkin lymphoma</u>; urogenital cancers such as <u>bladder</u> and <u>prostate cancer</u>; and <u>lung cancer</u>

* <u>Birth</u> defects: especially <u>spina bifida</u>

* Endocrinological dysregulation: <u>thyroid</u> disturbances, altered <u>testosterone</u> levels, <u>pathological glucose tolerance</u>

* <u>Neurotoxicity</u> (e.g., sedation, <u>headache</u>, blurry <u>vision</u>, sensorimotor <u>polyneuropathy</u>  , <u>chronic toxic encephalopathy</u>, <u>coma</u>)

* Cardiotoxicity (e.g., <u>arrhythmias</u>)

* <u>Nephrotoxicity</u> (e.g., nephritis, nephrosis, and <u>renal failure</u>)

* <u>Hepatotoxicity</u> (centrilobular fatty change, congestion, and <u>necrosis</u> of the <u>liver</u> due to <u>free radical damage</u>)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Chloracne</u> is a hallmark symptom of <u>dioxin</u> toxicity.




#### Polychlorinated biphenyls (<u>PCBs</u>)




* Overview

* Formerly used in coolants and insulators for transformers and electrical capacitors, hydraulic fluids, as plasticizers for paints and plastics, and as sealants for caulking

* Manufacturing was banned in the US in 1979.

* Very stable, <u>lipophilic</u> compounds that are absorbed via contaminated foods, mainly of animal origin (e.g., beef, dairy products, and chicken), or via environmental exposure, mainly as an <u>occupational hazard</u> involving inhalation or <u>skin</u> contact

* <u>PCBs</u> are mainly released into the environment from waste (e.g., electrical transformers, <u>PCB</u>-containing consumer products)

* Classified as probable human <u>carcinogens</u> (group 2A <u>carcinogens</u>)

* Examples: 3,3′,4,4′-tetrachlorobiphenyl, 3,3′,4,4′,5-pentachlorobiphenyl [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dQcoEX0)

* Clinical features

* Acute toxicity
* Yusho disease  : In 1968 in northern Kyushu, Japan, <u>PCB</u>-contaminated rice bran oil was sold to poultry farmers and consumers, resulting in a mass <u>poisoning</u> of about 14,000 people and the <u>death</u> of 400,000 birds.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TRc6mX0)
* Clinical features

* Acneiform eruptions; <u>hyperpigmentation</u> of <u>skin</u>, <u>nails</u>, <u>conjunctiva</u>, and <u>gingiva</u>

* Ocular discharge

* Decreased <u>immune response</u>

* Menstrual irregularities

* Numbness of limbs

* Psychomotor impairment

* Chronic toxicity [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cQcaEX0)

* Cancer (mainly <u>lung</u> and <u>liver</u>)

* <u>Birth</u> defects, delayed growth, <u>developmental delay</u>

* Endocrinological dysregulation: <u>estrogen</u> inhibition/imitation, reduced <u>thyroid hormones</u>

* <u>Nephrotoxicity</u>





#### Chlorinated alkenes




* Overview

* Used in the production of polyvinyl chloride (<u>PVC</u>) plastics , as refrigerants, and in the organic synthesis for adhesives

* Known to be toxic to aquatic life and classified as human <u>carcinogens</u> (group 1A <u>carcinogens</u>)

* Some substances (e.g., <u>trichloroethylene</u>) have high vapor pressure and emit volatile organic compounds (<u>VOCs</u>) [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1vc2_V0)

* Can be emitted from solids and liquids

* Pose short- and long-term health risks (see below)

* Household items that emit <u>VOCs</u> include paints, cleaning supplies, pesticides, office equipment, and glues.

* Examples: <u>chloroethylene</u> (<u>vinyl chloride</u>), <u>trichloroethylene</u>, trichloroethylene , tetrachloroethylene, methylene chloride

* Clinical features [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bQcHuX0)

* Acute toxicity

* Drowsiness, <u>ataxia</u>, visual disturbances, <u>coma</u>

* <u>Skin</u>, <u>eye</u>, <u>mucous membrane</u>, and respiratory tract irritation

* Chronic toxicity (vinyl chloride disease)

* <u>Scleroderma</u>-like <u>skin</u> changes

* Acroosteolysis

* Cancer: <u>hepatic angiosarcoma</u>, blood, <u>brain</u>, and <u>lung cancer</u>

* Thought to cause <u>birth</u> defects

* Diagnostics: detection of thiodiglycolic acid in urine





### Aromatic hydrocarbons




* Overview

* Hydrocarbons that contain at least one aromatic ring

* Found in solvents, glues, <u>nail</u> polishes, <u>cigarette smoke</u>, car emissions, paints

* Examples

* Benzene

* <u>Benzene</u> derivatives: a hydrocarbon (e.g., xylene, toluene), amine group (e.g., <u>aniline</u>), or a nitro-compound (e.g., nitrobenzene) attached to a <u>benzene</u> ring





#### Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons




* Overview [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4Rc3MX0)

* Class of organic substances characterized by the presence of at least two fused aromatic rings

* Found in natural sources (e.g., coal, bitumen), but also form as the result of incomplete combustion (e.g., wood, garbage, tobacco, fossil fuels and their derivatives)

* Ubiquitous contaminants in the environment

* Exposure via the respiratory tract (e.g., breathing in vehicle exhaust), digestive tract (e.g., intake of charcoal-grilled meat), and <u>skin</u> (e.g., coal, tar)

* Classified as possibly <u>carcinogenic</u> to humans (group 2B <u>carcinogens</u>)

* Examples: <u>naphthalene</u>, <u>methylbenzene</u>

* Clinical features

* Acute toxicity: possible <u>hyperpigmentation</u> of the <u>skin</u>

* Chronic toxicity

* Cancer: especially <u>lung</u>, <u>skin</u> (<u>chimney sweeps' carcinoma</u>), <u>esophagus</u>, <u>colon</u>, <u>pancreas</u>, <u>bladder</u>, and <u>breast</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZQcZuX0)

* <u>Hepatotoxicity</u>, <u>nephrotoxicity</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0QceuX0)

* <u>Cataract</u>





#### Chlorinated aromatic compounds




* Overview

* Used in the production of herbicides, pesticides, and rubber

* Classified as group D <u>carcinogen</u> (i.e., not classifiable as to human carcinogenicity due to limited data)

* No information available regarding developmental and/or reproductive effects

* Examples: chlorobenzene, chlorophenol

* Clinical features [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WQcPEX0)

* Acute toxicity: <u>loss of consciousness</u>, muscle spasms

* Chronic toxicity: <u>neurotoxicity</u> (e.g., numbness, <u>hyperesthesia</u>, muscle spasms)





### Aliphatic hydrocarbons




* Overview

* Hydrocarbons joined in a linear chain or by a non-aromatic ring

* Found in gasoline or kerosene (solvents, <u>paraffin wax</u>, lighter fluid, furniture polishes, and lamp oil)

* Examples: n-hexane, heptane, methane, ethane, propane, butane, octane





### Terpene hydrocarbons




* Overview

* Hydrocarbons containing isoprene

* Used in the rubber, painting, or welding industries (in form of pine/<u>turpentine</u> oil)

* Example: turpentine
