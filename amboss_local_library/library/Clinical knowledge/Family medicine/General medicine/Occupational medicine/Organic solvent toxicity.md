# Organic solvent toxicity

## Summary

Organic solvents are used in <u>detergents</u>, insecticides, and a number of industrial settings. Most of these substances are highly lipid-soluble and therefore capable of causing <u>CNS</u> disorders and <u>polyneuropathy</u>. Some organic solvents break down into metabolites that are <u>carcinogenic</u>. While the metabolites may be found in urine or blood, the diagnosis is usually established based on clinical features and a history of exposure. Most organic solvents do not have an <u>antidote</u>; therefore, treatment is generally supportive.




For an overview of other types of substance toxicity (e.g., with <u>organophosphates</u>), see the article on <u>poisoning</u>.

### High lipid solubility



* All organic solvents are highly lipid-soluble (i.e., <u>lipophilic</u>), which can lead to:

* Degreasing of the <u>skin</u>  → <u>eczema</u>

* <u>Mucosal</u> irritation

* Permeation of the <u>CNS</u>, with symptoms based on the area affected (e.g, <u>headache</u>)

* <u>Peripheral neuropathy</u> (e.g., <u>polyneuropathy</u> )
