# Personality disorders

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Family medicine > Psychiatric and behavioral disorders > Personality disorders*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nP072T)

---

## Summary

<u>Personality</u> disorders are characterized by deeply rooted, <u>egosyntonic</u> behavioral traits that differ significantly from the expected and accepted norms of an individual's culture. Consequently, regional and cultural characteristics should always be considered before diagnosing a patient with a <u>personality</u> disorder. <u>Personality</u> disorders usually arise during <u>adolescence</u> and are difficult to treat. A key feature of <u>personality</u> disorders is that they must cause impairment in social and/or occupational functioning. <u>Personality</u> disorders are associated with a higher risk of developing other psychiatric disorders, especially in times of <u>stress</u>. In acute care settings, individuals with <u>personality</u> disorders may present with somatic or psychiatric (e.g., self-harm or <u>suicidal ideation</u>) symptoms. Physician-patient encounters may be challenging, and steps should be taken to establish a productive <u>therapeutic relationship</u>.

## Overview

### <u>Personality</u>




* <u>Personality trait</u>: a stable, repetitive pattern of thoughts, feelings, and behaviors, characteristic of a particular individual as expressed in a wide range of social and personal contexts

* The <u>Big Five dimensions of personality</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-6cDnW0)

* The most widely recognized <u>personality</u> model that maps <u>personality</u> according to five distinct dimensions, namely the degree of conscientiousness, agreeableness, neuroticism, openness to experience, and extraversion expressed by an individual.

* For more information, see the dedicated "<u>Personality</u>" article.





> [!NOTE]
> Think OCEAN to remember the <u>Big Five personality traits</u>: Openness to experience, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism.




### <u>Personality</u> disorders




* Definition: pervasive, inflexible, and maladaptive <u>personality</u> patterns that lead to significant <u>distress</u> and/or functional impairment

* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Age of onset: late childhood or <u>adolescence</u>

* Antisocial and narcissistic <u>personality</u> disorders are more commonly diagnosed in male individuals.

* Histrionic and borderline <u>personality</u> disorders are more commonly diagnosed in female individuals.

* Etiology

* Multifactorial

* Caused by a combination of hereditary (e.g., <u>personality</u> disorders in parents) and psychosocial factors (e.g., <u>child neglect</u>, abuse)

* Classification: : The <u>DSM-5</u> divides <u>personality</u> disorders into three <u>clusters</u> (A, B, and C).





| <br>Classification of <u>personality</u> disorders according to the <u>DSM-5</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA) |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cluster | Characteristic behavior | <u>Personality</u> disorders | Commonly associated conditions |
| <u>Cluster A</u> | <br><br>* Odd<br><br>* Eccentric<br><br>* Unable to form close interpersonal relationships<br><br>* Typically: no <u>psychosis</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Paranoid personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Schizoid personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Schizotypal personality disorder</u><br><br><br> | <br>* <u>Psychotic disorders</u> (e.g., <u>schizophrenia</u>)<br><br> |
| <u>Cluster B</u> | <br><br>* Dramatic<br><br>* Emotional<br><br>* Erratic<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Antisocial personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Borderline personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Histrionic personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Narcissistic personality disorder</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Mood disorders</u><br><br>* <u>Recreational drug</u> use<br><br><br> |
| <u>Cluster C</u> | <br><br>* Fearful<br><br>* Avoidant<br><br>* Anxious<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Avoidant personality disorder</u><br><br>* <u>Dependent personality disorder</u><br><br>* Obsessive-compulsive <u>personality</u> disorder<br><br><br> | <br>* <u>Anxiety disorders</u><br><br> |





* Clinical features common to all <u>personality</u> disorders

* Affected individuals consider their symptoms normal and nonproblematic.

* Thinking and behavior that significantly differ from cultural expectations

* Diagnostic criteria

* At least two or more of the following deviate significantly from cultural expectations:

* <u>Cognition</u> (e.g., perceives events, others, or self in an inappropriate way)

* Affectivity

* Interpersonal functioning

* Impulse control

* Begins in early adulthood and remains stable over time

* Leads to significant <u>distress</u> and impaired functioning in important areas of life (e.g., social, occupational)

* Is not caused by another mental disorder, <u>substance abuse</u>, or other medical condition

* Can be diagnosed in individuals < 18 years of age if features have been present for ≥ 1 year (except <u>antisocial personality disorder</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Treatment

* <u>Psychotherapy</u>, <u>dialectical behavior therapy</u>, <u>group therapy</u>, and/or <u>cognitive therapy</u>

* Symptomatic medical therapy

* <u>Mood stabilizers</u>: <u>valproate</u>, <u>topiramate</u>, and <u>lamotrigine</u> have been shown to decrease symptoms such as affective dysregulation, impulsivity, and aggression.

* <u>Antipsychotics</u>: especially for symptoms of <u>delusion</u>

* <u>Antidepressants</u>: <u>selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors</u> (<u>SSRIs</u>) are the drug of choice, especially in depressive episodes, <u>anxiety disorders</u>, and <u>obsessive-compulsive disorders</u>.

* See “<u>Acute medical care of patients with personality disorders</u>” for special management considerations in acute settings.





> [!TIP]
> <u>Personality</u> disorders are associated with a high risk of psychiatric comorbidities, especially during times of <u>stress</u>.




> [!NOTE]
> For the general character of each cluster, remember WWW: Weird (A), Wild (B), Worried (C)

## Cluster A

### Overview




| Overview of cluster A personality disorders |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Characteristic features | Additional distinguishing features |
| <u>Paranoid personality disorder</u> | <br>* Excessive suspicion and distrust of others<br><br> | <br><br>* Perception of benign events or remarks as attacks<br><br>* Holding grudges<br><br><br> |
| <u>Schizoid personality disorder</u> | <br>* Social detachment and constricted affect<br><br> | <br><br>* Comfortable with social isolation<br><br>* Lack of interest in sexual contacts with others<br><br><br> |
| <u>Schizotypal personality disorder</u> | <br>* Excessive discomfort in social relations, eccentric behavior<br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Magical thinking</u><br><br>* Social <u>anxiety</u> and awkwardness<br><br><br> |





> [!NOTE]
> Cluster A: <u>paranoid</u> people are Accusatory, schizoid people are Aloof, and schizotypal people are Awkward.









### <u>Cluster A</u> personalities




#### Paranoid personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 4 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Pervasive distrust of others

* Unjustified fear that others will use information against them

* Unjustified suspicion about the loyalty of friends and family

* Unjustified suspicion that others are deceiving or harming them

* Suspicions of infidelity in sexual partners/spouse

* Disproportionate reactions to perceived attacks

* Perceives benign remarks as hidden humiliations

* Holding grudges

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Schizophrenia</u>

* <u>Delusional disorder</u>

* <u>Major depressive disorder with psychotic features</u>

* <u>Schizophreniform disorder</u>

* <u>Brief psychotic disorder</u>

* See “Differential diagnoses” in “<u>Schizophrenia</u>.”

* Side effects of medication

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

* <u>Alcohol withdrawal</u>





#### Schizoid personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 4 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Voluntary detachment from social relationships (e.g., family)

* Enjoys few activities

* Prefers solitary activities

* No or little interest in sexual relationships

* Lacks people to trust or close friends

* Indifferent to praise or criticism

* Restricted emotional expression, flattened affect

* Other features: comfortable with social isolation (unlike in <u>avoidant personality disorder</u>)

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>Dementia</u>

* Side effects of medication

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

* <u>Alcohol withdrawal</u>

* <u>Hypothyroidism</u>





#### Schizotypal personality disorder (a <u>schizophrenia spectrum disorder</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 5 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Odd and eccentric behavior or physical appearance

* <u>Magical thinking</u>

* Social awkwardness

* Excessive social <u>anxiety</u>

* <u>Ideas of reference</u>

* Unusual perceptual experiences (e.g., body <u>illusions</u>)

* Bizarre thinking/speech (e.g., metaphorical, overelaborate)

* <u>Paranoia</u> and suspicion of others

* Constricted affect

* Few or no close friends

* Social <u>anxiety</u> and preference for social isolation because of <u>paranoia</u>

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Schizophrenia</u>

* <u>Schizophreniform disorder</u>

* <u>Brief psychotic disorder</u>

* <u>Schizoaffective disorder</u>

* Side effects of medication

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

* <u>Alcohol withdrawal</u>

* <u>Thyroid disorders</u>





> [!NOTE]
> People with schizO-TYPE-al <u>personality</u> disorder are Odd TYPEs.

| <u>Borderline personality disorder</u> | <br>* Excessive impulsivity and unstable self-image, emotions, and relationships with others<br><br> | <br><br>* Fear of abandonment<br><br>* <u>Suicidal behavior</u><br><br>* Feelings of emptiness<br><br>* Intense anger<br><br>* Episodes of <u>paranoid</u> ideation and/or dissociative symptoms<br><br><br> |
| <u>Histrionic personality disorder</u> | <br>* Attention-seeking and excessively emotional behavior<br><br> | <br><br>* Inappropriate, sexually provocative, and/or seductive behavior during interactions with others<br><br>* Shallow and rapidly shifting emotions<br><br>* Easily influenced by others or circumstances<br><br>* Overestimation of the degree of intimacy in relationships<br><br><br> |
| <u>Narcissistic personality disorder</u> | <br>* Sense of <u>grandiosity</u>, excessive need for attention and admiration, low empathy<br><br> | <br><br>* Taking advantage of others to achieve own goals<br><br>* Envy<br><br>* Difficulties dealing with criticism<br><br><br> |





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Cluster B</u>: antisocial people are Bad, some people are Borderline, histrionic people are flamBoyant, and narcissistic people must be the Best.









### Antisocial personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>: : more common in men

* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>

* The patient is at least 18 years of age.

* Three or more of the following symptoms of <u>conduct disorder</u> are present from at least 15 years of age. 

* Deceitfulness, manipulation

* A history of hostility and repeated aggression

* Repeatedly engaging in criminal activity

* Impulsivity/failure to plan ahead

* A reckless disregard for one's own safety and/or the safety of others

* A failure to fulfill work-related or financial obligations

* A lack of remorse and/or emotional indifference to the plight of others

* Antisocial behavior that is not only due to manifestations of <u>bipolar disorder</u> or <u>schizophrenia</u>

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Intermittent explosive disorder</u>

* <u>Conduct disorder</u> (if < 18 years of age)

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

* <u>Alcohol withdrawal</u>

* Treatment: : extremely difficult to treat

* The aim of treatment is usually to prevent existing symptoms from progressing.

* Older individuals with <u>antisocial personality disorder</u> are typically incarcerated.





> [!NOTE]
> People with antiSOCIal <u>personality</u> disorders are SOCIopaths.




> [!NOTE]
> “Travel to CONey (CONduct disorder) island before age 15 and you will be sent to ANTarctica (ANTisocial <u>personality</u> disorder) after age 18”




### Borderline personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>: : more common in women

* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 5 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Unstable mood (e.g., irritability, <u>anxiety</u>)

* Intense anger that can be difficult to control

* Feelings of emptiness

* Self-damaging acts (e.g., unsafe sex, <u>substance abuse</u>, reckless behavior)

* Self-harm, <u>suicidal behavior</u>

* Unstable self-image

* Suspicion about what others think of them, even <u>paranoid</u> ideation and/or dissociative episodes

* Unstable personal relationships

* Fear of abandonment

* Other features: : <u>splitting (psychiatry)</u>, a <u>defense mechanism</u> in which relationships are categorically good or bad

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Bipolar disorder</u>

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>Schizophrenia</u>, <u>schizophreniform disorder</u>

* Side effects of medication

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

* <u>Alcohol withdrawal</u>

* <u>Hyperthyroidism</u>

* Treatment: : <u>dialectical behavior therapy</u>





### Histrionic personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 5 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Attention-seeking: drawing attention to oneself by way of physical appearance

* Uses dramatic speech

* Exaggerated emotional expression

* Not being the center of attention causes discomfort

* Feelings are often shallow and unstable.

* Exhibiting inappropriate, sexually provocative, and/or seductive behavior during interactions with others

* Easily influenced by others or circumstances

* Overestimating the degree of intimacy in relationships

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Delusional disorder</u>

* <u>Schizophrenia</u>, <u>schizophreniform disorder</u>

* <u>Bipolar disorder</u>

* <u>Substance abuse</u>





### Narcissistic personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>

* At least 5 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Excessive sense of self-importance (e.g., exaggerates achievements)

* Fantasizes disproportionately about success, power, etc.

* Believe in being special and a feeling of superiority

* Great need for admiration

* Expecting favorable treatment from others

* Exploitation of others to achieve their own goals

* Lack of empathy

* Often envious of others

* Often behaves arrogant and/or snobbish

* Important to distinguish from <u>manic</u> or <u>hypomanic episodes</u> in <u>bipolar disorder</u>

* Other features: : difficulty dealing with criticism (e.g., reacts with anger and/or defensiveness), fragile self-esteem

* Classification: 3 subtypes

* <u>Grandiose</u>, overt

* High-functioning

* Vulnerable/covert

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Delusional disorder</u>

* <u>Bipolar disorder</u>

* <u>Schizophrenia</u>, <u>schizophreniform disorder</u>

* <u>Substance abuse</u>

## Cluster C

### Overview




| Overview of cluster C personality disorders |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Characteristic features | Additional distinguishing features |
| <u>Avoidant personality disorder</u> | <br>* Excessive shyness and <u>sensitivity</u> to criticism, low self-esteem<br><br> | <br><br>* Avoiding risks and new activities for fear of failure<br><br>* Strong desire for social relationships hampered by involuntary social withdrawal<br><br><br> |
| <u>Dependent personality disorder</u> | <br>* Fears of separation, clingy behavior, and excessive need for the support of others<br><br> | <br><br>* Difficulty initiating projects or doing things alone<br><br>* History of abusive relationships<br><br>* Increased risk of <u>suicide</u><br><br><br> |
| <u>Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder</u> | <br>* Excessive sense of and need for order and control associated with low flexibility, openness, and efficiency<br><br> | <br><br>* Excessive attachment to worn-out/worthless items<br><br>* Excessive frugality<br><br><br> |










### Avoidant personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>

* At least 4 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Avoidance of interpersonal contact due to fear of criticism or rejection

* Only interacts with people if certain of being liked by them

* Restrained in intimate relationships due to fear of being shamed

* Preoccupation with and hypersensitivity to criticism

* Feelings of inadequacy resulting in involuntary social withdrawal

* Low self-esteem (sees themself as socially awkward, unappealing, or inferior to others)

* Avoids taking risks and seldomly engages in new activities

* Strong desire for social relationships (unlike schizoid personalities), but limited by extreme shyness and social <u>anxiety</u>

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Generalized anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Adjustment disorder</u>

* <u>Social anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Performance anxiety</u>





### Dependent personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>
* At least 5 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Disproportionate need for support

* Difficulty making everyday decisions (often requiring others to assume responsibility)

* Avoids disagreeing with others due to fear of losing their support

* Difficulty initiating projects (e.g., applying for jobs) because of a lack of self-confidence

* Makes extreme efforts to obtain support from others even if these efforts are unpleasant

* Feelings of helplessness when alone

* Urgently seeking new relationships when one fails

* Both afraid of being abandoned and afraid to abandon their partner

* Other features

* Often stuck in abusive relationships

* Associated with an increased risk of <u>suicide</u>

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Generalized anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Adjustment disorder</u>

* <u>Social anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Performance anxiety</u>

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>





### Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y5XniA)




* Diagnostics: according to the <u>DSM-5</u>

* At least 4 of the following criteria have to be met:

* Excessive preoccupation with rules, lists, details, etc.

* <u>Obsession</u> with work and productivity that often occurs at the expense of occupational success (e.g., missing deadlines), social relationships (e.g., excluding social activities to complete tasks), and pleasurable activities (e.g., not taking a vacation)

* Perfectionism that often interferes with task completion

* Unwillingness to delegate work or to collaborate with other people

* Great conscientiousness and fastidiousness, inflexible about matters of morality or ethics

* Cling to worn-out/worthless items (even if they have no sentimental value)

* Often show miserliness (e.g., obsessed with saving money for future disasters)

* Rigid routines

* In contrast to <u>obsessive-compulsive disorder</u> (<u>OCD</u>), <u>intrusive thoughts</u> and repetitive behaviors are not present.

* Other features: Perfectionism and <u>obsession</u> with control are often egosyntonic.

* <u>Egosyntonic</u> is a descriptor used in <u>psychoanalysis</u> for thoughts and behavior patterns that are in agreement with an individual's self-image

* Opposed to <u>OCD</u>, which is typically <u>egodystonic</u>

* Differential diagnoses

* <u>Obsessive-compulsive disorder</u>

* <u>Generalized anxiety disorder</u>

* <u>Performance anxiety</u>





> [!NOTE]
> Cluster C: avoidant people act Cowardly, some people are obsessive-Compulsive, and dependent people are Clingy.
