## Summary

<u>Acute stress disorder</u> and <u>posttraumatic stress disorder</u> (<u>PTSD</u>) are psychiatric conditions that arise following exposure to traumatic events, such as natural disasters, war, accidents, or personal assaults. <u>Acute stress disorder</u> occurs in up to 50% of individuals exposed to a traumatic event, and <u>PTSD</u> affects 6–9% of the US population at some point in their lives. Both conditions are characterized by intrusive memories, avoidance behaviors, mood disturbances, and increased arousal. <u>Acute stress disorder</u> symptoms last from three days to one month after trauma, while <u>PTSD</u> is distinguished by the persistence of symptoms beyond one month. Diagnosis is confirmed using the <u>DSM-5-TR</u> criteria for <u>stress</u>-related disorders. <u>Trauma-focused psychotherapy</u> is the first-line treatment; <u>SSRIs</u> or <u>venlafaxine</u> may also be used. Early intervention following trauma exposure is crucial in preventing the progression of <u>acute stress disorder</u> to <u>PTSD</u>. In children, features of <u>PTSD</u> may include <u>developmental regression</u> and trauma-related play. For children > 6 years of age, adult diagnostic criteria are used; separate diagnostic criteria are used for children ≤ 6 years of age. First-line treatment involves developmentally appropriate <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>. Pharmacotherapy can be considered for refractory symptoms.

## Etiology

* Triggers: exposure to traumatic events (either personally experienced or witnessed) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Gs1BwR0)

* <u>Sexual violence</u> (most common trigger) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Gs1BwR0)

* Physical violence

* Accidents

* Natural disasters

* War  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ts1X9R0)

* Diagnosis of a severe disease

* Witnessing the <u>death</u> of another person

* <u>Risk factors</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Psychiatric comorbidities

* Lower <u>socioeconomic status</u>

* Lack of social support

* Female sex

* <u>Adverse childhood experiences</u>, including prior traumatic exposure and/or subsequent reminders

* High perceived severity of the traumatic event

* Common comorbidities

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>Substance use disorders</u>

* <u>Somatic symptom disorder</u>

## Clinical features

Individuals with <u>acute stress disorder</u> and <u>PTSD</u> experience similar symptoms, which begin after a traumatic event. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Intrusive memories and/or dreams associated with the trauma

* Avoidance of reminders of the traumatic event

* Mood and cognitive disturbances (e.g., <u>dissociative amnesia</u>, guilt, shame, isolation)

* Increased arousal (e.g., <u>hypervigilance</u>, violent outbursts)

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances

* Difficulty concentrating

* <u>Distress</u> or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning





> [!TIP]
> <u>Acute stress disorder</u> and <u>PTSD</u> have the same clinical features and are distinguished by symptom duration.[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




> [!NOTE]
> To remember the features of <u>PTSD</u>, think of “TRAUMMA”: Traumatic event → Reexperience, Avoidance, Unable to function, More than a Month in duration, Arousal is increased

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)




* Screen patients who have experienced a traumatic event using the <u>Primary Care PTSD Screen for DSM-5</u> (<u>PC-PTSD-5</u>).

* Perform a detailed history, focusing on traumatic events and personal and family psychiatric history.

* Consider diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>urine drug screen</u>) if alternative causes are suspected.

* Confirm diagnosis using the <u>DSM-5-TR criteria for stress-related disorders</u>.

* Assess for psychiatric and medical comorbidities, e.g., <u>major depressive disorder</u>, <u>anxiety disorders</u>, <u>substance use disorder</u>.

* <u>Evaluate suicide risk</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Screen military veterans for <u>PTSD</u> annually for the first 5 years after separation from active duty. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7r14iR0)

#### Specifiers for <u>PTSD</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* PTSD with delayed expression: a subtype of <u>PTSD</u> in which individuals first meet the full diagnostic criteria ≥ 6 months after the associated traumatic event(s)

* PTSD with dissociative symptoms: a subtype of <u>PTSD</u> in which individuals meet the diagnostic criteria for <u>PTSD</u> and concomitantly experience symptoms of either <u>derealization</u> or <u>depersonalization</u>

## Management

### Approach [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)




* Provide education about the broad range of expected reactions to traumatic situations, the natural course of the disorder, and treatment options.

* Refer all patients for individual <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>.

* For patients with <u>PTSD</u>:

* Consider <u>SSRI</u> or <u>SNRI</u> therapy.

* Track symptom severity using, e.g., the <u>PTSD Checklist for DSM-5</u>.

* For patients with <u>suicidal ideation</u> or at risk of self-harm, refer to <u>psychiatry</u> and consider hospitalization.





> [!TIP]
> Early <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u> prevents progression to <u>PTSD</u> in patients with <u>acute stress disorder</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Cp1q730)

### <u>Psychotherapy</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)




* <u>Trauma-focused psychotherapy</u> is the first-line treatment for all patients with <u>acute stress disorder</u> or <u>PTSD</u>.

* Recommended modalities

* <u>Prolonged exposure therapy</u>

* <u>Cognitive processing therapy</u>

* Eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (<u>EMDR</u>): The patient recalls traumatic images, sensations, and emotions while undergoing bilateral hemispheric stimulation (e.g., moving eyes from left to right).  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cG1ayR0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IVdYDK0)





### Pharmacological treatment [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)




* <u>Acute stress disorder</u>

* There are no approved pharmacological treatments for <u>acute stress disorder</u>.

* <u>SSRIs</u> may be considered. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YednxK0)

* <u>PTSD</u>

* <u>SSRIs</u> or <u>SNRIs</u>

* Consider for patients who are incomplete responders to, decline to engage in, or are unable to access <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>.

* Agents include: <u>sertraline</u> DOSAGE, <u>paroxetine</u> DOSAGE , <u>venlafaxine</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)

* <u>Prazosin</u>: may be considered for <u>PTSD</u>-associated nightmares





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Benzodiazepines</u> and <u>cannabis</u> should be avoided due to the risk of misuse and lack of evidence supporting therapeutic benefit. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVdgwK0)

## Posttraumatic stress disorder in children

### Etiology




In addition to <u>risk factors for PTSD in adults</u>, <u>risk factors for PTSD</u> in children include the following:




* Development of emotional problems (e.g., <u>anxiety</u>) by 6 years of age [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Trauma inflicted by a caregiver or involving harm to a caregiver [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Parental psychiatric conditions, including <u>PTSD</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/21VTTt0)

* Acute post-trauma panic symptoms (e.g., <u>panic attacks</u>) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)

* Caregiver <u>distress</u> in reaction to the traumatic event  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IgVY9F0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T1V6Tt0)





### Clinical features




<u>Clinical features of PTSD</u> are similar in adults and children, with the following additions.




* Young children [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T1V6Tt0)

* <u>Developmental regression</u> (e.g., language regression, incontinence)

* Trauma-themed play (e.g., trauma reenactments, intervention fantasies)

* New or worsened <u>separation anxiety</u>

* New <u>phobias</u> unrelated to the traumatic event (e.g., fear of the dark)

* Aggressive or oppositional behavior

* <u>Adolescents</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Social disconnection from peers

* Impulsive behaviors

* <u>Suicidal ideation</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Symptoms of PTSD</u> in children (e.g., inattention, struggling with focus and <u>learning</u>) can be mistaken for <u>symptoms of ADHD</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)




### Diagnostics




#### Approach




* Screen children with trauma exposure or <u>symptoms of PTSD</u> using a developmentally appropriate tool.  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/21VTTt0)

* Diagnose <u>PTSD</u> using appropriate age-based criteria.

* Children ≤ 6 years of age: <u>DSM-5-TR</u> criteria for <u>PTSD in children</u> ≤ 6 years of age

* Children > 6 years of age: <u>DSM-5-TR criteria for stress-related disorders</u>

* Evaluate for comorbidities, e.g.: [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T1V6Tt0)

* <u>ADHD</u>

* <u>Depression in children</u>

* <u>Oppositional defiant disorder</u>

* <u>Separation anxiety disorder</u>

* Perform a <u>suicide</u> risk assessment. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)

* Diagnostic uncertainty: Refer to pediatric <u>psychiatry</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)





> [!TIP]
> Interview children and caregivers individually when appropriate; children may minimize symptoms in front of caregivers. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/21VTTt0)

| Arousal symptom | <br>* ≥ 2 of the following symptoms:<br><br>* Irritability and angry outbursts; may manifest as intense tantrums<br><br>* <u>Hypervigilance</u><br><br>* Heightened <u>startle reflex</u><br><br>* Poor concentration<br><br>* <u>Sleep</u> problems (e.g., falling or staying asleep)<br><br> |
| Other | <br>* All of the following criteria must be fulfilled.<br><br>* Significant <u>distress</u> or impaired social interactions<br><br>* Symptoms not explained by the effects of a substance (e.g., medication) or another medical condition (see "<u>Differential diagnoses of PTSD</u>")<br><br>* Symptoms last > 1 month<br><br> |

### Management




Management involves a multidisciplinary team (e.g., <u>pediatrician</u>, psychotherapist). [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)




#### Approach [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)




* Involve caregivers and consider school accommodations in treatment plans.

* Refer for <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>.

* Treat comorbid psychiatric conditions, e.g.:

* <u>Treatment of depression in children</u>

* <u>Treatment of separation anxiety disorder</u>

* For patients with symptoms refractory to <u>psychotherapy</u>, consider adjunctive pharmacotherapy. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)

* Medication-refractory symptoms : Refer to pediatric <u>psychiatry</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)





> [!TIP]
> Engaging caregivers in the treatment process improves outcomes. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)




#### <u>Trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>




* Preferred: trauma-focused <u>CBT</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/21VTTt0)

* Alternative options include: [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/21VTTt0)

* <u>EMDR</u>

* Psychodynamic <u>trauma-focused psychotherapy</u>

* Multidisciplinary programs that combine therapies





> [!TIP]
> Modify <u>psychotherapy</u> according to the child's developmental capacity. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IgVY9F0)




#### Pharmacotherapy [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)




* All medication use for <u>PTSD in children</u> is <u>off-label</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)

* Tailor medication to specific symptom management, e.g.:

* Hyperarousal symptoms (e.g., impulsivity, <u>anxiety</u>, <u>tachycardia</u>) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)

* <u>Alpha-2 adrenergic agonists</u> (e.g., <u>clonidine</u>, <u>guanfacine</u>)

* <u>Beta blockers</u> (e.g., <u>propranolol</u>)

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances: <u>prazosin</u> [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)

* There is a lack of consensus on the use of <u>SSRIs</u> to treat <u>PTSD in children</u>.  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U1VbTt0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GgVB9F0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ShVyW80)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ghVFW80)
