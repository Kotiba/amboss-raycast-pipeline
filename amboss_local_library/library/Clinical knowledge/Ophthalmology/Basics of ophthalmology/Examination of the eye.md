## Summary

The evaluation of the eyes is an essential component of the <u>physical examination</u>. There are two types of <u>eye</u> examinations, basic and comprehensive. This article focuses on the comprehensive <u>eye</u> examination, a series of at least twelve tests to screen and diagnose common <u>eye</u> disorders. It should be performed on patients with ophthalmologic symptoms and annually on all individuals ≥ 40 years of age. Tests typically include evaluation of <u>visual acuity</u>, <u>visual fields</u>, the external <u>eye</u> structure (<u>orbit</u>, eyebrows, eyelids and margins, <u>palpebral aperture</u>, <u>lacrimal apparatus</u>, <u>conjunctiva</u>, <u>sclera</u>, <u>cornea</u>), the <u>anterior</u> ocular chamber (<u>iris</u>, <u>lens</u>, <u>pupils</u>), <u>ocular movements</u>, and the optic fundus. Adjunct tests and procedures may be indicated depending on the findings of the initial exam.




For more information on the basic <u>eye</u> examination, see “<u>Head and neck examination</u>.”
