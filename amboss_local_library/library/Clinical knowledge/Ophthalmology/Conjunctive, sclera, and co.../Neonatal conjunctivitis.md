# Neonatal conjunctivitis

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nt07V3)

## Summary

Neonatal <u>conjunctivitis</u> is <u>inflammation of the conjunctiva</u> in <u>infants</u> < 4 weeks of age. Most cases are caused by <u>perinatally</u> transmitted infections (e.g., <u>gonorrhea</u>, <u>chlamydia</u>), however, rarely, neonatal <u>conjunctivitis</u> may result from a reaction to antimicrobial prophylaxis given at <u>birth</u> (<u>aseptic neonatal conjunctivitis</u>). Common features include <u>conjunctival injection</u>, swollen eyelids, and ocular discharge. Untreated infections may progress rapidly, causing blindness and systemic infection. Culture of the <u>conjunctiva</u> and <u>exudate</u> should be performed for <u>neonates</u> exhibiting <u>clinical features of neonatal conjunctivitis</u> before initiating immediate <u>antimicrobial therapy</u>. All patients should be monitored for signs of <u>neonatal sepsis</u> and specialists should be consulted early in management. Neonatal <u>conjunctivitis</u> is rare in high-income countries because of the implementation of comprehensive preventive measures (i.e., <u>counseling on sexually transmitted infection prevention</u>, antenatal screening and treatment of infections, and prophylactic <u>antibiotics</u> at <u>birth</u>).
