# Infantile colic

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Pediatrics > Neonatology > Infantile colic*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jC0_7R)

---

## Summary

Infantile colic is a benign condition characterized by <u>paroxysmal</u> bouts of crying in an otherwise healthy <u>infant</u> with appropriate weight gain. Crying is typically unprovoked, inconsolable, and often occurs in the late afternoon or evening. During these episodes, <u>infants</u> may have <u>facial flushing</u>, grimacing, <u>flexion</u> of the hips, and increased gas. Symptoms usually peak at approximately 6 weeks of age and typically resolve by 5 months. Infantile colic is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>; use of the <u>Rome IV diagnostic criteria for infantile colic</u> or the <u>rule of threes (colic)</u> may support the diagnosis. The condition is <u>self-limited</u>, and management consists of reassurance and identifying caregiver barriers to coping. While select probiotics may benefit exclusively breastfed <u>infants</u>, physical therapies and other medications are not effective.

## Clinical features

Infantile colic is characterized by bouts of crying in an otherwise healthy <u>infant</u> with appropriate weight gain. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)




* Crying episodes are often described as: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JiVss80) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)

* Unprovoked, prolonged, and inconsolable

* Occurring in the late afternoon or evening

* Physical manifestations may include: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JiVss80)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)

* Grimacing, clenching fists, flushing

* <u>Flexion</u> of the hips

* Increased gas





> [!TIP]
> Symptoms typically peak at ∼ 6 weeks and resolve by 5 months of age. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)




### Red flags for infantile colic [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qiVCs80)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)




* <u>Red flags</u> for inconsolable crying [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)

* <u>Fever</u>

* Gastroinestinal symptoms (e.g., <u>bilious</u> and/or <u>projectile vomiting</u>, explosive <u>diarrhea</u>, abdominal distension, bloody stools) [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qiVCs80)

* Poor feeding

* <u>Lethargy</u>

* Potential symptoms of <u>nonaccidental injury</u> (e.g., tenderness to palpation over the <u>clavicle</u>, scalp, <u>long bones</u>)

* Inguinal and/or scrotal swelling

* Extreme or high-pitched crying

* <u>Growth faltering</u> or weight loss

* <u>Family history</u> of <u>atopy</u> or <u>migraine</u>

* Abnormal examination findings

* Maternal drug use

* Symptoms persisting beyond 5 months of age

* Parental mental health disorders (severe <u>anxiety</u>, <u>depression</u>)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)

## Diagnosis

### General principles




* Infantile colic is primarily a diagnosis of exclusion. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)

* Obtain a detailed history, and perform a thorough <u>physical examination</u>, including assessment for: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)

* <u>Pediatric growth parameters</u>

* <u>Clinical features of dehydration</u>

* Screen for <u>red flags for infantile colic</u>.

* If abnormalities are present, investigate as needed (e.g., abdominal <u>ultrasonography</u>) to exclude <u>differential diagnoses of infantile colic</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)

* Consider asking parents to keep a diary of <u>infant</u> behavior.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)

* Rome IV diagnotic criteria for infantile colic are preferred but the <u>rule of threes (colic)</u> is also widely used. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)





> [!TIP]
> Diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>laboratory studies</u>, imaging) are not required in a healthy <u>infant</u> with normal growth and normal phsyical examination. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)




### Rome IV diagnostic criteria for infantile colic [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)




Must meet all of the following criteria:




* Symptoms that begin in <u>infancy</u> and resolve before 5 months of age

* Repeated and prolonged periods of crying, fussiness, and/or irritability without an obvious reason that the caregiver is unable to stop or soothe

* No signs of <u>growth faltering</u> or evidence of underlying illness (e.g., <u>fever</u>)





### Rule of threes (colic)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TjV6-80)




Crying in an otherwise healthy <u>infant</u> that lasts: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)




* ≥ 3 hours per day

* ≥ 3 days per week

* For ≥ 3 weeks

## Management

* Infantile colic is a <u>self-limited</u> condition but may lead to caregiver exhaustion and negatively affect bonding with the child. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)

* Provide reassurance that the condition typically resolves by 5 months of age. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)

* Identify caregiver barriers to coping,  and offer resources.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)

* Ensure appropriate nutritional intake and feeding volume and/or frequency. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qiVCs80)

* The following interventions are commonly offered but have little to no supporting evidence: 

* Modified feeding techniques  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JiVss80)

* Dietary interventions, e.g.: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQVBx80)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jOV_Hu0)

* Low-<u>allergen</u> diet for <u>breastfeeding</u> parents

* <u>Hydrolyzed formula</u> for formula-fed <u>infants</u>

* Probiotics in exclusively breastfed <u>infants</u>  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/piVLs80)





> [!WARNING]
> Medications are not recommended for infantile colic as they are either ineffective (e.g., <u>simethicone</u>, medications for <u>GERD</u>) or are contraindicated in this age group (e.g., <u>dicyclomine</u>). Physical therapies (e.g., chiropractic and osteopathic manipulation) are also ineffective for treating colic. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/__d5Is0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)
