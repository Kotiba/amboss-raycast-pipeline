## Summary

<u>Fever</u> in <u>infants</u> ≤ 60 days of age is a concerning clinical feature as it may be a sign of invasive bacterial infection. The <u>immune system</u> of <u>neonates</u> and young <u>infants</u> is immature and they face additional infection risks compared to older <u>infants</u>, such as <u>vertical transmission</u> of pathogens at <u>birth</u> and <u>vaccine</u>-preventable illnesses. While the etiology may be benign, <u>infant</u> ≤ 60 days old should be fully assessed even if they appear well. The extent of the diagnostic studies required depends on the age of the <u>infant</u> and their clinical status. Management usually consists of <u>empiric antibiotic therapy</u>. Younger <u>neonates</u> require admission; in <u>infants</u> > 21 days old home observation can be considered if select criteria are met.




Traditionally this guidance was extended to all <u>infants</u> ≤ 90 days of age with <u>fever</u>. In 2021, guidance from the <u>AAP</u> suggested that in well-appearing <u>infants</u> > 60 days of age, this strategy risks <u>iatrogenic harm</u> from over-investigation and treatment. For <u>management of fever in children</u> and <u>infants</u> > 60 days of age, see “<u>Pediatric fever</u>.”
