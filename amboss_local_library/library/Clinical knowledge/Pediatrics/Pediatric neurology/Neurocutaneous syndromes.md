# Neurocutaneous syndromes

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Pediatrics > Pediatric neurology > Neurocutaneous syndromes*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Rk0lnT)

---

## Summary

Neurocutaneous syndromes (phakomatoses) are a diverse class of congenital disorders that affect organs of ectodermal origin, especially the <u>skin</u>, the <u>central nervous system</u>, and the eyes. The disorders most typically included in this class are <u>neurofibromatosis</u> type 1 (<u>NF type 1</u>, <u>von Recklinghausen syndrome</u>), <u>neurofibromatosis</u> type 2 (<u>NF type 2</u>), <u>tuberous sclerosis</u>, <u>von Hippel-Lindau</u> syndrome, <u>Sturge-Weber</u> syndrome, and <u>ataxia telangiectasia</u>. With the exception of <u>Sturge-Weber</u> syndrome, which is caused by a noninherited developmental anomaly of <u>neural crest derivatives</u>, and <u>ataxia telangiectasia</u>, which follows an <u>autosomal recessive inheritance</u> pattern, neurocutaneous syndromes disorders follow an <u>autosomal dominant inheritance</u> pattern, although spontaneous mutations are also possible. Characteristic features include <u>neurofibromas</u>, <u>café au lait spots</u>, axillary freckling, <u>pheochromocytoma</u>, <u>optic glioma</u>, and <u>Lisch nodules</u> (NF 1); bilateral <u>vestibular schwannomas</u>, <u>meningiomas</u>, <u>ependymomas</u>, and bilateral <u>cataracts</u> (NF 2); <u>adenoma sebaceum</u>, <u>ash-leaf spots</u>, <u>shagreen patch</u>, <u>giant cell astrocytoma</u>, cardiac <u>rhabdomyoma</u>, and renal <u>angiomyolipoma</u> (<u>tuberous sclerosis</u>); <u>hemangioblastoma</u>, <u>angiomatosis</u>, bilateral <u>renal cell carcinoma</u>, <u>pheochromocytoma</u> (von Hippel-Lindau syndrome); <u>Port-wine stain</u> and <u>leptomeningeal angioma</u> (<u>Sturge-Weber</u> syndrome); <u>spider angioma</u>, <u>lymphoma</u>, <u>leukemia</u>, <u>gastric carcinoma</u>, and ocular <u>telangiectasia</u> (<u>ataxia telangiectasia</u>). Diagnosis is based on clinical findings and is confirmed by <u>genetic testing</u>. Since there is no curative treatment, the management of neurocutaneous syndromes is symptom-oriented.

## Neurofibromatosis

### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Incidence</u>

* <u>Neurofibromatosis type 1</u>: 1:2,500–3,000 [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPbvUF)

* <u>Neurofibromatosis type 2</u>: ∼ 1:33,000 [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5Pbi2F)




### Etiology



* <u>Neurofibromatosis</u> type 1 and type 2: <u>autosomal dominant inheritance</u> or spontaneous mutation




### Pathophysiology



* Mutation of <u>tumor suppressor</u> <u>gene</u> → loss of function → uninhibited cell growth → <u>neurofibroma</u> development 

* <u>NF type 1</u>: NF1 gene mutation (100% <u>penetrance</u>)

* Encodes neurofibromin protein

* Located on <u>chromosome</u> 17

* Inhibition of cell growth and <u>proliferation</u> via inhibition of the <u>Ras</u> <u>signal transduction pathway</u> (<u>Ras</u> activity is inhibited by the stimulation of <u>GTPase</u>)

* <u>NF type 2</u>: NF2 gene mutation

* Encodes merlin protein

* Located on <u>chromosome</u> 22




### Clinical features of neurofibromatosis




![Clinical features of neurofibromatosis type 1 and type 2](../../../../media/ziyt6krbv36cn1hsymdrrcqz.jpg)

Clinical features of neurofibromatosis type 1 and type 2




#### Neurofibromatosis type 1 (<u>von Recklinghausen syndrome</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9PbNSF)




* Multiple neurofibromas: benign peripheral nerve sheath tumors that originate from <u>neural crest cells</u> and affect the myelinated nerves. 

* Soft, painless <u>nodules</u>: typically manifest under or on the <u>skin</u>

* Age of onset: <u>adolescence</u>

* Malignant transformation possible

* Features due to <u>melanocyte</u> dysfunction:

* Café au lait spots

* Brown (<u>hyperpigmented</u>), flat <u>macule</u> or patch

* Age of onset: before 2 years

* Lisch nodules

* Pigmented <u>iris hamartomas</u>

* Age of onset: between 5–10 years

* Axillary and inguinal freckling: age of onset is between 3–5 years

* <u>Seizures</u> and/or <u>focal neurologic signs</u> due to <u>brain lesions</u> (especially <u>meningiomas</u>)

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* Bone involvement

* <u>Scoliosis</u>

* <u>Sphenoid</u> wing <u>dysplasia</u>

* <u>Short stature</u>

* Cortical thinning

* <u>Fractures</u>

* <u>Pseudoarthrosis</u>

* Additional findings

* Associated with certain tumors

* <u>Pheochromocytoma</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yPbdhF)

* <u>Nephroblastoma</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BPbzSF)

* Optic pathway <u>glioma</u> (most commonly involving <u>optic nerve</u>) causing unilateral <u>vision loss</u>, <u>proptosis</u>, or <u>precocious puberty</u>

* <u>Macrocephaly</u>

* <u>Hypertension</u>

* <u>Pulmonary stenosis</u>

#### Neurofibromatosis type 2 [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NPb-UF)




* Age of symptom onset: between 18–24 years [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5Pbi2F)

* Bilateral <u>vestibular schwannomas</u> (<u>acoustic neuromas</u>)  

* Located in the <u>internal acoustic meatus</u>, affecting the <u>vestibulocochlear nerve</u>

* Can cause <u>tinnitus</u>, <u>hearing loss</u>, or <u>vertigo</u>

* Early-onset <u>cataracts</u>, usually bilateral

* Multiple cerebral and spinal tumors (especially <u>meningiomas</u> and <u>ependymomas</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-PbDhF)

* Other features: <u>seizures</u>, <u>skin nodules</u>, and/or <u>cafe au lait spots</u> (not specific for <u>NF type 2</u>)





> [!NOTE]
> In NF Type 2, the mutation is in <u>chromosome</u> Twenty-2 and the clinical findings (<u>schwannoma</u> and <u>cataracts</u>) manifest on 2 sides.

### Diagnostics




* <u>MRI</u> of the <u>brain</u> and <u>spine</u> with contrast: to detect e.g., <u>neurofibromas</u>, <u>meningiomas</u>

* Ophthalmological exam: to detect <u>optic glioma</u> in <u>NF type 1</u>

* Auditory testing: to detect <u>acoustic neuromas</u> in <u>NF type 2</u>

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: mutations in <u>NF1 gene</u> or <u>NF2 gene</u>





### Differential diagnosis




* <u>Cafe au lait spots</u>

* <u>McCune-Albright syndrome</u>

* <u>Fanconi anemia</u>

* <u>Tuberous sclerosis</u>

* <u>Ataxia telangiectasia</u>

* <u>Noonan syndrome</u>

* Bloom syndrome [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eJbxGu)

* Etiology: mutations in the BLM gene → deficient <u>helicase</u> enzyme

* Clinical features

* <u>Short stature</u>

* <u>Skin</u> <u>rash</u> (butterfly shaped on nose and cheeks, photosensitive), teleangiectasia of hands and arms, <u>cafe-au-lait spots</u>

* Increased risk of cancer (e.g., <u>leukemia</u>)

* <u>Schwannoma</u>

* <u>Schwannomatosis</u>

* Sporadic <u>vestibular schwannoma</u>





### Treatment




* Excision or resection of tumors (e.g., <u>meningiomas</u>)

* <u>Surgery</u> for <u>kyphoscoliosis</u> in <u>NF type 1</u>

* Drugs targeting the <u>mTOR</u> pathway (e.g., <u>sirolimus</u>) to reduce <u>tumor</u> growth





### Prognosis




* Increased mortality due to malignant transformation of tumors

* Average <u>life expectancy</u>

* <u>NF type 1</u>: ∼ 60 years [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eNbxZ8)

* <u>NF type 2</u>: ∼ 40 years [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UNbb08)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2NbT08)

## Tuberous sclerosis

### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Incidence</u> is ∼ 1:6,000 [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b4bH3F)




### Etiology



* <u>Autosomal dominant inheritance</u> or spontaneous mutation




### Pathophysiology




* Mutation of <u>tumor suppressor genes</u> (variable expression) → loss of function → unchecked cell growth → <u>tumor</u> development

* <u>Tumor suppressor genes</u>

* TSC1 gene on <u>chromosome</u> 9 encodes hamartin protein

* TSC2 gene on <u>chromosome</u> 16 encodes tuberin protein





### Clinical features [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l4bvjF)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/94bNlF)




* <u>Intellectual disability</u> (caused by <u>brain lesions</u>)

* <u>Infantile spasms</u>

* Occur in children < 3 years of age

* Involves head bobbing, flexor spasms, extensor spasms, and/or movements that mimic the <u>startle response</u>

* May manifest with psychomotor regression or behavioral changes

* Other types of <u>seizures</u> (e.g., <u>focal seizures</u>)

* <u>Skin</u> manifestations

* Adenoma sebaceum (facial <u>angiofibroma</u>): <u>benign tumor</u> composed of <u>blood vessels</u> and fibrous <u>connective tissue</u>  

* Age of onset: between 3–4 years of age; development of lesions increases during <u>adolescence</u>

* Features

* Mostly located around the nose and cheeks

* Distribution resembles a butterfly shape

* Can have an <u>acne</u>-like appearance

* Ash-leaf spots: <u>hypopigmented</u> (white) <u>macules</u> on the trunk and extremities  

* Age of onset: present since <u>birth</u>

* Three or more spots are diagnostic

* Visualized using black light emitted by a <u>Wood lamp</u>

* Shagreen patch: flesh-colored <u>papule</u> in the lumbosacral region with an orange-peel appearance

* <u>Skin</u> <u>hamartomas</u>

* Ungual fibromas: flesh-colored <u>papules</u> that grow under (subungual) or around the <u>nails</u> (<u>periungual</u>)

* Small <u>benign tumors</u>

* <u>Brain tumors</u>

* <u>Hamartomas</u>: can be cortical (glioneuronal hamartoma or <u>tuber</u>), subcortical, and/or subependymal

* Giant cell astrocytoma

* Benign, slow-growing <u>tumor</u> of mixed <u>glial cells</u> that is associated with <u>tuberous sclerosis</u>.

* Typically arises in periventricular areas and, if symptomatic, presents subacutely with evidence of <u>obstructive hydrocephalus</u> (e.g., <u>headache</u>, <u>papilledema</u>)

* Cardiac <u>rhabdomyoma</u>

* Present in > 50% of affected individuals

* May cause symptoms of <u>mitral regurgitation</u> and/or <u>congestive heart failure</u>

* Renal disease: may manifest with a feeling of abdominal fullness and/or <u>macrohematuria</u>

* <u>Renal cysts</u>

* <u>Angiomyolipoma</u>

* Renal <u>carcinoma</u> [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/n4b7PF)





> [!NOTE]
> HAMAARTOMASS: Hamartomas, Ash leaf spots, Mind (<u>intellectual disability</u>), Adenoma sebaceum, renal Angiomyolipoma, Rhabdomyoma, Tumor suppressor <u>genes</u> (TSC1 <u>gene</u> and TSC2 <u>gene</u>), <u>autosomal</u> dOminant, Mitral <u>regurgitation</u>, Astrocytomas, Seizures, and Shagreen patches.

### Diagnostics [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4bhlF)




* <u>ECG</u>: cardiac <u>rhabdomyoma</u> can cause ventricular <u>hypertrophy</u> and <u>arrhythmias</u>

* <u>EEG</u>: <u>seizure</u> activity

* Imaging

* <u>Echocardiography</u>: <u>rhabdomyoma</u> (common in the apex of the <u>left ventricle</u>)

* Abdominal <u>MRI</u>: <u>renal cyst</u>, <u>angiomyolipoma</u>, and/or <u>carcinoma</u>

* Contrast cerebral CT/<u>MRI</u>

* Tumors (e.g., <u>giant cell astrocytomas</u>)

* Enlarged ventricles (tumors in the periventricular area commonly cause <u>obstructive hydrocephalus</u>)

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: mutation of <u>TSC1</u> or <u>TSC2 gene</u>

### Differential diagnosis




* Conditions associated with <u>ash-leaf spots</u>

* <u>Nevus anemicus</u>

* <u>Hypomelanosis of Ito</u>

* <u>Vitiligo</u>

* <u>Piebaldism</u>

* Conditions associated with <u>angiofibroma</u>

* <u>Birt-Hogg-Dube syndrome</u>

* <u>MEN-1</u> syndrome

* Conditions associated with <u>shagreen patch</u>

* <u>Birt-Hogg-Dube syndrome</u>

* <u>MEN-1</u> syndrome

* Other <u>hamartoma</u> syndromes

* <u>Cowden syndrome</u>

* <u>Juvenile polyposis syndrome</u>

* <u>Peutz-Jeghers syndrome</u>

* <u>Gorlin syndrome</u>





### Treatment [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4bhlF)




* <u>Seizure</u> control

* <u>Infantile spasms</u>: <u>vigabatrin</u> or <u>ACTH</u>

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u>

* <u>mTOR inhibitors</u>: to treat renal <u>angiomyolipoma</u> and inoperable <u>giant cell astrocytoma</u>

* Removal of <u>angiofibroma</u> (laser treatment or <u>electrosurgery</u>)

* <u>Surgery</u> in the case of:

* <u>Obstructive hydrocephalus</u> (with <u>↑ ICP</u>)

* Drug-resistant <u>seizures</u>





### Prognosis [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fNbk08)




* Renal disease and <u>epilepsy</u> are the most common <u>causes of death</u>.

* <u>Life expectancy</u> depends on severity of disease but can be normal if symptoms are mild and complications are treated accordingly.

## von Hippel-Lindau syndrome

### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Incidence</u>: ∼ 1:36,000 [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_kb5qF)




### Etiology



* <u>Autosomal dominant inheritance</u> or spontaneous mutation




### Pathophysiology



* VHL gene: <u>tumor suppressor gene</u> on the short arm of <u>chromosome</u> 3

* Encodes <u>VHL</u> protein (pVHL)

* Deletion of <u>VHL</u> <u>gene</u> → impaired <u>ubiquitination</u> and elimination of <u>hypoxia-inducible factor 1a</u>;   → loss of function → <u>tumor</u> and cyst development




### Clinical features [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YObnIF)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PObWsF)




Characterized by the development of numerous benign and <u>malignant tumors</u>




* Vascular tumors (<u>hemangioblastoma</u> or <u>angiomatosis</u>)

* Common in <u>retina</u>, <u>cerebellum</u>, <u>brainstem</u>, and/or <u>spine</u>

* Can cause <u>vision loss</u> or <u>focal neurological deficits</u>

* <u>Hemangioblastomas</u> are highly vascularized lesions whose cells have <u>hyperchromatic</u> <u>nuclei</u>.

* Bilateral <u>renal cell carcinoma</u>: can cause flank <u>pain</u>, <u>hematuria</u>, and/or renal dysfunction

* <u>Pheochromocytoma</u>: can cause episodic <u>hypertension</u> with <u>paroxysmal</u> <u>headaches</u>, <u>palpitations</u>, and/or <u>diaphoresis</u>

* Renal, <u>pancreatic</u>, and/or <u>adrenal</u> cysts

* <u>Endolymphatic sac tumor</u>

* Bilateral disease is a <u>pathognomonic</u> feature

* Can cause <u>hearing loss</u>, <u>tinnitus</u>, and/or <u>vertigo</u>

* <u>Cystadenoma</u>: located in the <u>broad ligament</u> or <u>epididymis</u>





> [!NOTE]
> Von HIPPEL-Lindau syndrome: Hemangioblastoma, Increased risk of <u>renal cell carcinoma</u>, Pheochromocytoma, Pancreatic lesions (cysts, <u>cystadenomas</u>, and <u>neuroendocrine tumors</u>), Eye Lesions (retinal angiomas or <u>hemangioblastomas</u>).

### Diagnostics [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PObWsF)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZmcZV10)




* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* <u>BUN</u> and/or <u>creatine</u> to rule out renal impairment

* Plasma <u>catecholamines</u> and urinary <u>catecholamine</u> metabolites to screen for <u>pheochromocytoma</u>

* Fundoscopic <u>eye exam</u>: retinal <u>hemangioblastoma</u>

* Imaging

* Abdominal <u>ultrasound</u>, CT, or <u>MRI</u>: to detect <u>renal cell carcinoma</u> and renal/<u>pancreatic cysts</u>

* CT/<u>MRI</u> of the <u>brain</u> and <u>spine</u>: to detect <u>hemangioblastoma</u>

* CT/<u>MRI</u> of internal auditory canal: <u>endolymphatic sac tumors</u>

* <u>Audiogram</u>: <u>endolymphatic sac tumors</u>

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: mutations in <u>VHL</u> <u>gene</u>

### Differential diagnosis




* Familial <u>pheochromocytoma</u> syndromes

* <u>MEN 2</u> syndrome

* <u>Neurofibromatosis type 1</u>

* <u>Hereditary paraganglioma-pheochromocytoma syndrome</u>

* Renal and <u>pancreatic cysts</u>: <u>autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease</u>





### Treatment [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XOb9IF)




Mainly consists of regular surveillance and, if necessary, surgical treatment of tumors




* Retinal or <u>CNS</u> <u>hemangioblastoma</u>: resection

* <u>Renal cell carcinoma</u>: <u>nephrectomy</u> or <u>radiofrequency ablation</u>
* Alternative to <u>surgery</u>: antiangiogenic medications (e.g., <u>receptor tyrosine kinase</u> inhibitors such as <u>sunitinib</u>)

* <u>Pheochromocytoma</u>: resection





### Prognosis [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TNb608)




* <u>CNS</u> <u>hemangioblastoma</u> is the main <u>cause of death</u>.

* Average <u>life expectancy</u>: 60 years for women and 67 years for men

## Sturge-Weber syndrome

![Sturge-Weber syndrome fact sheet](../../../../media/zomq076gnwxb807zpyu7rjp7.jpg)

Sturge-Weber syndrome fact sheet




### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Incidence</u>: ∼ 1:50,000 [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hObcHF)




### Etiology



* Congenital noninherited developmental anomaly of <u>neural crest derivatives</u>




### Pathophysiology




* Somatic <u>mosaic</u> mutation of GNAQ gene

* <u>Gain-of-function</u> mutation in one copy of <u>gene</u> → <u>malformation</u> of <u>capillaries</u>





### Clinical features [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3ObSHF)




Vascular <u>malformations</u> involving the following:




* <u>Skin</u>: <u>port-wine stain</u> (<u>nevus flammeus</u>)  

* Nonneoplastic birthmark

* Commonly seen in the <u>CN V1</u> and <u>CN V2</u> <u>dermatomes</u> of the face

* Typically unilateral

* <u>CNS</u>

* Leptomeningeal angioma: benign vascular <u>tumor</u> involving the arachnoid and <u>pia mater</u> (<u>leptomeninges</u>)

* <u>Ipsilateral</u> to <u>port-wine stain</u>

* Commonly involves the parietal or occipital lobes

* <u>Atrophy</u> and calcifications in the <u>cerebral cortex</u> underlying the <u>tumor</u>

* <u>Seizures</u>/<u>epilepsy</u>

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* Recurrent <u>stroke</u>-like episodes: manifests with <u>hemianopia</u> or <u>hemiparesis</u>

* <u>Eye</u>: episcleral angioma, causing ↑ <u>IOP</u> and early-onset <u>glaucoma</u> [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nlb-CF)





> [!NOTE]
> SSTURGGE-Weber: Sporadic, port-wine Stain, Tram-Track calcifications, Unilateral, Recurrent <u>strokes</u> or <u>seizures</u>, Glaucoma, GNAQ <u>gene</u>, Epilepsy.




![Port-wine stain (nevus flammeus)](../../../../media/h6e4wwbo8zfpwic0a116s53z.jpg)

### Diagnostics




* <u>Eye exam</u>: <u>IOP measurement</u> to rule out <u>glaucoma</u>

* <u>EEG</u>: to detect <u>seizure</u> activity

* Imaging

* CT or <u>skull</u> <u>radiograph</u>: gyriform, curvilinear, parallel opacities with a tram-track appearance

* <u>MRI</u> of the <u>brain</u> with contrast: <u>enhancement</u> of <u>leptomeningeal angiomas</u>

* <u>Angiography</u>: to detect abnormal vessel structure

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: mutation in <u>GNAQ gene</u>

* <u>CSF analysis</u>: elevated protein in case of secondary microhemorrhage

### Differential diagnosis



* <u>Port-wine stain</u>: <u>Klippel Trenaunay syndrome</u>




### Treatment




* <u>Seizure</u> control: <u>anticonvulsants</u> or <u>surgery</u>

* <u>Glaucoma</u> management: See “Treatment” in <u>glaucoma</u>.

* Pulsed laser therapy for removal of port-wine stain

* <u>Low-dose aspirin</u> to prevent <u>stroke</u>-like episodes  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6ObjtF)

* Severity of the disease depends on:

* Extent of <u>skin</u> and <u>brain</u> involvement

* Age of onset of <u>seizures</u>

* <u>Life expectancy</u> is normal.

## Ataxia-telangiectasia

### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Incidence</u> is 1:100,000 [[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AObREF)




### Etiology



* <u>Autosomal recessive</u> inheritance




### Pathophysiology



* ATM gene mutation → defective dsDNA break repair → accumulation of mutations → <u>tumor</u> development and <u>immunodeficiency</u> (combined B and <u>T cell</u> <u>immunodeficiency</u>)




### Clinical features [[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zObrEF)




* Neurological features

* <u>Cerebellar</u> involvement

* Age of onset: 6–18 months

* <u>Atrophy</u> of the <u>cerebellar vermis</u> and hemispheres

* Symptoms

* Truncal swaying

* <u>Gait ataxia</u>

* <u>Dyssynergia</u>

* Muscle <u>hypotonia</u>

* Sudden falls

* Movement abnormalities

* <u>Chorea</u>

* <u>Athetosis</u>

* <u>Dystonia</u>

* <u>Myoclonic</u> jerks

* <u>Eye</u> involvement

* <u>Oculomotor apraxia</u>

* <u>Nystagmus</u>

* <u>Strabismus</u>

* <u>Spider angiomas</u>: <u>telangiectasia</u> that mainly involves the <u>conjunctiva</u> and face

* <u>Immunodeficiency</u> (B and <u>T cell</u> deficiency)

* Manifests with recurrent sinopulmonary infections

* Commonly associated with <u>IgA</u> deficiency (e.g., <u>mucosal</u> infection, <u>transfusion</u>-related <u>anaphylaxis</u>)

* ↑ <u>AFP</u>

* Increased risk of <u>malignancy</u>

* <u>Lymphoma</u>

* <u>Leukemia</u>

* <u>Gastric carcinoma</u>





> [!NOTE]
> The 4 A's of <u>ataxia telangiectasia</u>: ATM <u>gene</u>, Ataxia, spider Angiomas, and IgA deficiency.




> [!WARNING]
> Avoid <u>x-ray</u> exposure because of high <u>sensitivity</u> to radiation and increased risk of <u>malignancy</u>.

### Diagnostics




* <u>Serology</u>

* ↑ <u>AFP</u>  [[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8lbOAF)[[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FlbgAF)

* ↓ <u>IgA</u>, <u>IgG</u>, and <u>IgE</u>

* <u>Lymphopenia</u>

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: mutation of <u>ATM gene</u>

* Contrast <u>MRI</u>: may show <u>cerebellar</u> <u>atrophy</u>





### Differential diagnosis [[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zObrEF)




* <u>Cerebral palsy</u>

* <u>Friedreich ataxia</u>

* <u>Ataxia-telangiectasia-like-disorder type 2</u>

* <u>Ataxia with oculomotor apraxia type 1</u>

* <u>Ataxia with oculomotor apraxia type 2</u>

* <u>RIDDLE syndrome</u>

* <u>Niemann-Pick disease</u>

* <u>Gaucher disease</u>





### Treatment




* <u>Antibiotic therapy</u>: for acute infection and/or prophylaxis

* <u>IV immunoglobulin</u> therapy, depending on the severity of Ig deficiency





### Prognosis




* Variable rate of progression

* Affected individuals typically require a wheelchair by <u>adolescence</u>.

* <u>Malignancy</u> is the most common <u>cause of death</u>. [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3NbSa8)

* Average <u>life expectancy</u>: 25 years of age [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hNbca8)
