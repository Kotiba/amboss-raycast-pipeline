# Neurodevelopmental disorders

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Pediatrics > Child development and wellbeing > Neurodevelopmental disorders*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4703Nh)

---

## Summary

Neurodevelopmental disorders are a group of conditions that begin in early childhood and affect the development of the <u>brain</u> and nervous system. These disorders impact areas such as <u>learning</u>, communication, behavior, motor skills, and social functioning. They are typically lifelong and vary in severity. Diagnosis is usually made by a trained developmental specialist (e.g., child <u>adolescent</u> <u>psychiatry</u>, geneticist) using the <u>DSM-5</u> criteria and involves assessing delays and/or impairment in cognitive and adaptive functioning. Management includes a combination of rehabilitative therapy, <u>behavioral therapy</u>, educational support, and, in some cases, medication. Early intervention is the key to improving outcomes.




<u>Autism spectrum disorder</u>, <u>attention deficit hyperactivity disorder</u> (<u>ADHD</u>), and <u>tic disorders</u> are covered in separate articles.

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* Communication disorders

* <u>Language disorder</u>

* <u>Speech sound disorder</u>

* <u>Childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> (<u>stuttering</u>)

* <u>Social (pragmatic) communication disorder</u>

* <u>Autism spectrum disorder</u>

* <u>ADHD</u>

* <u>Specific learning disorder</u>

* Neurodevelopmental motor disorders

* <u>Developmental coordination disorder</u>

* <u>Stereotypic movement disorder</u>

* <u>Tic disorders</u>





### Management of neurodevelopmental disorders [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Cd2IH0)




* Perform an <u>evaluation for abnormal pediatric development</u> to help exclude differential diagnoses.

* Refer to a specialist in pediatric development for assessment.

* Recommend publicly funded programs to address developmental concerns while awaiting formal diagnostic confirmation. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/40W3TP0)

* Children < 3 years of age: <u>early intervention program</u>

* Children ≥ 3 years of age: any public school, regardless of enrollment status

* Academic support (e.g., Individualized Education Program)

* Rehabilitative therapies (e.g., physical, occupational, speech, language)

* For children with a confirmed diagnosis, refer to relevant therapy services as needed, e.g.:

* <u>Behavioral therapy</u>

* <u>Physical therapy</u>

* <u>Occupational therapy</u>

* <u>Speech therapy</u>

* Manage medical problems that may exacerbate behavioral symptoms (e.g., <u>seizures and epilepsy</u>, <u>GERD in children</u>).

* Encourage extracurricular activities to improve social competence.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cCdaIH0)

* Provide caregivers with support as needed.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pSVL080)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSVC080)

## Intellectual developmental disorders

<u>Intellectual developmental disorders</u> are conditions with onset in the developmental period that affect intellectual and adaptive functioning. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Definitions [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Global developmental delay

* Provisional diagnosis given to children < 5 years of age who are considered too young to be adequately assessed

* The child must have missed <u>developmental milestones</u> in ≥ 2 areas of intellectual functioning

* If symptoms persist into later childhood, a diagnosis of <u>intellectual disability</u> is made.

* Intellectual disability

* A neurodevelopmental disorder that manifests during the developmental period

* Affects an individual's intellectual abilities (e.g., abstractive thinking, language, <u>memory</u>) and adaptive functioning (e.g., communication, independent living)

* Diagnosed in children ≥ 5 years of age who are able to participate in testing





### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nyd7fs0)




The <u>prevalence</u> of <u>intellectual developmental disorders</u> is ∼ 1–3%.




### Etiology [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)




* Congenital

* Genetic syndromes (e.g., <u>trisomy 21</u>, <u>fragile X syndrome</u>, <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>, <u>Rett syndrome</u>)

* <u>Inborn errors of metabolism</u> (e.g., <u>phenylketonuria</u>, <u>lysosomal storage diseases</u>, <u>maple syrup urine disease</u>)

* <u>Brain</u> <u>malformation</u> (e.g., <u>microcephaly</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)

* Prenatal

* <u>Birth complications</u> (e.g., <u>pre-eclampsia</u>, <u>premature rupture of membranes</u>, <u>hypoperfusion</u> injuries)

* Infections (e.g., <u>TORCH</u>, <u>herpes simplex virus</u>)

* <u>Congenital hypothyroidism</u>

* Intrauterine exposure to toxins and/or <u>teratogens</u> (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, <u>valproate</u>)

* Postnatal

* <u>Kernicterus</u> (due to untreated <u>neonatal jaundice</u>)  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uydpSs0)

* <u>Central nervous system</u> (<u>CNS</u>) infections (e.g., <u>encephalitis</u>, <u>meningitis</u>)

* <u>Brain tumor</u>

* <u>Seizure</u> and <u>epilepsy</u>

* <u>Traumatic brain injury</u>

* <u>Child maltreatment</u>

* <u>Poisoning</u> (e.g., <u>lead poisoning</u>) [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)





### Diagnosis of <u>intellectual developmental disorders</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)




* Confirm the diagnosis using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for intellectual disability</u>.

* Determine the extent of severity in the following domains to determine whether impairment is mild, moderate, severe, or profound:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Conceptual domain

* Social domain

* Practical domain

* Assess for underlying conditions (e.g., congenital syndromes).

* Evaluate for comorbid psychiatric conditions (e.g., <u>ADHD</u>, <u>impulse and conduct disorders</u>) and medical conditions (e.g., <u>epilepsy</u>, <u>obesity</u>). [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Intellectual developmental disorders</u> are typically identified during <u>well-child visits</u>, <u>pediatric growth</u> assessments, and/or <u>developmental screenings</u>.




> [!WARNING]
> <u>IQ</u> testing is no longer used to diagnose <u>intellectual developmental disorders</u>; assessment now focuses on adaptive functioning in daily life. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




#### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for intellectual disability [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria:




* Impaired intellect (e.g., reasoning, problem-solving, abstract thinking, judgment, academic <u>learning</u>) confirmed by clinical assessment and standardized <u>intelligence</u> testing  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Impaired adaptive functioning impacting independence across multiple settings (e.g., home, school)

* Onset in early childhood





> [!TIP]
> <u>Global developmental delay</u> is a provisional diagnosis in children < 5 years of age who do not meet <u>developmental milestones</u> in multiple intellectual domains. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)




#### Assessment for an underlying cause [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)




* <u>Family history</u> (≥ 3 generations) to identify <u>inheritance pattern</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)

* <u>Physical examination</u> findings suggestive of an underlying genetic disorder [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)

* Dysmorphic features: distinctive facial features, limb defects

* <u>Skin</u> findings: <u>café-au-lait-spots</u>, <u>skin</u> and/or <u>hair</u> abnormalities

* Abnormal body odor

* <u>Heart murmur</u>

* <u>Hepatosplenomegaly</u>  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)

* Neurological conditions: <u>seizures</u>, <u>focal neurological deficits</u>, <u>vision</u> or <u>hearing loss</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)

* Refer to a specialist (e.g., genetics, <u>neurology</u>) for consideration of further testing, e.g.: [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)

* Additional testing for <u>inborn errors of metabolism</u>

* <u>Genetic testing</u> (e.g., FMR1, <u>karyotype</u>, <u>genome</u> <u>sequencing</u>)

* Imaging (e.g., <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u>)

* See also "<u>Evaluation for abnormal pediatric development</u>."





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Developmental regression</u> is concerning for a <u>neurodegenerative brain disease</u> and warrants urgent referral to a specialist (e.g., <u>neurology</u>, genetics). [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)




> [!TIP]
> <u>Inborn errors of metabolism</u> are treatable if detected early. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lydwfs0)




### Differential diagnosis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Borderline intellectual functioning: a poorly defined condition characterized by intellectual limitations that impair daily functioning but do not meet the criteria for an <u>intellectual disability</u>  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5SViZ80)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSVAY80)

* <u>Major neurocognitive disorder</u> or mild neurocognitive impairment

* Other neurodevelopmental disorders (e.g., <u>autism spectrum disorder</u>, communication disorders)





### Management [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V1bGfs)




* Refer to a specialist (e.g., child <u>adolescent</u> <u>psychiatrist</u>, psychologist, <u>rehabilitation</u> therapist) for:

* Nonpharmacological treatment, e.g.:

* <u>Psychotherapy</u> (e.g., <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u>)

* <u>Occupational therapy</u> and/or <u>speech therapy</u>

* Applied behavioral analysis

* Pharmacological treatment (e.g., <u>atypical antipsychotics</u>) for severe irritability and aggression

* Address comorbid conditions (e.g., <u>ADHD</u>, <u>depressive disorders</u>).

* Reassess development in <u>school-age children</u> at least every 3 years. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/99dNpH0)

* See also "<u>Management of neurodevelopmental disorders</u>."

## Specific learning disorder

<u>Specific learning disorder</u> is a neurodevelopmental disorder caused by a combination of genetic, <u>epigenetic</u>, and/or environmental factors that affect an individual's ability to acquire and use academic skills despite having average or above average <u>intelligence</u> and adequate educational opportunities. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### <u>Epidemiology</u>




<u>Prevalence</u>: 5–15% in <u>school-age children</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### <u>Risk factors</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* <u>Family history</u> of <u>specific learning disorder</u>

* <u>Neurofibromatosis type 1</u>

* Exposure to environmental toxins (e.g., lead, <u>air pollution</u>, <u>nicotine</u>)

* Low <u>socioeconomic status</u>

* <u>Preterm delivery</u>

* <u>Very low birth weight</u>





### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for specific learning disorder [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Difficulties with <u>learning</u> and applying ≥ 1 of the following academic skills over ≥ 6 months despite strategies to improve the specific deficits:

* Inaccurate or slow word reading (e.g., reads single words aloud incorrectly, frequently guesses words)

* Understanding the meaning of words read

* Spelling

* Written expression (e.g., multiple grammatical errors, poor structure)

* Understanding of numbers (e.g., sense, facts, or calculation)

* Mathematical reasoning (e.g., applied concepts, problem-solving)

* Academic skills are lower than expected for the individual's age and interfere with academic performance and/or daily life (must be confirmed by a standardized assessment)  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Onset during school-age years

* Difficulty is not better explained by <u>intellectual disability</u>, neurological deficits, psychiatric conditions, and/or inadequate education

* Specifiers (frequently children have impairment in more than one domain)[[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QSVuzF0)

* With impairment in reading (e.g., poor <u>accuracy</u> in reading and comprehension, slow reading rate)

* With impairment in written expression (e.g., spelling, grammar, organization of work)

* With impairment in <u>mathematics</u> (e.g., difficulty understanding numerical information and performing calculations, also known as dyscalculia) [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ACdRuH0)





> [!TIP]
> The term dyslexia is often used to describe when impairments affect both reading (e.g., word recognition, decoding) and spelling. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yCdduH0)




> [!WARNING]
> Ensure difficulties with reading and writing are not secondary to limited English-language proficiency; inquire after difficulties in all languages that a child speaks. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Differential diagnoses [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Normal variation in academic achievement

* Intellectual development disorder

* <u>Learning</u> difficulties secondary to neurologic or sensory disorders

* Neurodegenerative cognitive disorders

* <u>ADHD</u>

* <u>Psychotic disorders</u>





### Management




* Academic support and accommodations are typically required. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QSVuzF0)

* See also "<u>Management of neurodevelopmental disorders</u>." [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Cd2IH0)





### Complications [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Poor educational attainment

* Lower income and workforce engagement

* Psychological <u>distress</u>

* <u>Depression</u> and <u>suicidal thoughts</u>

## Communication disorders

Communication disorders are a group of conditions affecting language, speech, and communication. They include <u>language disorder</u>, <u>speech sound disorder</u>, <u>childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> (<u>stuttering</u>), and <u>social (pragmatic) communication disorder</u>.




### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* <u>Prevalence</u>: up to 13% of children between 2–5 years of age [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fxdkDH0)




### <u>Risk factors</u> for communication disorders [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)




* Male sex

* <u>Hearing</u> deficits

* <u>Birth</u> weight â¤ 2500 g

* <u>Family history</u> of communication disorders





> [!TIP]
> Multilingual children may have initial mild delays in language acquisition but are not at increased risk for communication disorders. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)




### Clinical features




* Difficulties in speech, language, and/or social communication.

* Children may fail to meet <u>language developmental milestones</u>.

* May also present as difficulties with:

* Intelligibility of speech  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Fluency of speech

* Ability to communicate appropriately in different social settings





### Diagnostics for communication disorders [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Perform language assessment across all languages known to the child. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Diagnose communication disorders based on:

* <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for language disorder</u>

* <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria of speech sound disorder</u>

* <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for childhood-onset fluency disorder</u>

* <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria of social (pragmatic) communication disorder</u>





### Management of communication disorders [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)




* Exclude <u>hearing</u> impairments and craniofacial defects (e.g., <u>cleft palate</u>).

* Early referral to <u>speech therapy</u> is vital.

* See also "<u>Management of neurodevelopmental disorders</u>."





> [!TIP]
> <u>School-aged children</u> with persistent communication delay are five times more likely to have reading difficulties and adverse outcomes that persist into adulthood (e.g., lower-skilled jobs, psychosocial impairment). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)

## Language disorder

A <u>language disorder</u> is a communication disorder characterized by a delayed and/or impaired ability to comprehend and/or produce language (i.e., spoken, written, signed). [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Clinical features [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Delay in <u>expressive language</u> and/or <u>receptive language</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Shyness

* Communication limited to familiar individuals

* <u>Language delay</u> affects all languages known to the child

* May have features of common comorbid disorders (e.g., <u>specific learning disorder</u>, <u>ADHD</u>, <u>ASD</u>)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Language disorders</u> in children ≥ 4 years of age are likely to persist into adulthood. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for language disorder [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria:




* Enduring difficulties with acquiring and/or using language (e.g., written, spoken, signed) due to comprehension or production difficulties, including:

* Limited vocabulary

* Difficulty forming complex sentences

* Difficulty putting sentences together to communicate an idea

* Language skills are below the standard for expected age, resulting in difficulties in communication, social engagement, and/or academic or work achievements

* Onset in early childhood

* Not attributable to <u>hearing</u> or other sensory impairment, <u>global developmental delay</u>, or intellectual development disorder





> [!WARNING]
> Assess children in all the languages they speak to prevent limited proficiency in English being mistaken for a <u>language disorder</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Differential diagnosis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Normal variations in language acquisition (especially in children < 4 years of age)  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* <u>Specific learning disorders</u> (e.g., <u>dyslexia</u>)

* <u>Autism spectrum disorder</u>

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* Other neurological disorders (e.g., <u>epilepsy</u>, <u>hearing</u> impairment)

* <u>Child neglect</u>





### Management [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)




* <u>Speech therapy</u>

* See also “Management of communication disorders.”

## Speech sound disorder

Speech and sound disorder is a communication disorder characterized by difficulty producing speech sounds correctly or fluently due insufficient phonological knowledge or inability to create the sounds. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria of speech sound disorder [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria:




* Intelligible speech due to persistent difficulty producing the speech sound

* Limited ability to communicate impairs social participation and/or interferes with achievement (e.g., school, work)

* Onset in early childhood

* Symptoms are not due to an underlying condition (e.g., <u>hearing</u> impairment, <u>cleft palate</u>, <u>cerebral palsy</u>).





> [!TIP]
> <u>Speech sound disorder</u> is associated with genetic disorders (e.g., <u>FOXP2 gene mutation</u>, <u>22q11.2 deletion syndrome</u>, <u>Down syndrome</u>); refer to a geneticist for further testing as needed. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Differential diagnosis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Normal variations in speech (e.g., speech errors in multilingual children)

* <u>Hearing</u> impairment

* <u>Selective mutism</u>

* <u>Dysarthria</u> (e.g., due to <u>cerebral palsy</u>)

* Structural abnormalities (e.g., <u>cleft palate</u>)





### Management [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)




* <u>Speech therapy</u>

* See also “Management of communication disorders.”

## Childhood-onset fluency disorder

<u>Childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> (<u>stuttering</u>) is a communication disorder characterized by disturbances in the normal fluency of speech, with onset in early childhood. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### <u>Epidemiology</u>




The <u>incidence</u> of <u>childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> is 5–10% of children between 2–7 years of age. [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aAdQRs0)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gsXF8z)




> [!TIP]
> <u>Stuttering</u> is heritable; the risk of <u>stuttering</u> is three times higher in <u>first-degree relatives</u> of individuals who stutter compared to the general population. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria:




* Persistent disturbances in the normal fluency of speech of ≥ 1 of the following:

* Sound and syllable repetitions

* Prolongation of consonants and vowels

* Broken words (e.g., pauses)

* Circumlocutions (e.g., problematic words exchanged out for others)

* Words produced with excess force

* Repeating short, one-syllable words (e.g., "I-I-I have a ball")

* <u>Distress</u> about speaking or limitations in social engagement and/or academic or work achievement

* Onset in early childhood

* Not attributable to speech-motor or sensory impairment, neurological deficit, or other medical conditions





### Differential diagnoses [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Normal early childhood dysfluencies

* <u>Hearing</u> or other sensory impairment

* Adult-onset <u>dysfluency</u> (e.g., due to neurological insult, medical conditions, or psychiatric disorders)

* <u>Specific learning disorder</u> with impairment in reading

* <u>Tourette syndrome</u>

* Medication side effects





### Management [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gsXF8z)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/N_d-os0)




* Reassure the child and caregivers that resolution of the disorder is common.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Refer to <u>speech therapy</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bxdHEH0)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gsXF8z)

* Evaluate the child for psychosocial <u>stress</u> related to the condition.

* See also "<u>Management of neurodevelopmental disorders</u>."





> [!TIP]
> Most children with <u>childhood-onset fluency disorder</u> with onset before 7 years of age will eventually develop normal speech. [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gsXF8z)

## Stereotypic movement disorder

<u>Stereotypic movement disorder</u> is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by repetitive, purposeless movements that interfere with daily functioning and may cause self-injury. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)




### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)




* Common in individuals with <u>intellectual disability</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* More common in boys [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MSVMZ80)

* <u>Prevalence</u> is high in children with sensory deficits (e.g., blindness). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)





### <u>Risk factors</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Social isolation

* <u>Stress</u>

* Fear

* Painful medical conditions (e.g., <u>GERD</u>, <u>acute otitis media</u>, <u>dental cavities</u>)

* Inherited syndromes, e.g.:

* <u>Rett syndrome</u>

* <u>Lesch-Nyhan syndrome</u>

* <u>Cornelia de Lange syndrome</u>

* <u>Fragile-X syndrome</u>

* <u>Family history</u> of <u>stereotypic movement disorder</u>

* Frequently associated with other conditions (e.g., <u>ADHD</u>, other neurodevelopmental motor disorders)





### Clinical features [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)




* Often begins ≤ 3 years of age  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)

* Types of movements

* Noninjurious (e.g., rocking, hand flapping, mouth opening) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)

* Self-injurious movement (e.g., banging head, poking <u>eye</u>)

* Episodes

* Last seconds to minutes

* Can occur multiple times per day or have <u>lapses</u> of weeks between movements

* Movements are usually suppressible with redirection in otherwise typically developing children.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)





### Diagnosis of <u>stereotypic movement disorder</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)




* Use caregiver rating scales to identify:

* Severity

* Types of repetitive movements

* Related behaviors

* Evaluate for other neurological comorbidities (e.g., <u>ADHD</u>, <u>learning</u> disorders, <u>anxiety</u>).

* Diagnosis is confirmed using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for stereotypic movement disorder</u>.





#### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for stereotypic movement disorder [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* The diagnosis is confirmed based on the following criteria:

* Repetitive, compulsive movements without a practical purpose (e.g., hand-waving, head banging, body rocking)

* Movements interfere with social and academic functioning and may be self-injurious

* Onset in early childhood

* Not attributable to another medical condition or mental disorder (e.g., <u>obsessive compulsive disorder</u>, <u>trichotillomania</u>)

* Specifiers

* Injurious or noninjurious behavior

* Associated with a genetic and/or other medical condition

* Severity (mild, moderate, severe)





### Differential diagnosis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Normal development

* <u>Autism spectrum disorder</u>

* <u>Tic disorder</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* <u>Obsessive-compulsive disorder</u>

* <u>Tardive dyskinesias</u>

* Neurologic disorders causing <u>chorea</u>, <u>dystonia</u>, or <u>myoclonus</u>

* <u>Substance use disorder</u>

* <u>Functional neurological symptom disorder</u>





> [!TIP]
> Simple stereotypic movements are common in early childhood in otherwise normally developing children and often resolve spontaneously. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




### Management [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)




* For problematic stereotypic behavior: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)

* Encourage caregivers to interrupt and redirect behaviors.

* Refer for <u>habit reversal therapy</u>.

* Refer to pediatric <u>neurology</u> for possible pharmacological treatment for:

* Self-injurious behavior

* Refractory symptoms

* See also "<u>Management of neurodevelopmental disorders</u>."





> [!TIP]
> Noninjurious stereotypical behavior that does not cause emotional <u>distress</u> does not usually require any intervention. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UxdbDH0)
