## Summary

<u>Vaccinations</u> are one of the most effective methods of disease prevention, drastically reducing illness, hospitalizations, and deaths from infectious diseases. In order to prevent a resurgence of <u>vaccine</u>-preventable illnesses in the US, the <u>Centers for Disease Control and Prevention</u> (<u>CDC</u>) and <u>Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices</u> (<u>ACIP</u>) publish yearly <u>immunization</u> schedule recommendations. These schedules are formulated based on extensive research and aim to protect individuals at the age at which they have the highest risk for infection. Specific recommendations are available for the general population (children and adults), individuals with certain health conditions, and those traveling to areas with high rates of infection. Education on the importance of <u>vaccination</u> and the implementation of catch-up schedules for <u>underimmunized</u> individuals are vital to ensuring <u>herd immunity</u> and preventing infectious diseases.

## General principles

### Routine <u>immunization</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ptdLe70)




* Immunizations are a vital component of <u>primary prevention</u>; see “<u>Pathogens affecting unvaccinated individuals</u>.”

* Follow the recommended <u>ACIP immunization schedules</u>.

* <u>Age-appropriate vaccines</u> should be administered at the same visit when feasible; see <u>simultaneous vaccination</u> and <u>combination vaccines</u>.

* Be aware of <u>contraindications for each vaccine</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Yq0nCS)

* Identify individuals with <u>risk factors</u> and/or medical indications that necessitate altered <u>immunization</u> schedules.

* See “<u>Immunization schedule by medical indication</u>.”

* See the “Special patient groups” section

* Educate individuals on the disease that each <u>vaccine</u> aims to prevent and the possible adverse effects; provide <u>Vaccine Information Statements</u>.

* Report any <u>vaccine</u>-related <u>adverse events</u> to the <u>Vaccine</u> Adverse Event Reporting System (see “Tips and links”).





> [!TIP]
> <u>Vaccine</u> dose is not adjusted for weight but may vary based on age.




> [!TIP]
> It is US federal law to provide a <u>Vaccine Information Statement</u> before the administration of every <u>vaccine</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ku1mri0)

### <u>Vaccinations</u> against multiple diseases at the same visit [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




#### Simultaneous vaccination




Administration of > 1 <u>vaccine</u> on the same day, but in different syringes and at different anatomical locations




* May include a combination of <u>inactivated vaccines</u>, <u>live attenuated vaccines</u>, or <u>immunoglobulins</u>

* Most <u>vaccines</u> can be administered simultaneously; exceptions include:

* <u>Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine</u> (<u>PCV15</u> or <u>PCV20</u> or <u>PCV21</u>) and <u>PPSV23</u>: Do not administer simultaneously.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Td2p60)

* <u>Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine</u> (<u>PCV15</u> or <u>PCV20</u> or <u>PCV21</u>) and MenACWY-D: Do not administer simultaneously. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Td2p60)





> [!WARNING]
> If injectable or nasally administered <u>live vaccines</u> are not administered on the same day, they must be administered ≥ 4 weeks apart. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




> [!TIP]
> Live oral <u>vaccines</u> (e.g., <u>rotavirus vaccine</u>) may be administered on the same day or at any interval before or after other <u>live vaccines</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




#### Combination vaccines [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




Combination of different <u>vaccine</u> components in a single product




* Preferred over simultaneous single-component <u>vaccines</u>, when feasible

* Examples of commonly used <u>combination vaccines</u>:

* DTaP-IPV-Hib

* DTaP-HepB-IPV

* DTaP-IPV-HepB-Hib

* DTaP-IPV

* <u>MMRV</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/981NKi0)

* HepA-HepB





### Timing and spacing of <u>vaccines</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




* Follow the <u>ACIP recommendations</u> for the minimum age for the first dose and minimum intervals between doses to ensure optimal protection.

* Unapproved delayed or alternate <u>immunization</u> schedules put individuals at risk.





#### Delayed or missed <u>vaccines</u> (undervaccination) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ptdLe70)




* Follow recommended <u>catch-up immunization schedules</u> for <u>undervaccinated</u> individuals.

* Do not restart the <u>immunization</u> series from the beginning.

* Continue with the next dose in the <u>immunization</u> series.

#### <u>Vaccines</u> administered before the recommended minimum age or interval [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)




In general, any <u>vaccine</u> administered ≥ 5 days earlier than the minimum age or interval is considered invalid and needs to be repeated.




* First dose administered ≥ 5 days earlier than the recommended minimum age

* Repeat the dose when or after the individual attains the minimum recommended age.

* For <u>live vaccines</u>, repeat the first dose ≥ 28 days after the invalid first dose.

* Additional doses administered ≥ 5 days earlier than the recommended minimum interval
* Repeat the dose; the minimum interval restarts from when the invalid dose was administered.





> [!TIP]
> Doses administered ≤ 4 days before the minimum age or interval (grace period) are considered valid. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4u13ri0)

### Immunizations in individuals with HIV [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ptdLe70)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LdWwq40)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jrd_Sr0)




* <u>Inactivated vaccines</u> are generally safe.

* If feasible, <u>vaccination</u> of individuals with <u>HIV</u> should be done before they become <u>immunosuppressed</u>.

* The <u>immune response</u> may be diminished if a <u>vaccine</u> is administered during a period of intense <u>immunosuppression</u>.

* See the latest <u>CDC</u> recommendations for details.





#### Exceptions to the <u>routine immunization schedule</u>




The routine <u>ACIP immunization schedule</u> is applicable to individuals with <u>HIV</u> with the following exceptions:




* <u>Varicella vaccine</u>, <u>MMR vaccine</u>, and <u>dengue</u> <u>vaccines</u>

* Contraindicated in individuals with <u>CD4</u> percentage < 15% or <u>CD4 count</u> < 200/mmÂ³ or an <u>AIDS-defining condition</u>.

* In individuals with <u>CD4</u> percentage ≥ 15% and <u>CD4 count</u> â¥ 200/mmÂ³ for ≥ 6 months: 

* A 2-dose series of <u>MMR</u> is recommended for individuals with no <u>evidence of immunity to MMR</u>.

* Use shared-decision making to determine the need and schedule for the <u>varicella vaccine</u> (in adults) and <u>dengue vaccine</u>.

* The combination <u>MMRV vaccine</u> is contraindicated in all individuals with <u>HIV</u>.

* <u>Influenza vaccine</u>

* <u>Live attenuated influenza vaccine</u> is contraindicated in all individuals with <u>HIV</u>.

* Inactive <u>influenza vaccine</u> should be given instead.

* <u>RZV</u> [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pdWLI40)

* 2 doses, 2–6 months apart (regardless of previous history of <u>shingles</u>)

* Minimum age for first dose: ≥ 19 years of age

* <u>HPV vaccine</u>: Use a 3-dose series regardless of age at first <u>immunization</u>.

* <u>Rotavirus vaccine</u>: Use with caution in <u>infants</u>.

* Refer to the latest <u>CDC</u> recommendations for adjustments to the <u>immunization</u> schedule for: [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LdWwq40)[[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VudGJ70)

* <u>Pneumococcal vaccines</u>

* <u>MenACWY vaccine</u>  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mu1V7i0)

* <u>Hib vaccine</u>

* <u>COVID-19 vaccine</u>

* <u>RSV vaccine</u>

* <u>Mpox vaccine</u>

* <u>HPV vaccine</u>

* <u>HepA vaccine</u>

* <u>HepB vaccine</u>

> <u>Live vaccines</u> are contraindicated in individuals with a <u>CD4 count</u> < 200/mmÂ³ or <u>CD4</u> percentage < 15%. ### Immunizations in preterm infants [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6dWjI40)




* <u>Premature infants</u> weighing < 2,000 g (< 4 lb 5 oz) at <u>birth</u> should receive the first <u>HepB</u> dose at 1 month or at <u>hospital discharge</u>, whichever is first. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)

* <u>Preterm infants</u> should receive all other <u>recommended vaccinations</u> according to chronological age unless contraindicated.





### Immunizations in pregnancy and lactation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O71INR0)




#### Prior to <u>pregnancy</u> [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)




* Recommend all routine <u>age-appropriate immunizations</u>.

* Annually assess <u>immunization</u> status for <u>MMR</u>, <u>varicella</u>, <u>HepB</u>, and <u>Tdap</u> ;  [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bO1HIS0)

* Nonimmune women should receive appropriate <u>vaccinations</u>. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)

* If <u>live vaccines</u> are administered, advise individuals to avoid conceiving for 1–3 months.  [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[50]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FQ1gBg0)[[51]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GQ1Bxg0)

* Recommend current <u>COVID-19 vaccination</u>. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)

* Recommend <u>influenza vaccination</u> (especially if <u>pregnancy</u> is expected during <u>flu season</u>). [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)

* <u>Smallpox</u> <u>vaccine</u> is contraindicated in women who are trying to conceive. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)





#### During <u>pregnancy</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O71INR0)




* For all pregnant individuals, recommend:

* <u>Tdap vaccine</u> once every <u>pregnancy</u>, preferably at 27–36 weeks' <u>gestation</u>  [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O71INR0)

* A single dose of <u>RSV vaccine</u> (ABRYSVO) only if the following criteria are met:  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[52]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oLW0AN0)

* <u>RSV vaccine</u> not received during a prior <u>pregnancy</u>

* <u>Gestational age</u> between 32 weeks and 36 6/7 weeks during <u>RSV season</u>

* Inactivated <u>influenza vaccine</u> ; (IIV3 or RIV3)  during <u>flu season</u> (safe in all trimesters)  [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/571imR0)

* Current <u>COVID-19 vaccination</u> if not up to date

* <u>HepB vaccine</u> if not previously vaccinated  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)

* Consider additional immunizations based on <u>risk factors</u>, e.g.:

* <u>High-risk for HAV infection</u>: <u>HepA</u>

* <u>High-risk for invasive meningococcal disease</u>: <u>meningococcal vaccines</u>

* Increased risk for exposure to <u>poliovirus</u>: <u>IPV</u>

* Certain <u>travel vaccines</u>

* Avoid contraindicated <u>vaccines</u>.

* <u>Live attenuated vaccines</u> are contraindicated as they carry a theoretical risk of fetal infection (e.g., <u>congenital rubella syndrome</u>; , <u>congenital varicella syndrome</u>).  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O71INR0)[[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* <u>Vaccines</u> lacking safety data in pregnant individuals: <u>HPV vaccine</u>, <u>RZV</u>, PreHevbrio <u>HepB vaccine</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O71INR0)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>MMR vaccine</u>, <u>varicella vaccine</u>, <u>HPV vaccine</u>, and <u>live attenuated influenza vaccine</u> are not recommended during <u>pregnancy</u>. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)




#### After <u>pregnancy</u> and/or during lactation [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RH1lJR0)




Lactation is not a contraindication for most <u>vaccines</u>.




* If <u>live attenuated vaccines</u> were deferred during <u>pregnancy</u>, administer them postpartum.

* Contraindicated: <u>smallpox</u> <u>vaccine</u>

* Avoid the <u>yellow fever vaccine</u> unless the benefits outweigh the risks.





> [!TIP]
> Women who lack <u>immunity</u> to <u>rubella</u> and/or <u>varicella</u> on prenatal screening (see “<u>Prenatal care</u>”) should be vaccinated postpartum.




> [!TIP]
> Always consider if the benefits of <u>immunization</u> outweigh the risks during <u>pregnancy</u> and lactation; check contraindications. [[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)




### Immunizations for health care personnel (<u>HCP</u>) [[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CdcqHY0)[[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uUWpUk0)




The following immunizations are an essential part of minimizing the risk of <u>occupational exposure</u> to and transmission of <u>vaccine-preventable diseases</u> in <u>HCP</u>.




#### <u>Vaccine</u> recommendations for all <u>HCP</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uUWpUk0)




* All <u>HCP</u> should receive routine and <u>catch-up vaccinations</u> according to the <u>ACIP immunization schedule</u>.

* In <u>HCP</u> who do not have evidence of <u>pathogen</u>-<u>specific immunity</u> , the following <u>vaccines</u> are recommended:

* <u>HepB vaccine</u>; followed by post-<u>vaccination</u> <u>serology</u> 1–2 months after the last dose

* Annual <u>influenza vaccine</u> [[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FAdgls0)

* <u>MMR vaccine</u>

* <u>Varicella vaccine</u>

* One-time <u>Tdap</u> (if not previously administered); followed by <u>Tdap</u> or Td boosters every 10 years

* <u>COVID-19 vaccines</u>





#### Additional <u>vaccines</u> in high-risk groups [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CdcqHY0)




* Individuals with <u>altered immunocompetence</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6tdje70)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CdcqHY0)

* Additional <u>vaccinations</u> and/or boosters may be required, including:

* <u>Pneumococcal vaccine</u>

* <u>Meningococcal vaccines</u>

* <u>Hib vaccine</u>

* <u>HCP</u> who work with individuals at risk for <u>HAV infection</u> : <u>HepA vaccine</u> is recommended.
