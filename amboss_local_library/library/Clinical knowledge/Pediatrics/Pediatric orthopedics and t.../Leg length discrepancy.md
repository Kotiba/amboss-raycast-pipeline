## Summary

Leg length discrepancy is a <u>limb length discrepancy</u> in which the legs are uneven as a result of bony involvement (i.e., <u>anatomical leg length discrepancy</u>) or muscular abnormalities (i.e., <u>functional leg length discrepancy</u>). Leg length discrepancy may be <u>idiopathic</u>, congenital, or acquired. Individuals are often asymptomatic but, if present, symptoms include a limp and hip and/or <u>back pain</u>. Leg length discrepancy is diagnosed based on clinical leg length measurements and confirmed with imaging studies (e.g., <u>standing full-length hip to ankle x-ray</u>). Management depends on the underlying cause and degree of length discrepancy. Treatment for <u>anatomical leg length discrepancy</u> includes <u>watchful waiting</u>, shoe lifts, and <u>surgery</u> (e.g., leg-lengthening and/or leg-shortening procedures). Complications of leg length discrepancy include functional impairment, compensatory structural and gait abnormalities, and <u>osteoarthritis</u>.

## Etiology

### Anatomical leg length discrepancy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rcdfdK0)




* <u>Idiopathic</u> (most common)

* Congenital conditions

* <u>Hemihyperplasia</u>

* <u>Skeletal dysplasias</u>

* Absent or shortened leg bones (e.g., <u>fibular hemimelia</u>)

* <u>Developmental dysplasia of the hip</u>

* Acquired

* <u>Surgery</u>

* <u>Fracture</u>, especially if it involves an open <u>physis</u> (e.g., <u>Salter-Harris type II fracture</u>)

* <u>Tumor</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YWdnPK0)

* Infection

* Inflammatory <u>arthritis</u>

* Radiation





### Functional leg length discrepancy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rcdfdK0)




Muscular abnormalities as a result of:




* <u>Pelvic</u> tilt from muscle imbalances (e.g., hip <u>adduction</u> or <u>abduction</u> deformities)

* Neurological conditions (e.g., <u>cerebral palsy</u>)

* <u>Contractures</u>

* Compensation for bony abnormalities elsewhere in the body (e.g., <u>scoliosis</u>)

## Clinical features

### Symptoms [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)




* Often asymptomatic

* Limp

* Hip and/or <u>back pain</u>





### <u>Physical examination</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)




Observation of standing and <u>gait assessment</u> may show:




* Asymmetries in the <u>spine</u> and/or hips (e.g., laterally tilted <u>pelvis</u>, lumbar curvature)

* Attempts to even the legs (e.g., bending the knee of the longer leg, tiptoe standing on the shorter leg)

* Abnormal gait

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MfdM560)




* Perform an objective <u>assessment of leg lengths</u>, e.g. with:  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MfdM560)

* Block testing

* <u>Galeazzi test</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bWdHPK0)

* Use direct measurement to help differentiate anatomical and functional leg length discrepancies.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MfdM560)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XWd9PK0)

* <u>Anatomical leg length discrepancy</u>: Measure each leg from the <u>anterior superior iliac spine</u> to the <u>medial malleolus</u>.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rcdfdK0)

* <u>Functional leg length discrepancy</u>: Measure from the <u>umbilicus</u> to the <u>medial</u> malleoli of each ankle.

* Obtain imaging if the cause of the discrepancy is anatomical or unclear.

* Perform further diagnostics as appropriate to determine underlying etiology, e.g., <u>diagnostics for scoliosis</u>.





### Imaging




In patients with suspected leg length discrepancy, imaging is indicated to confirm and quantify the discrepancy. Imaging may also identify an underlying cause. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XWd9PK0)




* Initial imaging  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7cd4dK0)

* Preferred: standing full-length <u>anterior</u>-<u>posterior</u> hip to ankle <u>x-ray</u> (teleoroentgenogram)

* Alternative: <u>scanogram</u>

* Additional imaging studies

* <u>Bone age</u> (in children): to help estimate the predicted leg length discrepancy when skeletal maturity is reached [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ocd0WK0)

* <u>Ultrasound</u>, CT, or <u>MRI</u> may be used to assess etiology or plan <u>surgery</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MfdM560)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HcdKdK0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IcdYdK0)
