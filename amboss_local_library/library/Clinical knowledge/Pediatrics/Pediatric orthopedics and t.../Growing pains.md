# Growing pains

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Pediatrics > Pediatric orthopedics and trauma > Growing pains*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OD0IeR)

## Summary

Growing pains are a common, benign, and <u>self-limited</u> noninflammatory musculoskeletal <u>pain</u> syndrome that affects children. The cause remains unknown. Although growing pains have no standardized definition or diagnostic criteria, they are typically characterized by bilateral nonarticular <u>pain</u> in the lower extremities that occurs late in the day or during the night. <u>Pain</u> is absent during the daytime and when active. Growing pains are a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>. Individuals with atypical symptoms, abnormal examination findings, and/or features of systemic illness should receive a diagnostic evaluation to exclude alternative diagnoses (e.g., overuse syndromes, anatomical abnormalities, infections, <u>neoplasms</u>). Management is supportive and consists of massage, <u>heat therapy</u>, and nonopioid <u>oral analgesics</u>. Most cases resolve spontaneously by <u>adolescence</u>.

## Clinical features

### Clinical features of growing pains




<u>Pain</u> is the defining feature of growing pains and is typically characterized by the following: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pwdLPH0)




* Intensity: mild to severe

* Location [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pwdLPH0)

* Bilateral  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)

* Most commonly affects the lower extremities (e.g., shins, calves, thighs, <u>popliteal fossae</u>)

* Nonarticular

* Timing

* Episodic (lasts minutes to hours) [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RQXlDB)

* Occurs in the evening or nighttime and may:

* Manifest after increased physical exertion

* Awaken the child from <u>sleep</u>, but resolve by morning

* Absent during the day or when active





> [!TIP]
> <u>Pain</u> that limits activity should raise suspicion for <u>differential diagnoses of growing pain</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)




### Red flag features of growing pains [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




Any of the following clinical features suggests a <u>differential diagnosis of growing pains</u>.




* <u>Constitutional symptoms</u>

* Early morning <u>pain</u> or stiffness

* <u>Pain</u> during physical activity

* Unilateral or focal <u>pain</u>

* Persistent or worsening <u>pain</u>

* Abnormal examination findings (e.g., <u>joint</u> swelling, <u>weakness</u>, tenderness, gait changes, inability to bear weight)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




* Perform a comprehensive history and <u>physical examination</u> to evaluate for:

* <u>Clinical features of growing pains</u>

* <u>Differential diagnoses of growing pains</u>

* Diagnose clinically if all of the following are met:

* Consistent history

* Normal <u>physical examination</u>

* No <u>red flag features of growing pains</u>

* If growing pains cannot be diagnosed from clinical findings, evaluate for alternative causes with, e.g.,:

* Diagnostic studies

* Specialty referral





> [!TIP]
> Growing pains are a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




> [!TIP]
> Laboratory and/or imaging are used to evaluate for suspected alternative causes. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




### Diagnostic studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




Laboratory and imaging studies are normal in children with growing pains. Based on clinical suspicion, obtain diagnostic studies to exclude alternative causes. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)




* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* Systemic illness (e.g., inflammatory or infectious conditions)

* <u>CBC with differential</u>

* <u>BMP</u>

* <u>ESR</u> or <u>CRP</u>

* Suspected autoimmune <u>arthropathy</u>: <u>ANA</u>, anti-ds <u>DNA</u>, <u>complement</u> levels, <u>urinalysis</u>

* <u>Restless legs syndrome</u>: <u>CBC</u>, <u>iron studies</u>

* Imaging: to evaluate for structural abnormalities, trauma, bone tumors, or infection 

* Initial: <u>x-rays</u>

* Advanced imaging (e.g., <u>MRI</u> or <u>bone scan</u>): if <u>x-rays</u> are normal and there is a high level of clinical suspicion

## Management

* Reassurance that growing pains: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qwdCPH0)

* Are benign and <u>self-limited</u>

* Typically, resolve spontaneously by <u>adolescence</u>

* Symptomatic <u>pain</u> relief [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JwdsPH0)

* <u>Analgesics</u>: <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>ibuprofen</u> (see “<u>Nonopioid oral analgesia in children</u>” for dosing)

* Massage

* <u>Heat therapy</u>
