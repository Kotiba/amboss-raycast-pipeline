# Adenoid hypertrophy

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Pediatrics > Pediatric ENT and pulmonology > Adenoid hypertrophy*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dj01cT)

---

## Summary

Adenoid <u>hypertrophy</u> is <u>hyperplasia</u> of the <u>pharyngeal tonsils</u>. It only becomes symptomatic if it leads to congestion of the <u>choanae</u> and eustachian tubes. The condition is common in children with recurrent inflammation of the upper <u>airways</u>. Clinical features include mouth breathing, snoring, hyponasal speech, and <u>adenoid facies</u>. Adenoid <u>hypertrophy</u> is diagnosed using <u>flexible nasopharyngoscopy</u>, which should show enlarged adenoid tissue. An <u>adenotonsillectomy</u> is indicated in patients with chronic/<u>recurrent sinusitis</u>, chronic/recurrent <u>otitis media</u>, and/or symptomatic nasal obstruction.

---

## Epidemiology

* Common in children

* Very rare in adults  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6CXjGZ0)





> [!NOTE]
> Adenoid <u>hypertrophy</u> is the most common cause of nasal obstruction in children.




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

---

## Clinical features

### Mechanical congestion of the <u>choanae</u>




* Mouth breathing

* Hyponasal speech

* Adenoid facies 

* Elongated, open-mouthed face, short upper lip, thin nostrils

* Malpositioned and prominent upper <u>teeth</u>, <u>hypoplastic</u> upper <u>jaw</u>, high-arched <u>palate</u>

* Chronic or <u>recurrent rhinosinusitis</u>, <u>nasopharyngitis</u>, and/or <u>epistaxis</u>

* <u>Halitosis</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pCXLGZ0)

* <u>Obstructive sleep apnea</u> (<u>OSA</u>)





### Mechanical congestion of the eustachian tubes



1. * Recurrent <u>chronic otitis media</u>
2. * Tympanic effusion
3. * <u>Conductive hearing loss</u> → developmental disorders (e.g., <u>language development</u> disorder)




> [!NOTE]
> <u>Hyperplasia</u> of the pharyngeal tonsil does not itself cause symptoms. The patient only becomes symptomatic after congestion of the <u>choanae</u> and auditory tubes.

---

## Treatment

* Asymptomatic adenoid vegetations do not require treatment.

* Adenotonsillectomy, or surgical excision of adenoids, is indicated for:

* Chronic/recurrent <u>otitis media</u> in children

* Chronic/<u>recurrent sinusitis</u>

* Severe symptomatic nasal obstruction (e.g., <u>obstructive sleep apnea</u>)

* Treat the underlying cause of inflammation (e.g., intranasal <u>corticosteroids</u> for <u>allergies</u> or <u>antibiotics</u> for infection)
