## Summary

<u>Pneumonia</u>, a common infection in children, is a respiratory illness characterized by inflammation of the alveolar space and/or the <u>interstitial</u> tissue of the <u>lungs</u>. The etiology is typically bacterial or viral, with <u>respiratory syncytial virus</u> (<u>RSV</u>) being the most common <u>pathogen</u> overall, particularly in those < 2 years old. As with adults, <u>pneumonia</u> in children can be categorized as typical or atypical, according to clinical features. For <u>typical pneumonia</u>, the presentation may be similar to those in adults (including productive <u>cough</u>, <u>tachypnea</u>, <u>fever</u>, and <u>lethargy</u>), with possible additional features such as abdominal <u>pain</u>, difficulty feeding, and <u>grunting</u>. In <u>atypical pneumonia</u>, children may present with a mild, slowly-progressing course that includes <u>malaise</u>, low-grade <u>fever</u>, and widespread <u>wheeze</u>. Management depends on severity and etiology. The first step is to determine whether patients meet the <u>admission criteria for pediatric CAP</u>. All patients require a <u>respiratory viral panel</u> to help guide treatment; for children who meet admission criteria, additional diagnostic tests include imaging and blood studies. Empirical treatment consists of supportive measures (e.g., <u>respiratory support</u>, <u>antipyretics</u>), <u>antibiotics</u> for suspected bacterial infection, and antivirals when available (e.g., for <u>influenza</u>, <u>COVID-19</u>).




<u>Neonatal bacterial pneumonia</u> is detailed separately.

## Clinical features

### Classic presentation




* Similar to <u>clinical features of pneumonia</u> in adults, e.g.: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NM1-Kh0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mM1V6h0)

* Productive <u>cough</u> with yellow-green <u>sputum</u>

* <u>Tachypnea</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>

* <u>Increased work of breathing</u>

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Lethargy</u>

* <u>Chest pain</u>

* <u>Crackles</u> on <u>auscultation</u>

* Reduced air entry

* May be preceded by symptoms of an <u>upper respiratory tract infection</u>

* Additional features in children may include: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NM1-Kh0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mM1V6h0)

* Abdominal <u>pain</u>

* Difficulties feeding

* <u>Grunting</u>

### Features of atypical pediatric pneumonia [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* Slow progression

* Mild disease

* <u>Malaise</u>

* <u>Sore throat</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* Low-grade <u>fever</u>

* Widespread <u>wheeze</u>

* Development of <u>cough</u> over 3–5 days (suggestive of <u>Mycoplasma pneumonia</u>)

## Management

### Approach




* Perform an <u>ABCDE assessment</u>; initiate immediate stabilization measures when required.

* Screen for <u>admission criteria for pediatric CAP</u>.

* Order diagnostic studies as indicated.

* All children: <u>respiratory viral panel</u>

* Children requiring admission: blood studies and imaging

* Initiate recommended management (inpatient or outpatient).

* Provide <u>supportive therapy for pneumonia</u> (e.g., <u>respiratory support</u>, <u>antipyretics</u>) as needed.





### Immediate stabilization




* Consult <u>PICU</u>.

* Start <u>respiratory support</u>.

* <u>Hypoxemia</u>: Start <u>supplemental oxygen</u>.

* <u>Respiratory failure</u> or <u>signs of impending respiratory failure</u>: Start <u>mechanical ventilation</u>.

* <u>Hemodynamically unstable</u> patients: Initiate <u>management of septic shock</u>.





### Admission criteria for pediatric CAP [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* <u>Hypoxemia</u> (< 90%)

* <u>Signs of respiratory distress</u>

* Suspected bacterial <u>CAP</u> in <u>infants</u> < 6 months of age

* Suspected <u>pathogen</u> with increased <u>virulence</u> (e.g., <u>MRSA</u>)

* In caregivers, <u>risk factors for poor adherence</u> or <u>barriers to follow up</u>

* Underlying conditions/comorbidities that may affect illness severity





> [!TIP]
> Validated criteria for determining severity and/or need for admission in pediatric <u>pneumonia</u> (equivalent to <u>CURB-65</u> in adults) have not been successfully developed. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dt1oci0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Vt1Gci0)




### Diagnostics for pediatric CAP




All patients require a <u>respiratory viral panel</u>. Further studies are usually reserved for patients with refractory disease or those who require hospitalization.




#### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




* <u>Respiratory viral panel</u>

* Helps determine if <u>antibiotics</u> or targeted <u>antiviral therapy</u> is appropriate.

* For findings see “<u>Etiology of pediatric CAP</u>.”

* Blood tests

* <u>CBC</u>  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cn1aHh0)

* <u>CRP</u>/<u>ESR</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)

* <u>Procalcitonin</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)

* <u>Blood cultures</u>

* <u>Sputum</u> samples are not routinely performed because of:

* Difficulties obtaining a sample in younger children

* Poor yield





#### Imaging[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* CXR for pediatric pneumonia 

* Views: (AP and <u>lateral</u>)

* Indications include <u>hypoxemia</u>, <u>signs of respiratory distress</u>, or refractory disease

* Stable, well-appearing, immunocompetent children ≥ 3 months of age seen in ambulatory settings do not require <u>CXR</u> to confirm the diagnosis. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dDdoWH0)

* Findings: See “<u>X-ray findings in pneumonia</u>.”

* <u>Lung POCUS</u>

* Indications

* Can be used as an alternative to <u>CXR</u>

* Suspected <u>parapneumonic effusions</u>, <u>empyema</u>

* Findings: See “<u>Lung ultrasound in pneumonia</u>.”

#### Additional studies




* <u>Bronchoscopy</u>;  and <u>thoracentesis</u> are among the additional studies that may be performed in severe or complicated disease.

* For further information, see “<u>Advanced diagnostics for pneumonia</u>.”





### Inpatient management [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* Start <u>supportive therapy for pneumonia</u> including <u>respiratory support</u>.

* Consider infectious disease/pulmonology/<u>PICU</u> consults as appropriate.

* Start <u>pathogen</u>-directed management.

* See “<u>Management of bacterial pneumonia in children</u>.”

* See “<u>Management of viral pneumonia in children</u>.”

* Tailor treatment to culture results when available.

* Regularly reassess patients for <u>complications of pediatric CAP</u>; treat if present.

* Consider discharge when the following criteria are met: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)

* Clinical improvement for at least 12 hours

* Stable <u>pediatric vital signs</u>

* Able to tolerate oral medication

* Caregivers are able to give medication and there are no <u>barriers to follow-up</u>.





### Outpatient management [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* Initiate <u>supportive therapy for pneumonia</u>.

* Start <u>pathogen</u>-directed management.

* See “<u>Management of bacterial pneumonia in children</u>.”

* See “<u>Management of viral pneumonia in children</u>.”

* Educate caregivers on <u>return precautions</u> and arrange for a follow-up in 48–72 hours.

* At follow-up: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)

* Clinical improvement: Continue treatment.

* No improvement

* Assess for complications (see “<u>Diagnostics for pediatric CAP</u>”).

* Reevaluate for <u>admission criteria for pediatric CAP</u>.

* Outpatient oral <u>antibiotic therapy</u> is typically given for a maximum of 7 days. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)





> [!TIP]
> For pre-<u>school-aged children</u> (2–5 years old), <u>antibiotics</u> are not routinely recommended, as <u>viruses</u> are the most common cause.

## Management of bacterial pneumonia in children

### Overview [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)




* Start <u>empiric antibiotics</u> for all patients with suspected bacterial <u>pneumonia</u>.

* Patients requiring hospitalization: usually intravenous <u>antibiotic therapy</u>

* Outpatients: oral <u>antibiotic therapy</u>

* If <u>features of atypical pediatric pneumonia</u> are present, cover for atypical pathogens.

* <u>Symptoms of pediatric sepsis</u>: Initiate <u>treatment of pediatric sepsis</u>.

* Provide <u>supportive treatment for pneumonia</u>.

* Tailor treatment to culture results if possible.

* Switch patients on IV <u>antibiotics</u> to oral therapy when: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)

* Symptoms improve.

* Patients can tolerate oral intake.

* Regularly reassess patients, and escalate care as needed.





> [!TIP]
> If typical and atypical <u>CAP</u> cannot be distinguished from each other, combine a <u>beta-lactam</u> and a <u>macrolide</u> for empiric therapy. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)

## Complications

* Pulmonary complications [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)

* <u>Parapneumonic effusion</u>

* <u>Empyema</u>

* <u>Lung abscess</u>

* Multilobar disease

* <u>Pneumothorax</u>

* Bronchopleural <u>fistula</u>

* Dissemination of infection, causing: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5M1i6h0)

* <u>Sepsis</u>

* <u>Septic arthritis</u>

* <u>Osteomyelitis</u>

* <u>Meningitis</u>

* Cardiac involvement (<u>endocarditis</u> or <u>pericarditis</u>)

* <u>CNS</u> <u>abscess</u>




We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.

## Prevention

### General preventative measures




* Educate caregivers and patients about <u>hand hygiene</u> and <u>respiratory hygiene</u>.

* Encourage <u>breastfeeding</u>. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vG1Aai0)

* Children attending daycare have an increased risk of <u>pneumonia</u>; alternatives may be preferable for high-risk <u>infants</u>.  [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DG11Yi0)

* Advise caregivers to keep immunizations up-to-date (see also “<u>Immunization schedule</u>”).





### Immunizations [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)




* During each <u>pregnancy</u>, advise mothers to get a booster for:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lM1vKh0)

* <u>Influenza</u>

* <u>Diphtheria</u> and <u>tetanus toxoids</u>, <u>acellular pertussis</u>

* Ensure children receive <u>recommended immunizations</u>.

* <u>Pneumococcal vaccines</u>

* <u>Hib vaccine</u>

* <u>Pertussis vaccine</u> (DTap, <u>Tdap</u>)

* Annual <u>influenza vaccine</u>

* <u>COVID-19 vaccine</u>





### Pharmacotherapy for high-risk children




* For the <u>RSV season</u>: <u>RSV prophylaxis</u>

* <u>COVID-19</u>

* Consider <u>preexposure prophylaxis for COVID-19</u>.

* Confirmed infection: <u>COVID-19 pharmacotherapy for children</u>

* <u>Cystic fibrosis</u> patients: Consider long-term <u>azithromycin</u> (see “<u>Prevention of infection in CF</u>”).
