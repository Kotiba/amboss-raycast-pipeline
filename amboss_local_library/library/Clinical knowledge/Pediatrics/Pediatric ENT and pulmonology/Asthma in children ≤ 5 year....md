## Summary

<u>Asthma</u> is a respiratory disease that is characterized by chronic airway inflammation and commonly develops in childhood. In children ≤ 5 years of age, <u>risk factors for asthma</u> and <u>asthma triggers</u> are similar to older children and adults, but the diagnosis is based on clinical evaluation and does not include diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>pulmonary function testing</u>). A <u>clinical diagnosis</u> is made in children ≤ 5 years with all of the following: recurrent episodes of acute <u>wheezing</u>, alternative causes have been reasonably excluded, and there is a documented positive clinical response to pharmacological <u>asthma treatment</u>. Management of acute exacerbations is based on exacerbation severity and includes inhaled <u>short-acting beta agonists</u> (SABAs) with or without inhaled <u>ipratropium bromide</u> and <u>systemic corticosteroids</u>. Maintenance management follows a stepwise approach aimed at improving symptoms and quality of life while minimizing exacerbations and adverse effects of treatment. Pharmacological treatment typically includes as-needed <u>SABAs</u> for acute symptoms and <u>inhaled corticosteroids</u> (<u>ICS</u>) for more frequent symptoms. Long-term management should include regular follow-ups, a written <u>asthma action plan</u>, monitoring of response to pharmacological treatment, and adjustments as indicated.
