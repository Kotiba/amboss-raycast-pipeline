## Summary

Prenatal <u>urinary tract</u> dilation (UTD) is the most common <u>urinary tract</u> abnormality detected on <u>prenatal ultrasound</u>, occurring in up to 5% of all <u>pregnancies</u>. It is most often physiologic and resolves spontaneously within the first 3 years of life. <u>Neonates</u> with persistent or significant postnatal UTD should be evaluated for <u>vesicoureteral reflux</u> (<u>VUR</u>) and <u>congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract</u> (<u>CAKUT</u>). In addition to treating the underlying cause, postnatal management includes <u>antibiotic prophylaxis</u> and periodic ultrasounds to evaluate for resolution. <u>Surgery</u> may be indicated for patients with severe UTD or a high risk of <u>kidney failure</u>.
