## Summary

<u>Diabetic ketoacidosis</u> (<u>DKA</u>) is an acute, life-threatening <u>complication of diabetes mellitus</u> that occurs primarily in children with type 1 <u>diabetes mellitus</u> (<u>T1DM</u>). It is caused by <u>insulin</u> deficiency, often secondary to inadequate <u>insulin therapy</u> (e.g., <u>insulin pump</u> failure, missed doses), or increased <u>insulin</u> demand (e.g., infection, <u>stress</u>). <u>DKA</u> is frequently the first symptom in the initial diagnosis of <u>T1DM</u> in children. Early symptoms (e.g., <u>dehydration</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, fatigue) may be vague, especially in young and/or nonverbal children, but if untreated will progress to signs of severe <u>metabolic acidosis</u> (e.g., <u>Kussmaul breathing</u>), mental status changes, and/or circulatory compromise. <u>DKA</u> is confirmed in patients with <u>hyperglycemia</u>, <u>ketosis</u>, and <u>acidosis</u> and/or decreased <u>bicarbonate</u>. For patients with extremely elevated <u>glucose</u> but minimal or absent <u>ketones</u>, <u>hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) in children</u> should be considered, although this is much rarer in children than adults. Management includes fluid resuscitation, insulin therapy, <u>electrolyte repletion</u>, careful monitoring (e.g., <u>telemetry</u>, serum <u>ketones</u>, <u>venous blood gas</u>), and assessing for complications (e.g., <u>features of cerebral injury in pediatric DKA</u>). Prompt recognition and treatment are necessary to prevent complications (e.g., permanent <u>brain injury</u>, <u>death</u>).
