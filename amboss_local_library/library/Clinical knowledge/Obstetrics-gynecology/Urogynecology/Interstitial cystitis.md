# Interstitial cystitis

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Obstetrics/gynecology > Urogynecology > Interstitial cystitis*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Vv0G_3)

## Summary

<u>Interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> (<u>bladder</u> <u>pain</u> syndrome) is a <u>chronic pelvic pain syndrome</u> characterized by <u>bladder</u> <u>pain</u> and irritative <u>lower urinary tract symptoms</u> in the absence of infection or other identifiable causes. <u>Interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> is most common in female individuals aged ≥ 40 years. The etiology is not known. Clinical features include <u>pain</u> with <u>bladder</u> filling that is relieved with voiding, <u>urinary urgency</u>, <u>urinary frequency</u>, <u>nocturia</u>, and suprapubic and <u>pelvic</u> tenderness on examination. <u>Interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> is a diagnosis of exclusion, based on ≥ 6 weeks of symptoms, a negative <u>urinalysis</u> and <u>urine culture</u>, and the absence of identifiable causes such as infection, <u>malignancy</u>, or urological abnormalities. Management is based on symptoms. All patients benefit from nonpharmacological therapy, which may include avoidance of triggers (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, coffee, citrus), behavioral modification (e.g., fluid intake management, <u>bladder training</u>, <u>stress</u> management), and management of comorbidities (e.g., <u>IBS</u>, <u>depression</u>). Patients with <u>pelvic floor</u> tenderness or <u>high-tone pelvic floor dysfunction</u> may also benefit from <u>manual therapy</u> to relax tissues. Pharmacological treatment (e.g., <u>oral analgesia</u>, <u>pentosan polysulfate</u>) may also be considered. Specialist referral is indicated for presumed <u>Hunner lesions</u> and/or refractory symptoms or if an alternative diagnosis is suspected.

## Clinical features

* <u>Pain</u> with <u>bladder</u> filling that is relieved by voiding (most common feature)

* Suprapubic and/or perineal <u>pain</u>, pressure, or discomfort

* Increased <u>urinary urgency</u> and/or <u>urinary frequency</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* <u>Nocturia</u>

* Genitourinary tenderness of the:

* <u>Urethra</u> and <u>bladder</u> base

* <u>Scrotum</u> and <u>penis</u>

* <u>Lateral</u> and <u>anterior</u> vaginal wall

* Symptoms are typically:

* Gradual in onset

* Exacerbated by sexual intercourse, exercise, prolonged sitting, certain foods or drink  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)

* Patients may have a history of <u>UTI</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

## Diagnosis

<u>Interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> is a diagnosis of exclusion made in patients with symptoms lasting ≥ 6 weeks and a negative <u>urine culture</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)




### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* Diagnosis is clinical.

* Perform an initial assessment to evaluate for alternative diagnoses.

* Refer to urology or urogynecology for advanced studies (e.g., <u>cystoscopy</u> and/or <u>urodynamic studies</u>) if:

* There is diagnostic uncertainty

* <u>Hunner lesions</u> are suspected (e.g., age > 50 years, comorbid autoimmune diseases)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)





### Initial assessment [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* <u>Voiding diary</u>: to characterize symptoms and establish a baseline

* <u>Physical examination</u>

* <u>Abdominal examination</u> to assess for masses

* <u>Pelvic examination</u> including <u>vaginal examination of the pelvic floor musculature</u> (or <u>digital rectal examination</u> for male patients)  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)

* Brief <u>neurological examination</u>

* <u>Urinalysis</u> with microscopy and <u>urine culture</u>: to rule out bacterial <u>cystitis</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* <u>Postvoid residual volume</u>: to evaluate for <u>urinary retention</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* Depending on <u>risk factors</u>, consider: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* <u>Pregnancy test</u>

* <u>STI testing</u> for patients with <u>risk factors for STIs</u>

* <u>HbA1c</u> if there are <u>indications for diabetes screening</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)

* <u>Diagnostics for prostatitis</u>

* <u>Hematuria diagnostics</u>; consider also for patients without <u>hematuria</u> who use tobacco.





> [!TIP]
> Low voiding frequency and/or high volume suggest an alternative diagnosis. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




### Advanced studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




#### <u>Cystoscopy</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* Indications

* Concern for <u>Hunner lesions</u>, e.g.: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)

* Patients > 50 years of age

* Comorbid autoimmune disease

* Concern for <u>differential diagnosis of interstitial cystitis</u> (e.g., <u>clinical features of urinary tract cancer</u>)

* Findings

* Hunner lesions: acute inflamed, <u>friable</u>, denuded stellate-shaped lesions and <u>ulcers</u> on the <u>bladder</u> wall  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* Findings in <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> are nonspecific and include:

* Diffuse glomerulations

* <u>Mastocytosis</u> on <u>biopsy</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)





> [!TIP]
> If <u>Hunner lesions</u> are detected on <u>cystoscopy</u>, simultaneous treatment can be provided (e.g., <u>fulguration</u> and/or intralesional <u>triamcinolone</u> injection). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




#### <u>Urodynamic studies</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* There are no established urodynamic criteria for <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u>.

* Studies are indicated for refractory symptoms to evaluate for:

* <u>Lower urinary tract obstruction</u>

* <u>Atonic bladder</u>

## Management

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8sdO9r0)




* Advise patients that there is no standard approach; treatment is highly individualized.

* Initiate treatment using nonpharmacological management and/or oral pharmacological treatment.

* Treat common coexisting conditions, e.g.:

* <u>Chronic pain</u> syndromes  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I7dYMr0)

* <u>Depression</u> and <u>anxiety disorders</u>  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I7dYMr0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eCYxIr)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k7bmNE)

* Monitor symptom response to treatment using a validated tool (e.g., <u>Interstitial</u> <u>Cystitis</u> Symptom Index).

* Refer to a specialist (e.g., urogynecology, urology) for:

* Evaluation and treatment of <u>Hunner lesions</u>

* Refractory symptoms

* Advanced therapy





> [!TIP]
> Patients with <u>Hunner lesions</u> should be referred to a specialist promptly for definitive treatment. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




### Nonpharmacological management [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)




* Behavioral modifications, e.g.:

* Avoidance of triggers (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, coffee, citrus)

* Fluid intake management based on symptoms

* <u>Bladder training</u>

* Heat or cold applied to trigger points and hypersensitive areas (e.g., <u>bladder</u>, <u>perineum</u>)

* <u>Stress</u> management (e.g., <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u>, <u>meditation</u>) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I7dYMr0)

* <u>Manual therapy</u> (e.g., trigger point therapy, tissue release) for patients with <u>pelvic floor</u> tenderness and/or <u>high-tone pelvic floor dysfunction</u>





> [!TIP]
> Behavioral modifications and <u>stress</u> reduction are recommended for all patients with <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Pelvic floor</u> strengthening exercises (e.g., <u>Kegel exercises</u>) are contraindicated in <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> as they may worsen symptoms. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




### Oral pharmacological treatment [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8sdO9r0)




There is no standardized management algorithm. Use <u>shared decision-making</u>.




* Management of acute flares

* <u>Phenazopyridine</u> DOSAGE  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5udi770)

* Short courses of <u>glucocorticoids</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* Long-term symptom management  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8sdO9r0)

* <u>Pentosan polysulfate</u> DOSAGE)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)

* <u>Amitriptyline</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8sdO9r0)

* <u>Cimetidine</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)

* <u>Hydroxyzine</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7dfMr0)

* <u>Oral analgesia</u> (e.g., <u>NSAIDs</u>) can be used for both short- and long-term <u>pain</u> relief.





> [!TIP]
> Patients with complex or uncontrolled <u>pain</u> may require referral to a <u>pain management</u> specialist. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




> [!WARNING]
> Long-term oral <u>antibiotic</u> or <u>glucocorticoid</u> treatment is not indicated for <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




### Advanced treatments




Advanced treatments are performed by a specialist in urology or urogynecology and is typically reserved for patients with refractory symptoms and/or <u>Hunner lesions</u>.




#### Management of refractory <u>interstitial</u> <u>cystitis</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* Intravesicular therapy (e.g., dimethyl sulfoxide, <u>lidocaine</u>, <u>heparin</u>)

* Procedural interventions, e.g.:

* Hydrodistension

* Neuromodulation

* Intradetrusor <u>botulinum toxin</u> injection

* <u>Surgery</u> (e.g., substitution <u>cystoplasty</u>, urinary diversion)





#### Management of <u>Hunner lesions</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)




* First-line: <u>fulguration</u> and/or intralesional <u>triamcinolone</u> injection

* Refractory lesions: oral <u>cyclosporin A</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Hunner lesions</u> typically respond well to treatment. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jad_4o0)
