# Pelvic floor dysfunction

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Obstetrics/gynecology > Urogynecology > Pelvic floor dysfunction*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uE0px3)

---

## Summary

<u>Pelvic floor</u> dysfunction (PFD) refers to laxity (<u>low-tone PFD</u>) or tension (<u>high-tone PFD</u>) of the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles and/or <u>connective tissue</u>. Causes include muscle or nerve damage (e.g., from <u>surgery</u> or trauma), increased <u>intra-abdominal pressure</u>, and genetic conditions. PFD can manifest with urinary or bowel symptoms (e.g., <u>urinary incontinence</u>, <u>constipation</u>), <u>pelvic organ prolapse</u> (<u>POP</u>), <u>sexual dysfunction</u>, a feeling of pressure (e.g., <u>pelvic</u>, rectal), and/or <u>pelvic pain</u>. Diagnosis is based on a focused <u>clinical examination</u> to assess the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles and adjacent structures (e.g., the <u>rectum</u>). Management includes <u>pelvic floor muscle training</u> and management of associated symptoms (e.g., <u>urinary incontinence</u>).

---

## Clinical features

* Symptoms of PFD are nonspecific and include: [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5ndiFq0)

* Feeling of pressure in the <u>pelvic</u> region or <u>rectum</u>

* Urinary symptoms, e.g.:

* <u>Urinary incontinence</u>, <u>urinary urgency</u>

* <u>Dysuria</u>

* <u>Defecatory dysfunction</u>

* <u>Fecal incontinence</u>

* <u>Lower back pain</u> or <u>pelvic pain</u>

* <u>Sexual dysfunction</u> (e.g., <u>dyspareunia</u>)

* <u>High-tone PFD</u> more commonly manifests with: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)

* Chronic <u>constipation</u> with <u>defecatory dysfunction</u>

* Feeling of incomplete evacuation

* <u>Dyspareunia</u>

* Urinary hesitancy, slow stream, and/or straining to void

* <u>Bladder</u> <u>pain</u>

* <u>Low-tone PFD</u> more commonly manifests with: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)

* <u>Urinary incontinence</u>

* <u>POP</u>

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LJdwuI0)




* PFD is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>; perform:

* An external genitalia examination

* <u>Vaginal examination of the pelvic floor musculature</u>

* <u>Digital rectal examination</u>  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)

* <u>Musculoskeletal examination</u> of nearby structures (e.g., <u>lumbar spine</u>, <u>pelvis</u>)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LJdwuI0)

* Evaluate any associated symptoms (e.g., <u>urinary incontinence</u>).

* If there is diagnostic uncertainty or complexity, refer to a specialist for imaging studies, e.g.: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lpdvpI0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MpdMJI0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g51Fjh0)

* <u>Ultrasound</u>: transperineal or transvaginal

* <u>MRI</u>: <u>defecography</u> or dynamic movements (e.g., <u>Valsalva maneuver</u>)





### Vaginal examination of the pelvic floor musculature [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LJdwuI0)




* Assess for <u>POP</u>.

* Ask the patient to contract the <u>pelvic floor</u> and assess for normal elevation of the <u>perineum</u>.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)

* Insert a single finger into the vaginal introitus and use the pad of the finger to palpate the surrounding muscles and <u>connective tissue</u>.

* Slowly rotate the finger as though around an imaginary clock to palpate the <u>superficial</u> and deep muscles, <u>bladder</u>, and <u>urethra</u>.

* Ask the patient to contract the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles around the palpating finger and then relax.

* Signs of <u>high-tone PFD</u>

* Muscle tightness or thickening

* Tenderness or <u>pain</u> on palpation

* Strong contraction

* Delay or difficulty relaxing the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles after contracting

* Signs of <u>low-tone PFD</u>

* <u>POP</u>

* Weak or no contraction felt on assessment





> [!TIP]
> Before initiation, use a <u>trauma-informed approach</u> and explain the purpose and process of the vaginal examination to all patients. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LJdwuI0)




### Further assessment




* Obtain further studies guided by associated symptoms.

* Depending on symptom severity, patients may require specialist referral for assessment.

## Management

### General principles [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RLdlxq0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)




* Treatment varies based on the underlying pathophysiology.

* <u>Low-tone PFD</u>: Treatment focuses on increasing muscle strength.

* <u>High-tone PFD</u>: Treatment focuses on relaxing the <u>pelvic floor</u>.

* Refer all patients to <u>physical therapy</u> for <u>pelvic floor muscle training</u>.

* Manage any associated symptoms (e.g., <u>urinary incontinence</u>).

* Additional treatments (usually performed by a specialist) may include:

* <u>Low-tone PFD</u>: <u>surgery</u>

* <u>High-tone PFD</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)

* <u>Cognitive behavioral therapy</u>

* Vaginal <u>muscle relaxants</u> (e.g., local <u>benzodiazepines</u>)

* Trigger point injections

* <u>Botulinum toxin</u> injections

* <u>Sacral neuromodulation</u>





### Pelvic floor muscle training [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RLdlxq0)




* <u>Pelvic floor muscle training</u> encompasses techniques to strengthen, relax, and/or improve coordination of the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles.

* The specific techniques used depend on the patient's symptoms and underlying pathophysiology.

* Techniques to strengthen the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles include:

* Kegel exercises: tightening and relaxing of the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y51nih0)

* Electrical stimulation of the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles

* <u>Weighted vaginal cones</u>

* Techniques to help relax the <u>pelvic floor</u> muscles include: [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)

* <u>Myofascial release</u>

* Trigger point massage

* <u>Dry needling</u>

* <u>Biofeedback</u> can be used to assess exercise technique.  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Physical therapy</u> for PFD may also include exercises to strengthen core abdominal and spinal muscles. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RLdlxq0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)




### Symptom-based management [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xMdEIq0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hLdcxq0)




* Initiate symptom-based management alongside <u>pelvic floor muscle training</u>.

* Depending on symptom severity, patients may require specialist referral for management.
