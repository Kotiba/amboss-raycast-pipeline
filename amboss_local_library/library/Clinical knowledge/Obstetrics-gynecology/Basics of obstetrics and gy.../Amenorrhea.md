# Amenorrhea

## Summary

Amenorrhea is the absence of <u>menstruation</u>. It is classified as either <u>primary amenorrhea</u> or <u>secondary amenorrhea</u> depending on whether <u>menarche</u> has occurred. Causes include physiological factors (e.g., <u>pregnancy</u>, lactation, <u>menopause</u>), dysfunction of the <u>hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis</u>, anatomical reproductive tract abnormalities, gonadal dysfunction, and systemic diseases. Clinical evaluation involves a focused history and <u>physical examination</u>, including a <u>pelvic examination</u>, <u>Tanner staging</u>, and evaluation for signs of <u>common causes of amenorrhea</u>. All individuals with amenorrhea should be evaluated for <u>pregnancy</u> and, based on clinical evaluation, receive targeted testing for suspected causes. <u>Causes of primary amenorrhea</u> are often identified with clinical evaluation and, if indicated, <u>pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u> and certain <u>laboratory studies</u> (e.g., <u>FSH</u>, <u>karyotype</u>). <u>Causes of secondary amenorrhea</u> are identified using <u>hormone</u> testing and often a <u>pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u>, followed by additional testing if indicated. Management focuses on the underlying cause and may include lifestyle changes, <u>hormone replacement therapy</u> (<u>HRT</u>), surgical correction for anatomical abnormalities, and <u>infertility treatment</u>.

## Definitions

Amenorrhea is the absence of <u>menstruation</u>; it is classified as either <u>primary amenorrhea</u> or <u>secondary amenorrhea</u>.




* Primary amenorrhea: no <u>menarche</u> by 15 years of age or 5 years after <u>thelarche</u>, whichever comes first [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)

* Secondary amenorrhea is the absence of <u>menstruation</u> for more than: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)

* 3 months in individuals with previously regular cycles

* 6 months in individuals with previously irregular cycles





> [!TIP]
> <u>Delayed puberty</u> in female individuals is defined as an absence of <u>breast</u> development by 13 years of age, and its workup includes an evaluation for <u>causes of primary amenorrhea</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rodfWI0)

## Etiology

### Physiological causes [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k5dm4q0)




* <u>Pregnancy</u>

* Lactation

* <u>Menopause</u>

* <u>Constitutional delay of growth and puberty</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Pregnancy</u>, lactation, and <u>menopause</u> are the most <u>common causes of amenorrhea</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)




### Reproductive tract abnormalities [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k5dm4q0)




* <u>Transverse vaginal septum</u>

* <u>Imperforate hymen</u>

* <u>Agenesis of the lower vagina</u>

* <u>Uterine adhesions</u> (e.g., <u>Asherman syndrome</u>)

* Cervical stenosis

* <u>Müllerian agenesis</u>

* Individuals with XY <u>chromosomes</u> and <u>receptor</u> or enzyme defects, e.g.: 

* <u>17α-hydroxylase deficiency</u>

* <u>Androgen insensitivity syndrome</u>

* <u>5α-reductase deficiency</u>





### Ovulatory dysfunction [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k5dm4q0)




<u>Ovulatory dysfunction</u> includes abnormalities ranging from occasional <u>menstrual cycle</u> irregularities to chronic <u>anovulation</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OIdI1r0)




* <u>Primary ovarian insufficiency</u> (<u>hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u>)

* <u>Secondary ovarian insufficiency</u>: <u>hypothalamic</u> or <u>anterior pituitary</u> dysfunction (<u>hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ardQfr0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lIdv1r0)

* <u>Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea</u>

* <u>CNS</u>:

* Injury (e.g., <u>TBI</u>, radiation-induced <u>brain injury</u>)

* Infiltration (e.g., in <u>sarcoidosis</u>, <u>hemochromatosis</u>)

* Infection (e.g., <u>meningitis</u>)

* <u>Infarction</u> (e.g., in <u>Sheehan syndrome</u> after severe <u>postpartum hemorrhage</u>)

* <u>Idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u> (<u>Kallmann syndrome</u> in patients with <u>anosmia</u>)

* <u>GnRH</u> suppression

* <u>Hyperprolactinemia</u>;  (e.g., due to <u>pituitary adenoma</u>; , medications, <u>chronic kidney disease</u>; , <u>hypothyroidism</u>)

* <u>Cushing syndrome</u>

* <u>Chronic liver disease</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/htdcW70)

* Medications (e.g., <u>hormonal contraceptives</u>)

* <u>Hyperandrogenic anovulation</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zGdrb70)

* <u>Polycystic ovary syndrome</u> (<u>PCOS</u>)

* <u>Congenital adrenal hyperplasia</u>

* <u>Androgen</u>-producing tumors

* <u>Obesity</u> [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5odicI0)

## Clinical evaluation

### Focused history [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




* <u>Gynecologic and obstetric history</u>

* Age at <u>thelarche</u> and <u>menarche</u>

* <u>Menstrual history</u>

* <u>Sexual history</u> (e.g., <u>sexual activity</u>, history of <u>STIs</u>)

* Recent <u>pregnancy</u> or <u>breastfeeding</u>

* <u>Past medical history</u>

* Medication use (e.g., <u>antipsychotics</u>, <u>oral contraceptives</u>)

* Preexisting medical conditions

* History of <u>chemotherapy</u>

* Focused review of symptoms

* Symptoms of <u>estrogen deficiency</u>

* <u>Symptoms of hypothyroidism</u> or <u>symptoms of hyperthyroidism</u>

* <u>Symptoms of eating disorders</u>, including excessive exercise

* <u>Symptoms of hyperandrogenism</u>

* <u>Symptoms of hyperprolactinemia</u> (e.g., <u>galactorrhea</u>)

* Psychosocial stressors





### <u>Physical examination</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




* Blood pressure

* <u>BMI</u>

* Assessment for dysmorphic features (e.g., signs of <u>Turner syndrome</u>)

* <u>Examination of the thyroid gland</u>

* Evaluation for <u>signs of hyperandrogenism</u> (e.g., <u>hirsutism</u>, <u>acne</u>)

* <u>Tanner stage</u>

* <u>Pelvic examination</u> to assess for:

* Structural abnormalities (e.g., <u>imperforate hymen</u>, <u>transverse vaginal septum</u>, blind or absent <u>vagina</u>)

* Signs of low <u>estrogen</u> (e.g., prepubertal appearance, <u>urogenital atrophy</u>)  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fKdkfI0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fG1kAR0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Hypertension</u> may be present in patients with <u>congenital adrenal hyperplasia</u> due to <u>17α-hydroxylase deficiency</u> or in patients with <u>Cushing syndrome</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0JdesI0)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




Obtain a <u>pregnancy test</u> in all patients before initiating a diagnostic workup for amenorrhea.




#### <u>Primary amenorrhea</u>




* <u>Cervix</u> visualized on <u>pelvic examination</u> (i.e., a <u>uterus</u> is present): Obtain <u>FSH</u>; consider also obtaining other <u>initial laboratory studies for amenorrhea</u>.

* <u>FSH</u> ↓ or normal

* <u>Breasts</u> absent: Evaluate for <u>constitutional delay of growth and puberty</u> and <u>isolated GnRH deficiency</u> (e.g., <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>).

* <u>Breasts</u> present: Perform workup for <u>secondary amenorrhea</u>.

* <u>FSH</u> ↑:  Perform <u>karyotyping</u> to evaluate for genetic causes of <u>primary ovarian insufficiency</u> (<u>gonadal dysgenesis</u>).

* Blind or absent <u>vagina</u> on <u>pelvic examination</u>: Perform <u>pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u> to assess internal reproductive organs.

* <u>Uterus</u> absent: Perform <u>karyotyping</u> and measure <u>testosterone</u> level.

* <u>Uterus</u> present: Consider ; congenital reproductive outflow tract obstruction (e.g., <u>imperforate hymen</u>, <u>vaginal agenesis</u>, and <u>transverse vaginal septum</u>).

* See “<u>Common causes of primary amenorrhea</u>” for further details on interpretation of findings.





#### <u>Secondary amenorrhea</u>




* Obtain:

* <u>Initial laboratory studies for amenorrhea</u>

* Targeted testing for amenorrhea as clinically indicated

* <u>Pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u>, if readily available

* See “<u>Common causes of secondary amenorrhea</u>” for further details on interpretation of findings.

* If no cause is identified, consider <u>hormone withdrawal testing for amenorrhea</u>.





### Initial laboratory studies for amenorrhea [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




Tests commonly obtained include the following:




* <u>FSH</u> with or without <u>LH</u>

* <u>Estradiol</u> with or without <u>anti-Müllerian hormone</u> (<u>AMH</u>)

* <u>TSH</u>

* <u>Prolactin</u>





### Targeted testing for amenorrhea [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




Obtain targeted testing as indicated by clinical presentation, if not already obtained as part of initial testing.




* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* Signs of <u>estrogen deficiency</u>: <u>FSH</u>, <u>LH</u>, <u>estradiol</u>, <u>AMH</u>

* <u>Signs of hyperandrogenism</u>: free and total <u>testosterone</u>, <u>DHEA-S</u>, <u>17-hydroxyprogesterone</u>

* <u>Signs of hyperprolactinemia</u> (e.g., <u>galactorrhea</u>): <u>prolactin</u>

* <u>Signs of hypothyroidism</u> or <u>signs of hyperthyroidism</u>: <u>TSH</u>

* <u>Signs of adrenal insufficiency</u> or <u>hypercortisolism</u>: <u>cortisol</u>

* Signs of chronic systemic illness: <u>CBC</u>, <u>BMP</u>, <u>ESR</u>, <u>CRP</u>, <u>urinalysis</u>

* Weight loss and/or gastrointestinal symptoms: <u>diagnostics for celiac disease</u>

* Imaging

* Suspected <u>PCOS</u> and/or structural reproductive tract abnormalities: <u>pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u> or <u>MRI</u>

* Suspected <u>androgen</u>-secreting <u>tumor</u>: <u>adrenal</u> imaging

* Symptoms suggestive of <u>CNS</u> pathology (e.g., <u>visual field</u> defects) and/or persistent <u>hyperprolactinemia</u>: <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u>





> [!TIP]
> ↑ <u>TSH</u> suggests <u>hypothyroidism</u> as a cause of amenorrhea.




> [!TIP]
> Elevated levels of <u>androgens</u> (<u>testosterone</u>, <u>DHEA-S</u>) can be seen with <u>PCOS</u>, <u>congenital adrenal hyperplasia</u>, <u>Cushing syndrome</u>, or an <u>androgen</u>-secreting <u>tumor</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




#### Hormone withdrawal testing in amenorrhea [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyb0Tw)




The following studies can be used to evaluate patients with <u>secondary amenorrhea</u> if there is no identified cause after the initial workup.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)




* Progestin challenge test: typically the initial test

* Method: Administer a <u>progestin</u> for 5–10 days. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bzaHrM)

* Interpretation: Assess for withdrawal bleeding within 14 days of <u>progestin</u> cessation.

* Withdrawal bleeding: indicates <u>anovulation</u> (e.g., <u>PCOS</u>, <u>idiopathic</u> <u>anovulation</u>)

* No withdrawal bleeding: indicates <u>hypoestrogenism</u> (i.e., <u>hypogonadism</u>) or reproductive outflow tract obstruction

* <u>Estrogen</u>-progesterone challenge test: performed in patients with no withdrawal bleeding on initial testing

* Method

* Administer <u>estrogen</u> for 21–25 days.

* Add <u>progesterone</u> during the last 5–10 days of <u>estrogen</u> administration.

* Interpretation: Assess for withdrawal bleeding within 2–7 days of <u>progesterone</u> cessation.

* Withdrawal bleeding:  indicates <u>hypoestrogenism</u> (i.e., <u>hypogonadism</u>)

* No withdrawal bleeding:  indicates reproductive outflow tract obstruction or <u>endometrial</u> dysfunction (e.g., <u>Asherman syndrome</u>; , <u>endometritis</u>)

## Management

Management is based on the underlying cause. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/45d34q0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




* Goals

* Progression of <u>puberty</u> in patients with <u>primary amenorrhea</u>

* Prevention of complications (e.g., <u>osteoporosis</u>, symptoms of <u>estrogen deficiency</u>)

* Optimization of reproductive health (e.g., functional anatomy, <u>fertility</u>)

* Options

* Lifestyle changes (e.g., in <u>PCOS</u>, <u>functional hypothalamic amenorrhea</u>)

* Pharmacological treatment, e.g.:

* <u>HRT</u> and <u>COCs</u> for <u>hypogonadism</u>

* <u>Dopamine agonists</u> (<u>bromocriptine</u>, <u>cabergoline</u>) for <u>prolactinoma</u>

* <u>Surgery</u> for tumors or anatomical abnormalities

* Indications for specialist referral

* Procedural interventions: gynecology or <u>surgery</u>

* Hormonal abnormalities: <u>endocrinology</u>

* <u>Infertility treatment</u>: reproductive <u>endocrinology</u>
