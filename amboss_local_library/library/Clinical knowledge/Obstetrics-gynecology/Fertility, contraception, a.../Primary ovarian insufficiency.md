# Primary ovarian insufficiency

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Obstetrics/gynecology > Fertility, contraception, and reproductive endocrinology > Primary ovarian insufficiency*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tu0XG3)

---

## Summary

Primary <u>ovarian insufficiency</u> (POI) is impaired ovarian function before the age of 40 years due to a defect in the <u>ovaries</u> that decreases follicle numbers or results in an insufficient follicular response to <u>gonadotropin</u> stimulation (i.e., <u>hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u>). The underlying etiology is unknown in 70% of cases; known etiologies include genetic disorders, autoimmune diseases, and <u>iatrogenic</u> causes (e.g., <u>chemotherapy</u> or <u>pelvic</u> <u>surgery</u>). Because <u>ovarian insufficiency</u> affects <u>ovulation</u> and <u>estrogen</u> production, symptoms include <u>amenorrhea</u> or <u>abnormal uterine bleeding</u>, and <u>climacteric symptoms</u>. Unlike <u>premature menopause</u>, ovarian function in POI may not have permanently ceased; function is intermittent, and individuals may become pregnant, although <u>infertility</u> is common. POI is often discovered during <u>diagnostics for amenorrhea</u>; a low <u>estradiol</u> level with elevated <u>FSH</u> suggests POI. If <u>FSH</u> is normal or low, <u>secondary ovarian insufficiency</u> (inadequate gonadotropic stimulation of the <u>ovaries</u> by the <u>hypothalamus</u> and/or <u>pituitary</u> glands, i.e., <u>hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>) should be considered. POI is confirmed if values remain abnormal when repeated at least a month later. Assessment for the underlying cause involves genetic studies and, potentially, <u>autoantibodies</u>. <u>Management of POI</u> includes long-term <u>hormone replacement therapy</u> (<u>HRT</u>), <u>management of infertility</u>, and ongoing monitoring for the development of <u>complications of POI</u> (e.g., cardiovascular disease and <u>osteoporosis</u>).

## Etiology

* <u>Idiopathic</u> (up to 70% of cases) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)

* Genetic disorders

* Disorders associated with <u>gonadal dysgenesis</u>;  (e.g., <u>Turner syndrome</u>, <u>Swyer syndrome</u>)

* <u>Fragile X syndrome</u>

* Autoimmune diseases, e.g.:

* <u>Hashimoto thyroiditis</u>

* <u>Adrenal insufficiency</u>

* <u>Autoimmune lymphocytic oophoritis</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4ud3r70)

* <u>Type I diabetes mellitus</u>

* <u>Pernicious anemia</u>

* Toxins

* <u>Tobacco smoke</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* Environmental chemicals (e.g., phthalates, bisphenols)

* <u>Iatrogenic</u>

* Radiation and/or <u>chemotherapy</u>

* <u>Pelvic</u> <u>surgery</u> (e.g., cyst excision)

* Infectious diseases (e.g., <u>mumps</u>, <u>HIV</u>)

---

## Pathophysiology

Follicular dysfunction or depletion →  ↓ <u>estrogen</u>  levels → reduced feedback inhibition of <u>estrogen</u> on <u>FSH</u> and <u>LH</u> →  ↑ <u>FSH</u> and <u>LH</u> (usually <u>FSH</u> > <u>LH</u>)

---

## Clinical features

* <u>Amenorrhea</u> or <u>abnormal uterine bleeding</u>

* <u>Climacteric symptoms</u> such as vaginal dryness, <u>vasomotor symptoms</u>, <u>dyspareunia</u>, and irritability

* <u>Infertility</u>

* May be accompanied by features of the underlying etiology (e.g., <u>symptoms of hypothyroidism</u>)

## Diagnosis

POI is often detected on <u>diagnostic studies for amenorrhea</u>.




### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)




* Initial diagnostic studies, including <u>FSH</u> and <u>estradiol</u>

* Repeat in one month if results are abnormal. POI is likely if:   [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* Age is < 40 years

* There has been irregular <u>menstruation</u> for ≥ 3 consecutive months [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)

* Two sets of <u>laboratory studies</u> recorded > 1 month apart, in patients not using <u>hormonal contraception</u>, show: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)

* ↑ <u>FSH</u> levels (30–40 mIU/mL)

* ↓ <u>Estradiol</u> levels (< 50 pg/mL)

* Evaluate for underlying causes.

* Refer patients who want to preserve <u>fertility</u> to a reproductive endocrinologist for additional evaluation, including <u>ovarian reserve testing</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)





> [!TIP]
> If <u>estradiol</u> is low but <u>FSH</u> is normal or low, assess for <u>secondary ovarian insufficiency</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Hormonal contraception</u> can affect <u>gonadotropin</u> and <u>estradiol</u> values; stop any <u>exogenous</u> <u>hormones</u> before obtaining diagnostic studies. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)




### Initial diagnostic studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)




See also “<u>Diagnostics for amenorrhea</u>.”




* <u>Pregnancy test</u>

* <u>FSH</u>

* <u>Estradiol</u>

* <u>Prolactin</u>

* <u>Thyroid function tests</u>





### Evaluation of underlying causes [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)




* May not be required for patients with a clear cause of <u>iatrogenic</u> POI  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* For all other patients:

* Assess for genetic anomalies.

* <u>Karyotyping</u>

* <u>FMR1 gene</u> premutation for <u>fragile X</u> syndrome

* <u>Family history</u> of POI or <u>early menopause</u>: Refer for additional <u>genetic testing</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)

* If genetic studies are normal, evaluate for autoimmune disease with:

* <u>21-hydroxylase</u> <u>adrenal</u> <u>antibodies</u>   [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8KdORI0)

* <u>Anti-TPO antibodies</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* <u>Pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u>  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4ud3r70)

* Additional studies for other autoimmune disorders (e.g., <u>diabetes</u>, <u>rheumatoid arthritis</u>, <u>SLE</u>) depending on underlying symptoms

## Management

The following outlines management of postpubertal individuals with POI; refer patients who have not completed <u>puberty</u> to a specialist for management.




### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)




* Treat any underlying causes (e.g., autoimmune disease).

* Discuss reproductive goals.

* Not planning <u>pregnancy</u>: Initiate <u>HRT</u>.

* Planning <u>pregnancy</u>: Refer to a <u>fertility</u> specialist; <u>assisted reproductive technology</u> is usually required.

* Offer a referral for psychological counseling.

* Screen for and initiate measures to prevent <u>complications of POI</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* <u>Osteoporosis screening</u> (including a <u>vitamin D</u> level) and <u>osteoporosis prevention</u>  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* Check BP, <u>BMI</u>, <u>HbA1c</u>, and a <u>lipid panel</u>, and initiate <u>ASCVD prevention</u>.

* Inquire about <u>sexual dysfunction</u>.

* Treat <u>urogenital atrophy</u>.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* Refer for counseling as needed.

* Advise patients with noniatrogenic POI that female relatives are at increased risk and should seek testing.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)





### <u>HRT</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)




* Screen for <u>contraindications to HRT</u>; refer to a specialist as needed.

* Educate patients on the importance of <u>HRT</u> for reducing the risks of <u>POI complications</u>.

* Determine if <u>contraception</u> is required (<u>HRT</u> alone is not effective <u>contraception</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* Individuals not requiring <u>contraception</u>

* <u>Combined HRT</u>

* OR <u>estrogen-only HRT</u> plus cyclical <u>progestin</u> for <u>endometrial</u> protection  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iqdJyI0)

* Individuals requiring <u>contraception</u>

* <u>Estrogen-only HRT</u> plus <u>levonorgestrel IUD</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* Continuous combined <u>estrogen</u>-<u>progestin</u> <u>hormonal contraception</u> (<u>CHCs</u>)  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* <u>Nonhormonal contraceptive methods</u> can also be added to <u>HRT</u> regimens that do not provide <u>contraception</u>.

* For dosages, see:

* “<u>Options for HRT</u>”; doses at the higher end of the range are usually required. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* “<u>Hormonal contraception</u>”

* Continue until 50–51 years of age (the average age of <u>menopause</u>); can be continued longer to manage symptoms (see “<u>Treatment of menopause</u>”). [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)





> [!WARNING]
> Individuals who do not wish to become pregnant require <u>contraception</u>, since spontaneous <u>pregnancy</u> occurs in up to 10% of individuals with POI, and <u>HRT</u> does not provide adequate <u>contraception</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HH1K7R0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)




> [!TIP]
> Reassure patients that <u>HRT</u> has not been associated with <u>breast cancer</u> when used before the age of <u>menopause</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)




### Ongoing management [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)




* Follow up with patients annually.

* Assess for changes in their <u>reproductive life plan</u>; refer to a <u>fertility</u> specialist as needed.

* Check blood pressure.

* In patients with low <u>bone density</u> or <u>osteoporosis</u>, repeat <u>bone density scanning</u>.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FKdgRI0)

* Monitor periodically for development of <u>thyroid disease</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqd7_I0)

* Assess lipid levels every 5 years. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tPbXgF)
