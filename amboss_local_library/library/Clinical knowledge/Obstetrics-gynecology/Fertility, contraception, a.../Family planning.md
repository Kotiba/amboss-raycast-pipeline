# Family planning

## Summary

Family planning is the management of reproduction by individuals and couples to prevent <u>pregnancy</u>, optimize <u>pregnancy spacing</u>, determine the number of children conceived, and/or address <u>infertility</u> with the use of family planning services. A <u>reproductive life plan</u> establishes family planning goals in alignment with the individual's personal values and priorities and should be discussed with all individuals of reproductive age, regardless of race, ethnicity, <u>sexual orientation</u>, <u>gender identity</u>, or current <u>contraception</u> use. If preventing <u>pregnancy</u> is the goal, <u>contraceptive</u> options are provided, including <u>emergency contraception</u>. If <u>pregnancy</u> is the goal, guidance on <u>achieving pregnancy</u>, <u>pregnancy spacing</u>, and basic <u>infertility management</u> are offered as needed; <u>preconception care</u> is also provided to optimize physical and mental health. For those who want to raise children but for whom a <u>pregnancy</u> achieved through sexual intercourse is not possible or not desired, <u>alternative family-building options</u> (e.g., adoption, <u>surrogacy</u>) may be appropriate. Family planning services also include <u>diagnosis of pregnancy</u> and <u>counseling on pregnancy options</u>, <u>STI</u> care, and other <u>adult health maintenance</u> related to reproductive health.

See also “<u>Contraception</u>.”

## Approach to family planning

* Determine the individual's reproductive life plan, i.e., their goals related to conceiving or preventing <u>pregnancy</u>, in alignment with their values and priorities.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FA1glj0)

* Offer the following as needed: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FA1glj0)

* <u>Contraception</u> and <u>emergency contraception</u>

* For individuals wanting to conceive, offer guidance on <u>preconception care</u>, including:

* <u>Timing of conception</u>, including <u>pregnancy spacing</u>

* Optimizing management of preexisting medical, psychiatric, and gynecological conditions

* <u>Advice on how to achieve pregnancy</u>

* <u>Diagnostics for infertility</u>

* <u>Pregnancy diagnosis</u> and follow-up (including for <u>unintended pregnancy</u>)

* <u>Alternative family-building options</u>

* Address <u>adult health maintenance</u> related to reproductive health, including:

* <u>STI</u> care (<u>STI screening</u>, <u>STI prevention</u>)

* <u>Cancer screening</u> (<u>breast cancer screening</u>, <u>cervical cancer screening</u>)

* Immunizations (e.g., <u>HPV vaccine</u>, <u>HepB vaccine</u>)

* See also “<u>Reproductive health care for transgender individuals</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> Review an individual's <u>reproductive life plan</u> at every visit, as it may change. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FA1glj0)




> [!TIP]
> Because almost half of <u>pregnancies</u> in the US are unplanned, all individuals who can become pregnant are advised to take <u>folic acid supplementation</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/okd0KJ0)

## Preconception care

Preconception care is intended to prepare an individual for a planned <u>pregnancy</u> and reduce the risk of adverse outcomes by optimizing health. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)




### General principles [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FA1glj0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)




* Should be provided at any clinical encounter to anyone who wishes to conceive

* Many of the principles are the same as <u>prenatal care</u>.

* Comprehensive care includes:

* Identification and treatment of physical and mental health conditions that may affect <u>pregnancy</u>

* Genetic carrier counseling

* Counseling on <u>timing of conception</u>

* Ensuring patients are up-to-date with <u>recommended immunizations</u>, especially <u>MMR</u> and <u>varicella</u> <u>vaccines</u>

* <u>Preconception counseling on diet and lifestyle</u> recommendations

### <u>Teratogenic medications</u>




* Screen both partners for use of <u>teratogenic medications</u> (see also “<u>Pharmacotherapy during pregnancy</u>”).  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pkdLKJ0)

* Make medication adjustments before discontinuing <u>contraception</u>.

* Consider changing to safer medication, reducing the dose, or, if safe, stopping the medication.

* Consult a specialist if there are no safe alternatives or the medication should not be stopped.





### Genetic carrier screening [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)




* Offer to all individuals seeking <u>preconception care</u>  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* Provide or refer for <u>genetic counseling</u> prior to testing. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)





#### Recommended tests  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)




* <u>Spinal muscular atrophy</u> and <u>cystic fibrosis</u> testing  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* Additional testing is considered for individuals with:

* A <u>family history</u> of:

* Genetic disorders (e.g., <u>familial adenomatous polyposis</u>, <u>fragile X syndrome</u>) or <u>major congenital anomalies</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Cancers associated with <u>hereditary cancer syndromes</u> (e.g., ovarian, <u>breast</u>, <u>colon</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* Descent from a racial or ethnic group with an increased <u>prevalence</u> of certain <u>autosomal recessive</u> disorders  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)





> [!TIP]
> Individuals may choose to decline recommended <u>genetic testing</u> (e.g., for cultural or religious reasons). [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)




#### Further management




If <u>genetic testing</u> is positive:




* Offer testing to their reproductive partner (especially for <u>autosomal recessive</u> disorders). [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)

* Refer for further <u>genetic counseling</u> if: [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kz1mGj0)

* The individual has an <u>autosomal dominant</u> genetic disorder

* Both the individual and their partner are carriers of an <u>autosomal recessive</u> disorder

## Preconception counseling

Provide education (similar to <u>prenatal patient education</u>) on diet, exercise, and safe environments.




* Advise individuals to maintain a <u>normal body weight</u> prior to <u>conception</u>.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Discuss dietary recommendations, including:  

* <u>Vitamin and mineral supplementation in pregnancy</u>, including:

* Initiating <u>prenatal folic acid supplementation</u> at least 1 month before <u>conception</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Sufficient intake of other <u>micronutrients</u> (e.g., <u>calcium</u>, <u>iron</u>, <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>, <u>vitamin B<sub>9</sub></u>, <u>vitamin D</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* <u>Dietary restrictions during pregnancy</u>, e.g., reduced intake of seafood with potentially high levels of <u>mercury</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Recommend 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Avoid <u>teratogenic</u> exposures, e.g.:

* <u>Teratogenic medications</u>, <u>alcohol</u>, <u>recreational drugs</u>, and <u>tobacco products</u> (including <u>secondhand smoke</u>) [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GkdB6J0)

* Environmental <u>risk factors</u>, e.g., lead paint, <u>asbestos</u>, pesticides, <u>bisphenol A</u>, organic solvents, and radiation exposure  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

## Conception timing

For individuals planning <u>conception</u>, advise on the optimal timing of the first and any subsequent <u>pregnancies</u>.




### Optimal timing




* Advise individuals to address <u>risk factors</u> for adverse outcomes (e.g., optimize management of medical conditions, complete <u>recommended immunizations</u>, start <u>folic acid supplementation</u>) before trying to conceive.

* Recommend delaying <u>conception</u> in individuals who:

* Received a <u>live vaccine</u> (e.g., <u>MMR</u>, <u>varicella vaccine</u>) in the previous 28 days [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tkd6MJ0)

* Recently traveled to an area with high risk for <u>Zika</u>  [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ckda5J0)

* Recently used <u>teratogenic medications</u>, e.g., <u>methotrexate</u> for <u>ectopic pregnancy</u>  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vpYArJ)

* Had <u>bariatric surgery</u> in the previous 12–24 months  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TCW6rn0)

* Individuals can try to conceive immediately after stopping <u>contraception</u>.  [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YkdnmJ0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Xkd9mJ0)





### Pregnancy spacing




* <u>Pregnancy spacing</u> is intended to reduce the risk of adverse <u>pregnancy</u> outcomes, including: [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ul1pAS0)

* Maternal outcomes (e.g., mortality, severe <u>morbidity</u>) [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-SdDXp0)

* Fetal outcomes (e.g., <u>SGA</u>, <u>preterm birth</u>) [[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k6WmOm0)

* An interval of at least 18 months is recommended between a <u>live birth</u> and the next <u>pregnancy</u>.  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ul1pAS0)[[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WL1P9h0)

* Individuals can try to conceive as soon as they are ready after:

* <u>Miscarriage</u> or <u>induced abortion</u>   [[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W81Pli0)

* <u>Stillbirth</u>   [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZkdZmJ0)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0kdemJ0)

* Tailor counseling as needed. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* Shorter interpregnancy intervals are associated with a higher risk of adverse <u>pregnancy</u> outcomes in women with prior:

* Preterm deliveries

* <u>Cesarean deliveries</u>  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)

* Intervals of 6–18 months between delivery of a <u>live birth</u> and the next <u>pregnancy</u> may be considered in certain situations.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LsYwvq)
