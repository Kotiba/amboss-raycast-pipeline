## Summary

Male <u>hypogonadism</u> is a condition caused by reduced function of the <u>testes</u>, leading to decreased production of <u>testosterone</u>. It is classified as <u>primary male hypogonadism</u> or <u>secondary male hypogonadism</u> based on the underlying cause. <u>Primary male hypogonadism</u> is characterized by elevated <u>gonadotropin</u> (<u>LH</u> and <u>FSH</u>) levels and caused by disorders or abnormalities affecting the <u>testes</u>, including damage to the <u>testes</u> (e.g., due to <u>orchitis</u>, radiation, trauma, or torsion) and congenital conditions (e.g., <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>). <u>Secondary male hypogonadism</u> is characterized by <u>gonadotropin</u> deficiency and caused by disorders of the <u>hypothalamus</u> and/or <u>pituitary gland</u> (e.g., tumors, infiltrative diseases, <u>head trauma</u>, congenital conditions such as <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>). Clinical features may include decreased bone and muscle mass and <u>gynecomastia</u>. Certain features depend on the time of onset (i.e., prepubertal or postpubertal): Prepubertal manifestations include <u>microphallus</u> and <u>eunuchoidal proportions</u>, and postpubertal manifestations include loss of morning erections, low energy, and <u>hot flashes</u>. Low <u>fasting</u> morning total <u>testosterone</u> levels on two separate occasions confirms the diagnosis. Once the diagnosis is established, additional laboratory and/or imaging studies should be obtained to determine the etiology. <u>Functional male hypogonadism</u> is managed with lifestyle modifications and/or treatment of the underlying condition. <u>Testosterone</u> therapy is indicated for patients with low <u>testosterone</u> levels due to testicular disorders (e.g., genetic causes), <u>pituitary gland</u> disorders (e.g., tumors), and <u>brain</u> disorders. Use of <u>testosterone</u> for age-related <u>testosterone</u> decline is <u>off-label</u>. There are multiple <u>testosterone</u> formulations; the formulation used depends on drug availability, cost, and patient preference. Patients taking <u>testosterone</u> therapy require regular monitoring to assess for complications and response to treatment.

## Definitions

* <u>Primary male hypogonadism</u>: <u>hypogonadism</u> due to disorders or abnormalities affecting the <u>testes</u>, characterized by elevated <u>gonadotropin</u> levels

* <u>Secondary male hypogonadism</u>: <u>hypogonadism</u> due to disorders of the <u>hypothalamus</u> and/or <u>pituitary gland</u>, characterized by <u>gonadotropin</u> deficiency (due to decreased secretion of <u>GnRH</u> or <u>LH</u>/<u>FSH</u>)

* <u>Mixed male hypogonadism</u>: <u>hypogonadism</u> resulting from both disorders of the <u>testes</u> (primary) and the <u>hypothalamus</u> and/or <u>pituitary gland</u> (secondary)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aJdQsI0)

* <u>Organic male hypogonadism</u>: <u>hypogonadism</u> due to often irreversible <u>hypothalamic</u>-<u>pituitary</u>-testicular axis pathology

* <u>Functional male hypogonadism</u>: <u>hypogonadism</u> due to often reversible suppression of the <u>hypothalamic</u>-<u>pituitary</u>-testicular axis (e.g., due to <u>obesity</u>)





> [!TIP]
> Acquired causes of primary (hypergonadotropic) and secondary (hypogonadotropic) <u>hypogonadism</u> are further classified as organic vs. functional. See “<u>Etiology of male hypogonadism</u>” for examples.

## Etiology

### <u>Primary male hypogonadism</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PodWXI0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aJdQsI0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fodkYI0)




#### Acquired




* Organic

* <u>Orchitis</u> (e.g., due to <u>gonorrhea</u>, <u>mumps virus</u> infection)

* Bilateral <u>orchiectomy</u>

* Testicular radiation

* <u>Chemotherapy</u>

* Testicular trauma

* <u>Testicular torsion</u>

* <u>Hemochromatosis</u>  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Older age [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l0WvTP0)

* Functional

* Medications (e.g., <u>ketoconazole</u>)

* <u>Varicocele</u>





#### Congenital




* <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>

* <u>Down syndrome</u>

* <u>Cryptorchidism</u>

* Congenital anorchia (vanishing <u>testes</u> syndrome)

* <u>Y chromosome microdeletions</u>

* <u>LH</u> and <u>FSH</u> <u>receptor</u> genetic variants

* <u>Myotonic dystrophy</u>





### <u>Secondary male hypogonadism</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PodWXI0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fodkYI0)




#### Acquired




* Organic

* Damage to the <u>pituitary gland</u> (e.g., due to tumors, infiltrative diseases such as <u>sarcoidosis</u> or <u>hemochromatosis</u>, or infection)

* <u>Head trauma</u>

* Functional

* Critical systemic illness

* <u>Hyperprolactinemia</u>

* Medications (e.g., <u>opioids</u>, <u>GnRH agonists</u>, <u>GnRH antagonists</u>, <u>glucocorticoids</u>)

* <u>Cannabis</u> use [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l0WvTP0)

* <u>Anabolic steroid</u> use

* <u>Obesity</u>

* Excessive calorie restriction

* Excessive exercise

* <u>Sleep deprivation</u> and other <u>sleep disorders</u> (e.g., <u>OSA</u>) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l0WvTP0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ShXyWB)





#### Congenital




* <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>

* <u>Normosmic congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>

* <u>CHARGE syndrome</u>

* <u>Waardenburg syndrome</u>

* Congenital <u>adrenal</u> <u>hypoplasia</u>

* <u>Septo-optic dysplasia</u>





### <u>Mixed male hypogonadism</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PodWXI0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aJdQsI0)




* Acquired 

* <u>Heavy alcohol use</u>

* Chronic diseases (e.g., <u>HIV</u>, <u>IBD</u>, <u>post-COVID-19 condition</u>, <u>celiac disease</u>)

* Organ failure (e.g., <u>heart failure</u>, <u>chronic kidney disease</u>, <u>cirrhosis</u>)

* Congenital

* <u>Congenital adrenal hyperplasia</u>

* <u>Prader-Willi syndrome</u>
