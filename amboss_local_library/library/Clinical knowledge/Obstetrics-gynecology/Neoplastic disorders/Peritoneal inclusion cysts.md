## Summary

<u>Peritoneal</u> inclusion cysts (PICs) are uncommon <u>mesothelium</u>-lined, multiloculated <u>peritoneal</u> cystic lesions that predominantly occur in female individuals of reproductive age. They are generally considered a reactive process related to <u>peritoneal</u> irritation and inflammation, commonly in the setting of prior <u>pelvic</u> <u>surgery</u>, <u>pelvic inflammatory disease</u>, or <u>endometriosis</u>. Patients may be asymptomatic or present with chronic <u>pelvic pain</u>, abdominal distention, or a palpable mass. Diagnosis is suggested by clinical history and imaging, with <u>ultrasound</u> characteristically showing multiseptated cystic lesions and, when <u>pelvic</u>, an entrapped <u>ovary</u> (spider-in-web sign). <u>MRI</u> provides optimal anatomic delineation. Management is symptom-directed; surveillance is recommended for asymptomatic cysts, while symptomatic cysts may require hormonal therapy, image-guided drainage, and/or surgical excision. Recurrence is common, but malignant transformation is rare.
