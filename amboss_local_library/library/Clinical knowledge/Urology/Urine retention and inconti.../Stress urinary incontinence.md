# Stress urinary incontinence

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ai0R8f)

## Summary

<u>Stress</u> <u>urinary incontinence</u> (SUI) is a type of <u>urinary incontinence</u> characterized by the leakage of urine following activities that increase <u>intra-abdominal pressure</u> (e.g., <u>coughing</u> or sneezing). It is twice as common in women than men, and <u>prevalence</u> increases with age. The underlying mechanism is an increase in <u>bladder</u> pressure that exceeds sphincter resistance, leading to urine being expelled. SUI in women is caused by a variety of conditions (e.g., <u>pelvic</u> muscle dysfunction and/or <u>weakness</u>, intrinsic sphincter deficiency); in men, it is most common secondary to <u>prostate</u> <u>surgery</u>. SUI manifests with predictable, small-volume urinary leakage, typically with no history of irritative <u>bladder</u> symptoms (e.g., urgency or frequency). A <u>diagnosis of SUI</u> is made after performing an <u>initial evaluation of urinary incontinence</u>, assessing for <u>red flags in urinary incontinence</u>, and performing <u>urinary stress testing</u>. <u>Conservative management of urinary incontinence</u> includes <u>pelvic floor physical therapy</u>, <u>bladder training</u>, lifestyle modifications, management of comorbidities, and <u>incontinence products</u>. Additional management options include <u>vaginal pessary</u>, <u>periurethral bulking agents</u>, and <u>anti-incontinence surgery</u>.

## Etiology

* Pathophysiology: outlet <u>incompetence</u>

* Urethral hypermobility: loss of <u>pelvic floor</u> musculature and/or <u>connective tissue</u> support → weak <u>pelvic floor</u> → inability of the <u>urethra</u> to completely close

* Intrinsic sphincter deficiency

* <u>Risk factors</u>

* <u>Multiparity</u>

* <u>Pregnancy</u> and vaginal deliveries

* High-impact activity (e.g., jumping, running, hopping)

* <u>Menopause</u>

* <u>Obesity</u>

* Smoking

* <u>Chronic cough</u> (e.g., <u>COPD</u>)

* <u>Prostate</u> or <u>pelvic</u> <u>surgery</u>

* See “Etiology” in “<u>Urinary incontinence</u>.”

## Clinical features

* Physical activity that causes increased <u>intra-abdominal pressure</u> (e.g., laughing, sneezing, <u>coughing</u>, exercising) leads to leakage of urine.

* Frequent, predictable, small-volume urine losses with no urge to urinate prior to the leakage

* <u>Pelvic examination</u> may show <u>pelvic organ prolapse</u>, <u>cystocele</u>, and/or <u>rectocele</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)




* Perform an <u>initial evaluation for urinary incontinence</u> including: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* <u>Urinary stress test</u>

* <u>Urinalysis</u>

* <u>Postvoid residual volume</u>   [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y51nih0)

* Refer to a specialist (e.g., urology or urogynecology) for:

* <u>Red flags in urinary incontinence</u>

* <u>Urethral mobility testing</u> for female patients  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)

* Possible additional testing (e.g., <u>fluoroscopy</u> voiding cystourethrography, <u>urodynamic studies</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g51Fjh0)





### Urinary stress test [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y51nih0)




* Performed for all patients to distinguish SUI from other <u>types of urinary incontinence</u>

* With a full <u>bladder</u> (at least 300 mL), the patient performs the <u>Valsalva maneuver</u> or coughs.

* Urine leakage during increased abdominal pressure confirms the <u>diagnosis of SUI</u>.





### Urethral mobility testing [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g51Fjh0)




* An assessment performed for female patients to help determine treatment modality

* The patient lies <u>supine</u> and, during the <u>Valsalva maneuver</u>, the degree of urethral displacement is measured using one of the following : [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Urethral cotton swab test (most common): A cotton swab is placed into the <u>urethra</u> to assess how much the swab moves.

* Visual <u>urethral mobility</u> examination

* Transperineal <u>ultrasound</u>

* <u>Urethral mobility</u> of > 30° from the <u>horizontal plane</u> is associated with a higher degree of success for <u>anti-incontinence surgery</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SmdyUq0)

## Treatment

### Approach [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)




* Offer <u>incontinence products</u>  to prevent dermatological <u>complications of urinary incontinence</u>.

* Assess for features of <u>urgency urinary incontinence</u>; manage as <u>mixed incontinence</u> if present.

* Initiate <u>conservative management</u>.

* Refer to a specialist if:

* <u>Red flags in urinary incontinence</u> are present.

* Symptoms persist despite <u>conservative management</u>.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y51nih0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wndhvq0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ynddDq0)

* Patients wish to pursue <u>surgery</u> as first-line treatment.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L5dwkq0)

* Invasive options to manage SUI include:

* <u>Periurethral bulking agents</u>

* <u>Anti-incontinence surgery</u>





> [!TIP]
> Neuromuscular electrical stimulation can be considered for female patients with SUI; however, there is limited evidence to support its use. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)




### <u>Conservative management</u>




* <u>Conservative management of urinary incontinence</u> is first-line therapy for most patients. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)

* Recommend lifestyle and behavioral modifications (e.g., <u>bladder training</u>, <u>smoking cessation</u>, dietary changes).  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Identify and treat underlying conditions (e.g., <u>pelvic organ prolapse</u>, <u>obesity</u>, <u>genitourinary syndrome of menopause</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Refer for supervised <u>pelvic floor physical therapy</u> (e.g., <u>Kegel exercises</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Consider a <u>vaginal pessary</u>.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Treatment of genitourinary syndrome of menopause</u> with topical <u>estrogen</u> may improve <u>urinary incontinence</u> symptoms. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)




### Periurethral bulking agents [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)




* Injection of substances into the periurethral tissue to narrow the <u>urethra</u> and increase resistance

* Significantly less effective than <u>surgery</u> and repeat injections are often required

* Consider for patients with:

* Preference for nonsurgical management

* Contraindications to anesthesia or <u>surgery</u>

* Recurrence of symptoms after <u>surgery</u>

* Limited <u>urethral mobility</u> (female patients only)  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)





### Anti-incontinence surgery




Surgical management is recommended if there is inadequate improvement with <u>conservative management of urinary incontinence</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)




#### Female patients [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-5dD5q0)




* Midurethral mesh sling

* Preferred surgical treatment

* A mesh sling is placed under the <u>urethra</u> and <u>bladder neck</u> to lift, support, and exert pressure on the <u>urethra</u>; the sling may pass:

* Retropubically (tension-free vaginal tape)

* Through the <u>obturator foramen</u> (transobturator tape)

* Alternatives [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)

* <u>Autologous</u> fascial sling  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L5dwkq0)

* Urethropexy (Burch colposuspension): surgical fixation of a displaced <u>urethra</u> and <u>bladder neck</u> to nearby tissue to prevent involuntary urine leakage  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Midurethral sling</u> is the most effective <u>treatment for SUI</u> in female patients with <u>urethral mobility</u> > 30°. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DbX1D9)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L5dwkq0)




> [!WARNING]
> There is insufficient evidence to recommend either laser <u>surgery</u> or artificial urinary sphincters in female patients with SUI. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)




#### Male patients [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wndhvq0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ynddDq0)




* <u>Surgery</u> is typically reserved for patients with incontinence that: [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ynddDq0)

* Persists for more than 6–12 months following <u>prostate</u> <u>surgery</u>

* Has not responded to <u>pelvic floor physical therapy</u>

* Options include:

* Artificial urinary sphincter (preferred for severe incontinence)   [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Adjustable periurethral balloon  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)

* Male sling   [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g5dFjq0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wndhvq0)
