# Semen analysis

## Summary

Semen analysis is an important test in the evaluation of <u>male infertility</u> and is also used to confirm sterility after a <u>vasectomy</u>. A semen sample is examined for <u>sperm</u> concentration, morphology, motility, and semen biochemistry (e.g., <u>pH</u> level). Test results can include a number of pathologies, such as low <u>sperm</u> count (<u>oligospermia</u>) or complete lack of <u>sperm</u> in the ejaculate (<u>azoospermia</u>). However, semen analysis is not a definitive test for <u>infertility</u> and low <u>sperm</u> count does not necessarily rule out the ability to father children.

---

## Indications for semen analysis

* Evaluation of male <u>fertility</u> (see also causes of <u>male infertility</u>)

* Confirmation of sterility after <u>vasectomy</u>

## Semen analysis

### Semen analysis




| Characteristics | Normal findings |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | 1.5 - 5 mL |
| Color | Whitish, opalescent |
| <u>Liquefaction</u> | Complete within 30 minutes<br> |
| <u>pH</u> | Basic 7.2–8.0 <br> |
| Total number of <u>sperm</u> per ejaculate | <br>> 39 million per ejaculate |
| <u>Sperm</u> concentration per mL | <br>> 15 million per mL |
| Vitality (percentage of live <u>sperm</u>) | <br>> 58% live <u>sperm</u><br> |
| Morphology (percentage of normal forms) | <br>> 4% <u>sperm</u> are morphologically normal. <br> |
| Total motility (progressive and non-progressive <u>sperm</u>) | <br>> 40% <br> |
| Progressive motility | > 32% |
| Fructose in seminal plasma | <br>> 13 μmol/L <br> |
| <u>Leukocytes</u> per mL of semen | <br>< 1 million <br> |





### Pathological findings




| Conditions | Characteristics |
| --- | --- |
| Aspermia | No ejaculate |
| Hypospermia | Low ejaculate volume (< 1.5 mL) |
| Azoospermia | No spermatozoa in the ejaculate |
| Cryptozoospermia | <br>< 1 million spermatozoa/mL of ejaculate |
| Oligospermia | <br>< 15 million spermatozoa/mL of ejaculate |
| Asthenozoospermia | <br>< 32% of spermatozoa show progressive motility (category PR) |
| Teratozoospermia | Increased amorphous spermatozoa <br> |
| <br>Oligoasthenoteratozoospermia (OAT syndrome) | Low concentration, insufficient motility, and increased amorphous spermatozoa |
