## Summary

Hypogonadism is a clinical syndrome associated with impaired function of the <u>gonads</u>. Both males and females can be affected. It is classified as either primary or secondary: <u>Primary hypogonadism</u> (<u>hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u>) is typically caused by congenital differences (<u>differences of sex development</u> affecting the <u>gonads</u> (e.g., <u>Turner syndrome</u>, <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>) or acquired gonadal injury (e.g., <u>irradiation</u>, infection). <u>Secondary hypogonadism</u> (<u>hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>) is most often caused by <u>pituitary</u> or <u>hypothalamic</u> disorders (e.g, <u>craniopharyngioma</u>, <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>). Characteristic features in males include testicular <u>hypoplasia</u>, <u>gynecomastia</u>, and absent facial <u>hair</u> growth, while females commonly present with <u>amenorrhea</u>. Following clinical evaluation, the diagnosis is confirmed with <u>hormone</u> tests, and <u>genetic testing</u> may be considered. Treatment involves management of the underlying cause and <u>hormone replacement therapy</u>.

## Etiology

Hypogonadism is a clinical syndrome associated with impaired functional activity of the <u>gonads</u>.




### Hypergonadotropic hypogonadism (<u>primary hypogonadism</u>)




<u>Hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u> is caused by insufficient sex <u>steroid</u> production in the <u>gonads</u>.




* Primary gonadal insufficiency: <u>Turner syndrome</u> (females), <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u> (males), anorchia

* Secondary gonadal insufficiency (damage to <u>Leydig cells</u> or ovarian tissue): <u>chemotherapy</u>, <u>pelvic</u> <u>irradiation</u>, trauma/<u>surgery</u>, autoimmune disease (e.g, <u>autoimmune polyglandular syndrome</u>), infections (e.g., <u>mumps</u>, <u>tuberculosis</u>)





### Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (<u>secondary hypogonadism</u>)




<u>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u> is caused by an insufficient <u>gonadotropin</u>-releasing <u>hormone</u> (<u>GnRH</u>) and/or <u>gonadotropin</u> release at the <u>hypothalamic-pituitary axis</u>.




* Genetic disorders

* <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>

* Idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (IHH): a genetic disorder characterized by a defect in <u>GnRH</u> production/action in the absence of <u>anosmia</u>

* <u>Prader-Willi syndrome</u>

* <u>Gaucher disease</u>

* <u>Hypothalamic</u> and/or <u>pituitary</u> lesions

* <u>Neoplasm</u> (e.g. <u>prolactinoma</u>, <u>craniopharyngioma</u>, <u>astrocytoma</u>)

* Trauma, <u>surgery</u>, <u>irradiation</u>

* Infection

* <u>Eating disorders</u> (<u>functional hypothalamic amenorrhea</u>)

* Drugs (e.g., <u>opioids</u> ) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AWXRLC)

## Pathophysiology

Diminished functional activity of the <u>gonads</u> → reduced biosynthesis of sex <u>hormones</u> → impaired <u>secondary sexual characteristics</u> and <u>infertility</u>




* <u>Hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u> (primary): gonadal insufficiency;  → insufficient sex <u>steroid</u> production (↓ <u>testosterone</u>, ↓ <u>estrogen</u>) → increased <u>gonadotropin</u> secretion (↑ <u>FSH</u> and ↑ <u>LH</u>) from the <u>anterior pituitary</u> → lack of negative feedback from the impaired <u>gonads</u> → further ↑ <u>FSH</u> and ↑ <u>LH</u> levels

* <u>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u> (secondary)

* In <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>; : impaired migration of <u>GnRH</u> cells and defective <u>olfactory bulb</u> → ↓ <u>GnRH</u> in <u>hypothalamus</u> → ↓ <u>FSH</u> and ↓ <u>LH</u> → ↓ <u>testosterone</u> and ↓ <u>estrogen</u>

* In <u>hypothalamic</u> and/or <u>pituitary</u> lesions: ↓ <u>pituitary</u> <u>gonadotropins</u> (↓ <u>FSH</u> and ↓ <u>LH</u>) → ↓ <u>testosterone</u> and ↓ <u>estrogen</u>

* <u>Dopamine antagonists</u> (e.g., <u>antipsychotics</u>): increased <u>prolactin</u> secretion (<u>anterior pituitary</u>) → decreased <u>GnRH</u> secretion (<u>hypothalamus</u>) → decreased <u>LH</u> secretion (<u>anterior pituitary</u>) → decreased <u>testosterone</u> or <u>estrogen</u> production (<u>Leydig cells</u>)

## Clinical features

* <u>Delayed puberty</u> (see “<u>Tanner stages</u>”)

* <u>♂</u>

* Testicular <u>hypoplasia</u>

* ↓ Body <u>hair</u> growth (e.g., absent facial <u>hair</u>)

* High-pitched voice

* Smooth <u>skin</u> (no <u>acne</u>)

* ↓ Lean body mass

* <u>♀</u>: <u>primary amenorrhea</u>

* Developmental abnormalities with genitalia (<u>undescended testes</u>, <u>hypospadias</u>)

* <u>Infertility</u> (↓ <u>sperm</u> count), <u>impotence</u>, and/or ↓ libido

* <u>Secondary amenorrhea</u>

* Examine patient for features of:

* <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>: <u>gynecomastia</u>

* <u>Turner syndrome</u>: <u>webbed neck</u>, <u>short stature</u>

* <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>: <u>anosmia</u>, absent <u>breast</u> development, <u>uterus</u> is present, <u>syndactyly</u>, <u>cleft palate</u> or <u>cleft lip</u>

* <u>Prader-Willi syndrome</u>: muscular <u>hypotonia</u>, <u>short stature</u>, facial dysmorphia

* <u>Gaucher disease</u>: <u>hepatomegaly</u>, <u>splenomegaly</u>, painful bone crisis

## Diagnosis

* Routine tests

* ↓ Serum <u>testosterone</u> levels (in males) and ↓ serum <u>estradiol</u> levels (in females) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l0WvTP0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PodWXI0)

* Determine if the source is primary or <u>secondary hypogonadism</u>.

* <u>Hypergonadotropic hypogonadism</u>: ↑ <u>LH</u>/<u>FSH</u>

* <u>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>: ↓ <u>LH</u>/<u>FSH</u>

* <u>Bone scan</u> may support the diagnosis of hypogonadism: shows ↓ <u>bone density</u> (<u>osteoporosis</u> ) or delayed epiphyseal closure

* Further tests: based on suspected etiology

* <u>Genetic testing</u> (for, e.g., <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>, <u>Turner syndrome</u>, <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>)

* Serum <u>prolactin</u> (↑ in <u>prolactinoma</u>)

* <u>Pelvic</u> <u>ultrasound</u> (e.g., <u>gonadal dysgenesis</u> in <u>Klinefelter syndrome</u>)

* <u>Brain</u> <u>MRI</u>: for <u>CNS</u> lesion or <u>Kallmann syndrome</u> (e.g., absent olfactory bulbs in <u>Kallmann syndrome</u>)

* <u>Adrenocorticotropic hormone</u> stimulation test (<u>ACTH stimulation test</u>): to exclude <u>congenital adrenal hyperplasia</u>





> [!TIP]
> Although not routinely measured in clinical practice, <u>GnRH</u> levels would be high in <u>primary hypogonadism</u> and low in <u>secondary hypogonadism</u>.

---

## Treatment

* Treat underlying cause: e.g., surgical excision of tumors, pharmacotherapy for <u>prolactinomas</u>

* <u>Hormone replacement therapy</u>

* Trigger onset of <u>puberty</u> in prepubertal individuals at appropriate age 

* <u>Testosterone</u> replacement therapy in males

* <u>Estrogen</u> replacement therapy in females

* To improve <u>fertility</u> in postpubertal individuals with <u>hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>  and, if prepubescent, an alternative  to triggering onset of <u>puberty</u> and growth

* Pulsatile luteinizing <u>hormone</u>-releasing <u>hormone</u> (LHRH) or <u>human chorionic gonadotropin</u> (<u>HCG</u>) in males

* Pulsatile LHRH or <u>gonadotropins</u> in females
