# Erectile dysfunction

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Urology > Andrology > Erectile dysfunction*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/E808o3)

---

## Summary

Erectile dysfunction (ED) is the persistent or recurrent inability to acquire or maintain an <u>erection</u> of sufficient rigidity or duration for sexual performance. Causative factors are vascular, neurological, endocrinologic, and/or psychological. ED may also be related to certain medications or substance use. ED is the most common form of <u>sexual dysfunction</u> in men and its <u>prevalence</u> increases with age. Diagnosis is primarily clinical; diagnostic testing focuses on identifying the underlying cause, which can involve blood tests and, sometimes, specialized tests. ED is an early indicator and marker of <u>atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease</u>. Symptomatic treatment options include oral <u>phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors</u> (e.g., <u>sildenafil</u>), local application of <u>vasodilators</u> (e.g., <u>alprostadil</u>), <u>vacuum-assisted erectile devices</u>, and <u>penile prosthesis</u>.

---

## Epidemiology

* The most common form of <u>sexual dysfunction</u> in men

* Affects 10–25% of men

* Becomes more common with age




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

## Etiology

The following conditions and <u>risk factors</u> can cause and/or contribute to ED.




### Vascular




* <u>Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease</u> (<u>ASCVD</u>), e.g., <u>Leriche syndrome</u>

* <u>ASCVD risk factors</u>, e.g.:

* Smoking

* <u>Hyperlipidemia</u>

* <u>Diabetes mellitus</u>

* <u>Arterial hypertension</u>





### Neurological




* <u>Stroke</u>

* <u>Brain</u> or <u>spinal cord injury</u>

* <u>Dementia</u>

* <u>Parkinson disease</u>

* <u>Multiple sclerosis</u>

* <u>Pelvic fracture</u> or <u>surgery</u> (e.g., <u>radical prostatectomy</u>)





### Endocrinologic




* <u>Hypogonadism</u>

* <u>Hyperprolactinemia</u>

* <u>Thyroid disorders</u>





### Urological




* <u>Peyronie disease</u>

* <u>Urethral injury</u>

* <u>Penile fracture</u>





### Psychological




* Performance-related <u>anxiety</u>

* Stressors, e.g.:

* Relationship issues

* Traumatic experiences

* Social pressures, e.g., religious taboos

* Major life changes, e.g., <u>birth</u> of a child, losing a job

* Psychiatric disorders, e.g.: 

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>PTSD</u>

* <u>Generalized anxiety disorder</u>





### Medication [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPdvUJ0)




* <u>Dopamine antagonists</u> (e.g., <u>antipsychotics</u>): may cause <u>hypogonadotropic hypogonadism</u>

* <u>Antidepressants</u> (e.g., <u>SSRIs</u>)

* <u>Antihypertensives</u>;  (e.g., <u>beta blockers</u>, <u>thiazide diuretics</u>)





### Substance use




* <u>Heavy alcohol use</u>

* <u>Recreational drug</u> use





> [!NOTE]
> PENIS: The most common <u>causes of erectile dysfunction</u> are Psychological, Endocrinologic, Neurological, Insufficient blood flow, Substance use.

## Clinical features

* Persistent or recurrent inability to acquire or maintain an <u>erection</u> of sufficient rigidity or duration for sexual performance

* Can be generalized or situation-specific (e.g., with a specific partner)

* Onset can be sudden, which is typical for psychological causes (e.g., <u>performance anxiety</u> with a new partner), or gradual and progressive (e.g., in patients with vascular or other organic causes). [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I4dYkJ0)

* Associated signs (depending on the underlying etiology)

* Signs of <u>hypogonadism</u> (e.g., small <u>testes</u>, loss of <u>secondary sexual characteristics</u>)

* Signs of cardiovascular disease (e.g., abnormal peripheral pulses, blood pressure)

* Signs of a neurological cause (e.g., abnormal <u>cremasteric reflex</u>, <u>anal tone</u>; , and/or lower extremity sensation)

* <u>Skin</u> lesions, <u>plaques</u>, deformities

## Diagnosis

Erectile dysfunction is primarily a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>. Diagnostic tests are mainly used to identify causes, <u>risk factors</u>, and associated diseases.




### Clinical evaluation [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)




* Obtain a thorough <u>patient history</u> and perform a targeted <u>physical examination</u>, including:

* Onset and severity of symptoms

* Specifics on occurrence: e.g., erections during the night, <u>masturbation</u>, or with a specific partner

* Symptoms or history of potential <u>causes of ED</u>

* Targeted <u>physical examination</u>: <u>vital signs</u>, <u>BMI</u>, and genital examination

* Consider validated questionnaires to assess severity and support management decisions, e.g.:

* International index of erectile dysfunction (IIEF)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ulcpAc0)

* <u>Sexual health</u> inventory for men (SHIM)  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mmdVTq0)

* Evaluate all patients for <u>ASCVD</u>, including <u>family history</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Sudden onset, situational variability, and nocturnal erections generally indicate a psychogenic etiology. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I4dYkJ0)




> [!WARNING]
> ED can be an early indicator of <u>ASCVD</u>.




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPdvUJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)




* All patients

* Morning serum total <u>testosterone</u> to screen for <u>hypogonadism</u>

* < 300 ng/dL indicates <u>testosterone</u> deficiency; repeat testing is necessary for confirmation.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPdvUJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)

* If confirmed, consider further studies to differentiate between primary and <u>secondary hypogonadism</u>, e.g., <u>prolactin</u>, <u>SHBG</u>, <u>LH</u>, <u>FSH</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPdvUJ0)

* Serum <u>glucose</u> and/or <u>HbA1c</u> to <u>screen for diabetes mellitus</u>

* <u>Lipid panel</u> to screen for <u>lipid disorders</u>

* Patients with <u>symptoms of hypothyroidism</u>: <u>TSH</u> to screen for <u>thyroid disorders</u>





### Additional testing [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPdvUJ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I4dYkJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)




In consultation with urology, consider the following tests for certain patients to support management decisions.




* Nocturnal penile tumescence measurement

* Measures the number, rigidity, <u>tumescence</u>, and duration of erections during <u>sleep</u> (typically performed in a <u>sleep</u> laboratory)

* Used to differentiate psychogenic from organic erectile dysfunction

* Findings

* The absence of nocturnal erections may suggest an organic etiology (e.g., neurological or vascular).

* Normal findings suggests a psychogenic cause that warrants further psychiatric workup.

* Vascular imaging and functional tests, e.g.:

* Penile <u>duplex ultrasound</u> to assess for vascular issues

* Cavernosometry and/or <u>angiography</u> to evaluate vascular reconstructive treatment options

## Treatment

### General principles [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)




* Encourage lifestyle modifications (e.g., increased physical activity, healthy diet) in patients with comorbidities that may worsen ED (see also “<u>Primary prevention of ASCVD</u>”).

* Treat the underlying cause, e.g.:

* Low serum <u>testosterone</u> (e.g., in <u>hypogonadism</u>): <u>testosterone</u> replacement

* Psychological factors: <u>psychotherapy</u>, education, exercise, and/or couples counseling

* <u>Erectile disorder</u>: see “<u>Sexual dysfunction</u>.”

* Counsel patients on symptomatic treatment options.

* Consider referral to <u>psychotherapy</u> or counseling for all patients to improve adherence to therapy.





> [!TIP]
> Symptomatic treatment is still necessary to achieve a satisfactory <u>erection</u>, even if the underlying cause is being treated.




### Symptomatic <u>treatment of erectile dysfunction</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPdueJ0)




<u>Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors</u>;  or a <u>vacuum-assisted erectile device</u> are often considered first-line choices.




#### Oral <u>phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors</u>




* Agents: <u>sildenafil</u> DOSAGE , <u>tadalafil</u> DOSAGE, <u>vardenafil</u>, avanafil

* Arousal is still necessary to achieve an <u>erection</u>.

* Contraindicated in patients taking <u>nitrates</u> due to the high risk of severe <u>hypotension</u>

* May cause <u>orthostatic hypotension</u> in patients taking <u>alpha blockers</u> (e.g., for <u>BPH</u>) and should, therefore, be taken at least 4 hours apart





#### Vacuum-assisted erectile device




* An external hollow cylinder is placed on the <u>penis</u> and creates a vacuum to increase blood flow into the <u>corpora cavernosa</u>.

* Once the <u>erection</u> is sufficient, a <u>penis</u> ring is placed around the shaft to obstruct outflow.





#### Local pharmacotherapy




* Intracavernous injection

* Injection of <u>vasodilators</u> into the <u>corpus cavernosum</u>

* Options: <u>alprostadil</u> monotherapy or in combination, e.g., with papaverine (<u>off-label</u>)

* Intraurethral <u>alprostadil</u>: direct administration into the <u>urethra</u> using a catheter





#### Penile prosthesis




* Used as a last resort when other treatments have failed

* Inflatable cylinders are surgically implanted into the <u>corpora cavernosa</u>, and a saline reservoir and pump into the <u>scrotum</u>.
