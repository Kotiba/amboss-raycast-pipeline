## Summary

The female reproductive organs can be divided into the upper genital tract (i.e., <u>uterus</u>, <u>fallopian tubes</u>, <u>ovaries</u>, and <u>cervix</u>) and the lower genital tract (i.e., the <u>vagina</u> and <u>vulva</u>). The upper genital organs and the <u>vagina</u> are located in the <u>pelvis</u>, while the <u>vulva</u> is a part of the <u>perineum</u>. These organs participate in several hormonal and mechanical pathways that are responsible for secondary <u>sexual development</u> and reproduction. The <u>uterine cavity</u> is the site of embryonic implantation and <u>fetal development</u>. The <u>cervix</u> is the lower portion of the <u>uterus</u> and provides a passageway for the expulsion of uterine contents or the entry of spermatozoa into the <u>uterine cavity</u>. The <u>ovaries</u> are paired organs responsible for <u>gametogenesis</u> and sex <u>hormone</u> synthesis in females. In the middle of each <u>menstrual cycle</u>, a mature <u>ovum</u> ruptures out of a <u>Graafian follicle</u> and travels along the <u>fallopian tubes</u>, where it may be fertilized by <u>sperm</u>. The <u>vagina</u> is a hollow muscular structure that receives ejaculate during copulation and serves as a conduit for the expulsion of uterine contents. The <u>vulva</u> is the <u>anterior</u> portion of the <u>perineum</u> and is composed of the <u>labia majora</u>, the <u>labia minora</u>, the <u>clitoris</u>, and the <u>mons pubis</u>. The labia envelop the <u>clitoris</u>, the urethral orifice, and the vaginal introitus.

## Uterus and cervix

### Function [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* <u>Uterus</u>

* Site of implantation of the fertilized <u>ovum</u>

* Protects and supports the growth of the fetus

* Site of <u>menstruation</u>; see physiology of <u>the menstrual cycle</u>.

* <u>Cervix</u>

* Promotes entry of <u>sperm</u> into the <u>uterus</u> for <u>fertilization</u>

* Allows menstrual blood to flow from the <u>uterus</u> into the <u>vagina</u>

* The <u>endocervix</u> secretes a clear, alkaline fluid to facilitate the passage of <u>sperm</u>.





### Gross anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




#### Overview




* Location

* The <u>uterus</u> and <u>cervix</u> are located within the <u>pelvis</u>.

* The <u>uterus</u> is located <u>posterior</u> to the <u>bladder</u> and <u>anterior</u> to the <u>rectum</u>.

* The <u>cervix</u> is the most <u>distal</u> aspect of the <u>uterus</u> and protrudes into the fundus of the <u>vagina</u>.

* Most of the uterine body and <u>posterior</u> supravaginal <u>cervix</u> are covered by <u>peritoneum</u>.

* Position

* The <u>uterus</u> is most commonly <u>anteverted</u> by 90° and <u>anteflexed</u> by 120°.

* Other variants (e.g., retroflexion) occur in 10% of women and may impair <u>conception</u>.

* Neighboring structures

* Anteriorly: <u>uterovesical pouch</u>

* Posteriorly: <u>rectouterine pouch</u>

* Laterally: <u>fallopian tubes</u>, folds of <u>peritoneum</u> (including <u>broad ligament</u>), <u>round ligament</u>, <u>ovarian ligament</u>, <u>cardinal ligament</u>

* Vaginal <u>cervix</u>: portion of the <u>cervix</u> that protrudes into the fundus of the <u>vagina</u>

* Cervical canal

* Cervical os: the opening of the <u>cervix</u> into the <u>vagina</u>

#### <u>Uterus</u>




The <u>uterus</u> is composed of three layers. From internal to external:



1. * Endometrium: <u>mucosal</u> layer consisting of two layers
1. * Simple <u>columnar epithelium</u> with <u>tubular glands</u>

* <u>Functional layer of the endometrium</u>: proliferates and then sloughs during <u>the menstrual cycle</u>

* <u>Tubular glands</u> are long during the <u>proliferative phase</u> and coiled during the <u>secretory phase</u>.
2. * <u>Connective tissue</u> (stroma)
2. * Myometrium: <u>smooth muscle</u> layer

* Contains <u>blood vessels</u>, nerves, and <u>lymphatics</u>

* Composed of <u>smooth muscle</u> and <u>loose connective tissue</u>
3. * Perimetrium

* Uterine body: covered by <u>peritoneal</u> serosa

* <u>Anterior</u> <u>cervix</u> and <u>lateral</u> uterine body: adventitia (not covered by <u>peritoneum</u>)

#### <u>Cervix</u>




Composed primarily of fibroelastic <u>connective tissue</u>. Can be divided into:



1. * Endocervix: inner part of the <u>cervix</u> <u>proximal</u> to the uterine external os
* Composed of simple <u>columnar epithelium</u> with mucous-producing cells
2. * Cervical transformation zone: physiologic transition zone between the <u>endocervix</u> and <u>ectocervix</u>

* Exact location is variable

* Site of the <u>squamocolumnar junction</u>: the border between <u>metaplastic</u> <u>squamous epithelium</u> and simple <u>columnar epithelium</u>

* <u>Cervical cancer</u> most commonly occurs in this zone.
3. * Ectocervix: outer part of the <u>cervix</u> <u>distal</u> to the uterine external os
* Composed of <u>stratified</u> <u>squamous epithelium</u> (nonkeratinized)

## Fallopian tubes

### Function [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* During <u>ovulation</u>, an <u>oocyte</u> from the <u>ovary</u> enters the <u>distal</u> segment of a <u>fallopian tube</u>.

* Most common site of <u>fertilization</u> by <u>sperm</u>: the ampulla

* Movement of <u>cilia</u> and contraction of the muscular layer facilitate the transport of <u>ovum</u> to the <u>uterus</u> for implantation.

* Secretion of <u>nutrients</u> for <u>gametes</u>





### Gross anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




#### Overview




* Location

* Extend bilaterally from the horns of the <u>uterus</u>

* Contained in the <u>proximal</u> part of the <u>broad ligament</u>

* Neighboring structures: open near the <u>ovaries</u> into the <u>abdominal cavity</u>





#### Structure




* <u>Fimbria</u>

* Ciliated end of the <u>fallopian tube</u>

* Receives the <u>ovum</u> from the <u>ovary</u>

* Infundibulum: funnel-shaped opening with attached <u>fimbriae</u>

* Ampulla: widest part; most common site of <u>fertilization</u>

* <u>Isthmus</u>: narrowest part

* Intramural: located in the <u>myometrium</u> of the <u>uterus</u>; connects the <u>isthmus</u> to the <u>uterus</u>

## Ovaries

### Function




* Site of <u>oogenesis</u>

* Sex <u>hormone</u> synthesis: <u>estrogen</u>, <u>progesterone</u>, <u>testosterone</u>





### Gross anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




#### Overview




* Description: paired, ovoid-shaped reproductive organs

* Location

* <u>Lateral</u> to the <u>uterus</u> and close to the <u>lateral</u> <u>pelvic</u> wall

* Suspended in the <u>mesovarium</u> (attached to the <u>posterior</u> part of the <u>broad ligament</u>)

* Neighboring structures: close proximity to the <u>cecum</u> and <u>appendix</u> on the right side and the <u>sigmoid colon</u> on the left side

#### Layers of the <u>ovary</u>




* Outer layer: : <u>peritoneum</u> (simple <u>cuboidal epithelium</u>, germinal epithelium)

* Capsule (<u>tunica albuginea</u>): thick layer of <u>collagen</u>

* Cortex: <u>dense connective tissue</u> surrounding the <u>ovarian follicles</u> and corpora lutea

* Medulla: <u>loose connective tissue</u> containing vessels and nerves





#### Ovarian follicle




The <u>ovarian follicle</u> is a functional unit of the <u>ovaries</u> that matures during <u>the menstrual cycle</u> (see “<u>Folliculogenesis</u>” below).




* <u>Oocyte</u>: Each follicle contains a single <u>oocyte</u>.

* Granulosa cells (follicular cells): form the <u>stratum granulosum</u>

* Surround the <u>oocyte</u> and form the follicular cavity

* Stimulated by <u>FSH</u>

* <u>Theca cells</u>

* Surround the <u>granulosa cells</u> and form a <u>basement membrane</u>

* Theca interna: <u>androstenedione</u> synthesis

* Theca externa: layer of <u>connective tissue</u>

## Oogenesis and folliculogenesis

### Oogenesis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* <u>Oogenesis</u> is the development of an immature oocyte into a <u>secondary oocyte</u> or mature <u>ovum</u> (if <u>fertilization</u> occurs).

* Begins in utero during fetal growth and ends with <u>menopause</u>

* At <u>birth</u>, a normal <u>ovary</u> contains about 1–2 million primary <u>oocytes</u>.

* There is a continuous decline in the number of primary <u>oocytes</u> after <u>birth</u>.

### Folliculogenesis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* Definition: the maturation of <u>ovarian follicles</u> (required for <u>oogenesis</u>)

* Sequence: <u>primordial follicle</u> → <u>primary follicle</u> → <u>secondary follicle</u> → <u>tertiary follicle</u> → <u>corpus luteum</u>

* See “<u>Oogenesis</u>” above and “<u>Menstrual cycle</u> and <u>menstrual cycle abnormalities</u>.”





| Steps of <u>folliculogenesis</u> |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Period | Follicle stage | Description |
| <u>Birth</u> | <br>* Primordial follicle<br><br> | <br><br>* Primary <u>oocytes</u> surrounded by flat <u>granulosa cells</u><br><br>* If activated during <u>puberty</u> → <u>primary follicles</u><br><br>* If not activated → degenerate<br><br><br> |
| <u>Puberty</u> | <br>* Primary follicle<br><br> | <br><br>* Flat <u>granulosa cells</u> proliferate, transforming into a layer of cuboidal <u>granulosa cells</u> surrounding the <u>primary oocyte</u>.<br><br>* The <u>oocyte</u> secretes zona pellucida: a thick, <u>proteoglycan</u>-rich material (important for <u>fertilization</u>).<br><br>* Stromal cells differentiate into <u>theca interna</u> and <u>externa</u> cells.<br><br><br> |
| <u>Menstrual cycle</u>: 1<sup>st</sup> week | <br>* Secondary follicle<br><br> | <br><br>* Cuboidal <u>granulosa cells</u> continue to proliferate → multiple layers of thick <u>granulosa cells</u> surround eccentrically located <u>oocyte</u><br><br>* <u>Theca interna</u> cells produce <u>androstenedione</u> → transformed to <u>estradiol</u> by <u>granulosa cells</u> (under influence of <u>FSH</u>)<br><br>* Most <u>secondary follicles</u> become <u>atretic</u>, but usually only one becomes a <u>tertiary follicle</u><br><br><br> |
| <u>Menstrual cycle</u>: 2<sup>nd</sup> week | <br>* Tertiary follicle (<u>Graafian follicle</u>)<br><br> | <br><br>* The <u>oocyte</u> is surrounded by the corona radiata and floats in follicular fluid.<br><br>* <u>Primary oocyte</u> completes <u>meiosis I</u> → secondary <u>oocyte</u> + <u>polar body</u><br><br>* <u>Secondary oocyte</u> starts <u>meiosis II</u> → arrests in <u>metaphase II</u><br><br>* Follicle moves to surface of <u>ovary</u> → follicle ruptures and <u>secondary oocyte</u> is released<br><br><br> |
| After <u>ovulation</u> | <br>* Corpus luteum<br><br> | <br><br>* Empty <u>tertiary follicle</u> collapses<br><br>* <u>Granulosa cells</u> increase in size → granulosa <u>lutein</u> cells (secrete <u>lutein</u>, <u>progesterone</u>, <u>estradiol</u>)<br><br>* If no <u>fertilization</u> occurs → no <u>hCG</u> present → <u>corpus luteum</u> atrophies<br><br><br> |





> [!NOTE]
> Primary <u>oocyte</u> arrest occurs in <u>prophase I</u> of <u>meiosis I</u> (think PROphase = PRior to Ovulation).
> Secondary <u>oocyte</u> arrest occurs in <u>metaphase II</u> of <u>meiosis II</u> (think <u>MET</u>aphase = before egg <u>MET</u> <u>sperm</u>).




> [!NOTE]
> Primary <u>oocytes</u> are formed prior to <u>birth</u> to serve as an <u>ovarian reserve</u>.

## Vagina and vulva

### Function [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




* <u>Vagina</u>

* Passageway for menstrual blood

* Conduit for <u>childbirth</u>

* Barrier against ascending infections: due to its acidic <u>pH</u> 4–4.5

* <u>Vulva</u>

* Secretion of alkaline fluid for lubrication during sex (<u>greater vestibular glands</u>)

* Sexual arousal and female <u>sexual response</u>

* See “<u>Sexuality and sexual medicine</u>.”





### Gross anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




The <u>vagina</u> and <u>vulva</u> collectively make up the lower genital tract.




#### <u>Vagina</u>




##### Position




* Location

* Extraperitoneal, in the <u>pelvis</u>

* Extends from the <u>uterus</u> to the <u>vaginal vestibule</u>

* Neighboring structures

* Supported by the <u>levator ani muscle</u>, <u>urogenital diaphragm</u>, and several <u>ligaments</u>: <u>transverse cervical ligament</u>, pubocervical <u>ligament</u>, and sacrocervical <u>ligament</u>

* Anteriorly: <u>urethra</u> and <u>bladder</u>

* Posteriorly: <u>rectum</u> and <u>anal canal</u> (separated by <u>perineal body</u>)

* Superiorly: <u>rectouterine pouch</u>

* Distally: <u>pelvic floor</u> and <u>vulva</u>

* The <u>ureter</u> courses close to the <u>lateral</u> fornices.





##### Structures




* Vaginal fornix: recess between the <u>vagina</u> and the <u>cervix</u>

* Four fornices: <u>anterior</u>, <u>posterior</u>, and two <u>lateral</u>

* The <u>posterior</u> <u>vaginal fornix</u> is the deepest and lies below the <u>rectouterine pouch</u>.

* Hymen: membrane that partially covers or surrounds the vaginal opening





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Hymen</u> <u>atresia</u> causes complete occlusion of the <u>vagina</u>, which obstructs blood flow at <u>menarche</u>, causing <u>primary amenorrhea</u>.




![Organ fact sheet: vagina](../../../../media/o6eqx81l4ztxjzetf3iq44md.jpg)

Organ fact sheet: vagina




#### Vulva




##### Position




* Location: external to the <u>pelvis</u>

* Neighboring structures: continuous with the <u>perineum</u>





##### Structures




* Labia majora: folds of <u>skin</u> that surround and cover the vaginal ostium and the <u>labia minora</u>

* Labia minora: folds of <u>hairless skin</u> that surround the <u>vaginal vestibule</u>

* Vulval vestibule: cavity between the <u>labia minora</u> that contains the vaginal and urethral orifices

* Greater vestibular glands (<u>Bartholin gland</u>)

* Homologous to the male bulbourethral glands

* Secrete alkaline fluid for lubrication

* Skene glands:

* Homologous to the male <u>prostate gland</u>

* Exact function is unknown

* Bulbs of the vestibule: erectile tissue that flanks the vaginal orifice

* Clitoris: <u>anterior</u> to the <u>labia minora</u>; erectile tissue composed of <u>corpora cavernosa</u>





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Estrogen</u> stimulates <u>proliferation</u> and <u>keratinization</u> of the vulvar <u>epithelium</u>. <u>Menopause</u> with decreased <u>estrogen</u> levels results in vulvar <u>atrophy</u>.

### Microscopic anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ES08Yf)




#### <u>Vagina</u>




The <u>vagina</u> is a fibromuscular structure with numerous transverse vaginal rugae.




* Inner <u>mucosal</u> layer

* <u>Nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium</u>

* <u>Epithelial</u> cells are rich in <u>glycogen</u>.

* Vaginal bacteria break down <u>glycogen</u> → <u>lactic acid</u>, forming a highly acidic environment

* <u>Glycogen</u> content increases after <u>ovulation</u>

* External muscular layer

* Outer longitudinal <u>smooth muscle</u> layer

* Inner circular <u>smooth muscle</u> layer

* <u>Hymen</u>: <u>nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium</u>

* <u>Labia minora</u>

* Nonkeratinized <u>stratified</u> <u>squamous epithelium</u>

* Rich in <u>sebaceous glands</u>

* <u>Labia majora</u>

* Keratinized <u>stratified</u> <u>squamous epithelium</u>

* <u>Hair follicles</u>

* Eccrine, apocrine, and <u>sebaceous glands</u>

* Vestibule: <u>nonkeratinized squamous epithelium</u> with major vestibular and <u>minor vestibular glands</u>

* <u>Urethra</u>: <u>columnar epithelium</u> with <u>minor vestibular glands</u>
