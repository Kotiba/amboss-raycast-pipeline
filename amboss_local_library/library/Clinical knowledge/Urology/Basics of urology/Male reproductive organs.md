## Summary

The male reproductive system consists of the <u>penis</u>, <u>testes</u>, <u>epididymis</u>, <u>ejaculatory ducts</u>, <u>prostate</u>, and <u>accessory glands</u>. These organs function together to produce <u>sperm</u> and deliver semen from the <u>testes</u> via <u>ejaculation</u>. For more information on the embryological development of the male reproductive system, see “<u>Development of the reproductive system</u>”.

#### Structure




* Root: composed of the bulb of the <u>penis</u> and two crura

* Two crura anchor the <u>corpora cavernosa</u> to the pubic arch.

* The bulb is a continuation of the <u>corpus spongiosum</u>.

* Body (shaft): contains the erectile tissue

* Corpus spongiosum

* Location: lies ventrally between the <u>corpora cavernosa</u>

* Function: keeps the <u>urethra</u> open during <u>erection</u>

* Corpora cavernosa (paired)

* Location: <u>dorsal</u> and <u>proximal</u> to the <u>corpus spongiosum</u>

* Function: enable <u>erection</u>

* Glans penis: the <u>distal</u> end of the <u>corpus spongiosum</u>

* Attached to the frenulum and covered by foreskin

* Runs from the deep surface of the body to the <u>prepuce</u>

* <u>Medial</u> fold located ventrally

* Corona: margin of the <u>glans penis</u>

* Contains the <u>external urethral meatus</u>

* Fossa navicularis: opening of the external <u>urethra</u>

* Urethral glands (<u>glands of Littre</u>): present along the length of the <u>penile urethra</u>

* Muscles

* Bulbospongiosus muscle (paired)

* Cover the bulb of the <u>penis</u>

* Empty the <u>urethra</u> of residual semen/urine and contribute to <u>erection</u>

* Ischiocavernosus muscle (paired)

* Cover the crura of the <u>penis</u>

* Compress the crura to move blood into the body of the <u>penis</u> and maintain erections

## Testes, scrotum, and spermatic cord

* The <u>testes</u> are paired organs composed of <u>seminiferous tubules</u>, the site of <u>spermatogenesis</u>. They are also responsible for the secretion of <u>male sex hormones</u>.

* The <u>scrotum</u> encases the <u>testes</u> and is connected to the abdominal wall via the <u>spermatic cord</u>.

## Gross anatomy

### Structure [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XNY9-6)




* <u>Testes</u>

* Ellipsoid-shaped

* Composed of ∼ 600 seminiferous tubules, which connect to the rete testis

* Surrounded by a thick capsule (<u>tunica albuginea</u>)

* Connect to the abdominal wall via the <u>spermatic cord</u>

* Testicular lobules

* Interlobular septa divide the <u>testis</u> into testicular lobules.

* Each testicular lobule contains 1–4 <u>seminiferous tubules</u>.

* Spermatic cord

* Extension of the abdominal wall into the <u>scrotum</u>

* Contents of the <u>spermatic cord</u>

* Three <u>arteries</u>: <u>testicular artery</u>, <u>ductus deferens</u> <u>artery</u>, cremasteric <u>artery</u>

* Three nerves: genital branch of genitofemoral, cremasteric nerve, <u>sympathetic</u> nerve fibers

* Three other structures: <u>ductus deferens</u>, <u>pampiniform plexus</u>, lymphatic vessels

* Scrotum  

* <u>Distal</u> sac-like end of the <u>spermatic cord</u> that is continuous with the <u>anterior abdominal wall</u>

* Layers of the <u>scrotum</u> (from internal to external)

* Tunica albuginea: highly vascular, <u>fibrous layer</u> of <u>connective tissue</u>

* Tunica vaginalis: <u>visceral</u> layer; derived from <u>peritoneum</u>

* Internal spermatic fascia: derived from the <u>transversalis fascia</u>

* Cremasteric fascia and cremaster muscle: originates from the <u>internal oblique muscle</u>

* External spermatic fascia: derived from the <u>aponeurosis</u> of the <u>external oblique muscle</u>

* Dartos fascia and dartos muscle: contraction causes <u>skin</u> <u>wrinkles</u> → regulates temperature

* Scrotal <u>skin</u>: <u>wrinkles</u> → ↑ <u>skin</u> thickness → ↓ <u>heat loss</u>





> [!NOTE]
> “<u>TIE</u> turns into ICE”:
> Transversalis <u>fascia</u> → Internal spermatic <u>fascia</u>
> Internal oblique → Cremasteric muscle and <u>fascia</u>
> External oblique → External spermatic <u>fascia</u>

## Microscopic anatomy

### Microscopic anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)




#### Spermatogonia




* Description: undifferentiated germ cells lining the <u>seminiferous tubules</u>

* Type A (dark): do not undergo <u>mitosis</u>

* Type A (pale): form type B <u>spermatogonia</u>

* Type B: undergo <u>mitosis</u> → mature into primary spermatocytes

* Function: differentiate into primary spermatocytes





![Germinal epithelium (testicle)](../../../../media/xov8bp8h5gzm666ct86lzrps.jpg)

Germinal epithelium (testicle)




#### Sertoli cells




* Description

* Columnar <u>epithelial</u> cells lining <u>seminiferous tubules</u> (non-germ cells)

* Homologous to <u>granulosa cells</u> in females

* Connected to one another via <u>tight junctions</u>

* Form the <u>blood-testis barrier</u>: separation of <u>gametes</u> in <u>seminiferous tubules</u> from the <u>immune system</u>

* Sensitive to temperature: ↑ temperature (e.g., due to <u>cryptorchidism</u> or <u>varicocele</u>) → ↓ <u>inhibin B</u> secretion and ↓ production of spermatozoa

* Function

* Produce <u>inhibin B</u>

* Stimulated by <u>FSH</u>

* Increased <u>inhibin B</u> levels lead to decreased <u>FSH</u> secretion (negative feedback loop).

* Contain <u>aromatase</u>, which is responsible for the conversion of <u>testosterone</u> and <u>androstenedione</u> to <u>estrogen</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PNYWYp)

* Secrete <u>androgen</u>-binding protein

* Produce <u>Mullerian inhibitory factor</u>

* Regulate <u>spermatogenesis</u>

* Provide protection and nourishment for developing spermatocytes





> [!NOTE]
> The S-rule for Sertoli cells: line the Seminiferous tubes, Sustain Sperm Synthesis, and Suppress FSH.

Convoluted seminiferous tubule

![Seminiferous tubules and tunica albuginea](../../../../media/zktm6k5s3u3bvelg4b160c8q.jpg)

Seminiferous tubules and tunica albuginea




#### Leydig cells




* Description

* <u>Interstitial</u> endocrine cells with <u>cytoplasm</u> rich in <u>cholesterol</u> (used for <u>testosterone</u> production)

* Homologous to <u>theca interna</u> cells in females

* Not affected by temperature

* Function

* Produce <u>testosterone</u>  

* Stimulated by <u>LH</u>

* Increased serum <u>testosterone</u> levels lead to decreased <u>LH</u> secretion (negative feedback loop).

* <u>Testosterone</u> also diffuses to <u>Sertoli cells</u>, where it stimulates <u>spermatogenesis</u>.

* Secrete <u>androgens</u> during the <u>embryonic period</u>

* During <u>puberty</u>, <u>testosterone</u> production increases, which allows for the start/maintenance of full <u>spermatogenesis</u>.

* Conversion of <u>testosterone</u> to <u>estrogen</u> via <u>aromatase</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PNYWYp)





> [!NOTE]
> “Leydis Love <u>Testosterone</u>”: Leydig cells are LH-stimulated and secrete <u>Testosterone</u>.

#### Blood-testis barrier




* Formed by <u>tight junctions</u> between <u>Sertoli cells</u>

* Separates <u>gametes</u> in <u>seminiferous tubules</u> from the <u>immune system</u>

* Divides <u>seminiferous tubules</u> into:

* Basal compartment

* Contains <u>spermatogonia</u> and immature primary spermatocytes

* In contact with blood and <u>lymph</u>

* Adluminal compartment

* Contains mature spermatocytes and spermatids

* Not in contact with blood and <u>lymph</u>





> [!NOTE]
> “Sertoli fence for <u>sperm</u> defense”: Sertoli cells form the blood-<u>testes</u> barrier.

## Spermatogenesis and spermiogenesis

### <u>Spermatogenesis</u> and <u>spermiogenesis</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)




#### Spermatogenesis




* The process by which <u>spermatogonia</u> (see “Microscopic anatomy” above) become spermatids

* Begins in <u>puberty</u>

* Occurs in the <u>seminiferous tubules</u>

* As <u>spermatogonia</u> mature, they move from the basal layer of the <u>seminiferous tubule</u> to its lumen

* <u>Spermatogonia</u> (2n, <u>diploid</u>) cross the <u>blood-testis barrier</u> → primary spermatocytes (2n, <u>diploid</u>) → secondary spermatocytes (1n, <u>haploid</u>) → spermatids (1n, <u>haploid</u>)

* Duration: ∼ 2 months





![Spermatogenesis](../../../../media/sg6ym94jmss7kaauw6p4ptfe.jpg)

Spermatogenesis

![Oogenesis and spermatogenesis](../../../../media/v5n6szc9sk1kw7xyriv2a6ov.jpg)

Oogenesis and spermatogenesis




#### Spermiogenesis




* The process by which spermatids become spermatozoa

* Requires <u>testosterone</u>

* Formation of the acrosome

* Derived from the <u>Golgi apparatus</u>

* Contains acid <u>phosphatase</u>, <u>neuraminidase</u>, and <u>hyaluronidase</u>

* Loss of <u>cytoplasmic</u> content

* Condensation of genetic material

* Spermatid elongation

* Development of the <u>flagellum</u> (dysfunctional or absent in <u>primary ciliary dyskinesia</u>/<u>Kartagener syndrome</u>)





> [!NOTE]
> GONIUM is GOING to be <u>sperm</u>: Spermatogonia becomes a spermatid.
> ZOON ZOOMS to the egg: Mature spermatozoon travel to the egg.

## Embryology

* <u>Testes</u>: <u>SRY</u> <u>gene</u> on Y <u>chromosome</u> encodes for <u>testis determining factor</u> → primitive sex cords development → <u>testes</u> formation [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)

* <u>Testes</u> develop in the <u>retroperitoneum</u>, then descend into the <u>scrotum</u>:

* Transabdominal migration of both <u>testes</u> begins during week 7 and is usually complete by 32 weeks' <u>gestation</u>.

* <u>Testes</u> descend through the <u>inguinal canal</u>, along with their associated vasculature, nerves, and <u>ductus deferens</u>.

* The descending <u>testes</u> are supported by a layer of the <u>peritoneum</u> (<u>tunica vaginalis</u>), which is normally secured to the superior and inferior <u>posterior</u> pole of the <u>testis</u>.

* With the assistance of the <u>gubernaculum</u> at the lower pole, the <u>testes</u> are pulled into the <u>scrotum</u>.

* The <u>spermatic cord</u>, which holds the <u>vas deferens</u> and testicular vasculature, meets the <u>testis</u> within the <u>scrotum</u>.

* Play a role in further <u>sexual development</u>

* <u>Mullerian inhibitory factor</u> (<u>Sertoli cells</u>) suppresses the development of the <u>paramesonephric ducts</u>.

* <u>Androgens</u> (<u>Leydig cells</u>) stimulate the development of the <u>mesonephric ducts</u>.

* <u>Scrotum</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)

* Derived from the <u>labioscrotal swelling</u>

* Development driven by <u>dihydrotestosterone</u> (<u>DHT</u>)

* See “<u>Development of the reproductive system</u>” for more information.

## Epididymis, ductus deferens, and accessory glands

### Epididymis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)




* Gross anatomy: long, coiled duct along the <u>posterior</u> aspect of the <u>testis</u> → <u>distal</u> end is continuous with <u>ductus deferens</u>

* Function

* Storage and maturation of spermatozoa

* Propulsion of spermatozoa into the <u>ductus deferens</u>

* <u>Embryology</u>: derived from the <u>mesonephric duct</u> (differentiation requires <u>testosterone</u>)

### Ductus deferens [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)




* Gross anatomy

* Long, muscular duct that enters the <u>pelvis</u> at the <u>deep inguinal ring</u> (<u>lateral</u> to <u>inferior epigastric artery</u>)

* Crosses the <u>umbilical artery</u> and <u>obturator nerve</u> → passes superior to the <u>ureter</u> → expands at the <u>distal</u> end to form the ampulla → joins the <u>ejaculatory duct</u>

* Function: transports spermatozoa from the <u>epididymis</u> to the <u>ejaculatory duct</u> and provides fructose to the spermatozoa

* <u>Embryology</u>: derived from the <u>mesonephric duct</u> (differentiation requires <u>testosterone</u>)





### Ejaculatory ducts [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)




* Gross anatomy: formed by the <u>ductus deferens</u> and <u>seminal vesicle</u> ducts → open into the <u>prostatic urethra</u>

* Function: propel spermatozoa with seminal fluid into the <u>urethra</u>

* <u>Embryology</u>: derived from the <u>mesonephric duct</u> (differentiation requires <u>testosterone</u>)





### Accessory glands [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)




#### <u>Prostate</u> (see below)




#### Seminal vesicles




* Gross anatomy

* Paired glands located on the inferoposterior surface of the <u>bladder</u>

* Connect with the <u>ductus deferens</u> to form the <u>ejaculatory ducts</u> → empty into the <u>prostatic urethra</u>

* Microscopic anatomy

* Lobulated <u>glandular tissue</u>

* Lined with <u>pseudostratified</u> <u>columnar epithelium</u>

* Contain secretory granules

* Function: secrete an alkaline fluid that makes up the greater part of seminal fluid

* Contains <u>sorbitol dehydrogenase</u> which converts <u>sorbitol</u> into fructose and thus provides energy for spermatozoa motility

* Contains <u>citrate</u>, fructose, <u>prostaglandins</u>, and other <u>proteins</u>

* <u>Embryology</u>: derived from the <u>mesonephric duct</u> (differentiation requires <u>testosterone</u>)





#### Bulbourethral gland (Cowper gland)




* Gross anatomy: located in the <u>deep perineal pouch</u> within the <u>urogenital diaphragm</u>

* Function: secretes mucus into the <u>bulbar urethra</u> for lubrication during <u>ejaculation</u>

* <u>Embryology</u>: : derived from the <u>pelvic</u> portion of the <u>urogenital sinus</u> (differentiation requires <u>DHT</u>)

## Prostate gland

An <u>accessory gland</u> of reproduction located at the base of the <u>bladder</u> and composed primarily of glandular, fibrous, and <u>smooth muscle tissue</u>.




### Function [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)




Secretion of:




* Components of semen

* Proteolytic enzymes (regulated by <u>DHT</u>) to maintain the fluidity of semen

* Acid <u>phosphatase</u>, citric acid, <u>prostaglandins</u>, fibrinolysins, lipids, <u>amylase</u>

* <u>Prostate-specific antigen</u> (<u>PSA</u>)





### Gross anatomy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BfYzoo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)



* Location

* <u>Posterior</u> to the <u>pubic symphysis</u>, inferior to the <u>bladder</u>, <u>anterior</u> to the <u>rectum</u>, superior to the <u>perineal membrane</u>

* The base wraps around the neck of the <u>urinary bladder</u>.

* The apex surrounds the <u>proximal</u> <u>urethra</u> (<u>prostatic urethra</u>).

## Male sexual response

There are three parts of the <u>male sexual response</u>: <u>erection</u>, emission, and expulsion.




### Erection [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ydYdsL)




* Description: The <u>penis</u> becomes rigid as its <u>corpora cavernosa</u> fill with blood due to sensory and/or mental stimulation of primarily <u>parasympathetic</u> nerves.

* Autonomic control

* <u>Parasympathetic</u>: <u>pelvic splanchnic nerves</u> (<u>S2</u>–<u>S4</u>) → <u>cavernous nerves</u> → <u>nitric oxide</u> (NO) release → ↑<u>cGMP</u> → cavernous muscle relaxation → <u>vasodilation</u> → <u>erection</u>  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XKY9UJ)

* <u>Sympathetic</u>: <u>superior hypogastric plexus</u> (T11–L2) → fibers travel along <u>pelvic plexus</u> → <u>norepinephrine</u> release → ↑ <u>calcium</u> influx → cavernous muscle contraction → <u>vasoconstriction</u> → antagonizes <u>erection</u> (detumescence)

* Types of <u>erection</u>

* Reflex erection (mostly sensory stimulation)

* Genital stimulation → <u>pudendal nerve</u> afferents → sacral <u>erection</u> center <u>S2</u>–<u>S4</u> (<u>parasympathetic nervous system</u>) → <u>erection</u>

* Injuries to the cervical or thoracic spinal cord may result in an <u>erection</u>.

* Psychogenic erection (mostly mental stimulation)

* Cortical stimulation via visual, auditory, and sensory stimuli or fantasy → impact on the thoracolumbar <u>erection</u> center T11–L2 (<u>sympathetic nervous system</u>) → <u>inferior mesenteric plexus</u> and <u>superior hypogastric plexus</u> → some <u>sympathetic</u> <u>neurons</u> act synergistically with sacral <u>parasympathetic</u> signals (<u>S2</u>–<u>S4</u>) to increase blood flow to <u>corpora cavernosa</u> → <u>erection</u>

* <u>Sympathetic</u> innervation to the <u>smooth muscle</u> of <u>vas deferens</u>, <u>seminal vesicle</u>, and internal sphincter of the <u>bladder</u>

* Nocturnal penile tumescence

* Spontaneous <u>erection</u> while sleeping or upon waking up

* The cause is not entirely understood.

* <u>Nocturnal tumescence monitoring</u> serves as a means of differentiating psychological from physiological <u>erectile dysfunction</u>.





> [!NOTE]
> <u>PDE-5 inhibitors</u> (e.g., <u>sildenafil</u>) inhibit <u>hydrolysis</u> of <u>cGMP</u> → ↑ NO → <u>vasodilation</u> → prolonged <u>erection</u>




> [!NOTE]
> <u>S2</u>, <u>S3</u>, and <u>S4</u> keep the <u>penis</u> off the floor.




### Ejaculation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iRYJ5K)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ydYdsL)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4NY3Yp)




#### Emission




* During the first phase of <u>ejaculation</u>, <u>sperm</u> and secretions from the reproductive glands are transported through the reproductive ducts (<u>epididymis</u>, <u>vas deferens</u>, and <u>ejaculatory duct</u>) into the internal <u>urethra</u>.

* Ejaculate consists of <u>prostatic</u> secretion, <u>seminal vesicle</u> fluid, and spermatozoa

* Secretion of fluid from <u>accessory glands</u> promotes <u>sperm</u> survival.

* Stimulated primarily by the <u>sympathetic nervous system</u> (<u>hypogastric nerve</u>; T11–L2) → closure of the <u>bladder neck</u> and contraction of reproductive glands (<u>seminal vesicle</u>, bulbourethral, and <u>prostate glands</u>)





#### Expulsion




* During the second phase of <u>ejaculation</u>, the semen is transported through the <u>urethra</u> and expelled from the body.

* Stimulated by:

* <u>Sympathetic nervous system</u>: <u>hypogastric nerve</u> (T11–L2)

* Somatic nervous system: <u>pudendal nerve</u> → rhythmic contraction of the <u>bulbocavernosus muscle</u> → expulsion of semen from the <u>urethra</u>





> [!NOTE]
> Remember the ejaculatory pathway of <u>sperm</u> with SEVEn UP: Seminiferous tubules → Epididymis → Vas (ductus) deferens → Ejaculatory duct → Urethra → Penis




> [!NOTE]
> Innervation of <u>male sexual response</u>: Point, Squeeze, and Shoot.
> 1. Parasympathetic → Point (<u>erection</u>)
> 2. Sympathetic → Squeeze (emission)
> 3. Somatic (<u>pudendal nerve</u>) → Shoot (expulsion)
