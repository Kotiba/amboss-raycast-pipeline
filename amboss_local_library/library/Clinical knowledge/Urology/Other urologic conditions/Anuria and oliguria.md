## Summary

<u>Anuria</u> is the absence of <u>urine production</u>, and <u>oliguria</u> is reduced production of urine. <u>Anuria</u> and <u>oliguria</u> can be physiological (e.g., following physical exertion) or reflect significant underlying renal or systemic pathology. Pathological causes include <u>prerenal acute kidney injury</u> (<u>AKI</u>), <u>intrinsic AKI</u>, <u>postrenal AKI</u>, and <u>chronic kidney disease</u> (<u>CKD</u>). Clinical features vary according to the underlying cause. Initial evaluation includes <u>hemodynamic assessment</u>, laboratory tests (e.g., serum <u>electrolytes</u> and <u>creatinine</u>), <u>urinalysis</u>, and imaging (e.g., <u>renal and bladder ultrasound</u>) to detect obstruction. A focused approach to identify and address the underlying cause is essential to improve outcomes.
