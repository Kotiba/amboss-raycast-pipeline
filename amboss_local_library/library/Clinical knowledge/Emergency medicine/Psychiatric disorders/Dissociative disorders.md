# Dissociative disorders

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Psychiatric disorders > Dissociative disorders*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NP0-UT)

---

## Summary

Dissociative disorders are psychiatric conditions characterized by disruption and/or discontinuity of normal consciousness, <u>memory</u>, identity, and perception. Abnormalities may also be seen in behavior, control of motor functions, and body representation. The disorders are typically seen in individuals with a history of very stressful or traumatic events and often manifest already in childhood. <u>DSM-5</u> recognizes the following types: <u>dissociative identity disorder</u>, <u>dissociative amnesia</u> (with or without <u>dissociative fugue</u>), <u>depersonalization disorder</u>, as well as <u>other specified dissociative disorder</u> and <u>unspecified dissociative disorder</u>. Patients usually experience positive dissociative symptoms such as <u>derealization</u>, fragmentation of identity, and <u>depersonalization</u> (i.e., intrusive and involuntary changes in awareness and behavior) and/or negative dissociative symptoms, such as <u>amnesia</u> or difficulties controlling mental functions. Dissociative disorders are often associated with other psychiatric symptoms and conditions, including <u>anxiety</u>, <u>depression</u>, <u>somatic symptom disorders</u>, and <u>eating disorders</u>.

## Definitions

* (Nonpathological) dissociation: a psychological <u>defense mechanism</u> (natural protective response) to a traumatic or very stressful experience.

* Disruption of normal integration of memories, identity, perception, experiences, and emotions to cope with <u>stress</u> or stimulation overload

* Detachment from self (physical and emotional experiences) and/or surroundings

* Can be a fleeting phenomenon (e.g. daydreaming) or more severe with impaired consciousness

* Pathological dissociation: an excessive reaction to a traumatic or very stressful experience that is characterized by:

* Positive symptoms: <u>derealization</u>;  (the mind detaches from one's environment), fragmentation of identity; , and <u>depersonalization</u> (the mind detaches from one's self)

* <u>Negative symptoms</u>: <u>amnesia</u>, altered consciousness (e.g., narrowed awareness, trance), altered behavior [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GraBiN)

## Dissociative amnesia

* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Most common dissociative disorder (lifetime <u>prevalence</u> is ∼ 7%) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zbXrw9)

* More common in women than in men [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-bXDw9)

* Diagnostic criteria
1. * Inability to recall autobiographical information after a traumatic or stressful event  that is distinct from ordinary forgetting
* Can be described as:

* Localized (i.e. <u>amnesia</u> of a single event or time period)

* Selective (i.e. <u>amnesia</u> of a specific aspect of an occurrence)

* Generalized (i.e. <u>amnesia</u> of personal history and identity)
2. * Symptoms cause significant social or occupational impairment.
3. * Symptoms are not due to substance use or another medical condition.
4. * Symptoms cannot be explained by another psychiatric disorder (e.g., <u>dissociative identity disorder</u>, <u>acute stress disorder</u>, <u>posttraumatic stress disorder</u>).
5. * May present with dissociative fugue: wandering or purposeful travel (associated with <u>amnesia</u> of identity or autobiographical information)

* Treatment

* Primarily <u>psychotherapy</u>

* No effective pharmacotherapy is available

* Prognosis

* Temporarily inaccessible memories are often retrievable or return naturally

* Rarely generalizes, but can cause complete <u>memory</u> loss

* Associated with concurrent <u>major depressive disorder</u> and increased risk for <u>suicide</u>





> [!TIP]
> Patients may experience the return of memories as nightmares or flashbacks. The complete resolution of <u>amnesia</u> and full return of <u>memory</u> may be overwhelming and coincide with an increased risk for <u>suicide</u>.

## Depersonalization/derealization disorder

* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Lifetime <u>prevalence</u> ∼ 2% [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zbXrw9)

* Average age at onset 16 years [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZXXZ99)

* Diagnostic criteria
1. * Recurrent or persistent episodes of <u>depersonalization</u> and/or <u>derealization</u>

* <u>Depersonalization</u>: sense of unreality with detachment from oneself (e.g., body perception, feelings, thoughts, actions)

* <u>Derealization</u>: sense of unreality with detachment from one's environment
2. * Reality testing is intact during these episodes (as opposed to <u>psychosis</u>, in which reality is not intact)
3. * Symptoms cause significant social or occupational impairment.
4. * Symptoms are not due to substance use or another medical condition.
5. * Symptoms cannot be better explained by another psychiatric disorder (e.g., <u>schizophrenia</u>, <u>major depressive disorder</u>, <u>panic disorder</u>, <u>acute stress disorder</u>, <u>PTSD</u>, other dissociative disorders).

* Treatment: <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u>, hypnotherapy, and/or <u>psychodynamic therapy</u>

* Prognosis

* Often persistent, with fluctuations in severity

* Increased risk for concurrent major <u>depression</u> and <u>anxiety disorders</u>





> [!TIP]
> Patients with a <u>depersonalization/derealization disorder</u> may report out-of-body experiences or a feeling of being in a dream.

## Dissociative identity disorder

* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* <u>Prevalence</u>: < 1% [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0XXe99)

* More prevalent in women [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aXXQ99)

* May manifest at any age

* Associated conditions

* History of physical/<u>sexual abuse</u> and/or neglect during childhood

* <u>Major depressive disorder</u>

* <u>PTSD</u>

* <u>Borderline personality disorder</u>

* <u>Somatic symptom disorder</u>

* Diagnostic criteria
1. * Alternation of at least two separate <u>personality</u> states that cause identity disruption and dominate at different times, manifesting with the following:

* Distinct <u>memory</u>, behavior, affect, perception, consciousness, <u>cognition</u>, and/or sensory-motor functioning (<u>derealization</u>)

* Discontinuity in sense of self (<u>depersonalization</u>) and agency
2. * Frequent gaps in recall of normal daily events or personal information that are significantly different from ordinary forgetfulness
3. * Symptoms cause the patient significant social or occupational impairment.
4. * Symptoms are not related to substance use or another medical condition.
5. * Symptoms can not be explained by broadly accepted religious or cultural practice. In children, symptoms can not be attributed to <u>imaginary friends</u> or other fantasy play.

* Treatment [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lXYvyn)

* <u>Psychotherapy</u>

* <u>SSRIs</u> for comorbid conditions (<u>depression</u>, <u>PTSD</u>)

* Prognosis

* Chronic disease course with fluctuations in severity

* Increased risk of other psychiatric conditions (e.g., major <u>depression</u>, <u>borderline personality disorder</u>, <u>PTSD</u>, <u>eating disorders</u>, <u>substance use disorders</u>)

* High rates of self-harm and <u>suicide</u>





> [!TIP]
> Patients are often unaware of the other personalities and describe a feeling of being possessed or experiencing frequent gaps in recall.
