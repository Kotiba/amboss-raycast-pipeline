# Schizophrenia

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Psychiatric disorders > Schizophrenia*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pP0LfT)

---

## Summary

Schizophrenia is a psychiatric disorder that manifests with a variable collection of clinical features. <u>Negative symptoms of schizophrenia</u> (e.g., flat affect, inattention, and/or social withdrawal) typically precede <u>positive symptoms of schizophrenia</u> (e.g., <u>hallucinations</u>, <u>grossly disorganized behaviors</u>). The majority of individuals with schizophrenia experience symptoms early in adulthood. The pathophysiologic mechanism is thought to be related to increased <u>dopaminergic</u> activity in the <u>mesolimbic</u> neuronal pathway and decreased <u>dopaminergic</u> activity in the prefrontal cortical pathway. <u>Management of schizophrenia</u> includes ruling out underlying medical causes and initiating a comprehensive treatment plan with both pharmacological and psychosocial interventions. Lifelong therapy, including monitoring for the adverse effects of <u>antipsychotic medication</u>, is necessary.

* Lifetime <u>prevalence</u>: 0.3–0.7% [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Sex: <u>♂</u> = <u>♀</u> worldwide [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Age of onset: late teens to mid-30s [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Men: typically early 20s

* Women: typically late 20s

## Etiology

### <u>Risk factors</u>




* Genetic factors: risk significantly increased if relatives are also affected [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Environmental factors [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* <u>Stress</u> and psychosocial factors (e.g., economic deprivation, migration and/or refugee status, urban localization) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Frequent use of <u>cannabis</u> during early teens (associated with increased <u>incidence</u> and worse course of positive symptoms) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6UXjVx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pUXLVx)

* Advanced paternal age at <u>conception</u>

## Pathophysiology

### Dysregulation of <u>neurotransmitters</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YFYngI)




* ↓ <u>Dopamine</u> in prefrontal cortical pathway may cause negative <u>symptoms of psychosis</u>.

* ↑ <u>Dopamine</u> in <u>mesolimbic pathway</u> may lead to positive <u>symptoms of psychosis</u>.

* ↑ Serotonergic activity

* ↓ Dendritic branching

* ↓ Glutamatergic neurotransmission may lead to <u>psychosis</u>.

* ↓ <u>GABA</u> leads to ↑ <u>dopamine</u> activity.





### Structural and functional changes to the <u>brain</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aFYQgI)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H8YKLI)




* Enlarged <u>lateral</u> and <u>third ventricles</u>

* Decreased symmetry

* Decreased volume of the <u>limbic system</u>, <u>prefrontal cortex</u>, and <u>thalamus</u>

* ↓ Volume of the <u>hippocampus</u> and <u>amygdala</u>

* Hypoactivity of the frontal lobes and hyperactivity of the <u>basal ganglia</u>

## Clinical features

Schizophrenia manifests with a variable collection of clinical features. <u>Negative symptoms of schizophrenia</u> (e.g., flat affect, inattention, and/or social withdrawal) typically precede <u>positive symptoms of schizophrenia</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




### Negative symptoms of schizophrenia [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




* Flat affect: reduced or absent emotional expression

* Avolition: reduced or absent ability to initiate purposeful activities

* Alogia: impaired thinking that manifests with reduced speech output or <u>poverty of speech</u> (e.g., always replying to questions with one-word answers)

* Anhedonia: inability to feel pleasure from activities that were formerly pleasurable or from any new positive stimuli

* Apathy: lack of emotion or concern, especially with regard to matters that are normally considered important

* Emotional and social withdrawal





### Positive symptoms of schizophrenia




* <u>Psychosis</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* <u>Hallucinations</u> and/or <u>illusions</u>  (<u>auditory hallucinations</u> are most common)

* <u>Delusions</u> (e.g., <u>grandiosity</u>, <u>ideas of reference</u>, <u>paranoia</u>, <u>persecutory delusions</u>)

* <u>Disorganized thought</u> or <u>disorganized speech</u> (e.g., <u>loose associations</u>, <u>word salad</u>, <u>tangential speech</u>)

* Grossly disorganized behaviors [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* A range of behaviors during goal-directed activities that lead to difficulty with performing <u>activities of daily living</u>

* Manifestations may include <u>catatonia</u> and/or socially inappropriate emotional responses (e.g., inappropriate smiling or laughing, infantility)





### Other features of schizophrenia [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




* Cognitive symptoms in schizophrenia

* Inattention

* Impaired <u>memory</u>

* Poor executive functioning

* Mood symptoms and <u>anxiety</u>

* <u>Depression</u> (most common)

* <u>Social phobia</u>

* <u>Specific phobia</u>

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances

* Decreased food intake

* Neurological abnormalities: e.g., sensory disturbances and impaired coordination

* Impaired social and/or vocational function

## Subtypes and variants

### Catatonia




* Definition: a behavioral syndrome characterized by atypical movement and abnormal reactivity to the environment

* Classification:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* <u>Catatonia</u> associated with mental disorders, e.g.:

* <u>Psychotic disorders</u>

* <u>Bipolar disorder</u>

* <u>Depressive disorders</u>

* <u>Neurodevelopmental disorders</u>

* <u>Catatonia</u> associated with medical disorders, e.g.:

* <u>Hepatic encephalopathy</u>

* <u>Traumatic brain injury</u>

* <u>Paraneoplastic syndrome</u>

* Drug adverse effects (e.g., <u>antipsychotics</u>)

* Clinical features [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Key features: <u>stupor</u>, <u>catalepsy</u>, <u>mutism</u>, <u>negativism</u>, <u>waxy flexibility</u>, <u>abnormal posturing</u>, <u>mannerism (psychiatry)</u>, <u>stereotypy</u>, <u>agitation</u> (not influenced by external stimuli), grimacing, <u>echolalia</u>, and/or <u>echopraxia</u>

* Malignant catatonia resembles <u>neuroleptic malignant syndrome</u>, manifesting with <u>fever</u>, <u>autonomic instability</u> (e.g., <u>tachycardia</u>, <u>tachypnea</u>, abnormal BP, and sweating), rigidity, and <u>delirium</u>.

* Diagnosis

* <u>Clinical diagnosis</u>: confirmed with the presence of ≥ 3 key clinical features [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Determine severity using a validated scoring instrument e.g., the <u>Bush-Francis Catatonia Rating Scale</u>.  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aGdQBr0)

* Treatment [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/shXt2B)

* <u>Benzodiazepines</u> (Intravenous or sublingual <u>lorazepam</u>): first-line for all forms of <u>catatonia</u>

* <u>Electroconvulsive therapy</u>

* First-line for <u>malignant catatonia</u> and nonmalignant <u>catatonia</u> due to a <u>mood disorder with psychotic features</u>

* Second-line in case of inadequate response to <u>benzodiazepine</u> therapy

* Discontinue <u>dopamine</u>-blocking drugs (e.g., <u>antipsychotics</u>); only reinitiate after <u>catatonia</u> resolves

* Treat the underlying psychiatric condition with appropriate pharmacotherapy

* Supportive measures

* <u>DVT prophylaxis</u>

* <u>Pressure ulcer</u> prevention

* Adequate hydration and <u>specialized nutritive support</u>

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* Schizophrenia is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> based on the <u>DSM-5</u> criteria.

* Consult <u>psychiatry</u> urgently. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rVcfDY0)

* Diagnostic studies may be indicated to:

* Rule out an organic cause (e.g., using <u>diagnostic studies for secondary psychosis</u>)

* Establish a baseline before initiating pharmacological treatment

* Identify comorbidities (e.g., <u>diabetes mellitus</u>) or other conditions (e.g., <u>pregnancy</u>) that influence management

* Facilitate admission to a psychiatric facility with limited resources

* See “Diagnostics” in “<u>Approach to psychosis</u>” for details.





> [!TIP]
> Schizophrenia is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> that should be made by a <u>psychiatrist</u>.




> [!NOTE]
> <u>Brain</u> imaging is not required for the diagnosis of schizophrenia but can show cortical <u>atrophy</u>, decreased hippocampal and/or temporal mass, and/or enlargement of the <u>cerebral ventricles</u>. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7ic4GX0)




### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for schizophrenia[[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




* At least two of the following symptoms must be present, with at least one of these from the first three symptoms listed:

* <u>Delusions</u>

* <u>Hallucinations</u>

* <u>Disorganized speech</u>

* <u>Grossly disorganized behavior</u> or <u>catatonia</u>

* <u>Negative symptoms</u>

* The above symptoms persist for ≥ 1 month.

* Continuous signs of disturbance for ≥ 6 months

* Symptoms must cause social, occupational, or personal functional impairment for a significant period of time

* <u>Schizoaffective disorder</u> and <u>mood disorder with psychotic features</u> have been ruled out.

* Medical or <u>substance use disorder</u> has been ruled out.

* History of <u>autism spectrum disorder</u> or communication disorder of childhood onset: Prominent <u>delusions</u> or <u>hallucinations</u> must be present for ≥ 1 month.

## Management

### Approach [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JVcsvY0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZRcZlX0)




* Ensure patient and provider safety in cases of acute <u>psychoses</u>.

* Consider short-acting <u>antipsychotics</u> early if the patient is <u>agitated</u> or in <u>distress</u>. [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jtY_WI)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vVcAwY0)

* See “<u>Approach to the agitated or violent patient</u>” for details.

* Establish a <u>therapeutic alliance</u> when taking care of patients with <u>delusions</u>.

* Acknowledge the patient's emotional state.

* Avoid validation of <u>delusions</u> or confronting patients about the <u>delusional</u> nature of their symptoms.

* Hospitalize patients who are:

* At risk of harming themselves or others

* Experiencing a first episode of <u>psychosis</u>

* All patients should receive:

* Specialized psychiatric care and a comprehensive treatment plan

* Treatment with an <u>antipsychotic medication</u>

* Adjunctive treatment with nonpharmacological interventions

* <u>Integrated care</u> of psychiatric and medical comorbidities (e.g., <u>depression</u>, <u>metabolic syndrome</u>)

* Prior to starting treatment patients should have:

* Assessment of symptom severity using quantitative tools, e.g., the Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale   [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bRcHlX0)

* Baseline diagnostic studies to screen for and facilitate treatment of complications

* After starting treatment patients should be regularly reassessed to:

* Assess the <u>effectiveness</u> of treatment

* Screen for side effects caused by <u>antipsychotic</u> therapy

* Detect any signs of relapse





> [!TIP]
> The diagnosis of schizophrenia and the initiation of long-term <u>antipsychotic medication</u> should be determined and managed by a <u>psychiatrist</u>.

## Nonpharmacological and pharmacological treatment

### Nonpharmacological interventions [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* Provide adjunctive nonpharmacological interventions to all patients.

* <u>Cognitive-behavioral therapy</u>: improves the quality of life and reduces positive symptoms

* <u>Psychoeducation</u> : associated with improved social functioning and lower relapse rates

* <u>Cognitive remediation therapy</u> in patients with cognitive impairment

* <u>Social skills training</u>

* Supported employment services: improves employment outcomes

* Consider <u>assertive community treatment</u> in patients who are at ongoing risk of hospitalization, incarceration, and/or unstable housing.

* Family interventions : reduce core symptoms and relapse rates





### Baseline studies prior to starting pharmacological treatment [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* <u>Pregnancy test</u>: <u>Pregnancy</u> may alter the <u>management of schizophrenia</u>.

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> (to establish a pretreatment baseline) :

* <u>Fasting blood glucose</u> and/or <u>HbA1c</u>

* <u>Lipid panel</u>

* <u>Prolactin</u> level

* <u>ECG</u>: Assess for potential <u>QTc prolongation</u> or evidence of cardiovascular disease.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)





### Selection of an initial <u>antipsychotic medication</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* <u>Antipsychotics</u> (e.g., <u>risperidone</u>, <u>aripiprazole</u>, <u>quetiapine</u>) are the first-line treatment for schizophrenia.

* Treatment can be initiated with either a first generation or a <u>second generation antipsychotic</u>.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)
* <u>Clozapine</u> is not recommended as first-line treatment because of significant side effects (e.g., <u>agranulocytosis</u>).

* Base the selection of <u>antipsychotics</u> on:

* Side effects

* Available formulations

* Potential <u>drug interactions</u>

* For further information see “<u>Overview of antipsychotics</u>.”

* In pregnant patients, older generations of <u>antipsychotics</u> with a greater evidence base may be preferable.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* Suggested <u>first-generation antipsychotic</u>: <u>haloperidol</u> [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PRcWMX0)

* Suggested <u>second-generation antipsychotic</u>: <u>olanzapine</u> [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PRcWMX0)





> [!WARNING]
> Because of the risk of side effects, patients should not be started on long-acting injectable <u>antipsychotics</u> without a trial of the oral formulation of the same medication first. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Clozapine</u> and <u>olanzapine</u> are not recommended as first-line agents for patients experiencing their first episode of schizophrenia. <u>Clozapine</u> is associated with severe <u>agranulocytosis</u>, <u>olanzapine</u> with significant metabolic side effects. [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jtY_WI)




### Reassessment




Symptom severity should be reassessed with a quantitative tool 2–4 weeks after initiating treatment.




> [!TIP]
> <u>Negative symptoms</u> are more difficult to treat and may continue after positive symptoms have resolved. [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uRcpKX0)




#### Partial or no response to initial <u>antipsychotic medication</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* Successful <u>antipsychotic</u> therapy should improve symptoms by > 20% after approximately 2 weeks.

* If the patient has not had > 20% improvement by 2 weeks, or improvement subsequently plateaus at < 50% improvement:

* Consider contributing factors: <u>cannabis</u> use, medication interaction, poor absorption, effect of smoking on drug metabolism, concomitant disorder (e.g., <u>depression</u>). [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* Consider increasing the dose of the initial medication one time.

* If there is no response after this change, consider using a different <u>antipsychotic</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* If there is no response after an adequate trial of 2 different <u>antipsychotics</u>, the patient is considered to have <u>treatment-resistant schizophrenia</u>.





#### Treatment-resistant schizophrenia [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* Definition: persistent positive symptoms (i.e., <u>delusions</u>, <u>hallucinations</u>, and/or <u>disorganized speech</u>) despite trials of ≥ 6 weeks of 2 different <u>antipsychotics</u> at therapeutic doses  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* An estimated 25–30% of patients will be resistant to 2 medications. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JVcsvY0)

* <u>Clozapine</u> is the drug of choice for <u>treatment-resistant schizophrenia</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* Indications

* No response to 2 other <u>antipsychotics</u>

* Persistent aggressive behavior or suicidal intentions

* <u>Clozapine</u> is not used as first-line therapy because of its adverse effects, which include:

* Cardiovascular collapse with rapid dosage changes

* <u>Orthostatic hypotension</u>

* <u>Seizures</u>

* <u>Agranulocytosis</u>

* Enrollment in a national patient registry and frequent monitoring of <u>clozapine</u> levels and <u>CBC</u> are mandated for <u>clozapine</u> use. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)





> [!WARNING]
> All patients taking <u>clozapine</u> require regular monitoring of their <u>absolute neutrophil count</u> because of the risk of fatal <u>agranulocytosis</u>.




### Continuation of medication [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* <u>Antipsychotic medication</u> should be continued indefinitely if it has effectively reduced initial symptoms.

* <u>Pregnancy</u> is not an indication to stop treatment. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)

* Risks of discontinuing <u>antipsychotics</u> include disrupted <u>prenatal care</u>, poor nutrition, adverse risk behaviors, and relapse.

* Most individuals only become aware of their <u>pregnancy</u> after 8 weeks of <u>gestation</u>, when the risk of <u>teratogenicity</u> has already occurred.

* Long-acting (injectable) <u>antipsychotics</u> may be considered based on patient preference or <u>poor adherence</u> to the medication regimen. ;  [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZRcZlX0)

* Nonadherence is common for several reasons : [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JVcsvY0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZRcZlX0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vVcAwY0)

* Medication side effects (e.g., <u>galactorrhea</u>)

* Lack of patient insight into the need for treatment

* Difficulties adhering to a daily medication regimen

* Patients may prefer injectable medications because they are convenient and produce a stable drug effect.

* Examples of long-acting injectable <u>antipsychotics</u> include:

* <u>First-generation antipsychotics</u>: <u>fluphenazine</u>, <u>haloperidol</u>

* <u>Second-generation antipsychotics</u>: <u>risperidone</u>, <u>aripiprazole</u>, <u>olanzapine</u>, <u>paliperidone</u>





> [!WARNING]
> Stopping <u>antipsychotics</u> during <u>pregnancy</u> risks relapse; it should only be done under guidance from experts in <u>perinatal</u> <u>psychiatry</u>.

## Prognosis

Schizophrenia is a progressive disorder that causes significant impairment, with many patients presenting with psychosocial dysfunction.




* Predictive factors for a favorable course of illness [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NfW-mk0)

* Strong <u>treatment adherence</u>

* Older age at onset

* Strong network of social support

* Rapid onset of symptoms

* Few <u>negative symptoms</u>

* Female sex

* High level of functioning before onset

* Timely diagnosis and treatment

* Predictive factors for an unfavorable course of illness [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s8YtLI)

* <u>Family history</u>

* Early onset of disease

* Poor network of social support

* Slow onset of symptoms

* Many <u>negative symptoms</u>

* Male sex

* Cognitive impairment

* Concomitant <u>substance use disorder</u>

* <u>Suicidal ideation</u>/<u>suicide attempt</u>





> [!TIP]
> Depressive symptoms are associated with a higher risk of <u>suicide</u>, but may be linked to a lower risk of relapse and/or hospitalization. [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s8YtLI)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DdSVH0)




> [!NOTE]
> Patients with schizophrenia are at an increased risk for <u>alcohol use disorder</u>, <u>depression</u>, violence, and <u>suicide</u> (∼ 5% of affected individuals complete <u>suicide</u>). [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s8YtLI)

## Special patient groups

### Schizophrenia in children and adolescents




#### Definitions [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M3VMQ80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)




* Early-onset schizophrenia: onset before 18 years of age

* Childhood-onset schizophrenia (<u>COS</u>): onset before 13 years of age

* Adolescent-onset schizophrenia: onset between 13 and 17 years of age [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)[[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OkVILu0)





#### <u>Epidemiology</u>




* <u>COS</u>: extremely rare; <u>prevalence</u> of < 0.04% [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M3VMQ80)

* <u>Adolescent-onset schizophrenia</u>: <u>prevalence</u> of 0.5% [[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)





> [!TIP]
> In a large international <u>meta-analysis</u>, by 18 years of age, 18% of individuals with schizophrenia had experienced symptoms, but only 6.5% had been diagnosed. [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PkVWLu0)




#### Clinical features [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* <u>Prodromal</u> symptoms are more common in <u>early-onset schizophrenia</u>, e.g: [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)

* Social withdrawal and isolation

* Behavioral disturbances

* <u>Dysphoria</u>

* <u>Developmental delays</u>

* <u>Cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia</u> [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)

* Typically more severe in <u>COS</u> than in <u>adolescent-onset schizophrenia</u>

* May manifest as academic difficulties

* <u>Negative symptoms of schizophrenia</u> are prominent in <u>early-onset schizophrenia</u> compared to adult. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M3VMQ80)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)

* <u>Positive symptoms of schizophrenia</u> differ from those in adults in the following ways: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)

* <u>Hallucinations</u> are more common than <u>delusions</u>.

* <u>Auditory hallucinations</u> remain most common, but <u>visual hallucinations</u> occur more frequently in children.

* <u>Delusions</u> and <u>hallucinations</u> may be less complex in children.

* <u>Catatonia</u> is less common in <u>early-onset schizophrenia</u> compared to adults. [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Prodromal</u> symptoms of schizophrenia may be mistaken for <u>symptoms of depression</u>, <u>anxiety</u>, or <u>ADHD</u>. [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Hallucinations</u> are more common than <u>delusions</u> at younger ages, but they must be clearly differentiated from age-appropriate imaginative activity (e.g., engaging with <u>imaginary friends</u>, roleplay). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




#### Diagnosis [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)




Diagnosis is performed by a child <u>psychiatrist</u> after a comprehensive evaluation involving the following elements.




* <u>Evaluation and management of acute psychosis</u>  [[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)

* Assessment for <u>differential diagnoses of schizophrenia</u> in children and comorbidities, including:  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)

* <u>Secondary psychosis</u> (e.g., due to substance use; , <u>autoimmune encephalitis</u>); see "<u>Diagnostics for psychosis</u>"

* <u>Neurodevelopmental disorders</u>; see "<u>Evaluation for abnormal pediatric development</u>"

* Diagnostic confirmation

* The adult <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for schizophrenia</u> are also used in children.

* In children and <u>adolescents</u>, symptoms must cause impaired or delayed social and/or academic development.





> [!TIP]
> During psychiatric assessments, use validated tools (e.g., the Youth <u>Psychosis</u> At-Risk Questionnaire, the <u>Prodromal</u> Questionnaire – Brief) to screen for features of <u>psychosis</u>. [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)




> [!WARNING]
> Perform <u>suicide risk screening</u> in all patients presenting with <u>psychosis</u>, as they are at increased risk for <u>suicide</u>. [[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)




#### Management




* Refer all patients to child <u>psychiatry</u>; urgently refer to the emergency department for: [[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)

* Severe impairment in functioning

* Risk of self-harm or harm to others

* Severe <u>agitation</u> requiring pharmacological intervention

* Treatment includes: [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M3VMQ80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jiV_r80)

* <u>Antipsychotic medications</u> (e.g., <u>risperidone</u>, <u>aripiprazole</u>)

* Psychotherapeutic interventions (e.g., <u>CBT</u>, <u>social skills training</u>, family interventions)

* Provide <u>preventive health care</u> for complications of treatment. 

* At diagnosis [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)

* Document <u>BMI</u>, weight circumference, and blood pressure, and screen for symptoms of <u>diabetes</u>.

* Obtain baseline <u>laboratory studies</u> (i.e., <u>CBC</u>, <u>CMP</u>, <u>thyroid function tests</u>, <u>fasting</u> <u>glucose</u>, and a <u>lipid panel</u>).

* Screen for <u>family history</u> of <u>diabetes</u>, <u>obesity</u>, cardiovascular disease, <u>dyslipidemia</u>, and <u>hypertension</u>.

* Educate on healthy lifestyle (e.g., diet and exercise).

* Reassess metabolic parameters every 6 months, or sooner if clinically indicated. [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)





> [!TIP]
> Specialized programs (e.g., early intervention or first episode <u>psychosis</u> programs) may improve the prognosis for children and <u>adolescents</u> with a new diagnosis of schizophrenia. [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)




> [!WARNING]
> Children and <u>adolescents</u> are at increased risk of metabolic adverse effects of <u>atypical antipsychotics</u> (e.g., <u>diabetes</u>, <u>hyperlipidemia</u>, and <u>hypertension</u>); monitoring is needed. [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)




#### Differential diagnoses




* Similar to <u>differential diagnoses of schizophrenia</u> in adults

* Consider also: [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)

* Normal childhood behaviors (e.g., vivid fantasies, <u>imaginary friends</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/84VOOu0)

* <u>Neurodevelopmental disorders</u>, including <u>autism spectrum disorder</u> and attention deficit disorder

* <u>Lysosomal storage diseases</u>

* <u>22q11.2 deletion syndrome</u>

* <u>Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis</u>





#### Prognosis




Compared to adult-onset schizophrenia, the prognosis of <u>early-onset schizophrenia</u> is typically worse, with a higher likelihood of: [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/X3V9S80)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PiVW780)




* Poor outcomes

* <u>Treatment-resistant schizophrenia</u> [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/44V3ju0)
