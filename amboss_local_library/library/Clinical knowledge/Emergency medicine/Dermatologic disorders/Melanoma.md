# Melanoma

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qk0CKT)

## Summary

Melanoma, a <u>malignancy</u> arising from <u>melanocytes</u>, is the most common cause of <u>skin</u> cancer-related <u>death</u>. <u>Risk factors</u> include UV radiation exposure (especially in individuals with light <u>skin</u>), <u>dysplastic nevi</u>, a <u>family history</u> of melanoma, and <u>immunosuppression</u>. Melanoma is most commonly cutaneous in origin, but may also arise in <u>mucosal</u> surfaces and in the <u>uvea</u>. There are four main histological subtypes of melanoma. <u>Superficial spreading melanoma</u> is the most common, while <u>nodular melanoma</u> tends to grow rapidly in depth and has the worst prognosis of all the subtypes. Clinical features concerning for melanoma include asymmetry, border irregularity, color variegation, diameter > 6 mm, and evolving size, shape, and/or color over time (i.e., the <u>ABCDE criteria</u>). <u>Full-thickness excisional biopsy</u> of <u>skin</u> lesions is the best diagnostic test for melanoma and should be performed immediately in all patients with suspicious lesions. Additional diagnostic and staging tests (e.g., <u>sentinel lymph node biopsy</u> and/or imaging) may be indicated in certain patients. <u>Tumor</u> thickness is a key prognostic factor. Treatment is primarily surgical and includes wide excision of the lesion with <u>surgical margins</u> determined by the <u>tumor</u> thickness. Systemic drug therapy (e.g., with <u>immune checkpoint inhibitors</u>, <u>BRAF inhibitors</u>, and <u>MEK inhibitors</u>) or <u>radiation therapy</u> may be indicated in patients with <u>metastatic</u> or unresectable disease.

* Most common cause of <u>skin</u> cancer-related <u>death</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3lWS9l0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RlWl9l0)

* <u>Incidence</u> is higher among individuals who are: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ilWJ9l0)

* Non-Hispanic White

* Male

* ≥ 75 years of age

## Etiology

### Risk factors for cutaneous melanoma [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ncW7140)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/94WNll0)




* UV radiation exposure (e.g., nonadherence to <u>photoprotective measures</u>, previous episodes of <u>sunburn</u>, use of tanning beds)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C4Wqll0)

* Light <u>skin</u> types  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Vv1G_i0)

* Precursor lesions, including:  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4Whll0)

* <u>Dysplastic nevi</u>

* Giant <u>congenital nevi</u>

* Personal or <u>family history</u> of melanoma or other <u>skin</u> cancer  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/94WNll0)

* Predisposing inherited <u>skin</u> conditions (e.g., <u>xeroderma pigmentosum</u>, <u>familial atypical multiple mole melanoma syndrome</u>)

* Genetic mutations, including:

* <u>BRAF</u> <u>gene</u> mutations

* Seen in 50% of melanomas [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)

* V600E mutation (most common): an <u>activating mutation</u> in the <u>BRAF</u> <u>gene</u> that substitutes <u>glutamic acid</u> for <u>valine</u> at <u>amino acid</u> position 600 [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MdbMqs)

* <u>CDKN2A</u> <u>gene mutations</u>

* <u>Immunosuppression</u>

## Clinical features

The following are general features of <u>cutaneous melanoma</u>. See “<u>Types of melanoma</u>” for specific features of various melanoma subtypes.




### ABCDE criteria [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZbWZHP0)




The following <u>skin</u> lesion characteristics are concerning for melanoma:




* A = Asymmetry

* B = Border (irregular border with indistinct margins)

* C = Color (variegated pigmentation within the same lesion)

* D = Diameter > 6 mm

* E = Evolving (a lesion that changes in size, shape, or color over time)

## Subtypes and variants

### By site of origin [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B4Wzll0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UHWbp50)




* Cutaneous melanoma originates from <u>melanocytes</u> in the <u>epidermis</u>.

* Noncutaneous melanoma (much less common)

* Mucosal melanoma originates from <u>melanocytes</u> in <u>mucosal</u> membranes, most commonly in:

* Head and neck

* Anorectal and female genital regions

* <u>Uveal melanoma</u> originates from <u>melanocytes</u> in the <u>uvea</u>.

#### Amelanotic melanoma [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPWvUl0)




* Any of the other histological subtypes may present; , less commonly, as <u>amelanotic melanoma</u>.

* <u>Melanocytes</u> in the lesion produce little to no pigment, causing the color to range from <u>skin</u>-colored to bright pink or red.   [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XtW9XM0)

* Diagnosis typically requires histological confirmation.

### Genitourinary melanoma (GU melanoma) [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tKcXRW0)




#### Overview




* Rare (< 5%)

* Approx. 45% of all <u>mucosal melanomas</u>

* Most commonly affects female individuals (approx. 90%)

* Cause remains unknown.

* Expression of cell biomarkers such as <u>PD-1</u> and <u>PD-L1</u> in vulvar melanoma is significantly higher than in cutaneous melanomas.

* Prognosis is generally poor.

### Subungual melanoma [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/llWvCl0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YsWnt50)




* Rare subtype of cutaneous <u>acral lentiginous melanoma</u> that develops within the <u>nail</u> unit

* Commonly manifests as <u>melanonychia</u> (a brown to black longitudinal band), with additional features suggestive of <u>malignancy</u>:

* Extension of pigmentation proximally or laterally to involve the <u>nail</u> folds (Hutchinson sign of the nail)

* Width of pigmentation > 3 mm with triangular shape (<u>distal</u> tapering)

* <u>Nail</u> <u>dystrophy</u>, fissuring, or splitting [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lPWvUl0)

* Involvement of a single <u>nail</u>

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZbWZHP0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




* Individuals with suspicious <u>skin</u> lesion(s)

* Assess for <u>clinical features of melanoma</u>.

* Use <u>dermoscopy</u> (if available) to obtain a magnified view of the lesion(s).

* Confirm the diagnosis with a <u>skin biopsy</u> of the lesion.

* Individuals with <u>biopsy</u>-confirmed melanoma: See “<u>Melanoma staging</u>.”





### <u>Skin biopsy</u> [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




* <u>Full-thickness excisional biopsy</u>: preferred for all suspicious lesions [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* The sample should include:

* The full depth of the lesion (i.e., avoid transection of the lesion)

* 1–3 mm <u>surgical margins</u>

* Methods

* Fusiform (elliptical) excision <u>biopsy</u>

* <u>Punch biopsy</u>

* <u>Deep shave biopsy</u>

* Incisional (partial) <u>biopsy</u>

* May be performed for certain lesions (e.g., on the face or digits, or that are large in size)

* If the full thickness of the lesion is not captured on <u>biopsy</u>, accurate <u>melanoma staging</u> may not be possible.





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Excisional biopsy</u> of suspected melanoma should encompass the full depth of the lesion, as lesion depth is important for staging and prognostic purposes. [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




> [!TIP]
> Clearly document the lesion dimensions, size of <u>surgical margins</u>, and type of <u>biopsy</u> to guide subsequent management. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZbWZHP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

## Pathology

* Atypical <u>melanocytes</u> may be:  [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3OWSHl0)

* <u>Epithelioid cells</u> with large eosinophilic nucleoli and eosinophilic <u>cytoplasm</u>

* <u>Hyperchromatic</u>, <u>spindle-shaped cells</u>

* Negative prognostic features

* Increased Breslow depth (thickness from the <u>stratum granulosum</u> to the lowest detectable <u>tumor</u> cell, which correlates with the risk of <u>metastasis</u>)  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* <u>Ulceration</u>

* High <u>mitotic rate</u>

* <u>Melanoma microsatellitosis</u>

* Malignant cells at the margins of the <u>biopsy</u> specimen

* Specialized studies: may be obtained in select cases.

* Atypical lesions : to confirm the diagnosis (e.g., by testing for expression of <u>s100-protein</u>, a <u>tumor marker</u> for melanoma) [[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0fWekk0)

* <u>Metastatic</u> disease: to help determine treatment choices (e.g., by testing for <u>BRAF</u> mutations)

## Staging

### Determination of stage




* Perform a comprehensive clinical evaluation, including:

* History of recent systemic symptoms (e.g., weight loss) or <u>headaches</u>

* Total body <u>skin examination</u> for other suspicious lesions

* <u>Lymph node examination</u> in the areas surrounding the melanoma

* Obtain additional studies as needed for <u>melanoma staging</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Sentinel lymph node biopsy</u>

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* Imaging





#### <u>Sentinel lymph node biopsy</u> (<u>SLNB</u>) [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




<u>SLNB</u> is recommended in certain patients, after careful discussion of risks and benefits and with the involvement of surgical oncology.




* Offer <u>SLNB</u> for <u>cutaneous melanoma</u>  ≥ 1 mm in depth.

* Consider <u>SLNB</u> for <u>cutaneous melanoma</u> that is:

* 0.8–1.0 mm in depth

* < 0.8 mm in depth with concerning features  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>SLNB</u> is not indicated in patients with <u>cutaneous melanoma</u> < 0.8 mm in depth without <u>ulceration</u> or other concerning features. [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




#### Additional studies [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




* Not routinely recommended in asymptomatic patients with <u>stage II melanoma</u> or lower

* Indicated to evaluate for <u>metastasis</u> in select patients, e.g., with:

* Signs or symptoms of <u>metastatic</u> disease

* Positive <u>SLNB</u>

* May include:

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>, e.g., <u>CBC</u>, <u>BMP</u>, <u>liver chemistries</u>

* Imaging, e.g.:

* CT or <u>PET-CT</u> of the chest, abdomen, and <u>pelvis</u>

* <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u>

* <u>Lymph node</u> US

* <u>Bone scintigraphy</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>LDH</u> is not used to evaluate for <u>metastasis</u>, but may be used as a prognostic indicator in patients with <u>stage IV melanoma</u>. [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

## Treatment

### Approach [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




* Establish <u>multidisciplinary cancer care</u>, including referrals to specialists (e.g., dermatology, <u>surgery</u>, oncology, and/or radiation oncology) for:

* Complete surgical excision of the cutaneous lesion, if possible  [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lOWvsl0)

* Consideration of additional <u>medical therapy for melanoma</u> for specific indications, e.g., <u>metastatic</u> or unresectable disease

* Ensure regular <u>follow-up care for melanoma</u> based on disease stage.

* Educate patients on: [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HOWKFl0)

* Self-examination for new or changing <u>skin</u> lesions and/or <u>lymphadenopathy</u>

* <u>Photoprotective measures</u>

* Increased risk of melanoma in family members





### <u>Wide local excision</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mWdVNK0)




* Indicated for nearly all patients

* Excision should be performed with <u>surgical margins</u> determined by the <u>Breslow depth</u>.





| Recommended <u>surgical margins</u> for invasive melanoma resection |  |
| --- | --- |
| <u>Breslow depth</u> | <u>Surgical margin</u> |
| <u>Tumor in situ</u> | <br>* 0.5–1 cm<br><br> |
| ≤ 1 mm | <br>* 1 cm<br><br> |
| > 1 mm and ≤ 2 mm | <br>* 1–2 cm<br><br> |
| > 2 mm | <br>* 2 cm<br><br> |





> [!TIP]
> First-line treatment for melanoma is prompt, complete excision of the <u>tumor</u>. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZbWZHP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




> [!TIP]
> Tissue-sparing <u>Mohs micrographic surgery</u> may alternatively be considered for <u>lentigo maligna</u> on certain areas of the head and neck (e.g., face, <u>ears</u>, or scalp). [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mWdVNK0)




### Medical therapy for melanoma [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)[[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8YWOIP0)




#### Systemic drug therapy




* Indications

* Complete surgical resection not possible

* <u>Stage IV melanoma</u>

* <u>Adjuvant therapy</u> for <u>stage III melanoma</u>

* Regimens may include either or both of the following therapies:

* <u>Immune checkpoint inhibitor</u> combination therapy, e.g.:

* Anti-<u>CTLA-4</u> <u>monoclonal antibodies</u> (<u>ipilimumab</u>)

* PLUS anti <u>PD-1</u> <u>monoclonal antibodies</u> (<u>nivolumab</u> or <u>pembrolizumab</u>)

* Targeted combination therapy in patients with <u>BRAF</u> mutations, e.g.:

* <u>BRAF kinase inhibitors</u> (e.g., <u>vemurafenib</u>; , <u>dabrafenib</u>)

* PLUS <u>MEK kinase inhibitors</u> (e.g., <u>trametinib</u>, cobimetinib)  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)

* Adverse effects

* More common with <u>immune checkpoint inhibitor</u> therapy (see “<u>Adverse effects of immunosuppressants</u>”)

* Management may include: [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0bWeHP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* Dosing adjustments

* Topical or oral <u>glucocorticoids</u>

* <u>Antihistamines</u>





#### Other therapies




* <u>Lentigo maligna</u> that cannot be resected  may be treated with nonsurgical options such as: [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NWd-lK0)[[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lWdvlK0)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5WdiNK0)

* Topical <u>imiquimod</u>

* <u>Radiation therapy</u>

* <u>Cryotherapy</u>, <u>laser ablation</u> (limited evidence)

* <u>Expectant management</u> may be considered in individuals with limited <u>life expectancy</u>.

* <u>Metastatic</u> disease may be treated with <u>radiation therapy</u> for <u>palliation</u> or if systemic therapy cannot be used. [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lGWvz50)





### Follow-up care for melanoma  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)




#### Dermatologic examinations




* All patients should have lifelong follow-up with a dermatologist, focusing on: 

* Monitoring for melanoma recurrence

* Screening for new primary melanoma

* Tailor frequency of visits to disease stage, e.g., as shown in the table below.





| <br>Follow-up dermatologic examination schedule for <u>cutaneous melanoma</u> [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)<br> |  |
| --- | --- |
| Disease stage | Recommendation |
| <u>Stage 0 melanoma</u> | <br><br>* Every 6–12 months for 1–2 years<br><br>* Then annually<br><br><br> |
| <u>Stage I melanoma</u> and <u>stage IIA melanoma</u> | <br><br>* Every 6–12 months for 2–5 years<br><br>* Then annually<br><br><br> |
| <u>Stage IIB melanoma</u> and higher | <br><br>* Every 3–6 months for 2 years<br><br>* Then every 6 months for 3–5 years<br><br>* Then annually<br><br><br> |





#### Surveillance imaging




Surveillance imaging to detect occult <u>metastasis</u> may be performed in select patients for up to 3–5 years after diagnosis.




* <u>Lymph node ultrasound</u> surveillance is recommended if:

* <u>SLNB</u> is positive  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* <u>SLNB</u> was indicated but not performed  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* Other modalities

* May be considered in asymptomatic patients with <u>stage IIB melanoma</u> or higher  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* May include:

* <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u>

* CT or <u>PET-CT</u> of the chest, abdomen, and <u>pelvis</u>

* <u>Chest x-ray</u> (less preferred)





> [!TIP]
> Patients with positive <u>SLNB</u> (i.e., <u>stage III melanoma</u>) are now typically managed with <u>lymph node ultrasound</u> surveillance instead of complete <u>lymph node dissection</u>. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wYWhrP0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

## Complications

### <u>Metastatic</u> disease [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NcY-cL)




* <u>Metastatic</u> melanoma may spread to <u>lymph nodes</u>, <u>liver</u>, <u>lung</u>, <u>brain</u>, and bone

* May also <u>metastasize</u> to locations not typically affected by <u>metastatic</u> malignancies [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lcYvcL)

* Up to 5% of <u>metastatic</u> cases have no known primary <u>tumor</u>.

* Characteristic black pigmentation

## Prognosis

* Negative prognostic features

* Increased <u>Breslow depth</u> (thickness from the <u>stratum granulosum</u> to the lowest detectable <u>tumor</u> cell, which correlates with the risk of <u>metastasis</u>) [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjcXXc0)

* <u>Ulceration</u>

* High <u>mitotic rate</u>

* <u>Melanoma microsatellitosis</u>

* Malignant cells at the margins of the <u>biopsy</u> specimen




> [!TIP]
> <u>Lentigo maligna</u>, the premalignant lesion of <u>lentigo maligna melanoma</u>, has excellent prognosis if treated appropriately. [[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5WdiNK0)
