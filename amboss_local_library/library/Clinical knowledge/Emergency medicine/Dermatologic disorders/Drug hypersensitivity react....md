## Summary

Drug <u>hypersensitivity reactions</u> (DHR) are a group of adverse drug effects that resemble an <u>allergy</u>. They are relatively common, and may be classified by underlying pathophysiology (allergic DHR vs nonallergic DHR), or by onset of symptoms (<u>immediate DHR</u> vs <u>non-immediate DHR</u>). Clinical features can vary widely. Diagnosis is typically clinical but there are several methods available for <u>confirmatory testing</u>. Management depends on the underlying mechanism and clinical syndrome suspected, but all patients should stop the suspected offending drug and be provided with <u>supportive care</u>. <u>Severe cutaneous adverse reactions</u> (SCAR) refers to a group of four type IV DHRs that are associated with significant <u>morbidity</u> and mortality, including <u>DRESS</u>, <u>Stevens-Johnson syndrome</u> (<u>SJS</u>), <u>toxic epidermal necrolysis</u> (TEN), and <u>acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis</u> (<u>AGEP</u>), all of which require prompt evaluation and management.
