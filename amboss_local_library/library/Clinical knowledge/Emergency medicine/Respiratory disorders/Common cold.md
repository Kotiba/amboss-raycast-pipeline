# Common cold

---

## Summary

A common cold is a nonspecific, acute, <u>self-limited</u> infection of the <u>upper respiratory tract</u>. It more commonly occurs in children and most infections occur during fall and winter. Common cold is a viral infection of the <u>upper respiratory tract</u>, causing <u>rhinorrhea</u>, nasal obstruction, and <u>coughing</u>. Diagnosis is usually clinical and treatment is mostly symptomatic. The most important complication of common cold is secondary bacterial infection (e.g., <u>pneumonia</u>).

---

## Definitions

A nonspecific, acute, <u>self-limited</u> viral infection of the <u>upper respiratory tract</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

---

## Epidemiology

* Leading cause of ambulatory care visits in the US [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* More common in children  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VvcG_V0)

* Seasonal pattern: Most infections occur during the fall and winter.




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

---

## Etiology

* Viral infection [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ROalHk)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/evcx_V0)

* <u>Rhinovirus</u> (most common)

* Other <u>viruses</u>: <u>parainfluenza</u>, <u>influenza</u>, <u>coronavirus</u>, <u>adenovirus</u>, <u>respiratory syncytial virus</u>

* Transmission: direct contact, aerosols, and respiratory droplets

---

## Clinical features

* Main symptoms: <u>rhinorrhea</u>, nasal obstruction, sneezing, and <u>coughing</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ROalHk)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/evcx_V0)

* Symptoms typically resolve after 1 week.

* <u>Cough</u> may persist for 3 weeks.

* Other symptoms: <u>sore throat</u>, <u>hoarseness</u>, <u>fever</u> (uncommon), <u>malaise</u>, and fatigue

* <u>Physical examination</u> finding

* <u>Lungs</u> are usually clear to <u>auscultation</u>

* Typically no prominent localizing features (e.g., facial pressure )

---

## Diagnosis

* <u>Clinical diagnosis</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ROalHk)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/evcx_V0)

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>, viral detection techniques (e.g., <u>antigen</u> detection, <u>PCR</u> assays), and imaging are not routinely recommended.

---

## Treatment

* Symptomatic treatment: hydration, <u>analgesics</u> (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>NSAIDs</u>), and oral and/or topical decongestants (e.g., <u>oxymetazoline</u>, <u>chlorpheniramine</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ROalHk)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/evcx_V0)

* <u>Antibiotics</u> are not indicated unless a secondary bacterial infection is suspected.

---

## Complications

* Extension of the viral infection to surrounding structures (e.g., <u>ear</u>, sinuses, <u>larynx</u>)

* Secondary bacterial infection, i.e., <u>pneumonia</u> (mostly due to <u>S. pneumoniae</u>, <u>H. influenzae</u>, <u>M. catarrhalis</u>, <u>S. aureus</u>)

* Exacerbation of <u>asthma</u> and/or <u>chronic bronchitis</u>




We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.
