# Acquired tracheoesophageal fistula

## Summary

Acquired <u>tracheoesophageal fistula</u> (<u>TEF</u>) is an abnormal connection between the <u>trachea</u> and <u>esophagus</u> caused by another condition (e.g., <u>malignancy</u>, trauma). Acquired <u>TEF</u> typically manifests with <u>cough</u>, <u>dysphagia</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>, and/or recurrent <u>lung</u> infections. <u>Barium esophagogram</u> and/or endoscopy confirm the diagnosis. Treatment involves a multidisciplinary team focusing on preventing <u>aspiration</u>, managing the underlying cause, and closing the <u>fistula</u> via <u>surgery</u> or endoscopy.




See also “<u>Congenital tracheoesophageal fistula</u>.”

---

## Etiology

* <u>Malignancy</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)

* <u>Esophageal cancer</u> (most common malignant cause) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)

* <u>Lung cancer</u>

* Prolonged <u>endotracheal intubation</u> or <u>tracheostomy</u>

* Excessive cuff pressure of <u>tracheostomy</u> or <u>endotracheal tube</u>

* <u>Mediastinal</u> <u>surgery</u> (e.g., <u>esophagectomy</u>)

* Esophageal or <u>airway</u> <u>stent</u> <u>erosion</u>

* Trauma (e.g., <u>penetrating neck injury</u>)

* Infection (e.g., <u>tuberculosis</u>, <u>histoplasmosis</u>, <u>actinomycosis</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IJdYvI0)

* Ingestion (e.g., <u>foreign body ingestion</u>, <u>caustic ingestion</u>)

* Inflammatory diseases (e.g., <u>rheumatoid arthritis</u>, <u>inflammatory bowel disease</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IJdYvI0)

## Clinical features

Clinical features vary based on size, location, and rate of formation of the <u>fistula</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)




* Nonventilated patients [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IJdYvI0)

* <u>Cough</u>

* <u>Dysphagia</u>

* <u>Dyspnea</u>

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Chest pain</u>

* Ventilated patients [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)

* Air leak despite inflated cuff

* Inability to <u>wean off ventilator</u>

* Gastric distention

* Symptoms of underlying cause

* <u>Symptoms of esophageal cancer</u>

* <u>Symptoms of lung cancer</u>

* Symptoms of complications [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IJdYvI0)

* Recurrent <u>aspiration pneumonia</u>

* <u>Malnutrition</u>

---

## Diagnosis

* Confirm the diagnosis with <u>barium esophagram</u> and/or endoscopy (i.e., <u>esophagoscopy</u>, <u>bronchoscopy</u>). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)

* Obtain additional diagnostics under expert guidance to guide treatment, e.g.: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QrduSr0)

* CT chest

* <u>Esophagoscopy</u>

* <u>Bronchoscopy</u>
