## Summary

<u>Respiratory distress</u> and <u>respiratory failure</u> are common manifestations of critical illness in children. The initial evaluation should include <u>pediatric vital signs</u> assessment based on age, especially since respiratory rates vary between <u>neonates</u>, <u>infants</u>, <u>toddlers</u>, <u>preschoolers</u>, <u>school-aged children</u>, and <u>adolescents</u>. Child-specific clinical <u>signs of respiratory distress</u> include <u>retractions</u>, <u>grunting</u>, and <u>nasal flaring</u>. In patients with undifferentiated <u>respiratory distress</u> or failure, it is helpful to initially categorize the illness into one of four common <u>phenotypes</u> based on clinical presentation: <u>upper airway</u> obstruction (e.g., <u>stridor</u>), <u>lower airway</u> obstruction (e.g., <u>wheezing</u>), <u>parenchymal</u> <u>lung</u> disease (e.g., <u>crackles</u>), or disordered breathing control (e.g., irregular or absent respirations). Notable underlying causes include <u>croup</u>, <u>foreign body aspiration</u> (<u>FBA</u>), <u>anaphylaxis</u>, <u>bronchiolitis</u>, <u>asthma exacerbation</u>, <u>pneumonia</u>, <u>pulmonary edema</u>, <u>acute respiratory distress syndrome</u> (<u>ARDS</u>), <u>seizures</u>, <u>traumatic brain injury</u> (<u>TBI</u>), <u>poisoning</u>, and neuromuscular conditions.




Initial <u>respiratory support</u> must begin in tandem with clinical and diagnostic evaluation. Bedside tests such as <u>pulse oximetry</u>, <u>end-tidal CO<sub>2</sub></u> (<u>EtCO<sub>2</sub></u>) monitoring, portable <u>chest x-ray</u> (<u>CXR</u>), <u>lung POCUS</u>, and <u>blood gas analysis</u> are used to determine the severity of respiratory compromise and rapidly identify underlying conditions that cannot be <u>diagnosed clinically</u>. Management includes <u>pediatric airway management</u>, <u>oxygen therapy for children</u>, emergency therapies (e.g., <u>naloxone</u>, <u>epinephrine</u>, <u>bronchodilators</u>, <u>maneuvers to dislodge an aspirated foreign body</u>), and <u>mechanical ventilation in children</u>. These treatments may be integrated into broader management or resuscitation algorithms for children and require specialized approaches, equipment, and/or expertise. <u>Neonates</u> require dedicated resuscitation protocols and <u>respiratory support</u> strategies due to their unique physiology.




See also “<u>Airway obstruction</u>” and “<u>Wheezing</u>.” For adults, see “<u>Respiratory failure and arrest</u>” and “<u>Dyspnea</u>.”
