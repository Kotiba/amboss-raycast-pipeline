## Summary

Tinnitus is a common symptom in which sound (whistling, hissing, buzzing, ringing, pulsating) is perceived in the absence of an external source. Tinnitus can be unilateral or bilateral, acute or chronic, intermittent or constant, and is often but not always associated with <u>hearing loss</u>. <u>Primary tinnitus</u> is <u>idiopathic</u>, while <u>secondary tinnitus</u> results from an underlying cause such as noise exposure, infection, trauma, <u>turbulent</u> vascular flow, <u>ototoxicity</u>, or <u>tumor</u>. <u>Clinical evaluation for tinnitus</u> includes assessment of tinnitus characteristics, associated symptoms, and <u>risk factors for tinnitus</u>, as well as an ENT, neck, cardiac, and <u>neurological examination</u>. Patients with <u>tinnitus red flag features</u> require prompt further evaluation. Audiological examination is recommended for most patients who require testing, while imaging is reserved for patients with unilateral tinnitus, asymmetric <u>hearing loss</u>, <u>pulsatile tinnitus</u>, and/or <u>focal neurological deficits</u>. The management of <u>secondary tinnitus</u> focuses on treating the underlying cause. <u>Symptomatic therapy for tinnitus</u> is typically indicated for <u>persistent bothersome tinnitus</u>, while patient education is usually sufficient for <u>nonbothersome tinnitus</u>. Symptomatic therapy includes <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u> (<u>CBT</u>), <u>hearing aids</u> for concomitant <u>hearing loss</u>, and sound therapy. Management may also involve treating <u>risk factors</u> and comorbidities, including addressing the effects of tinnitus on <u>sleep</u>, mental health, and <u>cognition</u>. Prognosis depends on the underlying etiology. Tinnitus is more likely to be <u>self-limited</u> in younger patients with a short duration of symptoms.

## Definitions

* Tinnitus: perceived sound (e.g., whistling, hissing, buzzing, ringing, pulsating) without an external source

* Subjective tinnitus: only heard by the affected individual

* Objective tinnitus: can be heard by an examiner





> [!TIP]
> Objective tinnitus is caused by sounds originating from within the body such as <u>carotid bruits</u>, <u>cervical venous hum</u>, <u>heart murmurs</u>, or musculoskeletal sounds (e.g., due to <u>stapedial myoclonus</u>). [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)

## Etiology

Primary tinnitus is <u>idiopathic</u> and may be associated with <u>sensorineural hearing loss</u> (<u>SNHL</u>). The following are causes of secondary tinnitus, i.e., tinnitus provoked by an identifiable underlying condition. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZPdZWJ0)




### Otologic




* Infectious or inflammatory

* <u>Acute otitis media</u> (<u>AOM</u>)

* <u>Labyrinthitis</u>

* <u>Chronic suppurative otitis media</u>

* <u>Otitis media with effusion</u>

* Traumatic

* <u>Perforated tympanic membrane</u>

* <u>Ear barotrauma</u>

* <u>Acoustic trauma</u>

* Structural

* <u>Cerumen impaction</u>

* <u>Foreign body in the ear</u>

* <u>Cholesteatoma</u>

* <u>Eustachian tube dysfunction</u>

* <u>Idiopathic</u> or degenerative

* <u>Meniere disease</u>

* <u>Sudden sensorineural hearing loss</u> (<u>SSNHL</u>)

* <u>Presbycusis</u>

* <u>Otosclerosis</u>





### Neurologic




* Infectious

* <u>Meningitis</u>

* <u>Syphilis</u>

* <u>Lyme disease</u>

* Inflammatory

* <u>Multiple sclerosis</u>

* <u>Systemic lupus erythematosus</u>

* <u>Sarcoidosis</u>

* Other

* <u>Stroke</u>

* <u>Elevated intracranial pressure</u>

* Muscular <u>myoclonus</u> or spasm (e.g., of the <u>stapedius muscle</u>, <u>tensor tympani muscle</u>, <u>muscles of the palate</u>)

* <u>Chiari I malformation</u>

* <u>Vestibular migraine</u>





### <u>Neoplastic</u>




* <u>Acoustic neuroma</u>

* <u>Meningioma</u>

* Vascular tumors, e.g., <u>hemangioma</u>, <u>paraganglioma of the middle ear</u>





### Vascular




* <u>Carotid artery stenosis</u>

* <u>Arteriovenous malformation</u>

* <u>Cervical venous hum</u>

* <u>High-output heart failure</u>

* <u>Heart murmurs</u>





### Musculoskeletal




* <u>Temporomandibular joint disorder</u> (<u>TMJD</u>)

* <u>Paget disease of bone</u>

* <u>Head injuries</u>, e.g., <u>temporal bone fracture</u>





### Metabolic  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)




* <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency</u>

* <u>Hyperlipidemia</u>

* <u>Diabetes mellitus</u>

* <u>Thyroid disease</u>





### Ototoxic substances [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)




Examples of common <u>ototoxic substances</u> are listed below.




* <u>Antibiotics</u>: e.g., <u>aminoglycosides</u>, <u>macrolides</u>, <u>tetracyclines</u>, <u>vancomycin</u>

* <u>NSAIDs</u>: e.g., <u>aspirin</u> (<u>salicylate toxicity</u>), <u>ibuprofen</u>

* <u>Chemotherapeutic agents</u>: e.g., <u>platinum-based chemotherapy</u>, <u>taxanes</u>

* <u>Loop diuretics</u>: e.g., <u>furosemide</u>

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u>: e.g., <u>carbamazepine</u>, <u>pregabalin</u>

* <u>Immunosuppressants</u>: e.g., <u>cyclosporin</u>

* Other medications: <u>atorvastatin</u>, <u>bupropion</u>, <u>hydroxychloroquine</u>, <u>PDE-5 inhibitors</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ARdRpp0)

* Environmental or industrial toxins: <u>mercury</u>, <u>arsenic</u>, <u>toluene</u>, <u>carbon monoxide</u>

* <u>Vaccinations</u>: <u>pneumococcal vaccine</u>, <u>HPV vaccine</u>

## Clinical evaluation

### Focused history [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)




* Onset, duration, and effect on quality of life: Consider using a validated tinnitus questionnaire to quantify symptom severity. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)

* Recent-onset tinnitus: < 6 months

* Nonbothersome tinnitus: noticeable but tolerable; patients typically seek reassurance.

* Bothersome tinnitus 

* Negatively impacts quality of life and/or functioning

* Patients seek relief from tinnitus and associated <u>sleep</u>, mental health, or cognitive disturbances.

* Considered persistent if present for ≥ 6 months

* Tinnitus characteristics: laterality, symmetry, pulsatility, <u>sound frequency</u>, quality (e.g., ringing, hissing, clicking)

* Unilateral or asymmetric tinnitus suggests a focal auditory lesion, e.g., <u>acoustic neuroma</u>.

* <u>Pulsatile tinnitus</u> suggests a vascular etiology, e.g., <u>carotid bruits</u>, <u>heart murmurs</u>.

* Roaring tinnitus suggests <u>Meniere disease</u>.

* Rhythmic clicking tinnitus suggests <u>stapedial myoclonus</u> or <u>tensor tympani</u> <u>myoclonus</u>.

* Ringing, buzzing, or hissing tinnitus are characteristic of <u>primary tinnitus</u>.

* Related symptoms

* Auditory or vestibular disturbances, e.g., <u>hearing loss</u>, <u>vertigo</u>

* Infectious symptoms, e.g., <u>fever</u>, <u>ear</u> <u>pain</u>, congestion, <u>otorrhea</u>

* <u>Focal neurological symptoms</u>, <u>headaches</u>

* <u>Auditory hallucinations</u> (can mimic tinnitus)

* Exposures: <u>ototoxic substances</u> and other <u>risk factors for tinnitus</u>





### Focused <u>physical examination</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)




Identify abnormalities such as neurological deficits, vascular <u>bruits</u>, or head and neck lesions.




* <u>HEENT examination</u>

* <u>Ear examination</u> including <u>otoscopy</u>

* <u>Focused hearing examination</u>

* <u>Eye examination</u>, e.g., <u>fundoscopy</u>, <u>visual field testing</u>

* Examination of the <u>TMJ</u>

* <u>Red flag findings on head and neck examination</u>, e.g., palpable masses

* <u>Neurological examination</u>

* <u>Cranial nerve examination</u>

* <u>Cerebellar</u> examination, e.g., <u>Romberg test</u>, <u>nystagmus</u>, <u>gait assessment</u>

* Vestibular examination, e.g., <u>HINTS examination</u>

* <u>Mental status examination</u>

* <u>Cardiovascular examination</u>: e.g., <u>auscultation</u> for <u>bruits</u> or <u>murmurs</u>, especially if tinnitus is pulsatile

* <u>Cognitive assessment</u>: if indicated





### Red flags in tinnitus




Tinnitus with any of the following <u>red flag features</u> requires prompt workup for a potentially concerning underlying cause.




* Unilateral tinnitus

* <u>Pulsatile tinnitus</u>

* Sudden or unilateral <u>hearing loss</u>

* <u>Focal neurological deficits</u>

## Treatment

### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PPdWUJ0)




* Identify and address causes of <u>secondary tinnitus</u>, e.g., discontinue <u>ototoxic medications</u>.

* Initiate treatment for underlying <u>SSNHL</u> within 2 weeks of symptom onset. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5g1iDT0)

* Provide patient education to all patients.

* Offer <u>tinnitus symptom management</u> to patients with <u>persistent bothersome tinnitus</u>.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)

* Treat comorbidities and address reversible <u>risk factors for tinnitus</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> Avoid expensive or time-consuming interventions for recent-onset <u>primary tinnitus</u> (< 6 months), as the rates of spontaneous resolution are high. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)




### Patient education [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PPdWUJ0)




* Tinnitus causes and treatment options

* The <u>natural history and prognosis of tinnitus</u>

* Avoidance of loud noise exposure

* Management of individual <u>risk factors for tinnitus</u>

* See “Patient Education Discussion Points for Bothersome Tinnitus” in “Tips & Links” for details.





### Symptomatic therapy for tinnitus [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PPdWUJ0)




* The following applies to patients with <u>persistent bothersome tinnitus</u> and may be appropriate for other patients on a case-by-case basis : [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)

* Offer <u>CBT</u> to all patients.  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZidZJp0)

* Refer patients with <u>hearing loss</u> for <u>hearing</u>-aid evaluation.

* Consider sound therapy, e.g., acoustic stimulators, <u>hearing aids</u>, ambient sound generators.   [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0ideJp0)

* Consider tinnitus retraining therapy.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4Pd3UJ0)

* Avoid the following : [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NRd-Mp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aidQJp0)

* Pharmacological therapy (e.g., <u>antidepressants</u>, <u>anxiolytics</u>, <u>anticonvulsants</u>, betahistine) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_Rd5pp0)

* Dietary supplements (e.g., <u>zinc</u>, <u>Ginkgo biloba</u>, <u>melatonin</u>) [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zRdrpp0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-RdDpp0)

* <u>Transcranial magnetic stimulation</u>

* <u>Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation</u>

* <u>Hyperbaric oxygen therapy</u>

* <u>Acupuncture</u>





### Treatment of comorbidities




* <u>Treatment of depression</u>

* <u>Treatment of anxiety disorders</u>

* <u>Treatment of dementia</u>

* <u>Behavioral and cognitive therapies for insomnia</u> and <u>pharmacological therapy for insomnia</u>
