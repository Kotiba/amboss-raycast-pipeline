## Summary

Neck <u>pain</u> is a common symptom that can occur in a wide variety of conditions, ranging from benign musculoskeletal problems (e.g., <u>neck sprain</u>) to life-threatening neurological or vascular emergencies (e.g., <u>compressive spinal emergencies</u>, <u>myocardial infarction</u>). Initial management involves stabilizing the <u>spine</u> in case of <u>C-spine injury</u> and excluding <u>immediately life-threatening causes of neck pain</u>. Further clinical evaluation includes a focused <u>medical history</u> to evaluate for <u>red flags</u> (e.g., chronic inflammatory conditions, <u>malignancy</u>) and a focused <u>spine examination</u> and <u>neurological examination</u>. Diagnostic testing is guided by clinical evaluation findings and may include imaging and <u>laboratory studies</u>. Management depends on the underlying cause and symptom severity. Emergency surgical management is indicated in some <u>immediately life-threatening causes of neck pain</u> (e.g., <u>vertebral fractures</u>, <u>compressive spinal emergencies</u>, <u>aortic dissection</u>). Symptomatic treatment for non-life-threatening causes of neck <u>pain</u> involves <u>analgesia</u> and <u>physical therapy</u>. Referral for <u>surgery</u> can be considered in patients with chronic neck <u>pain</u> refractory to <u>conservative treatment</u>.
