# Approach to the poisoned patient

## Summary

This article provides a general approach to patients with possible acute <u>poisoning</u> due to potentially toxic substances, including medications, <u>recreational drugs</u>, and environmental substances. Although few poisonings are fatal, acute <u>poisoning</u> can be a challenging <u>medical emergency</u>. Toxicity depends on exposure characteristics (e.g., substance type, route, dosage, timing) and patient characteristics (e.g., age and comorbidities). Management is guided by a <u>toxicological risk assessment</u> based on the <u>clinical evaluation of poisoning</u> and individualized diagnostics and monitoring. Initial management of critically ill patients should follow an <u>ABCDE approach adapted to poisoning</u>, which includes rapid identification of <u>classic toxidromes</u>, acute stabilization, and, if indicated, early decontamination (e.g., <u>activated charcoal</u>), <u>enhanced elimination</u> (e.g., <u>hemodialysis</u>), and <u>antidote</u> administration (e.g., <u>naloxone for opioid overdose</u>). Consultation with a toxicologist or regional poison control center (in the United States, Poison Control is available 24/7 at 1-800-222-1222) is required in most cases. Admission and frequent reevaluation are often necessary due to altered substance <u>pharmacokinetics</u>, dynamic clinical manifestations, and the risk of delayed complications.

## Classic toxidromes

Recognition of <u>classic toxidromes</u> is essential to the evaluation of patients poisoned with an unknown substance. Note that, in practice, <u>toxidromes</u> may manifest more subtly than what is described here.

> [!TIP]
