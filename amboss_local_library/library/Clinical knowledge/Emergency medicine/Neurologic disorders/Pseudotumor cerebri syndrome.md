# Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Neurologic disorders > Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/u30p4f)

---

## Summary

Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome is a condition characterized by <u>elevated intracranial pressure</u> (<u>ICP</u>) and normal <u>cerebrospinal fluid</u> (<u>CSF</u>) composition, with no evidence of an intracranial mass, <u>hydrocephalus</u>, or other structural causes on imaging. <u>Primary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>, also known as <u>idiopathic intracranial hypertension</u>, predominantly occurs in women of reproductive age with <u>obesity</u>. <u>Secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u> includes <u>drug-induced intracranial hypertension</u> and <u>elevated ICP</u> due to cerebral venous abnormalities or systemic disorders. The most common symptom is <u>headache</u>, but visual disturbances and <u>pulsatile tinnitus</u> also frequently occur. Ophthalmologic examination typically shows bilateral <u>papilledema</u>. <u>MRI</u> is performed to rule out <u>mass effect</u> and evaluate for structural <u>causes of elevated ICP</u>. <u>Lumbar puncture</u> shows an elevated <u>opening pressure</u>. Treatment includes weight loss, addressing any causes of <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>, and, in some cases, pharmacological treatment (e.g., with <u>acetazolamide</u>). Surgical intervention is indicated for patients with <u>vision loss</u> on presentation or <u>vision</u>-threatening disease refractory to <u>conservative measures</u>. Despite treatment, persistent symptoms and <u>vision loss</u> are common.




For other causes of <u>intracranial hypertension</u>, see "<u>Elevated intracranial pressure and brain herniation</u>."

## Etiology

* Primary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome (<u>idiopathic intracranial hypertension</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YbVnHG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UBdbZs0)

* Predominantly affects women of reproductive age with <u>obesity</u>

* Additional <u>risk factors</u> include recent weight gain and <u>polycystic ovary syndrome</u>.

* Secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UBdbZs0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)

* Medications (<u>drug-induced intracranial hypertension</u>): e.g., <u>tetracyclines</u>, <u>vitamin A toxicity</u>, <u>retinoids</u>, <u>growth hormone</u>, <u>danazol</u>

* Cerebral venous disorders: e.g., <u>cerebral venous thrombosis</u>, decreased <u>CSF</u> resorption due to prior <u>CNS</u> infection or <u>subarachnoid hemorrhage</u>

* Systemic disorders: e.g., <u>anemia</u>, <u>Addison disease</u>, <u>hypercapnia</u>, <u>renal failure</u>, <u>systemic lupus erythematosus</u>





> [!NOTE]
> A female TOAD: female sex, Tetracyclines, Obesity, excessive intake of <u>vitamin</u> A, and Danazol are the major <u>risk factors</u> for pseudotumor cerebri syndrome.

## Pathophysiology

* A mismatch between production and resorption of <u>CSF</u> → <u>↑ ICP</u> → damage to structures of the <u>CNS</u> and especially to the optical nerve fibers [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YbVnHG0)

* Orthograde axoplasmic flow stasis at the <u>optic nerve head</u> leads to bilateral <u>papilledema</u>.

## Clinical features

Most presentations are subacute or chronic, but a minority of patients (< 5%) present with rapidly progressive <u>vision loss</u> over days to weeks. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2BdTZs0)




* <u>Headache</u>

* Visual changes

* Transient visual obscuration (darkening of <u>vision</u>)

* <u>Vision loss</u>

* <u>Diplopia</u>

* <u>Photopsia</u>

* Retrobulbar <u>pain</u>

* <u>Pulsatile tinnitus</u>

* <u>Cranial nerve palsies</u> (most commonly <u>CN VI palsy</u>)

* <u>Dizziness</u>

* Neck and/or <u>back pain</u>  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LGXw-z)

* Cognitive disturbance

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YbVnHG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




* Perform a <u>fundoscopic examination</u> to evaluate for <u>papilledema</u>.

* Assess <u>visual acuity</u> and evaluate for peripheral <u>vision loss</u>.

* Obtain <u>brain</u> imaging before <u>lumbar puncture</u> to rule out <u>mass effect</u> and to evaluate for structural <u>causes of elevated ICP</u>.

* Perform a <u>lumbar puncture</u> to confirm <u>elevated ICP</u> and obtain a <u>CSF</u> sample for analysis.

* Evaluate for <u>diagnostic criteria for pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>.

* Consider <u>laboratory studies</u> to evaluate for causes of <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>.





### Diagnostic criteria for pseudotumor cerebri syndrome




#### Revised <u>diagnostic criteria for pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UBdbZs0)




The following diagnostic criteria apply to both <u>primary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u> and <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>.




* Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome: All must be fulfilled.  
1. * <u>Papilledema</u>
2. * Normal <u>neurological examination</u>, except for <u>cranial nerve</u> defects
3. * No <u>hydrocephalus</u>, mass, structural lesions, or meningeal <u>enhancement</u> on <u>brain</u> imaging
4. * Normal <u>CSF analysis</u>
5. * <u>Lumbar puncture opening pressure</u> ≥ 25 cm H₂O

* Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome without <u>papilledema</u>: Both must be fulfilled.
1. * Criteria 2–5 for pseudotumor cerebri syndrome are met
2. * <u>CN VI palsy</u> and/or imaging evidence of pseudotumor cerebri





#### Modified Dandy criteria for idiopathic intracranial hypertension [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/liXv7B)




* Signs and <u>symptoms of increased ICP</u> (e.g., <u>headache</u>, visual changes, <u>pulsatile tinnitus</u>, <u>papilledema</u>)

* Absence of localizing neurologic findings on <u>neurologic examination</u>

* Absence of deformity, displacement, or obstruction of the <u>ventricular system</u> with otherwise normal neurodiagnostic studies, except for:

* Evidence of increased <u>CSF</u> pressure (> 20 cm H<sub>2</sub>O)

* <u>Neuroimaging</u> showing an <u>empty sella</u> turcica, an <u>optic nerve</u> sheath with filled out <u>CSF</u> spaces, and/or smooth-walled nonflow-related venous sinus stenosis or collapse

* Awake and alert

* No other apparent cause of <u>intracranial hypertension</u>





### Ophthalmologic examination [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




* <u>Fundoscopy</u>: bilateral <u>papilledema</u>

* <u>Visual acuity testing</u>

* <u>Visual field testing</u>: may show an enlarged <u>blind spot</u>, ; inferior nasal defects, and/or peripheral <u>vision loss</u>





### <u>Brain</u> imaging [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




<u>Brain</u> imaging is indicated for all patients before <u>lumbar puncture</u> to rule out <u>mass effect</u> and other <u>causes of elevated ICP</u>.




* Imaging modality

* <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u> with and without contrast and <u>MR venography</u>: preferred

* CT head with contrast and CT <u>venography</u>: if <u>MRI is contraindicated</u> or not available

* Imaging findings in pseudotumor cerebri syndrome

* Normal <u>brain</u> <u>parenchyma</u>; no <u>hydrocephalus</u>, mass, structural lesions, or meningeal <u>enhancement</u>

* Empty sella turcica: shrinkage of the <u>pituitary gland</u> due to displacement by <u>CSF</u>

* Flattening of the <u>posterior</u> optic globe, <u>optic nerve</u> changes

* Unilateral or bilateral <u>transverse sinus</u> stenosis: seen in up to 90% of patients with <u>primary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>

* Evidence of a cerebral venous disorder (e.g., <u>sinus vein thrombosis</u>): may be seen in <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>





### <u>Lumbar puncture</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2BdTZs0)




* Procedure

* Only perform after imaging has excluded space-occupying lesions with <u>mass effect</u>, <u>posterior fossa</u> masses, and <u>obstructive hydrocephalus</u>.  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_GY5bI)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kVXmuC)

* Measure <u>opening pressure</u> in the <u>lateral decubitus position</u> with legs extended.

* Obtain <u>CSF</u> samples for analysis.

* Findings

* Normal <u>CSF analysis</u>

* Elevated <u>opening pressure</u> (≥ 25 cm H₂O)

* May provide temporary <u>headache</u> relief





> [!TIP]
> <u>Lumbar puncture</u> is safe to perform in patients with suspected pseudotumor cerebri syndrome once conditions with a risk of <u>brain herniation</u> have been excluded on imaging.




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




Consider <u>laboratory studies</u> to evaluate for suspected causes of <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>, e.g.:




* <u>Complete blood count</u>

* <u>Renal function tests</u>

* <u>Hypercoagulability testing</u>

* Morning <u>cortisol</u>

* <u>Antinuclear antibodies</u>

## Treatment

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




* Urgently consult neurosurgery for rapid visual loss.

* Initiate <u>conservative management</u> of pseudotumor cerebri syndrome.

* Consider pharmacological treatment with <u>acetazolamide</u>.

* Routinely monitor <u>visual acuity</u>, peripheral <u>vision</u>, and <u>papilledema</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2BdTZs0)

* Refer to neurosurgery for <u>vision</u>-threatening disease refractory to other measures.





> [!TIP]
> Pseudotumor cerebri syndrome must be managed in consultation with <u>neurology</u> and ophthalmology (or neuro-ophthalmology).




### <u>Conservative management</u> [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XbV9HG0)




* Address causes of <u>secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome</u>: e.g., discontinue contributing medications.

* <u>Manage overweight and obesity</u>.   [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XbV9HG0)

* Screen for and treat comorbid <u>obstructive sleep apnea</u>. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XbV9HG0)

* Counsel patients on <u>pain management</u> and how to avoid <u>medication overuse headaches</u>.





### Pharmacological treatment [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2BdTZs0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




Evidence for the <u>efficacy</u> of pharmacological treatment is limited. Consider with specialist guidance. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tjVXXu0)




* First-line: <u>acetazolamide</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YbVnHG0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/79d4KH0)

* Alternatives: <u>topiramate</u>, <u>furosemide</u>





### Surgical treatment [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YbVnHG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eBdx-H0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bbVHHG0)




* Indications

* Rapid visual loss on presentation

* <u>Vision</u>-threatening disease refractory to other measures

* Procedures

* <u>CSF</u> shunt (e.g., lumboperitoneal shunt, <u>ventriculoperitoneal shunt</u>)

* Optic nerve sheath fenestration

* <u>Stenting</u> of the <u>dural venous sinus</u>
