## Summary

Medication-overuse <u>headache</u> (MOH) is a chronic <u>secondary headache disorder</u> caused by regular overuse of <u>analgesics</u> that are used to treat a preexisting chronic <u>headache</u> disorder. The most common underlying <u>headache</u> disorders are <u>migraine</u> followed by <u>tension-type headache</u>. Simple <u>analgesics</u> (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>NSAIDs</u>) alone or in combination with <u>caffeine</u>, followed by <u>triptans</u>, are the most commonly overused medications. Women aged 40–49 years are most commonly affected. The pathophysiology is not completely understood but likely involves dependence processes, <u>central sensitization</u>, and (possibly) genetic predisposition. The diagnosis is based on clinical criteria. Diagnostic studies are usually not necessary unless indicated to investigate the cause of the preexisting chronic <u>headache</u>. Treatment involves patient education, <u>weaning</u> of overused medications, management of <u>withdrawal symptoms</u>, and relapse prevention. MOH typically resolves with discontinuation of the overused medications.
