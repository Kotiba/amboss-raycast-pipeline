# Leukopenia

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Hematologic and oncologic disorders > Leukopenia*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9F0N43)

---

## Summary

<u>Leukopenia</u> is a reduction in the <u>white blood cell</u> (<u>WBC</u>) count to < 4,000/mmÂ³. The condition is further classified by the <u>WBC</u> type that is predominantly reduced (i.e., <u>neutrophils</u>, <u>lymphocytes</u>, <u>eosinophils</u>, <u>monocytes</u>, or <u>basophils</u>). The most common forms of <u>leukopenia</u> are <u>neutropenia</u> and <u>lymphopenia</u>, which have various causative factors that include infection, hematologic abnormalities, <u>malignancy</u>, medications, and autoimmune or inflammatory disorders. <u>Agranulocytosis</u> is the absence of <u>granulocytes</u> or severe <u>neutropenia</u> and is most commonly medication-induced. Diagnosis involves obtaining a <u>CBC with differential</u> and a <u>peripheral blood smear</u>. Further studies (e.g., infectious workup, <u>malignancy</u> evaluation) should be obtained based on the suspected cause. Management depends on the underlying cause of <u>leukopenia</u>.

## Agranulocytosis

### Definition




<u>Agranulocytosis</u> is the absence of <u>granulocytes</u> or severe <u>neutropenia</u> (usually defined as <u>ANC</u> < 500/mmÂ³).  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wOdhuJ0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ml1VxS0)




### Etiology




* Medications (most common) [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vOdA8J0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ml1VxS0)

* <u>Chemotherapeutic agents</u>

* <u>Antibiotics</u> (e.g., <u>trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole</u>, <u>dapsone</u>, <u>chloramphenicol</u>)

* Cardiovascular drugs (e.g., <u>ticlopidine</u>, <u>procainamide</u>, <u>flecainide</u>)

* <u>Antipsychotic drugs</u> (e.g., <u>clozapine</u>)

* <u>Antithyroid drugs</u> (e.g., <u>propylthiouracil</u>, <u>methimazole</u>)

* <u>Anticonvulsant drugs</u> (e.g., <u>carbamazepine</u>, <u>valproate</u>, <u>phenytoin</u>) [[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZndZ7q0)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DGb1Yv)

* Anti-inflammatory drugs (e.g., <u>sulfasalazine</u>, <u>NSAIDs</u>)

* <u>Antifungal agents</u> (e.g., <u>flucytosine</u>)

* Other: <u>colchicine</u>, <u>ganciclovir</u> [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DGb1Yv)

* <u>Bone marrow</u> disorders and malignancies: : e.g., <u>aplastic anemia</u>, <u>myelodysplastic syndromes</u>, <u>leukemia</u>  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xVXE9C)

* Nutritional deficiency: e.g., <u>protein-calorie malnutrition</u> [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p5dLOq0)

* Congenital: e.g., <u>severe congenital neutropenia</u> [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p5dLOq0)

* Autoimmune conditions: e.g., <u>SLE</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xVXE9C)[[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p5dLOq0)

* Viral infection: e.g., <u>EBV</u> [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p5dLOq0)





> [!NOTE]
> “Causes Pretty Major Collapse To Defense Cells”: Some drugs that can cause <u>agranulocytosis</u> include Clozapine, Propylthiouracil, Methimazole, Carbamazepine, Ticlopidine, Dapsone, and Chloramphenicol.




### Pathophysiology [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DGb1Yv)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9GbNYv)[[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wGbhYv)




* Medication-induced

* Immune-mediated (<u>type II hypersensitivity reaction</u>): Drugs or drug-protein complexes stimulate the development of <u>antibodies</u> against <u>granulocytes</u>.

* Drug toxicity: direct damage to myeloid precursors (i.e., <u>myelosuppression</u>)

* Autoimmune conditions: peripheral <u>granulocyte</u> destruction





### Clinical features [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ml1VxS0)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/75d4lq0)




In medication-induced <u>agranulocytosis</u>, symptoms usually begin within the first three months of treatment with the causative drug. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ml1VxS0)




* May be asymptomatic

* Triad of oral <u>ulcerations</u> with <u>stomatitis</u>, <u>sore throat</u>, and <u>fever</u> (<u>agranulocytic angina</u>) [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Xnd97q0)

* <u>Myalgias</u>, <u>arthralgia</u>, <u>malaise</u>

* Symptoms of infection

* Symptoms of underlying etiology (e.g., bleeding in hematologic disorders)





### Diagnostics




See “<u>Diagnostics for leukopenia</u>.”




> [!TIP]
> Monitor <u>WBC count</u> and differential after starting drugs that can cause <u>agranulocytosis</u>.




### Treatment [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vOdA8J0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wOdhuJ0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ml1VxS0)




* Stop causative agent if medication-induced.

* In <u>febrile</u> patients, initiate <u>management of neutropenic fever</u> (e.g., <u>antibiotics</u> for infection).

* Consider <u>G-CSF</u> (<u>off-label</u>), e.g., <u>filgrastim</u>, in select cases. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wOdhuJ0)

* Consult <u>hematology</u> for assistance in management.
