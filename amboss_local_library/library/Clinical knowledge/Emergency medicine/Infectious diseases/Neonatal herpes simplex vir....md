# Neonatal herpes simplex virus infection

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Infectious diseases > Neonatal herpes simplex virus infection*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KD0U2R)

---

## Summary

Neonatal <u>herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection</u> is a potentially life-threatening infection caused by <u>HSV-1</u> or <u>HSV-2</u>, most commonly transmitted from a birthing parent with active genital tract disease during delivery. Neonatal <u>HSV infection</u> can manifest as <u>skin</u>, <u>eye</u>, and mouth (SEM) disease; <u>central nervous system</u> (<u>CNS</u>) disease; and disseminated disease. SEM disease is characterized by <u>vesicular lesions</u> localized to the <u>skin</u>, eyes, and/or mouth; <u>CNS</u> disease manifests with <u>meningoencephalitis</u>; and disseminated disease is characterized by signs of <u>neonatal sepsis</u> and multiorgan involvement. Diagnosis is confirmed by detection of <u>HSV</u> via <u>NAAT</u> or viral culture from <u>skin</u> or <u>mucosal</u> surfaces, <u>vesicles</u>, <u>cerebrospinal fluid</u>, and/or blood. Immediate treatment with <u>acyclovir</u> is indicated for all forms of the disease. Asymptomatic <u>neonates</u> with exposure to <u>HSV</u> at delivery require additional monitoring, which includes assessment for signs of infection and diagnostic testing for <u>HSV</u>, regardless of the form of delivery (i.e., vaginal or cesarean). Prevention is critical and includes <u>management of genital herpes in pregnancy</u> and avoiding exposure of <u>neonates</u> to active lesions.

## Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>HSV-2</u> or <u>HSV-1</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Transmission [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Occurs more commonly after primary infection in the birthing parent rather than recurrent infection  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Modes of transmission

* Intrauterine (rare): contracted via ascending infection through intact or ruptured <u>amniotic membranes</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Peripartum (most common): contracted during <u>birth</u> via contact with active genital tract disease [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Postnatal: contracted through close contact (e.g., oral secretions, <u>skin</u> lesions) with infected individuals such as caregivers and siblings  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)





> [!WARNING]
> In over 75% of cases, <u>HSV infection</u> occurs in <u>neonates</u> whose birthing parent has no history or clinical features of genital <u>HSV</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

## Clinical features

Clinical features depend on when the infection occurs. Symptoms may manifest at <u>birth</u> or develop up to 6 weeks later. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FIdger0)




### Congenital HSV infection [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZhVZc80)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lF1vRi0)




<u>Congenital HSV</u> is rare and caused by intrauterine infection. Features include:




* <u>CNS</u> abnormalities (e.g., <u>microcephaly</u>, <u>hydranencephaly</u>)

* Ocular abnormalities (e.g., <u>microphthalmia</u>, <u>chorioretinitis</u>) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0hVec80)

* <u>Skin</u> abnormalities (e.g., scarring, <u>aplasia cutis congenita</u>)





### Peripartum or postpartum infection




* Localized <u>skin</u>, <u>eye</u>, and/or mouth (SEM) disease  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* <u>Vesicular</u> oral and/or <u>skin</u> lesions

* Ocular involvement (e.g., <u>neonatal HSV conjunctivitis</u>, <u>HSV keratitis</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/28dTN70)

* <u>CNS</u> disease

* Signs of <u>meningoencephalitis</u> (e.g., bulging <u>fontanelle</u>, <u>seizures</u>)

* <u>Skin</u> involvement is common. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lF1vRi0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XhV9c80)

* Disseminated disease [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* <u>Signs of sepsis</u> (see "<u>Clinical features of neonatal infection</u>")

* Symptoms of multiple organ involvement (e.g., <u>viral pneumonia</u>, <u>clinical features of liver failure</u>)

* Most patients also have <u>CNS</u> and <u>skin</u> involvement. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZhVZc80)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Vesicular</u> <u>skin</u> lesions occur in up to two-thirds of <u>infants</u> with <u>HSV</u> with <u>CNS</u> and/or disseminated disease but may not manifest concurrently with other symptoms. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)




> [!TIP]
> Consider <u>HSV</u> in <u>infants</u> up to 6 weeks of age with <u>vesicular</u> <u>skin</u> lesions, persistent <u>fever</u> with negative cultures, and/or <u>symptoms of meningitis</u>, <u>encephalitis</u>, or <u>sepsis</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

## Diagnosis

Indications for diagnostic testing include <u>neonates</u> with suggestive clinical features and asymptomatic <u>HSV</u>-exposed <u>neonates</u>. See also "<u>Approach to suspected neonatal bacterial infection</u>" and "<u>Management of the well-appearing febrile infant ≤ 60 days of age</u>."




### Neonatal HSV testing [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)




* Diagnostic studies: Obtain all of the following in patients with suspected neonatal <u>HSV infection</u>.

* Viral culture or <u>HSV</u> <u>NAAT</u> of samples from all of the following locations:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60); 

* <u>Conjunctivae</u>

* Mouth

* <u>Nasopharynx</u>

* <u>Anus</u>

* <u>Skin</u> <u>vesicles</u> (if present)

* <u>HSV</u> <u>NAAT</u> from <u>CSF</u> and serum

* Serum <u>alanine aminotransferase</u> (<u>ALT</u>)

* Findings 

* <u>HSV</u> isolation from any sample confirms diagnosis.

* <u>HSV</u> may be detected in the blood in all three disease forms (i.e., SEM, <u>CNS</u>, and disseminated disease).

* ↑ <u>ALT</u>: may suggest disseminated disease  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XhV9c80)





> [!WARNING]
> A positive serum <u>NAAT</u> cannot distinguish between disseminated disease, <u>CNS</u> disease, and SEM disease. Interpret findings in conjunction with clinical presentation. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)




### Additional diagnostic testing [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)




* All <u>neonates</u> with <u>HSV infection</u>

* Ophthalmology evaluation: to assess for <u>eye</u> involvement

* <u>Neuroimaging</u>: for a baseline evaluation of the <u>brain</u>

* Preferred: <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u>

* Alternatives: CT or <u>ultrasound</u> <u>brain</u>

* Additional studies based on suspected sites of involvement

* Disseminated disease

* <u>Diagnostics for neonatal sepsis</u>: to exclude other <u>neonatal infections</u>

* <u>Coagulation studies</u>: may show findings consistent with <u>disseminated intravascular coagulation</u> (<u>DIC</u>)

* <u>Chest x-ray</u>: may show <u>lung</u> involvement

* <u>CNS</u> disease [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yxbd_D)

* <u>CSF analysis</u>: may show <u>pleocytosis</u> with negative <u>Gram stain</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pJ1LE30)

* <u>Electroencephalography</u>: may show periodic lateralized epileptiform discharges  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yxbd_D)

* See also "<u>Diagnosis of HSV encephalitis</u>."

## Management

* <u>Neonates</u> with confirmed or suspected neonatal <u>HSV infection</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Admit to hospital and start IV <u>acyclovir</u>.

* See "<u>Management of neonates with confirmed or suspected HSV infection</u>" for details.

* Asymptomatic <u>neonates</u> with a birthing parent with <u>HSV</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bedza60)

* Management depends on:

* Presence of active <u>genital herpes</u> lesions at the time of delivery

* Birthing parent's history of <u>genital herpes</u> before <u>pregnancy</u>

* Timing of infection during <u>pregnancy</u>

* See "<u>Asymptomatic neonates exposed to HSV</u>" for details.





> [!TIP]
> SEM disease caused by <u>HSV</u> has a good prognosis if detected and treated early. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FIdger0)
