# Bioterrorism agents

## Summary

Bioterrorism agents are infectious agents that are deliberately released with the intent of causing illness or <u>death</u>. They are classified into three categories (A, B, and C) based on their ease of spread and impact on health; <u>Category A bioterrorism agents</u> (e.g., <u>anthrax</u>, <u>smallpox</u>, <u>plague</u>) pose the highest risk to <u>public health</u>. Clinical features that are concerning for a bioterrorism attack include any patients with <u>symptoms of Category A agents</u> in the absence of <u>risk factors</u>, or multiple patients presenting with <u>signs of possible biological agent deployment</u> (e.g., pulmonary and cutaneous syndromes). Initial management of suspected <u>biological agent</u> exposure includes <u>isolation precautions</u> and <u>body surface decontamination</u> as indicated, agent-specific diagnostics and treatment, and contacting local health departments or the <u>CDC</u>. Management of exposed contacts is based on the agent and may involve <u>postexposure prophylaxis</u> (PEP) with <u>vaccines</u> or <u>antibiotics</u>.
