## Summary

COVID-19 is an acute infectious disease caused by the <u>coronavirus</u> <u>SARS-CoV-2</u>. Transmission most commonly occurs via exposure to respiratory fluids. The <u>incubation period</u> varies between 2–14 days and may be shorter with newer variants. Common presenting symptoms include fatigue, <u>fever</u>, and symptoms of an <u>upper respiratory tract infection</u>; affected individuals may also be asymptomatic. Clinical courses range from asymptomatic infection, to mild with minimal symptoms, to severe with <u>pneumonia</u> and life-threatening complications (e.g., <u>acute respiratory distress syndrome</u>, <u>shock</u>, organ dysfunction). Diagnostic confirmation is made based on <u>COVID-19 testing</u>, which includes <u>nucleic acid amplification testing</u> (e.g., <u>PCR</u>) or <u>antigen</u> testing on a respiratory specimen. Imaging findings are nonspecific, resemble those in other viral respiratory tract infections, and should not be used to confirm the diagnosis of COVID-19. Individuals with <u>mild COVID-19</u> can typically be managed with <u>supportive care</u> at home. Pharmacotherapy can be considered for outpatients with <u>risk factors for progression to severe COVID-19</u>. Patients with moderate or severe COVID-19 or those with <u>risk factors</u> for severe disease may require hospitalization for <u>oxygen therapy</u>, pharmacotherapy, and <u>antithrombotic therapy</u>. The risk of infection can be reduced through preventive efforts, which include <u>vaccination</u>, <u>public health</u> measures (e.g., <u>social distancing</u>, mask-wearing), and getting tested after close contact with infected individuals.

## Etiology

### Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (<u>SARS-CoV-2</u>) [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyc0TU0)




* <u>SARS-CoV-2</u> is an enveloped, nonsegmented, <u>positive-sense</u>, ssRNA β-<u>coronavirus</u>.    [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LycwfU0)

* The <u>viral genome</u> encodes:

* Four structural <u>proteins</u>  

* Spike: facilitates entry by attaching to the ACE2 <u>receptor</u> of the host cell

* Envelope

* Membrane

* <u>Nucleocapsid</u>: packages the <u>viral genome</u> into a helical ribonucleocapsid

* 16 nonstructural <u>proteins</u>: form the replicase–transcriptase complex  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KycUTU0)

### Transmission [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2zcTHU0)




* The <u>basic reproduction number</u> (<u>R<sub>0</sub></u>) of the original strain is estimated to be ∼ 2–3.

* The <u>R<sub>0</sub></u> is higher in newer variants compared to the original strain.

* Factors affecting <u>R<sub>0</sub></u> include:

* Duration of infectiousness: Individuals with COVID-19 are infectious from ∼2–3 days before symptom onset until ∼ 8 days after symptom onset.  [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FZ1gW20)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IobYWu)

* Likelihood of infection spreading between individuals (see “Modes of transmission”)

* Rate of close contacts between individuals with and without infection

* Efforts to reduce the contact rate (e.g., <u>social distancing</u>, <u>quarantine</u>) aim to lower the <u>R<sub>0</sub></u>





![Modeling the spread of COVID-19 based on R0](../../../../media/ibzahg0037pmp6c8n3i048n5.jpg)

Modeling the spread of COVID-19 based on R0




> [!TIP]
> “Flattening the curve”, a reference to the number of new cases depicted on a graph, refers to reducing the number of people infected by each infectious individual. Flattening the curve means that new infections are distributed over a longer period of time.




#### Modes of transmission [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UZ1ba20)




* The primary mode of transmission is exposure to respiratory fluids via either:

* Inhalation of droplets (<u>droplet transmission</u>) or aerosol particles (<u>airborne transmission</u>)

* The concentration of aerosol particles is highest within 3–6 ft of the infectious source.

* Small aerosol particles can remain suspended in the air for minutes to hours.

* The risk of transmission is increased in poorly ventilated areas.

* <u>Mucous membrane</u> contact with respiratory particles

* <u>Fomite</u> transmission is possible but unlikely to be a major mode of transmission.  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2Z1Ta20)

* <u>Vertical transmission</u>: See “<u>COVID-19: pregnancy and breastfeeding</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> <u>SARS-CoV-2</u> can be transmitted by asymptomatic individuals.

## Pathophysiology

### <u>Viral life cycle</u>   [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyc0TU0)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Pc1Wcf0)




* Invasion of host cells  
1. * The spike protein binds to the <u>membrane protein</u> <u>angiotensin-converting enzyme</u> 2 (ACE2). [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R7bllE)
2. * Transmembrane <u>protease</u> <u>serine</u> 2 (TMPRSS2) activates the spike protein.
3. * Membrane fusion and uncoating of the viral <u>RNA</u> occur.

* Replication cycle

* Enzymes, such as <u>RNA polymerase</u> or <u>proteases</u>, which are virally induced by endosomal viral <u>RNA</u>, release replicate viral components.

* Endosomes with newly constituted <u>viruses</u> are released via exocytosis.





> [!NOTE]
> ACE2 is expressed in the <u>surface epithelium</u> of the <u>lungs</u>, <u>heart</u>, and other organs. TMPRSS2 is expressed in <u>respiratory epithelium</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oyc0TU0)

### Effects




* Direct cytopathic effects: Particularly on the alveolar <u>epithelium</u>; other organs (e.g., <u>liver</u>, <u>heart</u>) can be affected as well. [[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8Z1OW20)[[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uZ1pW20)[[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EZ18W20)

* Dysregulated <u>immune response</u> ;   [[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ea18m20)[[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/df1oOT0)

* Activation of the <u>immune system</u> → release of <u>cytokines</u>;  (e.g., <u>tumor necrosis factor</u>, IL-1β, <u>IL-6</u>) → <u>acute inflammatory response</u>

* Some patients demonstrate a very large <u>immune response</u> resembling a “<u>cytokine storm</u>” that can lead to organ failure and <u>death</u>.

## Clinical features

* <u>Incubation period</u>: 2–14 days, usually ∼ 5 days  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SzcyHU0)

* Symptoms [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ma1MO20)

* Often asymptomatic, especially in children  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q-cCyU0)

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Cough</u>

* <u>Shortness of breath</u>

* Fatigue

* Loss of <u>smell</u> and/or <u>taste</u>

* <u>Sore throat</u>

* <u>Rhinitis</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* <u>Myalgia</u>

* <u>Diarrhea</u>

* Loss of appetite

* <u>Nausea</u> and <u>vomiting</u>

* <u>Conjunctivitis</u> (see “<u>COVID-19 conjunctivitis</u>”)

* Severity [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s8ctLV0)[[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KzcUFU0)

* Severity ranges from asymptomatic to critical.

* A patient's clinical presentation may change over the course of the disease.

* See “<u>COVID-19 disease spectrum</u>” for details.





> [!TIP]
> Delta or Omicron variants of COVID-19 often manifest with milder symptoms (e.g., <u>headache</u>, <u>rhinitis</u>, <u>fever</u>, and <u>sore throat</u>) compared to prior strains.

## Diagnosis

### General principles [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)




* Diagnostic confirmation is based on viral testing for COVID-19.

* Test all individuals with symptoms of and/or exposure to COVID-19 for current infection (see the latest <u>CDC</u> indications for testing at: https://www.cdc.gov/<u>coronavirus</u>/2019-ncov/symptoms-testing/testing.html).

* Collect specimens from the <u>upper respiratory tract</u> (e.g., <u>nasopharyngeal</u>, oropharyngeal).

* Obtain a viral test (e.g., <u>nucleic acid amplification test</u>) or <u>antigen</u> test to diagnose current infection.

* Imaging

* Should not be used as a screening modality or the sole diagnostic modality for COVID-19

* May be indicated to assess disease severity and rule out alternative diagnoses

* See “<u>Approach to imaging in COVID-19</u>” for details.





### Collection of specimens [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m1bVhs)




* For all individuals: Obtain an <u>upper respiratory tract</u> specimen.

* Preferred method: <u>nasopharyngeal specimen</u> (NP specimen)

* Alternative methods

* Oropharyngeal (OP) swab (i.e., throat swab)

* Nasal midturbinate (NMT) swab

* <u>Anterior</u> nares specimen

* Saliva specimen

* <u>Nasopharyngeal</u> wash/<u>aspirate</u> or nasal <u>aspirate</u>

* If using both NP and OP swabs: Combine in a single tube.

* For mechanically ventilated individuals with a negative <u>upper respiratory tract</u> specimen: Obtain a <u>lower respiratory tract</u> <u>aspirate</u> or <u>bronchoalveolar lavage</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Obtain a <u>lower respiratory tract</u> sample if there is clinical concern for COVID-19 <u>pneumonia</u> in an intubated patient with negative <u>upper respiratory tract</u> testing. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)

## Management

### Approach [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wabh5H)




* Triage patients via <u>telemedicine</u> when possible.

* Determine site of care based on disease severity and the presence of <u>risk factors for severe COVID-19</u>. 

* Asymptomatic or mild disease in low-risk patients: outpatient management

* Moderate to severe disease, or <u>risk factors</u> for severe COVID-19: in-person evaluation; hospitalization if needed

* Patients requiring in-person evaluation: Consult institutional guidelines regarding the appropriate site of care.

* See also “<u>Preventing spread of COVID-19 in healthcare settings</u>.”

## Outpatient management

### General principles [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/91bNQs)




Patients with mild or asymptomatic disease who do not have <u>risk factors for progression to severe COVID-19</u> can usually be managed in an outpatient setting.




* Advise patients on methods to minimize the spread of infection, e.g.:

* Staying home and isolating in a separate well-ventilated room when possible

* Wearing a mask when it is necessary to be around other people

* <u>Hand hygiene</u>

* For more details, see https://www.cdc.gov/<u>coronavirus</u>/2019-ncov/if-you-are-sick/steps-when-sick.html

* Discuss symptom monitoring and advise patients to seek medical attention if they experience any of the following:

* Difficulty breathing

* Persistent <u>chest pain</u> or pressure

* <u>Change in mental status</u> (e.g., confusion)

* <u>Signs of hypoxia</u> (e.g., bluish <u>lips</u>)





### Medical management [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JT1s7T0)




#### <u>Supportive care</u>




* Rest, adequate hydration, and nutrition

* <u>Antipyretics</u> and <u>analgesia</u> as needed (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>ibuprofen</u>)

* <u>Antitussives</u> as needed (e.g., <u>dextromethorphan</u>, <u>benzonatate</u>)

* <u>Prone positioning</u> may relieve <u>dyspnea</u>.





#### Pharmacotherapy [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JT1s7T0)




* Indications: patients with <u>risk factors for progression to severe COVID-19</u>

* Preferred agents: <u>ritonavir-boosted nirmatrelvir</u>, <u>remdesivir</u>

* Alternatives (only if preferred agents are not available): <u>molnupiravir</u>

* Not currently recommended: <u>systemic glucocorticoids</u>, <u>chloroquine</u>, <u>hydroxychloroquine</u>, <u>azithromycin</u>, <u>ivermectin</u>, bebtelovimab

* For possible drug interaction, see “https://www.covid19-druginteractions.org/checker.”

> Do not start <u>antithrombotic therapy</u> in nonhospitalized patients with COVID-19. Chronic <u>antithrombotic therapy</u> for preexisting medical conditions should be continued. ### Monitoring and follow-up [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)




* Monitor oximetry readings and symptom progression via <u>telemedicine</u> or in-person visits.

* Evaluate patients with worsening symptoms in-person.

* Advise patients that the time to recovery from COVID-19 varies.

* Encourage self-isolation until criteria for <u>discontinuation of isolation in patients with COVID-19</u> are met.

## Management of hospitalized patients

### General principles




Patients with moderate to severe COVID-19 often require hospitalization.




* Determine patient's <u>goals of care</u> and obtain <u>advance directives</u>, if available.

* Determine severity of illness and admit patients to the appropriate <u>level of care</u>.

* Tailor additional diagnostics (<u>laboratory studies</u>, imaging) to the clinical presentation.

* For patients with complications related to COVID-19, see also the management sections in the following articles:

* <u>Sepsis</u>

* <u>Pneumonia</u>

* <u>Acute respiratory distress syndrome</u>

* <u>Airway management</u>

* <u>Mechanical ventilation</u>

* <u>Venous thromboembolism</u> (<u>VTE</u>)





### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ma1MO20)




Laboratory parameters in COVID-19 are nonspecific but are useful in evaluating for organ dysfunction




* Routine studies

* <u>CBC</u>: may show <u>lymphopenia</u>, <u>thrombocytopenia</u>  [[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/P_cWoU0)

* <u>Basic metabolic panel</u>

* Elevated serum <u>creatinine</u> in <u>acute kidney injury</u> [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ua1bP20)

* Possible <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities [[50]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2a1TP20)

* <u>Liver chemistry</u>: may show ↑ <u>ALT</u>, ↑ <u>AST</u> [[51]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ta16P20)

* Further evaluation based on clinical presentation [[52]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w-chzU0)

* <u>Inflammatory markers</u>: ↑ <u>CRP</u>, ↑ <u>ferritin</u>, ↑ <u>lactate dehydrogenase</u>

* <u>Coagulation studies</u>: ↑ <u>prothrombin time</u>, ↑ <u>D-dimer</u>

* <u>Cardiac enzymes</u>: ↑ <u>troponin</u> and ↑ <u>NT-proBNP</u> may indicate cardiac injury related to COVID-19

* <u>Arterial blood gas</u>: may reveal <u>hypoxemic respiratory failure</u>

* <u>Blood cultures</u>: Order if there is concern for bacterial <u>coinfection</u>.





### Chest imaging in COVID-19 [[52]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w-chzU0)[[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ua1pm20)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/na17O20)




* Indications

* Features of <u>moderate COVID-19</u> or <u>severe COVID-19</u>

* Worsening respiratory status

* Patients with <u>mild COVID-19</u> with <u>risk factors for progression to severe COVID-19</u>

* Modalities: Initial imaging studies may include <u>chest x-ray</u>, chest <u>ultrasound</u>, and chest CT.

* Important consideration: Imaging findings in COVID-19 are generally nonspecific and resemble findings in other viral respiratory infections.





> [!WARNING]
> Imaging alone cannot confirm a diagnosis of COVID-19. Imaging findings suggestive of COVID-19 should be verified via <u>COVID-19 virus testing</u>. [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ua1pm20)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/na17O20)




> [!TIP]
> Chest CT can detect <u>parenchymal</u> changes early in the course of COVID-19 and, compared to a <u>chest x-ray</u>, has higher <u>sensitivity</u> in detecting disease progression and alternative diagnoses. [[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/na17O20)




#### <u>Chest x-ray</u> [[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/na17O20)




* Indications: : often the initial test to assess disease severity and rule out differential diagnoses (e.g., <u>pneumothorax</u>, <u>pleural effusion</u>)

* Supportive findings 

* <u>Pulmonary consolidations</u>

* Unilateral or bilateral opacities

* Progression to diffuse opacities suggests development of <u>ARDS</u>.

#### Chest <u>ultrasound</u> [[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/La1wO20)




* Indications: an alternative to <u>chest x-ray</u> to assess disease severity and rule out other chest pathologies (e.g., <u>pneumothorax</u>, <u>pleural effusion</u>) in critically ill patients; often performed in emergency department and <u>ICU</u> settings (<u>lung POCUS</u>)

* Supportive findings

* Pleural thickening

* <u>B lines</u>

* Consolidations with <u>air bronchograms</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>POCUS</u> may also be used to assess hemodynamic status in critically ill patients (<u>IVC POCUS</u>) and those with <u>deep vein thrombosis</u>. [[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/La1wO20)




#### Chest CT (noncontrast) [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ua1pm20)[[56]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WT1PpT0)




* Indications: : hospitalized symptomatic patients with other specific clinical indications for a chest CT  [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ua1pm20)

* Findings 

* <u>Ground glass opacities</u> with or without consolidation (usually bilateral, peripheral, and <u>posterior</u>)

* Interlobular septal thickening

* The combination of the above findings is often referred to as a “<u>crazy-paving pattern</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> Imaging studies can not be used to diagnose COVID-19.

### Screening for <u>VTE</u> [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gT1FJT0)




* Indications

* Patients with <u>features of DVT</u> and/or <u>features of pulmonary embolism</u> (<u>PE</u>)

* Patients with sudden or rapid clinical deterioration

* Modalities

* Suspected <u>PE</u>: <u>CT pulmonary angiography</u>; see “<u>Diagnostic approach to suspected PE</u>” [[56]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WT1PpT0)

* Suspected <u>DVT</u>: <u>lower extremity venous ultrasound</u>; see “<u>Diagnostic approach to suspected lower-extremity DVT</u>”





> [!TIP]
> There is insufficient evidence to recommend routine screening for <u>VTE</u> in patients with COVID-19, even in those with elevated coagulation markers. [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gT1FJT0)




### <u>Supportive care</u> [[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qT1C7T0)[[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ST1yJT0)




* <u>Intravenous fluid therapy</u> as needed

* <u>Vasopressors</u> as needed (i.e., for <u>shock</u>): 

* <u>Norepinephrine</u> is recommended as the first-line agent.

* See “<u>Vasopressors and inotropes</u>” for dosages and further details.

* <u>Antipyretics</u> and <u>analgesia</u> as needed (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>ibuprofen</u>)

* <u>Antitussives</u> as needed (e.g., <u>benzonatate</u>)

* If available, <u>continuous renal replacement therapy</u> is preferred over <u>hemodialysis</u> for patients with <u>acute kidney injury</u> and <u>indications for dialysis</u>.





### <u>Oxygen therapy</u> [[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qT1C7T0)[[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ST1yJT0)




* Goal: Maintain <u>SpO<sub>2</sub></u> levels between 92–96% (≥ 95% in pregnant individuals).

* <u>High flow nasal cannula</u> (<u>HFNC</u>)

* Indications: <u>hypoxemic respiratory failure</u> despite the use of <u>basic oxygen delivery systems</u> (e.g., <u>nasal cannula</u>, face mask)

* <u>HFNC</u> unavailable: Consider <u>noninvasive positive pressure ventilation</u>.

* Persistent <u>hypoxemia</u> despite <u>HFNC</u> (and no <u>indications for invasive mechanical ventilation</u>): Consider a trial of awake <u>prone positioning</u> to improve oxygenation.  [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ST1yJT0)[[59]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4T13IT0)

* <u>Mechanical ventilation</u>

* Indications: See “<u>Indications for invasive mechanical ventilation</u>.”

* Recommended <u>ventilator settings</u> for patients with COVID-19 and <u>ARDS</u>:

* <u>Tidal volume</u> 4–8 mL/kg of <u>predicted body weight</u>

* <u>Plateau pressure</u> < 30 cm H<sub>2</sub>O

* Ventilation in the <u>prone position</u> for 12–16 hours/day

* High <u>PEEP</u> (> 10 cm H<sub>2</sub>O)

* <u>Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation</u> (<u>ECMO</u>): There is insufficient evidence to recommend for or against the use of <u>ECMO</u> in patients with COVID-19 and refractory <u>hypoxemia</u>.

* See also “<u>Oxygen therapy</u>” for more information.





> [!TIP]
> A trial of awake <u>prone positioning</u> can be considered in individuals with <u>hypoxemia</u> that persists despite HNFC, but the procedure is not routinely recommended in patients who require <u>supplemental oxygen</u> without <u>mechanical ventilation</u>. [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ST1yJT0)[[59]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4T13IT0)




> [!WARNING]
> Awake <u>prone positioning</u> should not prevent or delay <u>intubation</u> in patients with <u>indications for invasive mechanical ventilation</u>. [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ST1yJT0)




### Pharmacotherapy [[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qT1C7T0)




* Pharmacotherapy for hospitalized patients with COVID-19 varies according to disease severity.

* Pharmacologic options include <u>remdesivir</u>, <u>dexamethasone</u>, and other immunomodulators.

* For the latest guideline recommendations, see https://www.covid19treatmentguidelines.nih.gov/therapeutic-management.

* For possible drug interaction, see “https://www.covid19-druginteractions.org/checker.”

### Antithrombotic therapy in COVID-19 [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gT1FJT0)




* Indications: all hospitalized patients who do not have <u>contraindications for anticoagulation</u>

* Agents: <u>LMWH</u> or <u>UFH</u> is preferred over <u>oral anticoagulants</u>; <u>LMWH</u> is preferred over <u>UFH</u>.

* Dosage

* Therapeutic dose is recommended for:

* <u>Venous thromboembolism</u> (see “<u>Management of DVT</u>” and “<u>Management of pulmonary embolism</u>”)

* Increased <u>D-dimer</u> and/or low-flow O<sub>2</sub> requirement in hospitalized nonpregnant adults

* Prophylactic dose (<u>pharmacological VTE prophylaxis</u> ) is recommended for: 

* All hospitalized nonpregnant adults who do not meet indications for therapeutic anticoagulation

* Patients requiring <u>ICU</u> care, including those requiring <u>high-flow O<sub>2</sub></u>

* Hospitalized pregnant patients with COVID-19





> [!TIP]
> <u>Antithrombotic therapy</u> is recommended in all hospitalized patients with COVID-19 unless there are <u>contraindications for anticoagulation</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)




> [!TIP]
> Discontinue <u>VTE prophylaxis</u> at discharge. The decision to continue <u>VTE prophylaxis</u> at discharge in pregnant or postpartum patients should be individualized. [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gT1FJT0)




### Postdischarge management [[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qT1C7T0)




* All patients

* Arrange for a follow-up appointment soon after discharge.

* Stop <u>antithrombotic therapy</u> at the time of discharge unless the patient has a <u>VTE</u>.

* Encourage self-<u>isolation precautions</u> until the criteria for <u>discontinuation of isolation in patients with COVID-19</u> are met.

* <u>Supplemental O<sub>2</sub></u> no longer required: Discontinue <u>remdesivir</u>, <u>corticosteroids</u>, and <u>JAK inhibitors</u> at the time of discharge.

* Continued requirement of <u>supplemental O<sub>2</sub></u> at discharge: Arrange for home oxygen monitoring.

## Discontinuation of isolation

<u>CDC</u> recommendations for discontinuation of isolation vary based on symptoms and disease severity; see the latest <u>CDC</u> guidelines for details. [[62]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ta1Xm20)




* All individuals with COVID-19 should isolate for at least 5 days (day 0: day of symptom onset or positive COVID-19 test).

* Mildly symptomatic or asymptomatic individuals may consider an <u>antigen</u> test around day 5 to determine need for further isolation. [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9Y1Nr20)

* Positive <u>antigen</u> test: Isolation is required for a total of 10 days.

* Negative <u>antigen</u> test: Isolation may be discontinued if other parameters are fulfilled.





> [!TIP]
> Typically, patients with asymptomatic, mild, or moderate COVID-19 are no longer infectious 10 days after symptom onset. [[62]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ta1Xm20)

> Loss of <u>smell</u> and <u>taste</u> may persist for several weeks after recovery and should not delay the end of isolation. [[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1_c2MU0)

## Complications

* <u>Thromboembolic events</u> (e.g., <u>ischemic stroke</u>, <u>pulmonary embolism</u>)

* <u>Hypoxemic respiratory failure</u>

* <u>ARDS</u>

* <u>Septic shock</u>

* Cardiac disease (e.g., <u>ischemic heart disease</u>, <u>heart failure</u>) [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Aa1RM20)

* <u>Arrhythmias</u> [[65]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Aa1RM20)

* <u>Acute kidney injury</u>

* <u>Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children</u> (<u>MIS-C</u>)

* Postacute COVID-19 syndrome (“<u>long COVID</u>”) [[66]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0Ede870):

* Refers to symptoms persisting at least 3 months after confirmed or suspected COVID-19 infection

* Symptoms can include: fatigue, <u>headaches</u>, difficulty concentrating, <u>shortness of breath</u>, <u>sleep</u> disturbance, problems with <u>smell</u> or <u>taste</u>




We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

### Overview [[67]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IT1YHT0)




* Pregnant and recently pregnant individuals are at higher risk of <u>severe COVID-19</u>.

* Counsel pregnant individuals on ways to minimize the risk of infection (see “Protective measures for the general public” in the section “Prevention”).

* <u>Vaccination</u> against COVID-19 is recommended for pregnant and <u>breastfeeding</u> individuals. [[68]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9zcNEU0)

* Treatment options for pregnant patients with COVID-19 are generally the same as those for nonpregnant patients.





### <u>Management of COVID-19</u> during <u>pregnancy</u> [[67]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IT1YHT0)[[69]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yzcdvU0)




* Determine the need for in-person evaluation and hospitalization based on symptoms and comorbidities.  [[70]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BzczEU0)

* Order imaging (e.g., <u>chest x-ray</u> or chest CT) if clinically indicated.

* <u>Supplemental oxygen</u>

* Administer <u>supplemental oxygen</u> if <u>SpO<sub>2</sub></u> is < 95% on room air. [[71]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6_Xj600)

* Consider <u>prone positioning</u> or left <u>lateral decubitus positioning</u> in pregnant patients with persistent <u>hypoxemia</u> on HNFC who do not meet the criteria for <u>invasive mechanical ventilation</u>. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JT1s7T0)

* Consider fetal monitoring for hospitalized patients.

* Consider pharmacological treatment, if indicated. 

* Outpatients

* <u>Ritonavir-boosted nirmatrelvir</u>, <u>sotrovimab</u>, <u>remdesivir</u>

* See “<u>Pharmacotherapy for nonhospitalized adults with COVID-19</u>” for details.

* Hospitalized patients

* <u>Remdesivir</u>, <u>dexamethasone</u>

* See “<u>Pharmacotherapy for hospitalized patients with COVID-19</u>” for details.

* Anticoagulation is indicated for hospitalized patients with COVID-19 (see “<u>Antithrombotic therapy in COVID-19</u>”).  [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gT1FJT0)





> [!TIP]
> Patients with symptoms suggestive of moderate to severe disease, or those with <u>risk factors for severe COVID-19</u> require in-person evaluation.




> [!WARNING]
> <u>Molnupiravir</u> is potentially <u>teratogenic</u> and should be avoided, if feasible, in pregnant individuals. [[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JT1s7T0)




> [!TIP]
> In pregnant patients with COVID-19, maintain <u>oxygen saturation</u> ≥ 95% on room air. [[71]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6_Xj600)




### Management of COVID-19 in the peripartum period [[69]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yzcdvU0)




* Infection with COVID-19 is not a contraindication for vaginal delivery.

* Mothers with COVID-19 do not need to isolate from their babies after <u>birth</u>.

* <u>Breastfeeding</u>

* Individuals not receiving pharmacotherapy for COVID-19 can safely breastfeed <u>infants</u>.

* The decision to continue <u>breastfeeding</u> while on pharmacotherapy for COVID-19 should be individualized.





> [!TIP]
> <u>Vertical transmission</u> of <u>SARS-CoV-2</u> is uncommon.




### Complications [[72]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eX1xC20)




Symptomatic <u>COVID-19 during pregnancy</u> is associated with an increased risk of:




* <u>Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy</u>

* <u>Preterm birth</u>

* <u>Low birth weight</u>

* <u>Stillbirth</u> [[73]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UX1bx20)

* <u>Maternal mortality</u>

## Children

### Overview [[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q-cCyU0)[[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KT1U7T0)[[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PY1W620)




* Children are less likely to get COVID-19.

* Clinical presentation

* Children are often asymptomatic or develop <u>mild COVID-19</u>.

* Symptoms may be similar to other viral illnesses

* <u>Vaccination</u>

* The <u>Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine</u> is recommended for all children in the US ≥ 5 years of age

* See “<u>COVID-19 vaccines</u>” for details.





> [!WARNING]
> The Moderna and Janssen <u>vaccines</u> are not currently recommended for use in individuals < 18 years of age. [[76]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-Y1D720)




### Management [[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KT1U7T0)[[77]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Mb1M820)




Given limited data in children, the decision to use pharmacological treatments should be made after weighing the risks and benefits for each patient.




* Most children can be managed with symptomatic treatment only.

* COVID-19 pharmacotherapy for children (age ≥ 12 years; weight â¥ 40 kg)

* Symptomatic nonhospitalized children at <u>risk for progression to severe COVID-19</u>

* <u>Ritonavir-boosted nirmatrelvir</u>

* OR <u>remdesivir</u>

* Hospitalized children requiring <u>supplemental oxygen</u>: <u>remdesivir</u>





### Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (<u>MIS-C</u>) [[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KT1U7T0)[[75]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PY1W620)




MISC-C is a complication of COVID-19 in children that manifests with hyperinflammation, severe illness, and involvement of multiple organ systems.




* Clinical features may include:

* <u>Fever</u>

* Gastrointestinal symptoms

* Mucocutaneous symptoms

* <u>Shock</u>

* Diagnostic criteria: All of the following must be met. [[78]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lT1vIT0)

* Age < 21 years

* <u>Fever</u> (documented <u>fever</u> â¥ 38Â°C (â¥ 100.4Â°F) OR report of subjective <u>fever</u> lasting ≥ 24 hours

* Laboratory evidence of inflammation (e.g., ↑ <u>CRP</u>, ↑ <u>ESR</u>, ↑ <u>neutrophils</u>)

* Involvement of ≥ 2 organ systems (including the hematological system)

* Severe illness requiring hospitalization

* Confirmed current or recent <u>SARS-CoV-2</u> infection OR exposure to an individual with COVID-19 < 4 weeks prior to symptom onset

* No other plausible diagnosis

* Treatment: combination of immunomodulatory therapy and <u>antithrombotic therapy</u> [[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KT1U7T0)

* Immunomodulatory therapy 

* Initial treatment: <u>intravenous immunoglobulin</u> PLUS <u>methylprednisolone</u>

* No improvement within 24 hours of initial therapy: Consider alternative immunomodulators.

* <u>Antithrombotic therapy</u> (unless contraindicated)

* All patients: <u>low-dose aspirin</u> DOSAGE

* Patients with large <u>coronary artery</u> <u>aneurysms</u> or moderate to severe <u>left ventricular dysfunction</u>: therapeutic anticoagulation (with <u>LMWH</u> or <u>warfarin</u>) PLUS <u>low-dose aspirin</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)[[79]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BU1z2T0)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Remdesivir</u> is not recommended for patients with <u>MIS-C</u>. [[74]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KT1U7T0)

## People with disabilities

### Overview [[80]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fVbkFs)




* Factors that may increase the risk of infection in patients with physical or <u>intellectual disabilities</u> include:

* Need for a support person to assist with mobility

* Difficulty implementing personal protective measures, e.g., due to cognitive impairment

* Living in a congregate setting, e.g., a <u>nursing home</u>

* Delayed diagnosis, e.g., individuals with impaired speech or <u>cognition</u> may have difficulty communicating symptoms

* Some comorbidities that are <u>risk factors for severe COVID-19</u> are more common in individuals with disabilities.





### Patient considerations [[80]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fVbkFs)




* Vaccinate eligible individuals.

* Work with the patient to develop an individualized plan for reducing infection risk.

* Organize backup support in case the current support person becomes sick or is quarantined.

* Establish reliable methods for seeking immediate assistance (e.g., preprogrammed <u>phone numbers</u>)

* Ensure that the patient has sufficient supplies of household items, medication, and medical equipment.

* See also “Protective measures for the general public” in the section “Prevention.”

## Prognosis

* The risk of <u>death</u> from COVID-19 increases with age.  [[81]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xY1Er20)[[82]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BY1zr20)

* <u>Death</u> rates are higher among unvaccinated individuals compared to vaccinated individuals. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VzcG7U0)

* <u>Death</u> rates are higher among vaccinated individuals who have not received a booster compared to those who have received a booster. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VzcG7U0)





> [!TIP]
> Prior infection with <u>SARS-CoV-2</u> provides <u>immunity</u> against reinfection, but it is not yet known how long that protection lasts. [[83]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CY1qr20)

## Prevention

### Protective measures for the general public [[84]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JTbs7G)




* Practice <u>hand hygiene</u>, <u>cough etiquette</u>, and avoid touching the face (see “<u>Hand hygiene</u>” for details).

* Masks can provide additional protection for individuals ≥ 2 years old. 

* Masks should have a close fit over the nose and mouth.

* N95 and K95 masks offer greater protection than <u>surgical masks</u>.

* Cloth masks provide the least protection.

* Follow <u>public health</u> guidance to reduce spread of COVID-19

* E.g., guidance on <u>social distancing</u>, <u>quarantine</u>, and lockdowns

* See also “Prevention of community spread.”

* COVID-19 testing is recommended:

* After close contact with infected individuals

* Individuals with symptoms suggestive of COVID-19

* All eligible individuals should be vaccinated.

* See the <u>CDC</u> website for additional guidance: https://www.cdc.gov/<u>coronavirus</u>/2019-ncov/prevent-getting-sick/prevention.html

### Management of high-risk exposures (close contact) [[91]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gTbFJG)




* Close contact is defined as being < 6 ft from an infectious individual for ≥ 15 min.

* Management

* <u>COVID-19 virological testing</u>

* Indicated for all vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals who have not had COVID-19 in the past 90 days

* Testing should be performed at least five days after close contact.

* The need for and duration of isolation varies by <u>vaccination</u> status: See https://www.cdc.gov/<u>coronavirus</u>/2019-ncov/your-health/<u>quarantine</u>-isolation.html for the most recent guidelines.

* All close contacts should wear well-fitting masks around others for 10 days and avoid contact with high-risk individuals.





### Preexposure prophylaxis for COVID-19 [[92]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sT1tHT0)




* Indications

* <u>Immunocompromised individuals</u>

* Individuals unable to be vaccinated due to severe <u>allergic reactions</u>

* Regimen: tixagevimab PLUS cilgavimab (long-acting <u>anti-SARS-CoV-2 monoclonal antibodies</u>)





### Preventing spread of COVID-19 in healthcare settings




* Consider <u>telemedicine</u> for triage and outpatient monitoring of patients with <u>mild COVID-19</u> when feasible.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-AbDmw)

* <u>Personal protective equipment</u> (<u>PPE</u>) [[93]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I1bYRs)

* All individuals caring for patients with confirmed or suspected COVID-19 should wear appropriate <u>PPE</u>.

* See “<u>Personal protective equipment</u>” for instructions on safely putting on and <u>removing PPE</u>.

* Strategies to reduce spread in healthcare settings include: 

* Separate locations dedicated to the evaluation of patients with known or possible COVID-19

* Limiting the number of visitors allowed per patient

* Screening all people entering the <u>health care facility</u> for symptoms of COVID-19

* Nonurgent procedures and elective surgeries may be postponed to preserve hospital beds and resources.
