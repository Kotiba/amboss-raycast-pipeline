# Malignant otitis externa

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Infectious diseases > Malignant otitis externa*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gG0FAh)

---

## Summary

Malignant <u>otitis externa</u> (MOE) is a severe variant of <u>acute otitis externa</u> (<u>AOE</u>) in which <u>necrotizing inflammation</u> of the <u>external auditory canal</u> (<u>EAC</u>) develops. MOE is usually caused by <u>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</u> and most frequently affects older adults and individuals with <u>diabetes</u> and/or <u>immunosuppression</u>. <u>Clinical features of MOE</u> include severe <u>pain</u>, an <u>erythematous</u> and <u>edematous</u> <u>EAC</u>, and <u>otorrhea</u>; <u>granulation tissue</u> is visible on <u>otoscopy</u>. Because of the rapid spread of infection, patients may have signs of complications (e.g., <u>cranial nerve palsies</u> secondary to <u>osteomyelitis</u> of the <u>skull base</u>) on initial presentation. Imaging and <u>laboratory studies</u> are recommended to confirm the diagnosis. Treatment is with IV <u>antibiotics</u>; <u>surgery</u> may be required for <u>debridement</u> or <u>abscess</u> drainage. The prognosis with treatment is good, with overall mortality < 10%.

## Etiology

* Pathogens [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kj1m0S0)

* Similar to <u>acute otitis externa</u> (see “<u>Etiology of OE</u>”)

* <u>P. aeruginosa</u> is the most common cause, particularly in patients with <u>diabetes</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kj1m0S0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Vj1Gzg0)

* Risk factors for MOE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kj1m0S0)

* Older adults (> 60 years of age)

* <u>Diabetes mellitus</u>

* <u>Immunosuppression</u>

---

## Clinical features

* Severe, persistent <u>ear</u> <u>pain</u> and/or <u>jaw</u> <u>pain</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)

* Symptoms of extension of infection, e.g.:

* <u>Headache</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)

* <u>Facial nerve palsy</u> in <u>osteomyelitis</u> of the <u>skull base</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)

* <u>Conductive hearing loss</u>

* Red and swollen;  <u>EAC</u> and periauricular <u>soft tissue</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OP1IUS0)

* <u>Otorrhea</u>

* Otoscopic findings: <u>granulation tissue</u> at the <u>cartilage</u>-bone junction of the <u>EAC</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)

## Diagnosis

All patients require <u>laboratory studies</u> and imaging.




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




* <u>CBC</u>: for <u>WBC</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)

* <u>BMP</u>: for serum <u>glucose</u> and <u>creatinine</u>

* <u>ESR</u> and <u>CRP</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)

* Culture of <u>ear</u> canal : Obtain a sample prior to initiating <u>empiric antibiotics</u>.





### Imaging [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




More than one imaging modality is often required.




* Modalities [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ej1xzg0)

* CT head with IV contrast: preferred initial study

* <u>MRI</u> head: best modality for detecting <u>soft tissue</u> extension and intracranial abnormalities

* <u>Radionuclide scans</u>: may be useful for early detection  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ej1xzg0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sl1ywS0)

* <u>PET/CT scan</u>: helpful for diagnosis and monitoring treatment response

* Findings

* Bone <u>erosion</u>

* <u>Soft tissue</u> involvement

* Intracranial extension

* <u>Abscess</u>





> [!WARNING]
> A negative <u>CT scan</u> does not exclude early MOE, as changes may not be evident until one-third of bone mineral is eroded. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)




### Surgical <u>biopsy</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




Consider if there is diagnostic uncertainty or insufficient response to treatment.

## Treatment

Treatment involves early <u>empiric antibiotic therapy</u> and the control of <u>risk factors for MOE</u> (e.g., <u>diabetes</u> or <u>immunosuppression</u>), in consultation with an otolaryngologist. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




### <u>Antibiotic therapy</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kj1m0S0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)




* Systemic <u>antibiotic therapy</u>: typically lasting for 6–8 weeks ;  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kj1m0S0)

* Initial empiric therapy: Use two antipseudomonal agents from different classes for ≥ 2 weeks, e.g., <u>ciprofloxacin</u> DOSAGE PLUS one of the following:

* <u>Ceftazidime</u> DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)

* <u>Piperacillin/tazobactam</u> DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)

* <u>Cefepime</u> DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)

* Once culture results are available, tailor <u>antibiotics</u> to <u>sensitivities</u> and continue for an additional 4–6 weeks.

* Local treatment [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JXYs_n)

* Clean and remove debris from the <u>EAC</u>.

* Consider <u>topical antimicrobial therapy for AOE</u>.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OP1IUS0)

* Obtain a culture first.

* Use an antipseudomonal agent plus a <u>glucocorticoid</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TL16Ch0)





### <u>Surgery</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




* May be required in select cases, e.g., <u>abscess</u> drainage or <u>debridement</u> of <u>bony sequestra</u>

* Send any surgical specimens for pathology and microbiological testing.





> [!TIP]
> Patients unresponsive to <u>antibiotic therapy</u> may require a surgical <u>biopsy</u> to rule out fungal etiology or <u>malignancy</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




### Disposition [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Urgent otolaryngology consult

* Patients typically require initial hospitalization followed by outpatient care for prolonged IV <u>antibiotics</u>.





### Monitoring of treatment response [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gR1Fmg0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




* Repeat <u>ESR</u> every few weeks.

* Consider <u>PET/CT scan</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ej1xzg0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sl1ywS0)

## Complications

* <u>Osteomyelitis</u> of the <u>skull base</u> manifesting with: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OP1IUS0)

* <u>Facial nerve palsy</u> and, less commonly, other <u>cranial neuropathies</u> (e.g., of IX, X, XI, and XII) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)

* <u>Meningitis</u>

* <u>Cerebral abscess</u>

* <u>Septic cerebral venous thrombosis</u>

* Relapse in up to 20% of patients [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CP1qSS0)




We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.
