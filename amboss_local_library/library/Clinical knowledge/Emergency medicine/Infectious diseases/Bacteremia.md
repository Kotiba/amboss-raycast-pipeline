## Summary

Bacteremia is a condition characterized by the presence of viable pathogens in the bloodstream. Bacteremia can occur transiently in healthy individuals (e.g., after <u>tooth</u> brushing) or as the result of a localized infection spreading to the bloodstream. <u>Risk factors</u> for bacteremia include conditions that cause <u>immunosuppression</u> (e.g., <u>diabetes mellitus</u>, <u>malignancy</u>) and those that facilitate <u>pathogen</u> entry or adhesion (e.g., presence of <u>intravascular</u> catheters, prosthetic material). Diagnosis is confirmed with <u>blood cultures</u> to identify the causative <u>pathogen</u> and its <u>antimicrobial resistance</u> pattern. If <u>blood cultures</u> are positive, bacteremia is considered a <u>bloodstream infection</u> (<u>BSI</u>). <u>BSI</u> most commonly manifests with nonspecific symptoms of infection, such as <u>fever</u>, or clinical features related to the site of the primary infection (e.g., urinary symptoms in <u>urinary tract infection</u>). Further investigations may be required depending on the suspected source of the <u>BSI</u>. Treatment includes <u>antibiotic therapy</u> and source control (e.g., <u>abscess</u> drainage, change/removal of prosthetic material). Empiric <u>broad-spectrum antibiotic</u> therapy is indicated in acutely unwell patients and can be deescalated according to culture results.




For patients with bacteremia who are hemodynamically compromised or have other signs of severe systemic infection, see “<u>Sepsis</u>.” For details on technique of <u>blood culture</u> acquisition, see “<u>Blood cultures</u>.” For patients with bacteremia and a known <u>intravascular</u> device, see “<u>Catheter-related bloodstream infections</u>; for those with surgical implants, see “<u>Device-related infections</u>.” For patients with <u>fungemia</u>, see “<u>Management of systemic fungal infections</u>.”
