# Vaginal bleeding

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Gynecologic and obstetric disorders > Vaginal bleeding*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oH00rh)

---

## Summary

Vaginal bleeding that cannot be attributed to expected menstrual bleeding is a common clinical presentation. The most likely underlying cause depends on the patient’s <u>pregnancy</u> status, reproductive stage, and other factors (e.g., use of <u>hormone replacement therapy</u>, <u>risk factors</u> for gynecologic malignancies). <u>Vaginal bleeding in nonpregnant adults</u> encompasses <u>abnormal uterine bleeding</u> (<u>AUB</u>) in <u>premenopausal</u> individuals, <u>postmenopausal bleeding</u>, and <u>postcoital bleeding</u>. Common etiologies in these individuals include <u>causes of AUB</u> (i.e., <u>PALM-COEIN</u>), <u>cervicitis</u>, and <u>vulvovaginal atrophy</u>. Vaginal bleeding in pregnant individuals has different causes at < 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u> (e.g., <u>ectopic pregnancy</u>, <u>spontaneous abortion</u>) and > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u> (e.g., <u>placenta previa</u>, <u>placental abruption</u>); the latter are detailed in “<u>Antepartum hemorrhage</u>.” Premenarchal vaginal bleeding can be caused by <u>precocious puberty</u>, retained vaginal foreign bodies, trauma, and rarely, tumors. Vaginal bleeding in <u>adolescents</u> is usually due to <u>causes of AUB</u>. In all patients, diagnosis involves a focused history and examination and targeted testing based on clinical suspicion. A urine <u>pregnancy test</u> should be obtained in patients who can become pregnant. When imaging is required, <u>transvaginal ultrasound</u> (<u>TVUS</u>) is the preferred modality in <u>premenopausal</u> individuals and early <u>pregnancy</u>; transabdominal <u>ultrasound</u> is an option in later <u>pregnancy</u> and in children and <u>adolescents</u>. <u>Malignancy</u> (e.g., <u>endometrial cancer</u>) must be ruled out in all <u>postmenopausal</u> individuals; <u>endometrial sampling</u> must be performed if <u>indications for endometrial sampling</u> exist. Trauma (including sexual trauma), <u>coagulopathy</u>, and endocrine disorders are common nonobstetric, nongynecologic causes of bleeding that should be evaluated if clinically suspected. Treatment is based on the underlying cause.

## Vaginal bleeding in nonpregnant adults

### Definitions




* <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> (<u>AUB</u>): any of the following (see “<u>FIGO-AUB classification system 1</u>” for details) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-YXD79)

* Abnormal bleeding (classified according to frequency, duration, regularity, and volume)

* <u>Intermenstrual bleeding</u>

* <u>Unscheduled bleeding</u>

* Postmenopausal bleeding: bleeding that occurs ≥ 12 months after the onset of permanent <u>amenorrhea</u> due to age-related loss of ovarian follicular function  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEcL9V0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oLd0Aq0)

* Postcoital bleeding: spotting or bleeding that occurs during or after intercourse and is unrelated to <u>menstruation</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lodvXI0)





### Etiology [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g21FST0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ce1aBf0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xodEVI0)




Many of these conditions manifest with either painful or painless bleeding depending on several factors (e.g., severity, stage, location). See also “<u>Causes of abnormal uterine bleeding</u>.”




#### Painless bleeding




* <u>Endometrial</u> causes

* <u>Endometrial polyps</u>

* <u>Endometrial hyperplasia</u>

* <u>Endometrial cancer</u> (early stages)

* <u>Endometrial</u> <u>atrophy</u>  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xodEVI0)

* Cervical causes

* <u>Cervical ectropion</u>

* <u>Cervical polyps</u>

* <u>Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia</u> (<u>CIN</u>) or <u>cervical cancer</u> (early stages)

* Other causes

* <u>Ovulatory dysfunction</u> (e.g., due to endocrine disorders such as <u>hypothyroidism</u>, <u>hyperprolactinemia</u>, or <u>polycystic ovary syndrome</u>)

* Coagulopathies (e.g., <u>von Willebrand disease</u>)

* <u>Iatrogenic</u>, e.g.,

* <u>IUD</u> displacement

* Adverse effects of medications such as <u>hormone replacement therapy</u>, <u>oral contraceptives</u>, or <u>tamoxifen</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEcL9V0)





#### Painful bleeding




* <u>Endometrial</u> causes

* <u>Adenomyosis</u>

* <u>Uterine leiomyoma</u>

* <u>Endometrial</u> dysfunction (e.g., <u>pelvic inflammatory disease</u>, chronic <u>endometritis</u>)

* <u>Endometriosis</u>

* <u>Endometrial cancer</u> (advanced stages)

* Cervical causes

* <u>Cervicitis</u>

* <u>Cervical cancer</u> (advanced stages) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEcL9V0)

* Vulvovaginal causes

* <u>Vaginal injuries</u>, including <u>sexual abuse</u>

* <u>Vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia</u> (<u>VaIN</u>) or <u>vaginal cancer</u>

* <u>Vulvovaginal atrophy</u>, e.g., due to <u>genitourinary syndrome of menopause</u> (<u>GSM</u>) [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rbXfE9)

* <u>Vulvovaginitis</u>

* <u>Retained foreign body</u>

* Other causes

* <u>Ruptured ovarian cyst</u>

* <u>Postoperative complications</u>





### Clinical evaluation




#### Focused history [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g21FST0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uEWpxM0)




* Onset, duration, frequency, and amount of bleeding

* Associated symptoms, e.g.:

* <u>Pelvic pain</u>

* <u>Vaginal discharge</u>

* <u>Dyspareunia in women</u>

* <u>Symptoms of bleeding disorders</u>

* <u>Symptoms of hypothyroidism</u>

* <u>Sexual history</u>

* <u>Gynecologic and obstetric history</u>

* Current and past <u>medication review</u>

* Relevant <u>risk factors</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Risk factors for ectopic pregnancy</u>

* <u>Risk factors for cervical cancer</u>

* <u>Risk factors for endometrial cancer</u>

* <u>Risk factors for STIs</u>

* <u>Family history</u> (e.g., of gynecological malignancies, <u>bleeding disorders</u>)





#### Focused examination [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g21FST0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uEWpxM0)




* <u>Vital signs</u>: for features of <u>hemodynamic instability</u>

* <u>Abdominal examination</u>

* <u>Pelvic examination</u>

* External genital examination: Inspect the external anogenital region and urethral orifice for any sources of bleeding.

* <u>Bimanual examination</u>: Assess for uterine enlargement, <u>adnexal masses</u>, and <u>cervical motion tenderness</u>.

* <u>Speculum examination</u>: Assess for vaginal and cervical sources of bleeding.

* Assessment for <u>clinical features of genitourinary trauma</u>

* Evaluation for nonobstetric and nongynecologic causes of bleeding as indicated, e.g.:

* <u>Clinical features of bleeding disorders</u>

* <u>Examination of the thyroid gland</u>

* <u>Clinical features of hyperandrogenism</u>





> [!TIP]
> Use a <u>trauma-informed approach</u> during the clinical evaluation, especially if <u>sexual assault</u> is suspected.




### Diagnosis




* Obtain a urine <u>pregnancy test</u> in all individuals who can become pregnant if <u>pregnancy</u> status is unknown. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g21FST0)

* Perform <u>diagnostics for menopause</u> if the patient's <u>menopausal</u> status is unclear.

* Tailor diagnostic approach and management based on <u>menopausal</u> status and nature of bleeding; see the respective sections.

## Premenopausal bleeding

### Approach [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g21FST0)




See “<u>Diagnosis of AUB</u>” and “<u>Treatment of AUB</u>” for comprehensive management.




* <u>Hemodynamically unstable</u> patients

* Manage <u>hemorrhagic shock</u> with <u>immediate hemodynamic support</u> including <u>emergency blood transfusion</u>.

* <u>Reverse anticoagulants</u> if indicated.

* Consult <u>OB/GYN</u> and/or interventional radiology urgently.

* Consider acute <u>pharmacological treatment of AUB</u> (e.g., high-dose <u>estrogen</u>, <u>tranexamic acid</u>).

* Perform <u>emergency preoperative evaluation</u> if <u>surgery</u> or <u>uterine artery embolization</u> is indicated (see “<u>Surgery for AUB</u>”).

* Hemodynamically stable patients: Identify and treat the underlying cause.





> [!WARNING]
> Rule out <u>pregnancy</u> in any <u>premenopausal</u> patient who presents with vaginal bleeding.

## Postmenopausal bleeding

### Approach [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEcL9V0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ce1aBf0)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lFdvR70)




#### Individuals not on <u>HRT</u>




* Assess for <u>risk factors for endometrial cancer</u>.

* No <u>risk factors</u> and first episode of bleeding: Perform <u>TVUS</u> or <u>endometrial sampling</u>.

* <u>Risk factors</u> or other <u>indications for endometrial sampling</u> present: Perform <u>endometrial sampling</u>.

* Determine need for further testing.

* Abnormal <u>endometrial sampling</u>: Refer to a specialist  for additional evaluation.

* <u>Endometrial</u> thickness ≤ 4mm on <u>TVUS</u> and/or normal <u>endometrial sampling</u>: No further testing.

* <u>Endometrial</u> thickness > 4 mm or inadequate visualization on <u>TVUS</u>: Perform <u>endometrial sampling</u> if not already performed.

* <u>Endometrial sampling</u> insufficient for diagnosis: Consider <u>TVUS</u> if not already performed.  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ce1aBf0)

* Persistent or recurrent symptoms:

* Obtain <u>hysteroscopy</u>-guided <u>endometrial sampling</u> if not already performed.

* Otherwise, refer to specialist for additional evaluation.

* Evaluate for other <u>common causes of PMB</u> as indicated.

* Treat underlying cause.





#### Individuals on <u>HRT</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oLd0Aq0)




* Refer to a specialist.

* Diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>TVUS</u>, <u>endometrial sampling</u>) may be indicated depending on:

* Presence of <u>risk factors for endometrial cancer</u>

* Extent of bleeding

* Time since <u>HRT</u> initiation or dosage change  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEcL9V0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oLd0Aq0)

* Adjust <u>HRT</u> regimen if indicated.





> [!WARNING]
> A comprehensive evaluation of all individuals with <u>PMB</u> is essential to rule out <u>endometrial cancer</u> and <u>endometrial intraepithelial neoplasia</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ce1aBf0)

## Postcoital bleeding

### Approach [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lodvXI0)[[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kKdmgI0)




There is currently no standard diagnostic algorithm for <u>postcoital bleeding</u>. Depending on extent and nature of bleeding, perform <u>diagnostics for AUB</u> and <u>evaluation of PMB</u> as indicated.




* Obtain a <u>pregnancy test</u> in all individuals who can become pregnant.

* Evaluate for cervical pathology, including:

* <u>Diagnostics for cervicitis</u>

* <u>Cervical cancer screening</u> (if not up-to-date)

* <u>Colposcopy</u> in selected patients, e.g.:

* As part of <u>risk-based management for abnormal cervical cancer screening results</u>

* With <u>features suggestive of cervical cancer</u> on <u>pelvic examination</u>

* For persistent bleeding, refer to gynecology for consideration of additional workup.





> [!WARNING]
> Rule out <u>endometrial cancer</u> in <u>postmenopausal</u> patients with <u>postcoital bleeding</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lodvXI0)

## Vaginal bleeding in pregnancy

### Etiology [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9odNVI0)[[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g2XFSx)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V81Gli0)[[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/821OjT0)




#### All patients




* <u>Postcoital bleeding</u>

* <u>Trauma in pregnancy</u>

* <u>Bleeding disorders</u>





#### Early <u>pregnancy</u> bleeding (patients < 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>)




* Painless

* <u>Implantation bleeding</u>

* <u>Threatened abortion</u>

* Painful

* <u>Ectopic pregnancy</u> or <u>heterotopic pregnancy</u>

* <u>Spontaneous abortion</u>

* <u>Subchorionic hematoma</u>

* <u>Gestational trophoblastic disease</u>





#### <u>Antepartum hemorrhage</u> (patients > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>)




* Painless

* <u>Placenta previa</u>

* <u>Vasa previa</u>

* Painful

* <u>Placental abruption</u> (e.g., due to <u>trauma in pregnancy</u>)

* <u>Uterine rupture</u>

* See “<u>Differential diagnosis of antepartum bleeding</u>” for a more comprehensive list.





#### <u>Postpartum hemorrhage</u> (delivery to 12 weeks postpartum)




* Primary <u>postpartum hemorrhage</u> (≤ 24 hours postpartum)

* <u>Uterine atony</u>

* <u>Abnormal placental separation</u>

* <u>Birth trauma</u>

* Secondary <u>postpartum hemorrhage</u> (24 hours to 12 weeks postpartum)

* <u>Retained products of conception</u>

* <u>Coagulation disorders</u>

* See “<u>Differential diagnosis of postpartum hemorrhage</u>” for a more comprehensive list.





### Initial management [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g2XFSx)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V81Gli0)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eS1xAT0)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bobH0u)




* Assess hemodynamic status.

* <u>Hemodynamically unstable</u> patients

* Manage <u>hemorrhagic shock</u> with <u>immediate hemodynamic support</u> including <u>emergency blood transfusion</u>.

* Urgently consult <u>OB/GYN</u> to evaluate the need for emergency <u>surgery</u>.

* <u>RhD-negative</u> individuals: Provide <u>management of RhD-negative individuals during pregnancy</u>.

* Identify and treat the underlying cause (see “<u>Common causes of vaginal bleeding in pregnancy</u>”).

* See also:

* “<u>Management of ruptured ectopic pregnancy</u>”

* “<u>Initial management of antepartum hemorrhage</u>” for patients > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>

* “<u>Postpartum hemorrhage</u>”





> [!TIP]
> Administer <u>anti-D immunoglobulin</u> to all nonsensitized <u>RhD-negative</u> pregnant patients with vaginal bleeding, ideally within 72 hours of symptom onset. [[43]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eqYxxJ)




### Clinical evaluation [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g2XFSx)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V81Gli0)




#### Focused history




* Onset, duration, frequency, and amount of bleeding

* Prior bleeding episodes during <u>pregnancy</u>

* Associated symptoms (e.g., <u>pain</u>)

* <u>Obstetric and gynecologic history</u>

* Relevant <u>risk factors</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Risk factors for ectopic pregnancy</u>

* <u>Risk factors for spontaneous abortion</u>





#### Focused examination




* <u>Vital signs</u>: for features of <u>hemodynamic instability</u>

* <u>Clinical assessment of volume status</u>

* <u>Abdominal examination</u>, including assessment for <u>peritoneal signs</u>

* <u>Pelvic examination</u>

* All patients: external genital examination

* In patients < 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>, perform a bimanual and <u>speculum examination</u> to assess for:

* Nonobstetric causes of bleeding (e.g., infection, <u>cervical polyps</u>)

* <u>Adnexal masses</u> or tenderness

* <u>Products of conception</u>

* Open or closed <u>cervical os</u>

* In patients > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>, do not perform a <u>pelvic examination</u> until after <u>placenta previa</u> and <u>vasa previa</u> have been excluded on <u>TVUS</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> In pregnant patients > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u> with vaginal bleeding, evaluate the <u>placenta</u> with <u>TVUS</u> before performing a <u>pelvic examination</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### Diagnostics [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g2XFSx)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V81Gli0)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bobH0u)[[44]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RtdlW70)




* All patients

* Obtain <u>CBC</u> and <u>type and screen</u>.

* Consider additional <u>laboratory studies</u> based on clinical suspicion (e.g., <u>coagulation studies</u> in patients with suspected <u>bleeding disorders</u>). [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g2XFSx)

* Patients < 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u>: Obtain <u>β-hCG</u> and <u>TVUS</u>.  [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/821OjT0)[[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BUWz2k0)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B1czQY0)

* <u>Sonographic signs of intrauterine pregnancy</u> (<u>IUP</u>): Manage as <u>threatened abortion</u>; see “<u>Spontaneous abortion</u>” for details. [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W81Pli0)

* <u>TVUS findings of pregnancy failure</u>: See “<u>Spontaneous abortion</u>.” [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W81Pli0)

* <u>TVUS findings suggestive of ectopic pregnancy</u>: Perform <u>diagnostics for ectopic pregnancy</u>. [[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HMWKJN0)

* <u>Pregnancy of unknown location</u>: Arrange close follow-up (e.g., within 48 hours) for re-evaluation.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5Tcirb0)

* Patients > 20 weeks' <u>gestation</u> [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/821OjT0)

* Obtain <u>TVUS</u> and/or transabdominal <u>ultrasound</u> to assess for <u>placenta previa</u> and <u>vasa previa</u>.

* Perform <u>electronic fetal heart rate monitoring</u>.

* See “<u>Antepartum hemorrhage</u>” for additional details.

* <u>Postpartum hemorrhage</u>: usually a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>; may be supplemented by <u>ultrasound</u> (see “<u>Postpartum hemorrhage</u>” for details) [[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bobH0u)





> [!WARNING]
> Suspect <u>ectopic pregnancy</u> in all patients with vaginal bleeding unless <u>IUP</u> is confirmed on <u>ultrasound</u>. Consider <u>heterotopic pregnancy</u> in patients receiving <u>infertility treatment</u>. [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BUWz2k0)[[46]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B1czQY0)




> [!TIP]
> <u>TVUS</u> is the preferred imaging modality to evaluate bleeding in the <u>first trimester</u> of <u>pregnancy</u>. [[45]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BUWz2k0)
