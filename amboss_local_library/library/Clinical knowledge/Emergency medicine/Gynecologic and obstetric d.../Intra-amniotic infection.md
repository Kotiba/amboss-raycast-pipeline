# Intra-amniotic infection

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yw0dlR)

## Summary

Intra-amniotic infection, also called chorioamnionitis, is confirmed infection and inflammation of intrauterine structures of <u>pregnancy</u> (e.g., fetus, <u>placenta</u>, <u>amniotic fluid</u>). Intra-amniotic infection most commonly occurs during <u>labor</u> due to ascending vaginal flora. Intra-amniotic infection is presumptively diagnosed based on maternal and fetal signs and symptoms (i.e., maternal <u>fever</u>, fetal <u>tachycardia</u>, prurulent cervical discharge) and presence of maternal <u>leukocytosis</u>; confirmatory diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>amniotic fluid</u> studies, <u>placental</u> pathology) are typically not available until after delivery. Management includes immediate <u>empiric antibiotic therapy for intra-amniotic infection</u> and delivery of the fetus. Serious complications include <u>sepsis</u>, <u>postpartum hemorrhage</u>, and <u>intrauterine fetal demise</u>.

---

## Definitions

* Intra-amniotic infection (chorioamnionitis) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* Confirmed microbial invasion of the <u>amniotic cavity</u> causing infection and inflammation of the fetus, <u>amniotic fluid</u>, fetal membranes, <u>placenta</u>, and/or <u>endometrial</u> <u>decidua</u>

* Usually only diagnosed after delivery, from studies performed on <u>amniotic fluid</u> or <u>placenta</u>

* Clinical chorioamnionitis: a clinical syndrome comprising maternal <u>fever</u> and signs of maternal and/or fetal inflammation, usually caused by intra-amniotic infection or, less commonly, <u>sterile intra-amniotic inflammation</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)





> [!WARNING]
> Although intra-amniotic infection and <u>clinical chorioamnionitis</u> are distinct entities, they cannot usually be distinguished from one another before delivery. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

## Etiology

### Route of spread [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)




* Most common: ascending vaginal flora, particularly during <u>labor</u>  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_bV5wG0)

* Rarely

* After <u>invasive prenatal diagnostic testing</u> or fetal procedures

* Through hematogenous spread via the <u>placenta</u> (e.g., <u>Listeria monocytogenes</u>)





### <u>Risk factors</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)




* <u>Prolonged labor</u> and/or <u>PROM</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* Repeated digital cervical examinations

* Use of internal <u>intrapartum</u> monitoring (e.g., fetal scalp monitor)

* Maternal <u>colonization</u> with <u>group B Streptococcus</u> or <u>STIs</u>

* Presence of <u>meconium</u>

* Low <u>parity</u>

* History of intra-amniotic infection [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)





### Common organisms [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)




Polymicrobial infection with aerobic and <u>anaerobic bacteria</u> is most common.




* <u>Ureaplasma urealyticum</u> (> 60% of infections) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* <u>Mycoplasma</u> hominis

* <u>Bacteroides</u> spp.

* <u>E. coli</u>

* <u>Group B Streptococcus</u>

* <u>Gardnerella vaginalis</u>

* <u>Staphylococcus aureus</u>

* <u>Listeria monocytogenes</u>

* Species commonly found in the oral <u>microbiota</u> (e.g., Porphyromonas spp., <u>Fusobacterium</u> spp., <u>Streptococcus</u> oralis)

* <u>Candida</u> spp.

## Clinical features

The following features indicate <u>clinical chorioamnionitis</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)




* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Tachycardia</u>

* Uterine tenderness

* Malodorous <u>amniotic fluid</u>

* <u>Purulent</u> cervical discharge

* Fetal <u>tachycardia</u>

* Maternal or neonatal <u>complications of intra-amniotic infection</u>





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Fever</u> is not always present in intra-amniotic infection. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)

## Management

### Approach




* Check <u>CBC</u> to evaluate for <u>leukocytosis</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* Start immediate treatment if the criteria for suspected intra-amniotic infection are met. 

* Start <u>empiric antibiotics</u> for intra-amniotic infection.

* <u>Treat sepsis</u> if present.

* Give <u>antipyretics</u>.

* Consult with specialists. 

* Consult obstetrics and/or <u>maternal-fetal medicine</u> for pregnant patients. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)

* <u>Manage as high-risk pregnancy</u>.

* Delivery is required to resolve the infection; begin <u>augmentation of labor</u> if protracted.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)

* Consult <u>pediatrics</u> for neonatal management; see also: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* "<u>Approach to suspected neonatal bacterial infection</u>"

* "<u>Neonatal risk assessment for early-onset GBS infection</u>"

* Obtain <u>confirmatory diagnostic studies for intra-amniotic infection</u>.





> [!TIP]
> The route of delivery is determined by standard indications; infection alone is not typically an indication for <u>cesarean delivery</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)




> [!WARNING]
> Intra-amniotic infection before the <u>third trimester</u> is uncommon. Consult a specialist, as management is based on <u>gestation</u> and <u>pathogen</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AbVRwG0)




### Criteria for suspected intra-amnitoic infection  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)




* Maternal temperature 38.0–38.9°C and ≥ 1 of the following: 

* Maternal <u>leukocytosis</u> > 15,000 cells/μL

* <u>Purulent</u> cervical drainage

* Fetal <u>tachycardia</u> > 160 beats/minute

* OR isolated maternal temperature ≥ 39.0 °C [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* OR <u>clinical features of intra-amniotic infection</u> in the absence of maternal <u>fever</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0aVeQG0)





### Empiric antibiotic therapy for intra-amniotic infection  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)




* First-line therapy

* <u>Ampicillin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* PLUS <u>gentamicin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* Following <u>cesarean delivery</u>

* At least one additional dose of <u>ampicillin</u> and <u>gentamicin</u>

* PLUS <u>clindamycin</u> DOSAGE OR <u>metronidazole</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE

* <u>Nonsevere penicillin allergy</u>[[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* <u>Cefazolin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* PLUS <u>gentamicin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE

* <u>Severe penicillin allergy</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* <u>Clindamycin</u> DOSAGE OR <u>vancomycin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* PLUS <u>gentamicin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE





> [!TIP]
> After <u>cesarean delivery</u>, one additional dose of the <u>antibiotic</u> regimen is recommended; after vaginal delivery, discontinue <u>antibiotics</u> unless there is evidence of ongoing infection. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)




### Confirmatory diagnostic studies for intra-amniotic infection




The following results, combined with clinical features of infection, confirm the diagnosis; however, results are typically unavailable until after delivery.




* Analysis of <u>amniotic fluid</u>: Obtain via <u>amniocentesis</u> or transcervical <u>amniotic fluid</u> collector. ;  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* <u>Gram stain</u> and culture showing bacteria

* White cells: ≥ 50 cells/mm<sup>3</sup>

* <u>Glucose</u>: < 1–4 mg/dL at term  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* If available, consider the following: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)

* IL–6 and IL–8: elevated

* Matrix metalloproteinase-8 (MMP–8): > 23 ng/mL

* <u>Molecular diagnostics</u> (e.g., <u>PCR</u>)

* <u>Placental</u> <u>histopathology</u> consistent with infection and/or inflammation [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)





> [!TIP]
> If there are no signs of infection on confirmatory diagnostic studies, consider <u>differential diagnoses for intra-amniotic infection</u>.

## Complications

### Maternal [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)




* <u>Protracted labor</u> leading to interventions

* Postpartum <u>uterine atony</u>, hemorrhage

* Additional infections (e.g., <u>endometritis</u>, <u>peritonitis</u>, wound infection)

* Complications of severe infection, e.g.:

* <u>Sepsis in pregnancy and postpartum</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* <u>Disseminated intravascular coagulation</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* <u>Acute respiratory distress syndrome</u>

* <u>Hypercoagulable state</u> (e.g., thromboembolic disease, <u>septic pelvic thrombophlebitis</u>)

* <u>Death</u>





### Neonatal [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Crdqjr0)




* Infection and/or <u>sepsis</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* <u>Asphyxia</u>, <u>intraventricular hemorrhage</u>, <u>cerebral palsy</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_ZV5VG0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zZVrVG0)

* <u>Premature birth</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Z0VZeG0)

* <u>Meconium aspiration syndrome</u>

* <u>Encephalopathy</u>

* <u>Bronchopulmonary dysplasia</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)

* Fetal <u>death</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2IdTXr0)





We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.
