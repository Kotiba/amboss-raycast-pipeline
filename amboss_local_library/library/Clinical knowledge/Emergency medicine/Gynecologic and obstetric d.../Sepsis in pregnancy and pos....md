# Sepsis in pregnancy and postpartum

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Gynecologic and obstetric disorders > Sepsis in pregnancy and postpartum*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tD0XTR)

---

## Summary

<u>Sepsis</u> in <u>pregnancy</u> and the <u>postpartum period</u> is characterized by life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated <u>immune response</u> to infection. It can be due to obstetric-related causes (e.g., <u>intra-amniotic infection</u>, <u>septic abortion</u>) or nonobstetric causes (e.g., <u>urinary tract infection</u>, <u>pneumonia</u>). Clinical features are similar to those in nonpregnant or postpartum individuals, but some normal physiological changes in <u>pregnancy</u> can mask early signs. Diagnosis and management are similar to those for <u>sepsis</u> in nonpregnant individuals; when <u>sepsis</u> is suspected, early <u>fluid resuscitation</u>, <u>empiric antibiotics</u>, and referral to a specialist (e.g., maternal fetal-medicine, intensive care) is recommended to reduce <u>morbidity</u> and mortality.

---

## Definitions

* Maternal <u>sepsis</u>: organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated <u>immune response</u> to infection that occurs during <u>pregnancy</u>, <u>childbirth</u>, or up to 42 days after the end of <u>pregnancy</u> (i.e., delivery, abortion, or <u>pregnancy loss</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w-dhzs0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* Postpartum sepsis: maternal <u>sepsis</u> that develops between <u>birth</u> and 6 weeks postpartum [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/G6cBmW0)

## Etiology

### Common sources [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)




* Obstetric

* <u>Septic abortion</u>

* <u>Intra-amniotic infection</u>

* <u>Postpartum endometritis</u>

* Soft-tissue infection or wound infection (e.g., from <u>cesarean delivery</u>, perineal wound, <u>mastitis</u>)

* Nonobstetric

* <u>Urinary tract infection</u> (<u>UTI</u>) and <u>pyelonephritis</u>  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* <u>Pneumonia</u>

* Gastrointestinal causes (e.g., <u>appendicitis</u>, <u>cholangitis</u>)

* See also “<u>Etiology of bloodstream infections</u>.”





### Pathogens




* Common bacterial pathogens (infection is often polymicrobial) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* Often polymicrobial

* <u>Escherichia coli</u>

* <u>Streptococci</u> spp. (e.g., group A <u>streptococcus</u>)

* <u>Staphylococcus aureus</u> (<u>MRSA</u>)

* <u>S. epidermidis</u>

* <u>Klebsiella pneumoniae</u>

* <u>Proteus</u>

* <u>Haemophilus influenza</u>

* <u>Listeria monocytogenes</u>

* Viral and fungal pathogens [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* <u>Influenza</u>

* <u>Varicella zoster</u>

* <u>Herpes simplex virus</u>

* <u>COVID-19</u>

* <u>Candida</u> spp. (rare but high mortality)





### <u>Risk factors</u> for <u>sepsis</u> in <u>pregnancy</u> and postpartum [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/G6cBmW0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tAdXls0)




* Chronic conditions (e.g., <u>diabetes mellitus</u>, chronic <u>hypertension</u>, <u>obesity</u>, <u>chronic kidney disease</u>, <u>congestive heart failure</u>) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4Ad3Ps0)

* <u>Cesarean delivery</u>

* <u>Retained products of conception</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* <u>Prolonged rupture of membranes</u>

* Invasive procedures (e.g., <u>amniocentesis</u>, <u>cerclage</u>, uterine instrumentation) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* Trauma (e.g., <u>obstetric lacerations</u>)

* Prolonged uterine bleeding [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

* <u>Multifetal gestation</u>

* <u>Nulliparity</u>

* Adverse <u>social determinants of health</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4Ad3Ps0)





> [!WARNING]
> Immunological down regulation that occurs during <u>pregnancy</u> and persists into the <u>postpartum period</u> increases the risk for <u>sepsis</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)

## Management

<u>Management of sepsis</u> is similar in pregnant and postpartum individuals and nonpregnant individuals.




### Initial management




* <u>Sepsis screening tools</u> (e.g., <u>quick SOFA score</u>) have not been validated for use in obstetrics.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QAdujs0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Start <u>management of maternal sepsis</u> if new-onset organ dysfunction or ≥ 2 of the following occur: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Temperature: â¥ 38.0Â°C

* <u>Heart rate</u>: > 110/minute

* <u>Respiratory rate</u>: > 24/minute

* <u>WBCs</u>: > 15,000/mmÂ³ or < 4,000/mmÂ³ or > 10% bands

* Initiate <u>fluid resuscitation</u> with balanced <u>crystalloid</u> fluid.

* No <u>signs of shock</u>: 1–2 L over 3 hours  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BAdzNs0)

* <u>Signs of shock</u> (e.g., <u>mean arterial pressure</u> < 65 mm Hg): <u>Manage as maternal septic shock</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Start <u>empiric antibiotics</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Use source-directed <u>antibiotics</u> if a focus is suspected.

* Use <u>empiric antibiotics for maternal sepsis</u> with coverage for obstetric causes if the source is unknown.

* Consult specialists as needed.

* Infectious diseases: to tailor <u>antibiotic</u> choices [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Obstetrics and/or <u>maternal-fetal medicine</u>: for obstetric management

* <u>ICU</u>: for management of patients with <u>septic shock</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* Neonatology: to stabilize premature <u>neonates</u> and evaluate for <u>neonatal sepsis</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XLW9wN0)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Sepsis</u> is a <u>medical emergency</u>. Monitor closely for early <u>signs of sepsis</u>, and start the <u>hour-1 bundle</u> immediately if <u>sepsis</u> is suspected. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)




### Empiric antibiotics for maternal sepsis [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VUdGX60)




* <u>Ampicillin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VUdGX60)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* PLUS <u>gentamicin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VUdGX60)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)

* PLUS <u>clindamycin</u>  DOSAGE OR <u>metronidazole</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VUdGX60)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)





### Management of maternal septic shock [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)




* Give fluid boluses (≤ 30 mL/kg) to maintain <u>MAP</u> ≥ 65 mm Hg.

* If <u>vasopressors</u> are required for <u>hypoperfusion</u>, give <u>norepinephrine</u>.

* Consider low-dose <u>steroids</u> if there is insufficient response to <u>vasopressors</u> after 4 hours.

* Initiate <u>deep vein thrombosis prophylaxis</u>.

* Initiate <u>insulin therapy</u> if <u>glucose</u> rises to > 180 mg/dL.

* See also "<u>Resuscitation in septic shock</u>."





### Obstetric management [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/iAdJjs0)




* <u>Manage as high-risk pregnancy</u>.

* For viable fetuses:

* Monitor fetal well-being; consider continuous <u>electronic fetal heart rate monitoring</u>.

* Consider <u>corticosteroids for fetal lung maturation</u>.

* Perform the following as needed for source control:

* Delivery in patients with <u>intra-amniotic infection</u>

* Evacuation of <u>retained products of conception</u>
