# Ventricular fibrillation

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vS0AYf)

## Summary

Ventricular fibrillation (Vfib) is a life-threatening, pulseless <u>arrhythmia</u> that results in <u>cardiac arrest</u> and requires immediate <u>cardiopulmonary resuscitation</u> and <u>defibrillation</u> according to <u>advanced cardiac life support</u> (<u>ACLS</u>) protocols. Patients may experience <u>prodromal</u> symptoms such as <u>palpitations</u>, <u>dizziness</u>, and <u>chest pain</u> before Vfib rapidly results in <u>loss of consciousness</u> due to hemodynamic collapse. The most common cause is <u>ischemic heart disease</u> (e.g., <u>acute myocardial infarction</u>); other causes include <u>heart failure</u>, <u>cardiomyopathy</u>, electrophysiological disorders, and <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities. The diagnosis is confirmed by <u>ECG</u>, which shows a chaotic and irregular waveform with no discernible <u>P waves</u>, <u>QRS complexes</u>, or <u>T waves</u>. After <u>return of spontaneous circulation</u> (<u>ROSC</u>), management focuses on identifying and treating the underlying cause and <u>tertiary prevention</u> with an <u>automated implantable cardioverter-defibrillator</u> (<u>AICD</u>).

## Etiology

* Underlying cardiovascular disease [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)

* Most common: <u>ischemic heart disease</u>;  (e.g., <u>acute coronary syndrome</u>, previous <u>myocardial infarction</u>)

* <u>Myocarditis</u>

* <u>Cardiomyopathy</u>

* <u>Heart failure</u>

* <u>Valvular heart disease</u>

* <u>Congenital heart defects</u> (e.g., <u>tetralogy of Fallot</u>, <u>aortic stenosis</u>)

* Electrophysiological disorders

* <u>Wolff-Parkinson-White</u> syndrome [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5jViau0)

* <u>Long-QT syndrome</u> → <u>torsades de pointes</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jDY_Vr)

* <u>Brugada syndrome</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)

* <u>Electrolyte</u> abnormalities (e.g., <u>hypokalemia</u>, <u>hyperkalemia</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e1Vxft0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KgVUwF0)

* Precipitating drugs, e.g., <u>drug-induced long QT syndrome</u>, drug-induced <u>Brugada syndrome</u>, <u>stimulant intoxication</u>, <u>tricyclic antidepressant</u> (<u>TCA</u>) overdose, <u>cardiovascular drug poisoning</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)

* <u>Commotio cordis</u>, <u>blunt cardiac injury</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qdWCI40)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CgVqxF0)

* <u>Electrical injury</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ut1p2i0)

## Pathophysiology

* Normal electrical conduction can be disrupted by re-entry;   → chaotic, circulating excitation of the <u>myocardium</u> (= Vfib);  → simultaneous contractions at multiple foci → insufficient <u>cardiac output</u> → hemodynamic collapse → <u>loss of consciousness</u> and possibly <u>death</u> (<u>sudden cardiac death</u>)

* Re-entry can be caused by

* Changes to the conduction pathway;  (e.g., unexcitable <u>scar</u> tissue as a result of past <u>myocardial infarction</u>)

* Abnormal pattern of excitation, for example:

* If the period of activation and recovery of <u>myocardial cells</u>  becomes greater than the duration of an <u>action potential</u> (as in <u>long-QT syndrome</u>)

* If excitation occurs outside of the normal pattern of activation (<u>premature ventricular complex</u>, <u>PVC</u> )

## Clinical features

* Rapid <u>loss of consciousness</u> and <u>cardiac arrest</u>: requires immediate <u>CPR</u> and <u>defibrillation</u>

* Possible early signs 

* <u>Chest pain</u>

* <u>Palpitation</u>

* Fatigue

* <u>Shortness of breath</u>

* <u>Dizziness</u>

---

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)




* Identify Vfib based on <u>ECG</u> or <u>rhythm strip</u>.

* Begin <u>initial management of Vfib</u> immediately.

* Evaluate for underlying <u>causes of Vfib</u> concurrently with <u>CPR</u> and <u>defibrillation</u> or as soon as feasible; see also "<u>Reversible causes of cardiac arrest</u>."





### <u>ECG</u> findings [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)




* Ventricular fibrillation  

* Arrhythmic, fibrillatory baseline; , usually > 300/minute

* Erratic undulations with indiscernible <u>QRS complexes</u>

* No atrial <u>P waves</u>

* No <u>T waves</u>

* Commonly preceded by <u>ventricular tachycardia</u>

* Ventricular flutter 

* Ventricular rates of ∼ 300/minute

* Regular, monomorphic, sinusoidal waveform

* Can degenerate into Vfib

## Management

### Initial management of Vfib [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LgVwDF0)




Vfib is a pulseless <u>shockable rhythm</u>; follow standard <u>cardiac arrest</u> protocols.




* All patients: Begin <u>ACLS</u>. 

* Immediate <u>CPR</u> and <u>defibrillation</u>; see "<u>Acute management checklist for shockable rhythms</u>."

* Administer <u>amiodarone</u> and <u>lidocaine</u> according to the <u>ACLS algorithm</u>.

* Concurrently manage acutely <u>reversible causes of cardiac arrest</u>.

* No response or unclear response to ≥ 3 standard shocks (i.e., persisting Vfib) [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LgVwDF0)

* Troubleshoot standard <u>defibrillation</u> technique, e.g., check pad contact and energy dosage.

* The benefits of alternative <u>shock</u> techniques (e.g., <u>double sequential defibrillation</u>), <u>procainamide</u>, <u>bretylium</u>, and <u>sotalol</u> are unclear in persisting Vfib. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LgVwDF0)

* <u>ROSC</u> achieved: Begin <u>post-resuscitation care</u>.

* <u>Secure the airway</u>.

* Maintain target respiratory and <u>hemodynamic parameters</u>.

* Obtain <u>ECG</u> to:

* Identify patients who need emergency <u>coronary artery revascularization</u>.

* Screen for <u>ECG</u> <u>red flags</u> for <u>sudden cardiac death</u>, e.g., accessory pathways, prolonged <u>QTc</u>, <u>epsilon wave</u>, <u>Brugada pattern</u>.

* Initiate <u>targeted temperature management</u> for persistently unresponsive patients.[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/c9Wamn0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-SVDX80)

* Admit to <u>ICU</u>.   [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7jV4bu0)

* Recurrence of Vfib after <u>ROSC</u>: Treat as <u>electrical storm</u> and consider IV antiarrhythmic infusions (e.g., <u>beta blockers</u>). [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LgVwDF0)





> [!WARNING]
> Do not routinely administer <u>calcium</u>, <u>magnesium</u>, or <u>sodium bicarbonate</u> to treat Vfib. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LgVwDF0)




### Further management [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)




* Further evaluation and management of underlying <u>causes of Vfib</u>: based on clinical suspicion and under specialist guidance

* Cardiovascular disease (e.g., <u>acute coronary syndrome</u>)

* <u>Cardiac biomarkers</u>

* <u>Echocardiography</u>

* <u>Coronary angiography</u>

* Other <u>cardiac imaging</u> (e.g., <u>cardiac MRI</u>)

* Drugs that can induce Vfib, e.g., <u>cocaine</u>, <u>TCAs</u>, <u>digoxin</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0SVeyF0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0rXefz)

* <u>Tertiary prevention</u>: depends on the underlying cause

* <u>AICD</u> implantation: indicated for patients with an irreversible <u>cause of Vfib</u> and expected survival of > 1 year (See also "<u>Indications for AICD</u>.") [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xBYE17)

* Definitive treatment of the underlying <u>cause of Vfib</u> (e.g., <u>antiarrhythmics</u>, <u>ablation</u>) under specialist guidance
