# Management of tachycardia

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Emergency medicine > Cardiovascular disorders > Management of tachycardia*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tq0XZh)

## Summary

<u>Tachycardia</u> is defined as a <u>heart rate</u> that exceeds 100/minute. <u>Signs of unstable tachycardia</u> include <u>chest pain</u>, <u>shock</u>, and impaired consciousness. <u>Unstable tachycardia</u> is considered an emergency and should be managed with immediate <u>electrical cardioversion</u>. In stable tachycardias, both the cardiac rhythm (i.e., regular or irregular) and the <u>QRS complex</u> (i.e., <u>narrow-complex tachycardia</u> and <u>wide-complex tachycardias</u>) should be evaluated. Undifferentiated regular <u>supraventricular tachycardia</u> (<u>SVTs</u>) should be initially managed with a trial of <u>vagal maneuvers</u> and, if necessary, IV <u>adenosine</u>. Irregular <u>SVTs</u> are usually caused by <u>atrial fibrillation</u> (<u>Afib</u>) or <u>atrial flutter</u>, which are managed either with a <u>rate control strategy</u> (e.g., with <u>β-blockers</u> or <u>calcium channel blockers</u>) or a <u>rhythm control strategy</u> (i.e., with <u>antiarrhythmics</u> or <u>electrical cardioversion</u>) in combination with risk-based anticoagulation therapy. Undifferentiated <u>wide-complex tachycardias</u> (<u>WCTs</u>) should be managed with <u>electrical cardioversion</u> or <u>pharmacological cardioversion</u>. If a stable patient becomes unstable at any point during management, immediate <u>electrical cardioversion</u> is recommended regardless of the rhythm. The content in this article is organized to first provide management steps for undifferentiated <u>arrhythmias</u> but also includes management for specific rhythms, once identified. <u>Unstable tachycardia</u> without a <u>pulse</u>, <u>ventricular fibrillation</u>, and <u>cardiac arrest</u> are not addressed here (see “<u>ACLS</u>”).

## Unstable tachycardia with pulse

For <u>unstable tachycardia</u> without a <u>pulse</u>, start <u>CPR</u> (see “<u>ACLS</u>”).




![Advanced cardiac life support (ACLS)](../../../../media/m3ie0paa7ynmnz59ahpo8z9p.jpg)

Advanced cardiac life support (ACLS)




### Signs of unstable tachycardia [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)




* Acutely <u>altered mental state</u> or <u>loss of consciousness</u>

* <u>Hypotension</u>

* <u>Shock</u>

* <u>Acute heart failure</u>

* <u>Ischemic</u> <u>chest pain</u>





### Initial management of unstable tachycardia with pulse




* Obtain a <u>crash cart</u>, <u>defibrillator</u> device, suction, bag-mask device, and <u>airway</u> and <u>intubation</u> equipment.

* Call for help (e.g., urgent cardiology consult and/or anesthesia consult)

* Administer <u>supplemental oxygen</u>, if needed.

* If unstable signs of <u>tachycardia</u> are present and instability is likely due to <u>tachycardia</u>, perform immediate <u>electrical cardioversion</u>. 

* HR > 150/min: Unstable signs are likely due to <u>tachycardia</u>.

* HR 100–150/min: Suspect another underlying condition.





### Electrical cardioversion [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EYNBI)




* <u>Synchronized electrical cardioversion</u> is indicated in almost all unstable patients with <u>tachycardia</u> and a <u>pulse</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)

* <u>Defibrillation</u> is only indicated if either of the following is the case: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5DYiUr)

* An irregular wide-complex cardiac rhythm is identified.

* The device fails to synchronize with the patient's cardiac rhythm.

* If <u>Afib with RVR</u> or rapid <u>atrial flutter</u> is identified, administer anticoagulation as soon as possible (see “<u>Pericardioversion anticoagulation for Afib</u>”).  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_8Y56I)

* Procedural sedation for cardioversion is typically necessary if the patient is conscious, but should not delay lifesaving intervention.

* Administer any one of the following: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DDY1gr)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/P9YWnr)

* <u>Etomidate</u> DOSAGE: preferred for <u>hemodynamically unstable</u> patients

* <u>Propofol</u> DOSAGE

* <u>Midazolam</u> DOSAGE

* Consider the addition of an <u>analgesic</u> (e.g., <u>fentanyl</u> DOSAGE).

* See “<u>Sedatives for procedural sedation</u>” for details.





> [!WARNING]
> Avoid using <u>sedatives for procedural sedation</u> at doses that can worsen <u>hypotension</u> in unstable patients.




> [!WARNING]
> Ensure oxygen is turned off or that the flow of oxygen is moved away from the patient during the procedure since <u>cardioversion</u> may trigger combustion.

Cardioversion vs defibrillation: performing the procedures




#### <u>Synchronized electrical cardioversion</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EYNBI)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5DYiUr)




* Definition: A form of <u>electrical cardioversion</u> in which a low-energy <u>shock</u> is delivered at or just after the peak of the R-wave of the <u>QRS complex</u>.

* Indications

* Unstable <u>narrow-complex tachycardia</u> with <u>pulse</u> (e.g., <u>supraventricular tachycardia</u>, including <u>Afib with RVR</u> and <u>atrial flutter</u>)

* Unstable regular <u>wide-complex tachycardia</u> with <u>pulse</u> (e.g., <u>monomorphic ventricular tachycardia</u>)

* Steps
1. * Preparation and <u>procedural sedation for cardioversion</u>
2. * Place paddles or electrode pads firmly on the thorax of the patient (anteroapical or anteroposterior position).
3. * Choose the synchronized (SYNC) mode of <u>shock</u> on the <u>defibrillator</u> device.
4. * Select the recommended dose of electrical energy according to the patient's cardiac rhythm. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EYNBI)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5DYiUr)

* Regular <u>narrow-complex tachycardia</u>: 50–100 J biphasic waveform

* Irregular <u>narrow-complex tachycardia</u>: 120–200 J biphasic waveform (preferred) OR 200 J monophasic [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/49Y3nr)

* Regular <u>wide-complex tachycardia</u>: 100 J biphasic waveform
5. * “Clear” the patient.
6. * Deliver <u>shock</u>.
7. * Reassess the rhythm and check the <u>pulse</u>.





#### Defibrillation [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/juY_II)




* Definition: A form of <u>electrical cardioversion</u> in which a high energy <u>shock</u> is delivered transthoracically to the precordium but not timed with any part of the <u>cardiac cycle</u>

* Indications

* Unstable irregular <u>wide-complex tachycardia</u> with <u>pulse</u> (e.g., <u>polymorphic ventricular tachycardia</u> with a <u>pulse</u>)

* Pulseless patient/<u>cardiac arrest</u> (See “<u>ACLS</u>.”)

* Steps
1. * Preparation and <u>procedural sedation for cardioversion</u>
2. * Place paddles or electrode pads firmly on the thorax of the patient (anteroapical or anteroposterior position).
3. * Choose the unsynchronized mode of <u>shock</u> on the <u>defibrillator</u> device.
4. * Select the <u>defibrillator</u> dose: 120–200 J biphasic waveform.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)
5. * “Clear” the patient.
6. * Deliver <u>shock</u>.
7. * Reassess the rhythm and check the <u>pulse</u>.





### Subsequent management




* Reversion to <u>sinus rhythm</u> (i.e., successful <u>cardioversion</u>)

* Urgent cardiology consult

* Consider anticoagulation.

* Transfer to <u>ICU</u> or CCU.

* Continuous <u>telemetry</u> and <u>pulse oximetry</u>

* Persistent <u>arrhythmia</u> (i.e., failure to <u>cardiovert</u>)

* Administer a second dose of <u>cardioversion</u>

* Call for expert help.

* Consider antiarrhythmic infusion in consultation with a cardiologist.

* Continue resuscitation.

* Deterioration to a life-threatening rhythm or pulseless patient: Start <u>CPR</u> (see “<u>ACLS</u>”).

## Stable, irregular narrow-complex tachycardia

### Initial management of stable, irregular narrow-complex tachycardia [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)



1. * Determine the underlying rhythm.

* If the underlying rhythm cannot be identified, see “<u>Management of undifferentiated SVT</u>.”

* If the patient has <u>rapid Afib</u> or <u>atrial flutter</u>, choose between <u>rate control</u> or <u>rhythm control strategy</u> and start anticoagulation (see “<u>Management of Afib with RVR</u>”).

* If the patient has <u>MAT</u>, start medications to control <u>heart rate</u> (See “<u>Management of MAT</u>”).

* If the distinction between <u>MAT</u> and <u>atrial fibrillation</u>/flutter cannot be made and expert consultation is not immediately available, start <u>rate control</u> (preferably with <u>diltiazem</u> DOSAGE) until expert opinion can be obtained.
2. * Consult cardiology for definitive management and follow-up.
