## Summary

<u>Heart failure</u> (HF) in children is rare and most often results from <u>congenital heart disease</u> or <u>primary cardiomyopathies</u> rather than acquired conditions. Symptoms are often nonspecific (e.g., feeding intolerance, <u>growth faltering</u>, <u>respiratory distress</u> in <u>infants</u>, and gastrointestinal symptoms in older children and <u>adolescents</u>). Diagnosis relies on <u>echocardiography</u> to define anatomy, ventricular function, and stage, and it is supported by <u>natriuretic peptide</u> levels and findings from basic <u>laboratory studies</u>, <u>ECG</u>, chest <u>x-ray</u>, as well as advanced testing performed by specialists. HF in children is classified based on stage (<u>ACC</u>/<u>AHA</u> staging adapted for children) and functional status (<u>Ross classification</u> in <u>infants</u> and young children, and <u>NYHA</u> in older children). Management of chronic HF focuses on treating the underlying cause, medication tailored to ventricular structure and function, and preventive care. <u>Mechanical circulatory support</u> or transplant may eventually be required. <u>Acute heart failure</u> (<u>AHF</u>) requires urgent stabilization, identification of reversible causes, and the targeted use of <u>diuretics</u>, <u>inotropes</u>, and/or mechanical support based on <u>perfusion</u> and congestion status.
