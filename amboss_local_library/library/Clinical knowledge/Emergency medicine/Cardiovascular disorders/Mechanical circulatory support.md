## Summary

Mechanical circulatory support (<u>MCS</u>) devices are used to manage refractory <u>cardiogenic shock</u> and <u>congestive heart failure</u> by improving systemic and <u>coronary artery</u> <u>perfusion</u> while reducing <u>myocardial oxygen demand</u>. <u>MCS</u> devices are categorized into temporary and long-term devices. <u>Left ventricular assist devices</u> (LVADs) are a type of long-term support that provide continuous blood flow through a surgically implanted pump connected to an external controller and power source. Since LVADs generate nonpulsatile flow, traditional <u>vital signs</u> such as <u>pulse</u>, blood pressure, and <u>pulse oximetry</u> may be inaccurate or absent, requiring the use of alternative measures (e.g., <u>Doppler opening pressure</u>). <u>LVAD parameters</u> are displayed on the <u>LVAD</u> controller and are used to assess for evidence of complications. <u>LVAD-associated complications</u> include infection, <u>shock</u>, <u>pump thrombosis</u>, and <u>suction events</u>.
