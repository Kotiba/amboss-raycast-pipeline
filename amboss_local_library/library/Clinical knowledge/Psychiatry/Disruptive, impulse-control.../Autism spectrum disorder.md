# Autism spectrum disorder

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Psychiatry > Disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorders > Autism spectrum disorder*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IP0YTT)

## Summary

Autism spectrum disorder (<u>ASD</u>) is a <u>neurodevelopmental disorder</u> characterized by persistent impairment in social interaction and communication and restricted stereotyped patterns of behavior, interests, and activities. Although onset occurs in early childhood, features of <u>ASD</u> may not become fully apparent until later, when social demands exceed abilities. The etiology of <u>ASD</u> is complex and multifactorial; genetic and environmental factors likely play a role. Clinical features of <u>ASD</u> may be noted by parents and/or caregivers, or detected during <u>screening for autism spectrum disorder</u>. Diagnosis is clinical and based upon the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ASD</u>; additional assessment for <u>conditions associated with autism</u> is recommended. Multimodal management should be initiated early and is typically carried out by a specialist (e.g., developmental <u>pediatrician</u>, child psychologist), a multidisciplinary team (e.g., <u>psychiatrist</u>, social worker, speech and language therapist), and/or publicly funded services (i.e., early intervention services or school services). Treatment targets impairments in communication and social interaction, as well as repetitive behaviors through behavioral, developmental, and educational interventions. Pharmacological treatment can be considered for specific problematic behaviors (e.g., <u>atypical antipsychotics</u> for irritability and aggression) and comorbid conditions (e.g., <u>ADHD</u>, <u>anxiety</u>).

## Etiology

The etiology of autism spectrum disorder is complex and multifactorial. Contributing factors include the following: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)




* Genetics

* Strong genetic component

* Many <u>genes</u> are associated with <u>ASD</u>.

* Environmental factors: Prenatal exposures (e.g., medications, infections) increase the risk of <u>ASD</u>, but no specific causes have been identified.





> [!TIP]
> There is no link between autism and <u>vaccines</u> or their ingredients. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sVXtDC)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/62ajiP)

## Clinical features

### Core features of ASD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* Persistent impairment in communication and social interaction, e.g.:

* Inability to initiate or participate in social interactions and conversational back-and-forth

* Difficulty sharing thoughts or feelings with others

* Poor <u>eye</u> contact

* Limited use and understanding of nonverbal communication

* Difficulty adjusting behavior to social situations

* Lack of interest in peers or sharing in imaginative play

* Restricted, stereotyped patterns of behavior, interests, and activities

* Repetitive movements or sounds, e.g.:

* Hand flapping

* <u>Echolalia</u>

* Rigidity in routines, e.g.:

* <u>Distress</u> at times of transition

* Restrictive or repetitive eating patterns

* Specific and abnormally focused interests (e.g., on atypical items)

* Sensory abnormalities, e.g.:

* Reduced or enhanced responses to sounds or <u>pain</u>

* Excessive touching/smelling





### Additional features [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)




* Language impairment, e.g.:

* Delayed <u>speech development</u>

* Regression in language skills

* Behavioral challenges (e.g., aggression, self-harm)

* Delays in achieving <u>developmental milestones</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OEdID70)

* By 9 months: responding to name or showing facial expression

* By 12 months: engaging in interactive games (e.g., pat-a-cake) or waving goodbye

* By 15 months: sharing interests with other individuals (e.g., pointing to desired objects)

* By 18 months: <u>protodeclarative pointing</u>

* Larger head circumference in <u>toddlers</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)





### Conditions associated with ASD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




* <u>Epilepsy</u>

* Other <u>neurodevelopmental disorders</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Intellectual disability</u>

* <u>Language disorder</u>

* <u>ADHD</u>

* Psychiatric disorders, e.g.:

* <u>Mood disorders</u>

* <u>Anxiety disorders</u>

* <u>Obsessive-compulsive disorder</u>

* <u>Catatonia</u>

* Genetic disorders, e.g.:

* <u>Tuberous sclerosis</u>

* <u>Fragile X syndrome</u>

* <u>Rett syndrome</u>

* Gastrointestinal disorders (e.g., <u>constipation</u>) and <u>eating disorders</u> (e.g., <u>pica</u>)

* <u>Sleep disorders</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>ASD</u> is associated with <u>hearing</u> and <u>vision</u> impairment, which sometimes manifest with similar features to the condition. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

## Diagnosis

### General principles [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uIdper0)




* <u>ASD</u> is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> typically made by a specialist, using the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ASD</u>.  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* <u>ASD</u> severity is assessed based on functional impairment and support needs. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)

* Additional testing should be performed as indicated to:

* Identify <u>conditions associated with ASD</u>

* Exclude alternative diagnoses





> [!WARNING]
> Do not delay referral for services while awaiting diagnostic confirmation for <u>ASD</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)




### DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ASD [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/muWV7M0)




Diagnosis of <u>ASD</u> is confirmed in patients who meet all of the following criteria. See “<u>Core features of ASD</u>” for examples of specific manifestations.




* Persistent impairments in communication and social interaction in all of the following areas: 

* Social-emotional reciprocity

* Nonverbal communication

* Interpersonal relationships

* Repetitive and restricted interests, activities, and behavior in ≥ 2 of the following areas:

* Repetitive and/or stereotyped movements

* Insistence on sameness, rigid adherence to routines and behavior patterns

* Abnormally focused interests

* Sensory abnormalities

* Onset in early childhood

* Features of <u>ASD</u> may not become fully apparent until later, when social demands exceed abilities.

* Affected individuals may develop strategies that mask symptoms later in life.

* Symptoms cause functional impairment and are not due to alternative diagnoses.





### Assessment for <u>conditions associated with ASD</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uIdper0)




Assessment is generally performed by a multidisciplinary team.




* Developmental evaluation

* Cognitive and language testing

* Gross and fine motor assessment

* Sensory evaluation

* <u>Assessment for hearing loss</u>

* <u>Vision</u> testing

* Genetic evaluation

* Offer all patients referral to genetics to assess for hereditary causes of <u>ASD</u>.

* Testing may include

* <u>Chromosomal microarray</u> analysis

* <u>Fragile X syndrome</u> testing

* Testing for any suspected genetic syndrome (e.g., <u>tuberous sclerosis</u>)





> [!TIP]
> Routine <u>EEG</u> and metabolic testing are not recommended and should only be performed if an underlying pathology is suspected. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)

## Treatment

The goals of management are to improve the <u>core features of ASD</u>, increase functional independence, minimize problematic behaviors, and treat associated conditions. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)




### General principles [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uIdper0)




* Refer patients for multimodal management as early as possible. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dsd1Cr0)

* Publicly funded programs can address developmental concerns while awaiting formal diagnostic confirmation. 

* < 3 years of age: early intervention services

* ≥ 3 years of age: school services

* Treatment is individualized based on the patient's age, strengths, and needs and should include:

* Early initiation of behavioral interventions [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

* Nonpharmacological and pharmacological treatment as needed for

* <u>Developmental delays</u>

* Specific behavioral concerns

* <u>Conditions associated with ASD</u>

* Coordinate ongoing care and family support for patients and caregivers.





> [!TIP]
> Services for <u>management of ASD</u> should be integrated across the child's home and educational settings. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)




### Behavioral interventions  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)




* Applied behavioral analysis (ABA) (e.g., discrete trial training, pivotal response training)

* Uses <u>positive reinforcement</u> to teach new social and communication skills and minimize functional impairment

* Can be highly structured or naturalistic (implemented in the context of the child's daily activities)

* Developmental approaches (e.g., Developmental Individual Differences and Relationship-based/Floortime model) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

* Teach communication through social interaction.

* Promote skill development (e.g., <u>joint attention</u>, symbolic play, social skills) and prevent aggressive and/or unsafe behaviors.

* Focused interventions address specific behaviors or build a single skill (e.g., <u>social skills training</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)





> [!TIP]
> Early initiation of behavioral interventions greatly improves outcomes. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)




### Additional nonpharmacological interventions




* Speech-language therapy: for delayed language

* Occupational and/or <u>physical therapy</u>: for impaired fine and gross motor skills

* <u>Cognitive behavioral therapy</u> (<u>CBT</u>): for

* <u>Anxiety</u> and <u>depression</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances  [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Brdzjr0)





### Pharmacological treatment [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/a7dQ4r0)




* Specific problematic behaviors

* Irritability, disruptive, and/or repetitive stereotyped behavior: <u>atypical antipsychotics</u> (i.e., <u>aripiprazole</u> or <u>risperidone</u>)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Lrdw3r0)

* Behavioral rigidity: <u>antiepileptics</u> (e.g., divalproex <u>sodium</u>, <u>valproic acid</u>)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)

* Comorbid conditions

* <u>ADHD</u>: <u>methylphenidate</u>, <u>atomoxetine</u>, <u>guanfacine</u>

* <u>Anxiety</u> and/or <u>depression</u>: <u>SSRIs</u>

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances: <u>melatonin</u>

* <u>Epilepsy</u>: <u>antiepileptic drugs</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Risperidone</u> and <u>aripiprazole</u> are the only <u>FDA</u>-approved drugs for controlling irritability in patients with <u>ASD</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)

## Prognosis

* <u>ASD</u> is typically a lifelong condition that persists into adulthood. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

* Symptoms may evolve.

* <u>Adolescents</u> can experience social exclusion due to difficulty communicating with peers.

* Adults with <u>ASD</u> are less likely to work and live independently.

* Prognosis varies, ranging from significant functional limitations to the development of exceptional skills in certain areas (e.g., in art or music). [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JrdsRr0)

* Factors associated with worse prognosis include: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uIdper0)

* Severe core symptoms

* Intellectual impairment

* Poor or absent <u>language development</u>

* Late initiation of treatment

* Up to 30% of individuals with <u>ASD</u> never develop the ability to speak. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qv1CaQ0)
