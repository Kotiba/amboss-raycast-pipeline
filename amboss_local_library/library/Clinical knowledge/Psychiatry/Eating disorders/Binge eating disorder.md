# Binge eating disorder

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Psychiatry > Eating disorders > Binge eating disorder*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dt01f3)

---

## Summary

Binge <u>eating disorder</u> is an <u>eating disorder</u> characterized by recurrent uncontrollable <u>binge eating episodes</u> that occur at least once a week for 3 months. <u>Binge eating episodes</u> are associated with significant <u>distress</u>, without compensatory behaviors to counteract weight gain. Causes are multifactorial and similar to those of <u>anorexia nervosa</u> (e.g., genetic factors, psychiatric disorders, and psychosocial factors such as bullying). It is important to assess for <u>malnutrition</u> severity in affected individuals, regardless of body weight or <u>body mass index</u> (<u>BMI</u>). The diagnosis is confirmed if individuals fulfill all of the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for binge eating disorder</u>. Individuals should be evaluated for associated complications (e.g., comprehensive assessment of patients who are <u>overweight or obese</u>) and underlying conditions that may affect weight or cause a change in eating behaviors (e.g., <u>thyroid disorder</u>). Treatment is typically provided in an outpatient setting, but the presence of <u>red flags in eating disorders</u> may indicate the need for hospitalization. All individuals should be referred for <u>psychotherapy</u> (preferably <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u>) and nutritional management, including <u>binge eating prevention strategies</u>. Pharmacotherapy (e.g., with <u>antidepressants</u> or <u>lisdexamfetamine</u>) may be considered to help reduce the frequency of <u>binge eating episodes</u>.

## Epidemiology

* <u>Prevalence</u>: most common <u>eating disorder</u> in adults in the US (∼ 2–5% of general population)

* Peak age of onset: <u>adolescents</u> and young adults; can also begin in adulthood

* Sex: <u>♀</u> > <u>♂</u>




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

---

## Etiology

The etiology of binge <u>eating disorder</u> is multifactorial and not entirely understood. Contributory factors are similar to those associated with <u>anorexia nervosa</u>; see “Etiology of <u>Anorexia nervosa</u>” for details. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hZXcY9)




* Genetic factors (<u>family history</u> is common)

* Strict dieting and having access to preferred binge foods

* Psychological issues (e.g., poor body self-image, <u>stress</u>, childhood bullying)

---

## Clinical features

* Recurrent <u>binge eating episodes</u> that are not associated with <u>inappropriate weight compensatory behaviors</u> : See also “<u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for binge eating disorder</u>.”

* Pronounced <u>obesity</u> at a young age is common; <u>BMI</u> may also be normal.

* Can be associated with:

* <u>ASCVD risk factors</u>: i.e., <u>hyperlipidemia</u>, <u>metabolic syndrome</u>, <u>type 2 diabetes</u>, cardiovascular disease

* Psychological conditions: e.g., major <u>depression</u> disorder, <u>alcohol use disorder</u>, <u>suicidal ideation</u>

* Course: typically chronic, relapsing disease





> [!WARNING]
> Approximately 25% of affected individuals experience <u>suicidal ideation</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

## Diagnosis

### General principles




* See “<u>Screening for eating disorders</u>” for indications and screening modalities. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y11djf0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)

* Determine if individuals fulfill all of the <u>DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for binge eating disorder</u> to confirm the diagnosis. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Evaluate for complications and comorbidities, and rule out possible organic etiologies for change in weight and/or eating behaviors: See “<u>Initial evaluation for a suspected eating disorder</u>.”

| --- | --- |
| A | <br>* Recurrent binge eating episodes, which are characterized by both of the following:<br><br>* Consumption of an excessive amount of food within a given period of time (usually ≤ 2 hours)<br><br>* Lack of control in relation to what and/or how much food is consumed<br><br> |
| B | <br>* <u>Episodes of binge eating</u> are associated with ≥ 3 of the following:<br><br>* Eating faster than normal<br><br>* Eating until uncomfortably full<br><br>* Eating large amounts when not hungry<br><br>* Eating alone because of embarrassment over the amount of food eaten<br><br>* Feeling of disgust, <u>depression</u>, and/or guilt after eating<br><br> |

> [!TIP]
> All individuals with binge <u>eating disorder</u> experience emotional <u>distress</u> about their binge eating, but not all individuals necessarily experience <u>distress</u> over their weight or appearance. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)




> [!WARNING]
> Individuals with binge <u>eating disorder</u> often conceal their eating behaviors (e.g., by eating alone). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




#### Severity (according to the <u>DSM-5</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




Based on the number of <u>binge eating episodes</u> per week




* Mild: 1–3 episodes/week

* Moderate: 4–7 episodes/week

* Severe: 8–13 episodes/week

* Extreme: ≥ 14 episodes/week

## Treatment

### General principles [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cm1aeh0)




* Determine the most appropriate care setting: See “<u>Disposition for eating disorders</u>.”

* Establish treatment goals, e.g., [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)

* Decrease the number of <u>binge eating episodes</u>

* Improve disordered thoughts and beliefs about weight, body image, and eating behaviors

* Improve knowledge of healthy eating habits, including <u>binge eating prevention strategies</u>.

* Comanage nutritional management with a dietitian.

* Refer all individuals for <u>psychotherapy</u> (e.g., <u>cognitive behavioral therapy</u>).

* Pharmacotherapy may help reduce the frequency of <u>binge eating episodes</u> (e.g., by reducing the impulse to binge); options include:

* <u>Antidepressants</u> (e.g., <u>SSRIs</u>)

* <u>Lisdexamfetamine</u> (<u>topiramate</u> may be considered as an alternative): may also help with weight reduction

* Consider additional management for patients who are <u>overweight or obese</u> as needed.

* Identify and manage associated comorbidities; see “Clinical features.”

* Regularly reassess for remission. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)

* Partial remission : <u>binge eating episodes</u> occur < 1 time per week (on average) for a sustained period of time

* Full remission : none of the criteria for binge <u>eating disorder</u> are met for a sustained period of time





### Nutritional management [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)




* Evaluate nutritional intake.

* Assess for <u>food insecurity</u>.

* Provide education on healthy eating habits, including <u>binge eating prevention strategies</u>.





### <u>Psychotherapy</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cm1aeh0)




First-line therapy for binge <u>eating disorder</u>




* Preferred modalities

* <u>Cognitive behavioral therapy</u> (in-person; individual or group format)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b01He20)

* <u>Interpersonal therapy</u>

* Alternatives

* Web-based <u>CBT</u> or <u>CBT</u>-based guided self-help programs  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b01He20)

* <u>Dialectical behavioral therapy</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cm1aeh0)





### Pharmacotherapy [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cm1aeh0)




Research on pharmacotherapy for binge <u>eating disorder</u> has primarily involved adults, therefore, there are no clear recommendations for <u>adolescents</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)




* Indications

* Individuals who prefer medication rather than <u>psychotherapy</u>

* Adjunctive therapy to <u>psychotherapy</u> for patients with:

* Moderate or severe binge <u>eating disorder</u>

* Lack of response to <u>psychotherapy</u> alone

* Preferred agents

* <u>Antidepressants</u> (e.g., <u>SSRIs</u>)  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b01He20)

* <u>Lisdexamfetamine</u> DOSAGE  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b01He20)

* <u>Lisdexamfetamine</u> is a stimulant and therefore carries a risk of misuse

* Use with caution in patients with <u>hypertension</u> and/or cardiac disease. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)

* Alternative agent: <u>Topiramate</u> may be considered if the benefits outweigh the risks. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)

* Potential adverse effects: cognitive effects, <u>paresthesias</u>, and <u>teratogenicity</u>.

* <u>Topiramate</u> is <u>teratogenic</u>; women of childbearing age must use effective <u>contraception</u> during treatment.





> [!WARNING]
> The <u>antidepressant</u> <u>bupropion</u> lowers the <u>seizure</u> threshold and is contraindicated in individuals with a history of <u>anorexia nervosa</u>, <u>bulimia nervosa</u>, or <u>purging behaviors</u>, and those with <u>seizure disorders</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)




> [!TIP]
> Weight reduction is a possible side effect of the binge <u>eating disorder</u> medications <u>lisdexamfetamine</u> and <u>topiramate</u>, although neither are specifically approved for weight loss. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i_cJLU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)




### Management of patients with <u>overweight or obesity</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cm1aeh0)




* <u>Pharmacotherapy for weight loss</u> or <u>bariatric surgery</u> may be considered if appropriate.

* Identify and treat underlying conditions contributing to weight gain (e.g., <u>PCOS</u>) and/or <u>complications of obesity</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Recommend <u>binge eating prevention strategies</u> instead of dietary restrictions (e.g., avoiding specific food groups, limiting calories), as restrictions can inadvertently increase binge eating. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ym1nVh0)
