# Older adult abuse

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Legal medicine and professionalism > Older adult abuse*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Z80ZO3)

---

## Summary

Older adult abuse, also referred to as elder abuse, is any form of maltreatment of an individual ≥ 60 years of age by a trusted person or caregiver. <u>Signs of older adult abuse</u> include lack of control of medical conditions, delays in seeking appropriate medical care, and unexplained <u>soft tissue</u> injuries. <u>Screening recommendations</u> for older adult abuse vary, and there are no tests or screening tools to definitively confirm the diagnosis. If older adult abuse is suspected on clinical evaluation or on <u>laboratory analysis</u> (e.g., evidence of <u>dehydration</u>, <u>malnutrition</u>, and/or medication nonadherence), further evaluation is warranted. Management includes treating acute medical conditions, ensuring <u>patient safety</u>, and mandatory reporting in most states.

---

## Definitions

* Older adult abuse: any form of physical, emotional, sexual, psychological, or financial mistreatment or neglect of an adult ≥ 60 years of age by a caregiver or someone the individual trusts [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)

## Risk factors

* Female sex

* Large household

* Residence in an <u>assisted living facility</u>

* <u>Dementia</u>

* Functional impairment or poor physical health

* Social isolation and/or lack of support

* Low income

* Financial dependency of the caregiver on the older adult





> [!TIP]
> Not all individuals with these <u>risk factors</u> are mistreated or abused. Not all individuals who experience abuse have these <u>risk factors</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)

## Red flags

### On history [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fC1krQ0)




* Delay between onset of illness or injury and seeking medical care

* Lack of control of medical conditions despite an adequate <u>care plan</u>

* Frequent ED visits

* Reports of theft or unusual loss of belongings or money

* <u>Depression</u>, <u>suicidal ideation</u>





### On examination [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)




* <u>Soft tissue</u> injuries that are unexplained or characteristic of older adult abuse 

* <u>Bruises</u> > 5 cm; especially in unusual locations (e.g., abdomen, <u>medial</u> aspect of arms, and <u>posterior</u> aspect of legs and neck)

* Injuries to the head, neck, and upper extremities

* Facial injuries

* <u>Ligature marks</u>, bite marks

* <u>Pressure ulcers</u>, multiple <u>burns</u>, cuts [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fC1krQ0)

* Anogenital trauma, <u>pelvic pain</u>, and/or <u>postmenopausal bleeding</u>

* <u>Dehydration</u> and/or <u>malnutrition</u>

* Unusual or unexplained <u>fractures</u>





### Observed interactions with caregivers [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fC1krQ0)




* An inconsistent history between the patient and caregiver

* Patient shows fearful, avoidant, or hostile behavior (e.g., avoiding <u>eye</u> contact), especially if this is reported to be new behavior.

* Caregiver is frustrated, overwhelmed, or lacks knowledge about the patient's needs.

## Management

* General principles [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* If presenting in the emergency room, conduct a <u>primary survey</u>; see “<u>Trauma in older adults</u>.”

* Obtain <u>informed consent</u> for every diagnostic and management step.

* Attempt to speak to the patient privately.

* Maintain a low threshold for notifying hospital security about suspected older adult abuse to ensure the safety of the patient and staff.

* Involve a multidisciplinary team early.

* Follow local protocols for preparing accurate medical documentation for potential criminal proceedings.

---

## Diagnostic evaluation

### Approach [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)




There are no diagnostic studies that can definitively confirm older adult abuse.




* Perform a clinical evaluation in patients with either of the following:

* <u>Red flags for older adult abuse</u>

* Positive routine screening for older adult abuse

* Consider additional diagnostic studies, based on clinical suspicion, to:

* Identify findings that may be consistent with older adult abuse

* Exclude organic causes of signs and symptoms





> [!TIP]
> <u>Signs of older adult abuse</u> may be difficult to distinguish from features of age-related conditions (e.g., poor <u>wound healing</u>, <u>osteoporosis</u>, <u>dementia</u>) or adverse effects of prescribed medication (e.g., <u>steroid</u> <u>purpura</u>).  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)




### Clinical evaluation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




The goal of clinical evaluation is to identify and/or further evaluate <u>red flags for older adult abuse</u> to determine whether there is a reasonable suspicion for abuse. It is essential to interview patients and their caregivers separately.




* Perform a <u>cognitive screening test</u>.

* Cognitive impairment likely: Depending on the nature of the deficit, a reliable history may be difficult to obtain; consider modifying or proceeding to <u>physical exam</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lSWv-k0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_kW5ql0)

* Cognitive impairment unlikely: Proceed through the following steps.

* Obtain a detailed <u>medical history</u>.

* Ask specific questions about abuse.

* Indirect questions may be less intimidating.

* Ask direct questions if necessary.

* Perform a <u>medication review</u>.

* Perform a brief <u>functional status assessment</u>.

* Perform a thorough <u>physical examination</u>.

* See “<u>Red flags for older adult abuse</u>.”

* If there is concern for <u>sexual assault</u>, a forensic exam should be performed by a trained professional.





> [!TIP]
> Patients' self-reported clinical history may be unreliable, e.g., due to cognitive impairment, fear, shame, or cultural and/or language barriers. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1B12-Q0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lSWv-k0)




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gC1FrQ0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)




* <u>CMP</u>: <u>Hypernatremia</u> and increased <u>BUN:creatinine ratio</u> may indicate <u>dehydration</u>.

* <u>Urinalysis</u>: Increased <u>urine concentration</u> may indicate <u>dehydration</u>.

* <u>CPK</u> and urine <u>myoglobin</u>: Increased <u>CPK</u> or urine <u>myoglobin</u> may indicate <u>rhabdomyolysis</u> secondary to prolonged immobility.

* <u>CBC</u>: <u>Anemia</u> may indicate <u>malnutrition</u>.

* <u>Coagulation studies</u>: <u>Coagulopathy</u> may explain excessive <u>bruising</u>.

* Prescribed medication drug levels:

* Low or undetectable levels may indicate neglect in providing medications as prescribed.

* Supratherapeutic levels may suggest intentional or unintentional overdose.





### Imaging [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gC1FrQ0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z0cria0)




* Obtain imaging studies based on history and <u>physical examination</u>.

* Notify the radiologist if abuse of an older adult is suspected.

## Subsequent management

### Treatment [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gC1FrQ0)




* Treat traumatic injuries (e.g., <u>fractures</u>) and acute medical conditions.

* Manage any exacerbations of chronic conditions.

* See also “<u>Trauma in older adults</u>.”





### Reporting [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




#### General principles




* Physicians have a legal obligation to protect their patients and report older adult abuse in most states, even if reporting goes against the patient's wishes. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TC16rQ0)

* State-specific information can be found on the state's Department of Health website. [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PpcWpW0)





#### Specific scenarios [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gpcFKW0)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LpcwJW0)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Qpcu6W0)




* Danger from the perpetrator is immediately life-threatening: Notify law enforcement authorities.  [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2C1TrQ0)

* Danger from the perpetrator is not immediately life-threatening.

* Notify <u>Adult Protective Services</u> if the patient lives in the community.

* Notify the <u>Long-Term Care Ombudsman program</u> if the patient lives in a facility (e.g., <u>nursing home</u>).





### Disposition




Admit to the hospital or create a safe discharge plan.




> [!TIP]
> In the US, the appropriate agency for reporting older adult abuse can be found by calling Eldercare Locator (1-800-677-1116) or visiting the National <u>Adult Protective Services</u> Association website (https://www.napsa-now.org/help-in-your-area/).
