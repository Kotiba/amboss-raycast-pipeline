# Intimate partner violence

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Legal medicine and professionalism > Intimate partner violence*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/080eO3)

---

## Summary

Intimate partner violence (<u>IPV</u>) is any form of physical, psychological, or <u>sexual violence</u> that is carried out by a current or former intimate partner who may or may not be a cohabitant. <u>Domestic violence</u> is a broad term for any form of violence committed by one member of a household against another, and can include <u>IPV</u>, <u>older adult abuse</u>, and <u>child maltreatment</u>. <u>IPV</u> affects nearly half of individuals in the US during their lifetime and up to 20% of pregnant individuals. Signs include multiple and/or unexplained injuries (e.g., injuries inconsistent with the <u>medical history</u>, in various stages of healing, or consistent with an act of defense), fearful behavior, frequent visits to the emergency department, and a partner who insists on speaking on the patient's behalf. All women of reproductive age should be screened routinely (e.g., at annual well-visits, and more frequently during <u>pregnancy</u>), using a validated <u>IPV screening tool</u>. Management includes treating physical injuries, performing a danger assessment, and facilitating contact with support services.




<u>Management of recent sexual violence</u> is detailed separately.

## Definitions

* Intimate partner violence (<u>IPV</u>): any form of physical, psychological, or <u>sexual violence</u> that is carried out by a current or former intimate partner who may or may not be a cohabitant [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YZXnZ9)

* Domestic violence: a broad term for any form of violence committed by one member of a household against another, and can include <u>IPV</u>, <u>older adult abuse</u>, and <u>child maltreatment</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)

## Risk factors

<u>Risk factors</u> include: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S_1ynj0)




* Evidence of discord in the relationship, e.g., hostility, conflict, verbal aggression

* Low <u>socioeconomic status</u> and/or level of education, unemployment

* History of abuse or exposure to <u>IPV</u>

* Substance misuse

* Mental or physical disabilities

* Mental health disorders, e.g., <u>depression</u>

* Younger age (ages 18–24)

* <u>Pregnancy</u>, especially unplanned

* Unmarried, divorced or separated relationship status

* Racial minority populations

* Sexual and <u>gender</u> minority populations, including:

* <u>Transgender</u> individuals [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UOWb7l0)

* Same-sex couples [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2OWT7l0)





> [!TIP]
> There is a risk of escalation of violence during <u>pregnancy</u> and/or in the <u>postpartum period</u>. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)

## Red flags

The presence of any of the following should prompt consideration of <u>IPV</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)




* History

* Hesitation to seek medical care

* Frequent visits to the emergency department

* Unexplained; , unusual, and/or multiple injuries

* Unexplained somatic symptoms  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)

* Substance misuse

* Symptoms of mental health disorders (e.g., <u>suicidal ideation</u>, <u>depression</u>, <u>anxiety</u>, <u>PTSD</u>)

* Examination findings: Specific injury patterns can indicate <u>IPV</u>.

* <u>Contusions</u>, <u>lacerations</u>, and/or <u>fractures</u> of the head, neck, and face

* <u>Signs of strangulation</u>

* Signs of intentional injuries, such as injuries that are:

* In a central location (e.g., <u>breast</u> or chest)

* Bilateral (e.g., both arms)

* Defensive (e.g., ulnar aspect of the forearm)

* <u>Signs of traumatic brain injury</u> (from repeated head and neck trauma)

* Behaviors [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntW7VM0)

* Fearful, avoidant, hostile behavior (e.g., avoiding <u>eye</u> contact)

* Partner insists on being present and speaking on the patient's behalf

## Screening

### Approach [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




* Assess for <u>indications for IPV screening</u>.

* Before screening:

* Ensure the patient is in a private setting (i.e., without their partner or family member) and is clothed.

* Explain the universal nature of the screening and ask for permission.

* Provide reassurance that all information will remain confidential aside from anything required to be disclosed by law.

* Administer an <u>IPV screening tool</u> using a <u>trauma-informed approach</u>.

* Follow-up appropriately.

* Positive screen: Provide <u>management of IPV</u>, including facilitating <u>IPV referral and reporting</u>.

* Negative screen: Provide standard educational and reference materials to all individuals, e.g., a list of contact details for local and national resources.





> [!WARNING]
> There are several reasons why individuals may be reluctant to disclose the presence or extent of <u>IPV</u>, including shame, fear of stigma, financial dependence. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)




### Indications for IPV screening




* The <u>USPSTF</u> and <u>ACOG</u> recommend universal screening of all women of reproductive age for <u>IPV</u>, e.g., at: ;  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UOWb7l0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1IW2b50)

* New patient visits

* Annual gynecological exams

* Prenatal and <u>postpartum visits</u> (see “<u>IPV in pregnancy</u>”)

* Hospital admissions [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aIWQY50)

* The WHO recommends targeted screening for patients with <u>red flags for IPV</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/38WSmM0)





### IPV screening tools [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/M8WMMM0)




* There are several validated tools that can be used to screen women for current <u>IPV</u> or <u>IPV</u> in the past year.

* Screening tools may be administered in a written or verbal format depending on patient preference. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)





#### HARK screening tool [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NlW-Cl0)




* A validated four-question screening tool that assesses for different manifestations of <u>IPV</u> within the past year

* An affirmative response to ≥ 1 of the questions is considered a positive screen.

* Within the past year:

* H: Has your partner humiliated or emotionally abused you?

* A: Have you been afraid of your partner?

* R: Have you ever experienced <u>rape</u> or been forced to have any kind of <u>sexual activity</u> by your partner?

* K: Has your partner kicked, hit, slapped, or otherwise physically hurt you?





#### HITS score [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/p_1L6j0)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/StWy1M0)




* A validated four-question verbal or written screening tool used to assess how often an individual has been hurt by an intimate partner

* The E-HITS screening tool has a fifth question that assesses <u>sexual violence</u>.

#### STaT questions [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KqWUzm0)




* A validated three-question screening tool used to identify a history of <u>IPV</u> (high <u>sensitivity</u>; moderate <u>specificity</u>)

* An affirmative response to ≥ 1 of the questions warrants further evaluation for <u>IPV</u>.

* Have you ever been in a relationship where your partner has:

* S: pushed or slapped you?

* T: threatened you with violence?

* T: thrown, broken, or punched things?





#### Woman Abuse Screening Tool (<u>WAST</u>) [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mlWVxl0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5lWixl0)




* <u>WAST</u>-Short

* Consists of two questions:

* Do you and your partner work out arguments with: great difficulty, some difficulty, or no difficulty?

* In general, would you say your relationship has: a lot of tension, some tension, or no tension?

* Scoring: A response of either “great difficulty” to the first question or “a lot of tension” to the second question is considered a positive screen.

* Positive screen: Explore further with the additional <u>WAST</u> questions.

* Additional <u>WAST</u> questions (answer options: often, sometimes, or never)

* Do arguments ever result in you feeling put down or bad about yourself?

* Do arguments ever result in kicking, hitting, or pushing?

* Do you ever feel frightened by what your partner says or does?

* Has your partner ever abused you physically?

* Has your partner ever abused you emotionally?

* Has your partner ever abused you sexually?





#### Screening for sexual and <u>reproductive coercion</u> [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1IW2b50)




The <u>ACOG</u> recommends using questions such as the following to assess for sexual or <u>reproductive coercion</u>.




* Has your partner refused to wear <u>condoms</u> or forced you to do something sexual against your will?

* Has your partner ever tried to interfere with your <u>birth control</u>, or get you pregnant when you did not want to be pregnant?

* Is your partner supportive of your decisions around <u>pregnancy</u>?

* Pregnant individuals: Are you concerned that your partner will hurt you if you do not comply with their wishes around <u>pregnancy</u>?

## Management

### General principles [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




* Evaluate the patient in a private setting.

* Clearly explain the <u>confidentiality</u> policy.

* Use an empathetic, <u>trauma-informed approach</u>.

* Validate the patient's experiences; avoid blaming.

* Ask <u>open-ended questions</u>.

* Obtain <u>informed consent</u> for every diagnostic and management step.

* Allow the patient to undress to the extent they are comfortable with.

* Maintain a low threshold for notifying hospital security about suspected <u>IPV</u> to ensure the safety of the patient and staff.

* Consider early involvement of a multidisciplinary team.

* Consider legal requirements and follow local protocols for:

* Preparation of accurate medical documentation for potential criminal proceedings

* <u>Sexual health forensic examination</u>

* <u>IPV referral and reporting</u>





> [!WARNING]
> Be aware when documenting <u>IPV</u> that patient-accessible parts of the <u>electronic health record</u> may also be read by abusive partners. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)




### Evaluation [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




Evaluation may be prompted by positive <u>screening for IPV</u> and/or the presence of signs or symptoms of <u>IPV</u>.




#### Clinical evaluation




* Obtain a detailed history and perform a thorough examination. Identify and/or further evaluate:

* <u>Red flags for IPV</u>, including associated psychological trauma  and <u>signs of strangulation</u>

* <u>Red flags for sexual violence</u>

* <u>Red flags for human trafficking</u> [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hx1KBQ0)

* Assess the immediate safety of the patient and any children; this may include use of an <u>IPV</u> danger assessment tool such as the danger assessment-5 (DA-5). [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/glWFwl0)

* The DA-5 consists of the following five questions:

* Has the severity or frequency of physical violence increased in the past year?

* Has your partner ever tried to choke or strangle you?

* Is your partner violently and constantly jealous of you?

* Has your partner used or threatened to use a weapon against you?

* Do you believe your partner is capable of killing you?

* A positive DA-5 screen is an affirmative response to ≥ 2 questions.

* Further management for patients with a positive DA-5 screen

* Inform the patient that they are at increased risk of lethal or severe injury by their partner.

* Refer or connect the patient to a support service of their choice: See “<u>IPV referral and reporting</u>” for details.





> [!WARNING]
> Patients who have experienced strangulation are at increased risk of becoming a victim of homicide. [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/glWFwl0)




#### Diagnostic studies




* Diagnostic testing is based on the injury and clinical suspicion.

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> may include <u>pregnancy testing</u> and <u>STI screening</u>.  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1IW2b50)

* Imaging may include:

* <u>X-rays</u> to assess for <u>fractures</u>

* <u>CTA</u> head and neck and/or CT neck or <u>spine</u> for patients with <u>blunt neck trauma</u> resulting from strangulation  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PlWWCl0)

* CT head for <u>traumatic brain injury</u>

* See also “<u>Blunt trauma</u>.”





### Initial medical management




* Treat acute conditions.

* This includes medical, traumatic, and psychological conditions.

* See “Management of <u>sexual violence</u>” if relevant.

* Refer to specialists as appropriate, e.g.:

* Orthopedics for <u>fractures</u>

* Plastics for <u>lacerations</u>





### IPV referral and reporting [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4C13HQ0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




#### Referral




Connect patients to support resources; consider the level of immediate danger, local protocols, and patient preference when determining appropriate resources and methods of referral.




* Resources

* National Domestic Violence Hotline

* Community resources (e.g., a local <u>domestic violence</u> agency)

* Social worker (e.g., to arrange home visits and/or counseling; , to obtain emergency housing)

* <u>IPV</u> legal advocate   [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* Patient advocate

* Methods

* Warm <u>handoff</u>, i.e., introducing the patient in person or joining the phone call

* Offering a private phone for the patient to use. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)

* Providing contact details for local and/or national resources, e.g.: 

* As a printed list

* By programming important numbers into the patient's phone under a code name





> [!TIP]
> Referring individuals who have experienced <u>IPV</u> to ongoing support services can decrease abuse and adverse consequences of <u>IPV</u> (e.g., <u>PTSD</u>, <u>depression</u>. <u>anxiety</u>, <u>substance abuse</u>, and <u>suicidal behavior</u>). [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kRcmMX0)




> [!TIP]
> In the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline can be contacted by calling 1-800-799-SAFE (7233) or texting “START” to 88788.




#### Reporting




Determine and follow local reporting requirements. Generally, the following guidelines apply.




* Law enforcement: Report if required by law or if patient requests it.  [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AiWR8O0)

* <u>Child protective services</u>: Report if children are involved.  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)

* Adult protective services: Report if the patient is an <u>at-risk adult</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> Patient consent is not required to report <u>IPV</u> in some states and in certain situations (e.g., if children are involved).




### Subsequent management [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3UWS1k0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




* Determine if the individual wishes to leave their partner immediately.

* If so: Consider admission if they feel unsafe returning home and no alternative housing is available.

* If not: Nonjudgmentally support their choice and emphasize that help is always available.

* Recommend creating a safety plan for leaving, including: 

* Making copies of important personal documents

* Ensuring they have easy access to money and other essentials if they need to leave their home quickly

* Identifying a safe place to go

* Establishing a code word or phrase with trusted friends or family to communicate if they are in imminent danger

* Offer the following:

* New prescriptions for their regular medications  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DcW1e40)

* Discrete <u>contraception</u> options

* Determine the patient's preferred (i.e., safest) form of future medical communication.

* Arrange follow-up care with a primary care provider to: [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jC1_7Q0)

* Identify and manage acute and long-term conditions associated with <u>IPV</u>

* Ensure ongoing assessment of the patient's risk of immediate harm (e.g., with the DA-5 at each visit)

* For pregnant patients, additional considerations are detailed in “<u>Management of perinatal IPV</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> Individuals experiencing <u>IPV</u> may choose not to leave or report their partner for various reasons, including financial concerns, concern about the well-being of their children, and fear of further harm. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)




> [!WARNING]
> Advise all individuals experiencing <u>IPV</u> to develop a safety plan for leaving. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/flWkwl0)
