## Summary

<u>Health care providers</u> are required to follow laws and codes of conduct and to perform their duties with professionalism. Most health professions are subject to a combination of self-regulation (e.g., through licensing boards) and regional laws that aim to ensure adherence to ethical and legal standards. Regulatory domains for clinicians include <u>medical malpractice</u>, <u>professional misconduct</u>, <u>physician impairment</u>, <u>conflicts of interest</u>, and <u>referral of patients</u>. Other rules guide engagement with the public, such as <u>clinician-patient romantic relationships</u> and <u>clinician use of social media</u>. Professional codes of conduct and laws governing, e.g., <u>medical malpractice</u> and <u>physician impairment</u> vary by jurisdiction and over time. Clinicians must adhere to the regulatory requirements that apply to their practice.




See also “<u>Principles of medical law and ethics</u>.”

## Malpractice, misconduct, and physician impairment

### Medical malpractice




For more information about different types of errors leading to negligence, see “<u>Medical error</u>” in “<u>Patient safety</u>.”




* Definition: Negligent conduct on the part of a <u>health care provider</u> or performance of a medical task with unreasonable lack of skill.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wCah85)

* Elements of <u>malpractice</u>

* The <u>clinician-patient relationship</u> has been established.

* This obligation to provide care has been neglected.

* There is damage to the patient.

* The <u>medical negligence</u> is directly responsible for the damage to the patient.

* Reporting <u>malpractice</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rtXfU-)

* Clinicians are ethically obliged to report any violations resulting from their colleagues' <u>incompetence</u>, negligence, and/or unethical conduct.

* Contact the hospital authorities at the first instance; if the consequences of the <u>malpractice</u> pose a threat to patients' health, contact the <u>state licensing board</u>.

* Defensive medicine: testing and treatment that is not medically necessary but is performed by the clinician to avoid legal liability [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ItXYU-)

* Is unethical and should be avoided because it increases risk of patient harm and does not add significant benefits (e.g., an unnecessary <u>CT scan</u> causes radiation exposure)

* Clinicians should openly communicate with patients about the risks and benefits of tests or treatments that they believe are not medically necessary.





> [!NOTE]
> The 4 D's of <u>malpractice</u>: Duty (obligation to deliver proper medical care to the patient), Dereliction of duty, Damage to the patient, Direct cause of damage.




### Professional misconduct




* <u>Professional misconduct</u> is any clinician behavior that goes against the ethical principles of medical practice.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/itXJW-)

* <u>Clinician misconduct</u> can occur outside the established <u>clinician-patient relationships</u> and thus does not always constitute <u>malpractice</u>.

* Examples of <u>misconduct</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/itXJW-)

* Bullying

* Inappropriate comments towards colleagues or patients

* Sexual harassment of colleagues or patients

* <u>Billing fraud</u>

* Inadequate record keeping (e.g., intentional falsification of medical records)

* Accepting expensive gifts from patients or industry

* Colleagues who suspect a clinician of <u>misconduct</u> should report their concerns to the <u>state medical board</u> and, if the <u>misconduct</u> implies any legal liability, to the proper federal authorities. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RtXlW-)





### Physician impairment




* Definition:  the inability of a clinician to provide adequate medical care due to mental health disorder, substance-related disorder, or physical condition that limits the use of motor, cognitive, or perceptive skills [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/btXHX-)

* Potential signs of impairment [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YtXnX-)

* Disruptive behavior (e.g., increased conflicts with colleagues or patients, irritability, <u>anxiety</u>, aggression)

* Physical signs of <u>substance use disorder</u> (e.g., needle marks, <u>alcohol</u> <u>smell</u>)

* Nonadherence with working schedule (e.g., being late or absent at work for no reason) or sudden changes in schedule (e.g., unusually early or late appointments)

* Decreased quality of care (e.g., increased rate of <u>medical errors</u>, incorrect charting)

* Personal life problems (e.g., divorce, withdrawal from family, debts)

* Physician Health Program: a program that is supervised by a <u>state medical board</u> and is aimed to prevent, detect, and manage disorders that cause impairment in clinicians [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0tXeX-)

* Colleagues of a clinician should contact the PHP if they suspect impairment.

* The PHP performs a thorough assessment of the potentially <u>impaired clinician</u> and arranges management if impairment is confirmed.

* If an <u>impaired clinician</u> accepts the treatment voluntarily, the PHP is not obliged to notify the <u>state medical board</u> about the clinician's condition.

* If the clinician refuses treatment, the PHP will report the clinician's condition to the <u>state medical board</u>, which may lead to an involuntary referral for treatment and disciplinary actions.

* After treatment, the PHP monitors the clinician with regular assessments and/or laboratory tests for 1–5 years depending on the condition underlying the impairment.





### Clinician-patient romantic relationships [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VZXG09)




* Romantic relationships with current patients are always unethical and inappropriate.

* A romantic <u>clinician-patient relationship</u> compromises the objectivity of the clinician's decisions in regard to the care of that patient.

* Such relationships make patients more vulnerable to exploitation.

* Romantic relationships with former patients are also inappropriate if:

* Less than one year has passed since the end of <u>the patient-clinician relationship</u>.

* The clinician has a position of influence or influence from their previous experience with the former patient (e.g., knowledge of trauma expressed during therapy).

* The former relationship was a patient-<u>psychiatrist</u> relationship

* Should a clinician feel that their actions may be perceived as sexual and/or lead to a romantic relationship with a current patient, the clinician should take active measures to avoid unnecessary contact with the patient.

* Use direct, <u>close-ended questions</u>.

* Conduct interviews with a chaperone present.

* Romantic relationships with patient-accompanying third parties (e.g., their children, friends, surrogates) may also be inappropriate in case the third party plays a considerable role in clinician-patient interaction and may be emotionally dependent on the clinician. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CtXqf-)

## Conflicts of interest

### Overview [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9CaN85)




* Definition: A <u>conflict of interest</u> (<u>COI</u>) occurs when a clinician's objectivity regarding their primary interest (i.e., patient welfare) is potentially affected by a secondary interest (e.g., personal financial gain).

* Minimizing COIs [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/daXoj9)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gYXFo9)

* Clinicians should always disclose COIs to patients, employing institutions, and when presenting medical results (e.g., at a medical <u>conference</u> or in a peer-reviewed journal).

* Gifts of significant value from the medical industry should be declined.

* Gifts may only be accepted if they directly benefit patients and do not have substantial monetary value (e.g., pens, notepads, medical textbooks).

* Cash should never be accepted.

* Gifts that have “strings attached” should not be accepted (e.g., a gift that affects the clinicians's prescribing practices).

* Clinicians should not allow pharmaceutical industry-funded advertisements in their practice.

* Industry subsidies for clinician travel, lodging, or personal expenses should be declined.

* Acceptable gifts and donations [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/daXoj9)

* Gifts from patients that are small and do not have substantial monetary value (e.g., home-cooked meals, flowers, knitted quilts) ;  [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hYXcK9)

* The gift should not influence the patient's care.

* Gifts that may represent a financial sacrifice for the patient should be declined.

* No amount of direct cash exchange (e.g., checks, deposits) is acceptable.

* The clinician may suggest that the patient donate to a charitable organization in lieu of a personal gift.

* When declining gifts, clinicians should <u>stress</u> that the <u>clinician-patient relationship</u> remains intact.

* Medical industry honoraria to attend medical education <u>conferences</u>

* Industry-funded simple meals or social events

* Remuneration for medical consultation for a pharmaceutical or medical device company

* Industry-funded scholarships for travel to academic <u>conferences</u> by medical students or residents.





> [!TIP]
> The clinician must disclose all COIs to all affected parties and, in the event of a <u>COI</u>, refer patients to an unbiased colleague whenever possible.




### Conscientious refusal




* Definition: <u>Conscientious refusal</u> is the denial of indicated medical care because it poses a conflict with the personal, religious, or moral beliefs of the provider (e.g., abortion, <u>birth control</u>).

* <u>Health care provider</u> responsibilities

* Timely referral to a different provider who offers the required care

* In case of emergency, when a referral is not possible, or if the patient's physical or mental health is at risk, provision of necessary care regardless of moral conflict

* Provide patients with accurate and unbiased information and resources to facilitate informed decision-making.

* Notify patients about moral conflicts related to the provision of standard care (e.g., abortion, <u>contraceptives</u>, sterilization).

* Abortion services and reproductive medicine

* Individuals should be referred within an appropriate time frame to a provider who offers the required medical care.

* Resource-poor areas

* Denying safe and legal access to reproductive services in low-income communities promotes inequality

* Providers who object to providing care on moral grounds should only practice in areas where other providers are available and/or where referrals can be made.

* Key considerations

* Patient well-being and access to ethical medical care are the foremost priorities.

* Conditions in which <u>conscientious refusal</u> is unethical:

* When it imposes religious or moral beliefs on patients

* When it has a negative effect on patient well-being

* When it is based on scientific misinformation

* When it reinforces racial or socioeconomic inequalities

## Referral of patients

* The physician may refer a patient to another physician or diagnostic or therapeutic service if they consider the referral beneficial to the patient. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8tXO2-)

* The referral may involve nonphysician <u>health care providers</u>.

* It is never ethical to refer a patient to illegal medical treatment.

* Referrals to <u>complementary and alternative medicine</u> providers may be ethical if the use of such practices is grounded in scientific knowledge (e.g., chiropractic interventions or <u>acupuncture</u> for <u>lower back pain</u>). [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8tXO2-)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9uXNt-)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CuXqt-)

* Stark law prohibits the physician from <u>self-referrals</u> (i.e., referring patients to entities with which the physician or one of their immediate family members has a financial relationship).  [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9tXNf-)

* Physicians who perform <u>self-referrals</u> should ensure that the referral is indeed required, disclose all the potential COIs to the patient, and provide them with information about alternative care providers. [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wtXhf-)
