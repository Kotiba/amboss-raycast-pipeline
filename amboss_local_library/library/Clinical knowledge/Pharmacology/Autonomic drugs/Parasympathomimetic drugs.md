## Summary

Parasympathomimetic drugs activate the <u>parasympathetic nervous system</u> (PSNS). As the <u>neurotransmitter</u> of the PSNS is <u>acetylcholine</u> (<u>ACh</u>), parasympathomimetics are also called cholinomimetic agents. These are classified according to whether they act as direct <u>agonists</u> of <u>acetylcholine receptors</u> (<u>AChR</u>) or indirect <u>agonists</u> of <u>AChR</u> (also called <u>anticholinesterase</u> inhibitors). While direct <u>agonists</u> act by binding directly to muscarinic or <u>nicotinic ACh receptors</u>, indirect <u>agonists</u> prolong the action of <u>endogenous</u> <u>acetylcholine</u> by inhibiting <u>acetylcholinesterase</u> (<u>AChE</u>). Direct <u>agonists</u> of <u>AChR</u> are used topically in ophthalmology to induce <u>miosis</u>, while indirect <u>agonists</u> are used to treat conditions such as <u>postoperative ileus</u>, <u>urinary retention</u>, and <u>myasthenia gravis</u>.

## Adverse effects

### <u>Direct parasympathomimetics</u>




* Local side effects: blurred <u>vision</u> due to <u>miosis</u> when applied to the eyes

* Systemic side effects: identical to those of <u>indirect parasympathomimetics</u> (see below)





### <u>Indirect parasympathomimetics</u>




* Cardiovascular symptoms 

* <u>Bradycardia</u>

* <u>Hypotension</u>

* Gastrointestinal symptoms

* <u>Diarrhea</u>

* <u>Nausea</u>

* Abdominal <u>pain</u>

* Genitourinary symptoms: uncontrolled <u>urination</u>

* <u>Exocrine glands</u>

* ↑ Sweating

* ↑ Salivation

* ↑ <u>Gastric secretion</u>

* Ocular symptoms

* <u>Miosis</u>

* <u>Lacrimation</u>

* <u>CNS</u>-related symptoms: can culminate in <u>coma</u>

* <u>Hypoventilation</u>

* <u>Lethargy</u>

* <u>Tremor</u>

* Restlessness

* <u>Anxiety</u>

* <u>Ataxia</u>

* Musculoskeletal symptoms

* <u>Weakness</u>

* <u>Paralysis</u> → peripheral neuromuscular <u>respiratory failure</u>

* Spasms

* <u>Fasciculations</u>





#### <u>Cholinergic poisoning</u>




* Clinical features

* Muscarinic hyperstimulation

* <u>Diarrhea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>

* <u>Urinary incontinence</u>

* <u>Miosis</u>, blurred <u>vision</u>

* <u>Bronchospasm</u>, bronchorrhea

* <u>Bradycardia</u>, <u>hypotension</u>

* Increased <u>lacrimation</u> and salivation

* <u>Diaphoresis</u>

* Nicotinic hyperstimulation

* <u>Fasciculations</u>

* <u>Muscle weakness</u>

* <u>Respiratory failure</u> secondary to <u>paralysis</u>

* <u>CNS</u> effects

* Confusion, <u>agitation</u>, <u>tremors</u>, <u>seizures</u>, <u>lethargy</u>, <u>coma</u>

* Decreased <u>respiratory drive</u>

* Treatment

* <u>Atropine</u> (<u>antimuscarinic agent</u>)

* <u>Pralidoxime</u>

* See “<u>Treatment of cholinergic poisoning</u>.”





> [!NOTE]
> <u>DUMBBBELLSS</u>: Diarrhea, Urination, Miosis, Bradycardia, Bronchospasm, Bronchorrhea, Emesis, Lacrimation, Lethargy, Sweating, and Salivation are the main symptoms of <u>cholinergic crisis</u>.
