# Bisphosphonates

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tm0Xhg)

## Summary

Bisphosphonates (e.g., etidronate, alendronate) are used for the <u>treatment of hypercalcemia</u> and bone metabolism disorders, such as <u>osteoporosis</u> or <u>tumor</u>-induced <u>osteolysis</u>. All bisphosphonates primarily slow down the degradation of bone substance by interfering with <u>osteoclast</u> function. Important side effects of bisphosphonate therapy include <u>hypocalcemia</u>, renal impairment, and <u>aseptic osteonecrosis of the jaw</u>. Therefore, bisphosphonates are contraindicated in patients with <u>hypocalcemia</u> and those with a limited <u>glomerular filtration rate</u> (<u>GFR</u>). Additionally, oral bisphosphonates may cause esophageal damage while IV bisphosphonates can induce <u>flu-like</u> symptoms.

## Pharmacodynamics

* Bisphosphonates are <u>pyrophosphate</u> analogs that bind to <u>hydroxyapatite</u> binding sites on the surface of <u>bone tissue</u>: uptake by <u>osteoclasts</u> during phases of bone resorption → interference with <u>osteoclast</u> function and promotion of <u>osteoclast</u> <u>apoptosis</u> → reduced bone resorption 

* Simple bisphosphonates (tiludronate, etidronate)  : reduction of <u>osteoclast</u> proton production, suppression of <u>osteoclast</u> maturation and recruitment, and promotion of <u>osteoclast</u> <u>apoptosis</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i6bJku)

* Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, <u>zoledronate</u>): inhibition of the enzyme farnesyl <u>pyrophosphate</u> <u>synthase</u> → disturbance of <u>osteoclast</u> metabolism and signaling  → reduced <u>osteoclast</u> function and promotion of <u>osteoclast</u> <u>apoptosis</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Q6buku)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/j6b_ku)

* Inhibit mineralization (structurally similar to <u>pyrophosphate</u>, a natural inhibitor of bone mineralization)





> [!TIP]
> The nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates have a stronger effect than the simple bisphosphonates! [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Q6buku)




> [!NOTE]
> Bisphosphonates also reduce bone formation since bone resorption and formation are intrinsically connected. However, bone resorption is reduced more severely than bone formation.

## Adverse effects

* General 

* <u>Hypocalcemia</u>;  (usually mild, but may lead to <u>osteomalacia</u>; see “<u>Osteomalacia and rickets</u>”)  and <u>hypophosphatemia</u> (usually transient and mild; <u>hyperphosphatemia</u> has also been reported) [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PoXWX_)

* Bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw: adverse effect of bisphosphonates, usually seen with high-dose IV administration in <u>tumor</u> patients 

* Etiology: Trauma to the <u>jaw</u> (e.g., <u>tooth</u> extractions or other dental procedures) is a trigger in many cases.

* Pathophysiology: not yet fully understood

* Clinical features: chronic swelling, mild <u>pain</u>, exposed <u>necrotic</u> bone

* Treatment: supportive, e.g.,:

* Improving oral hygiene

* <u>Antibiotic</u> rinses

* Limited <u>debridement</u>

* Prevention

* Dental examination in conjunction with treatment of preexisting dental conditions (e.g., extraction of unrestorable <u>teeth</u>) prior to initiation of therapy

* Establishing a daily <u>oral care</u> plan that involves daily oral rinsing, brushing, and flossing

* <u>Atypical femoral fractures</u>

* Musculoskeletal <u>pain</u>

* <u>Atrial fibrillation</u>

* Renal impairment

* Ocular inflammation and visual disturbances

* Oral bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, etidronate, tiludronate)

* Esophageal inflammation and cancer

* To avoid this complication, patients should swallow medication with a sufficient amount of water and maintain an upright position for 30 minutes.

* IV bisphosphonates (zolendronate); : acute-phase reaction with <u>flulike symptoms</u>;  (e.g., <u>fever</u>, <u>joint</u>, and muscle <u>pain</u>) 24–72 hours after administration





> [!TIP]
> Bisphosphonates should be taken in the morning with sufficient water and in an upright position at least 60 minutes before eating!

## Indications

* <u>Osteoporosis</u>

* <u>Hypercalcemia</u>

* <u>Tumor</u>-induced <u>osteolysis</u> (e.g., <u>metastasis</u> to the bone)

* <u>Multiple myeloma</u>

* <u>Paget's disease of bone</u>

* Hereditary skeletal disorders (e.g., <u>osteogenesis imperfecta</u>)





References: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gA0FQi)

## Contraindications

* Common to all bisphosphonates

* Hypersensitivity

* Esophageal abnormalities (e.g., strictures)

* Inability to stand or sit upright for at least 30 minutes after oral bisphosphonate therapy

* For <u>zoledronic acid</u>

* <u>Hypocalcemia</u>

* Reduced <u>creatinine clearance</u> (< 30–35 ml/min/1.73 m2)

* <u>Pregnancy</u>/lactation period: no clear contraindications, individual risks/benefits must be weighed




We list the most important contraindications. The selection is not exhaustive.
