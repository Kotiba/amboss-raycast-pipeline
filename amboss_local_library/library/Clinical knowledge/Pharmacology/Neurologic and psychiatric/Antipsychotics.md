## Summary

Antipsychotics are a heterogeneous group of substances used primarily to treat <u>schizophrenia</u>, <u>psychosis</u>, <u>mania</u>, <u>delusions</u>, and states of <u>agitation</u>. The term neuroleptics was formerly used interchangeably with antipsychotics because early antipsychotic drugs induced <u>apathy</u>, quiescence, and reduced psychomotor activity, but newer antipsychotic drugs have a decreased risk of these effects. The antipsychotic effect of <u>first-generation antipsychotics</u> (also called <u>typical antipsychotics</u>, e.g., <u>haloperidol</u>) is based on D<sub>2</sub> <u>antagonism</u>, while <u>second-generation antipsychotics</u> (also called <u>atypical antipsychotics</u>) interact with several <u>receptors</u> (e.g., D<sub>2</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, D<sub>4</sub>, <u>5-HT</u>). <u>Extrapyramidal symptoms</u>, which include <u>acute dystonia</u>, <u>akathisia</u>, and <u>tardive dyskinesia</u>, are the most common side effects of <u>first-generation antipsychotics</u>. Metabolic side effects (e.g., weight gain, <u>insulin resistance</u>), on the other hand, are more typical of <u>second-generation antipsychotics</u>. A potentially life-threatening side effect of both first-generation and <u>second-generation antipsychotics</u> is <u>neuroleptic malignant syndrome</u>, which manifests with <u>fever</u>, muscle rigidity, <u>autonomic instability</u>, and mental status changes.

## Overview

### Description




* <u>First-generation antipsychotics</u> (also called <u>typical antipsychotics</u>): block <u>D2 receptor</u> → ↑ <u>cAMP</u>

* <u>High-potency antipsychotics</u> have a strong antipsychotic effect even at relatively low doses, but they also more commonly cause neurologic side effects (e.g., <u>extrapyramidal symptoms</u>) than <u>low-potency antipsychotics</u>.

* <u>Low-potency antipsychotics</u> more commonly cause <u>anticholinergic</u>, <u>antihistamine</u>, and <u>sympathetic</u> α<sub>1</sub>-blockade effects.

* Stored in <u>fat tissue</u> (lipid soluble) and, therefore, only slowly eliminated from the body.

* <u>Second-generation antipsychotics</u>:  (also called <u>atypical antipsychotics</u>): most are 5-HT<sub>2</sub> and D<sub>2</sub> <u>antagonists</u> with varying α and <u>H<sub>1</sub> receptor</u> effects

## Indications

* All antipsychotics, except for <u>clozapine</u> (used for <u>treatment-resistant schizophrenia</u>), have similar clinical <u>effectiveness</u>.

* The choice of drug depends on the side effect profile of the antipsychotic drugs and the patient's clinical status.

* <u>SGAs</u> are preferred in many cases because they carry a lower risk of <u>EPS</u>; however, in some patients (e.g., those with significant metabolic <u>risk factors</u>), FGAs may be more suitable.

### Extrapyramidal symptoms (<u>EPS</u>) [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Jecs-Y0)




These recommendations are consistent with the 2020 American Psychiatric Association guidelines for the <u>management of schizophrenia</u>. Consult <u>psychiatry</u> if an adjustment of psychiatric medications is required. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EVc8wY0)




* Definition: a collection of movement disorders that are typically due to disruption of <u>dopaminergic pathways</u> in the <u>basal ganglia</u>, resulting in <u>bradykinesia</u>, rigidity, <u>dystonia</u>, <u>athetosis</u>, <u>chorea</u>, <u>ballismus</u>, <u>akathisia</u>, <u>tics</u>, and <u>tremors</u>

* Etiology

* All antipsychotics that interact with the <u>D<sub>2</sub> receptor</u> may cause <u>EPS</u>, but the <u>probability</u> of this side effect is significantly higher with <u>high-potency FGAs</u> than with <u>SGAs</u>;  or <u>low-potency FGAs</u>.

* <u>Metoclopramide</u>, although not an antipsychotic, may also cause <u>EPS</u>.

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u> (e.g., <u>carbamazepine</u>) may also cause movement disorders. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S5cyj10)

* <u>Antidepressants</u> [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l5cv410)

* Pathophysiology: Inhibition of the <u>nigrostriatal</u> <u>dopaminergic pathways</u> results in <u>EPS</u>. ;  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W5cPQ10)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6NYjXp)

* First-generation <u>high-potency antipsychotics</u>: D<sub>2</sub><u>antagonism</u> → <u>EPS</u>

* <u>Second-generation antipsychotics</u>: weaker D<sub>2</sub><u>antagonism</u> → fewer <u>EPS</u>
