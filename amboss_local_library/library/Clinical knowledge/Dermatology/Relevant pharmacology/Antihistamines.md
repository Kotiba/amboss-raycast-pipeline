## Summary

<u>Histamine</u> is a biologically active substance that potentiates the inflammatory and <u>immune responses</u> of the body, regulates physiological function in the gut, and acts as a <u>neurotransmitter</u>. Antihistamines are drugs that antagonize these effects by blocking or inhibiting <u>histamine</u> <u>receptors</u> (H <u>receptors</u>). They are categorized as either H1 or H2 according to the type of H <u>receptor</u> targeted. <u>H1 antihistamines</u> are mostly used to treat <u>allergic reactions</u> and <u>mast cell</u>-mediated disorders. This subtype is further divided into two generations. While <u>first-generation H1 antihistamines</u> have a central effect and, thus, are also used as sedatives, <u>second-generation H1 antihistamines</u> have less central effects and are primarily used as antiallergic drugs. <u>H2 antihistamines</u> are indicated primarily for gastric reflux disease because they reduce the production of <u>stomach acid</u> by reversibly blocking the <u>H2 histamine receptors</u> in the <u>parietal cells</u> of the gastric <u>mucosa</u>. Most H1 and <u>H2 antihistamines</u> are contraindicated during <u>pregnancy</u> and childhood. <u>First-generation H1 antihistamines</u> are specifically contraindicated in <u>angle-closure glaucoma</u> and <u>pyloric stenosis</u>.

## H1 antihistamines

### Examples




* First-generation antihistamines

* <u>Diphenhydramine</u>

* <u>Meclizine</u>

* <u>Doxylamine</u>

* Promethazine

* Clemastine

* <u>Dimenhydrinate</u>

* Brompheniramine

* Hydroxyzine

* Chlorpheniramine

* Dimetindene

* <u>Cyproheptadine</u>

* Second-generation antihistamines

* Loratadine

* Desloratadine

* Cetirizine

* Levocetirizine

* Azelastine

* Fexofenadine

* Ketotifen





### General physiology




* Target: <u>histamine H1 receptors</u>

* Location of H1 receptors

* <u>Smooth muscles</u> (especially bronchial and <u>nasopharyngeal</u> lining)

* Vascular <u>endothelial</u> cell surfaces

* <u>Central nervous system</u>

* <u>Heart</u>

* <u>Histamine</u> effects on <u>H1 receptors</u>

* ↑ <u>Capillary</u> dilation and permeability → <u>hypotension</u> and <u>edema</u>

* ↑ Bronchiolar <u>smooth muscle</u> contraction (via <u>IP3</u> and <u>DAG</u> release) → bronchoconstriction

* ↑ Nasal and bronchial mucus production

* ↑ Activation of peripheral <u>nociceptive</u> <u>receptors</u> → ↑ <u>pain</u> and <u>pruritus</u>

* ↓ Conduction in <u>AV node</u>





### Effects



* Competitive, reversible <u>antagonism</u> or <u>inverse agonism</u> of <u>histamine H1 receptors</u> (<u>G<sub>q</sub> protein-coupled receptor</u>) 

* Inhibition of increased vascular permeability

* Inhibition of allergic bronchial constriction

* Central action: sedation (especially <u>first-generation antihistamines</u> due to crossing of <u>blood-brain barrier</u>)




### Side effects




* Sedation (significantly less pronounced in <u>second-generation antihistamines</u> due to their limited <u>CNS</u> activity)

* <u>Anticholinergic effects</u>, e.g., dry mouth and eyes, <u>mydriasis</u>, <u>urinary retention</u>, <u>tachycardia</u>, <u>dizziness</u>, <u>tinnitus</u> (mostly with <u>first-generation antihistamines</u>)

* Anti-<u>α-adrenergic</u> effects, e.g., <u>postural hypotension</u> (can lead to falls), weight gain

* <u>Headaches</u>





### Indications




* <u>First-generation antihistamines</u>

* <u>Allergies</u>

* <u>Anaphylactic shock</u> (e.g., <u>clemastine</u>, <u>dimetindene</u>)

* <u>Insomnia</u>: used as sedatives (<u>sleep</u> aid)

* <u>Nausea</u>: used as <u>antiemetic agents</u> (e.g., <u>diphenhydramine</u> in hyperemesis during <u>pregnancy</u>) because of their central effects

* <u>Pruritus</u>

* <u>Motion sickness</u>

* <u>Second-generation antihistamines</u>

* <u>Allergies</u>: used as <u>antiallergic agent</u> (e.g., in cases of <u>rhinitis</u>, <u>conjunctivitis</u>, hypersensitivity to drugs, <u>urticaria</u>)

* <u>Pruritus</u>

* <u>Anaphylactic shock</u>





> [!TIP]
> Antihistamines are ineffective in acute <u>hereditary angioedema</u> attacks. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DpY17J)




### Contraindications




* <u>First-generation antihistamines</u>

* <u>Benign prostatic hyperplasia</u>

* <u>Angle-closure glaucoma</u>

* <u>Pyloric stenosis</u>

* <u>Second-generation antihistamines</u>: childhood and <u>pregnancy</u>

## H2 antihistamines

### Examples




* Ranitidine  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ANXRdA)

* Famotidine

* Nizatidine

* Cimetidine





### General physiology




* Target: <u>histamine H2 receptors</u>

* Location of H2 receptors

* <u>Gastric parietal cells</u> (<u>oxyntic cells</u>)

* Vascular <u>smooth muscle</u>

* <u>Neutrophils</u>

* <u>Central nervous system</u>

* <u>Heart</u>

* <u>Uterus</u>

* <u>Histamine</u> effects on <u>H2 receptors</u>

* Increased <u>gastric acid</u> secretion

* Positive inotropism and enhanced <u>automaticity</u>

* <u>Smooth muscle</u> relaxation leading to vasodilatation





### Effects




Competitive, reversible <u>antagonism</u> of <u>histamine H2 receptors</u> (<u>G<sub>s</sub> protein-coupled receptor</u>) on <u>parietal cells</u> → ↓ <u>adenylyl cyclase</u> activity → ↓ <u>cAMP</u> levels → ↓ <u>protein kinase A</u> activity → ↓ <u>phosphorylation</u> and activation of <u>H<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup> ATPase</u> → ↓ <u>gastric acid</u> (H<sup>+</sup>) secretion




### Side effects




* Almost all side effects are caused by <u>cimetidine</u>; side effects in other <u>H2 blockers</u> are rare.

* <u>Cimetidine</u>

* Antiandrogenic effect (via release of <u>prolactin</u>) → <u>erectile dysfunction</u>, <u>gynecomastia</u>, low libido in men

* Inhibition of <u>cytochrome P450</u> (<u>CYP2C19</u>) → various drugs interactions, e.g., <u>clopidogrel</u>

* <u>Headaches</u>, <u>dizziness</u>, confusion due to its ability to cross the <u>blood-brain barrier</u>

* Can cross the <u>placenta</u> (but is considered safe)

* Reduces renal <u>creatinine</u> excretion (along with <u>ranitidine</u>)





### Indications




* <u>Anaphylactic shock</u> (together with <u>H1 antihistamines</u>)

* Symptomatic treatment of <u>peptic ulcers</u>: reduce the production of <u>hydrochloric acid</u> (less effective than <u>PPIs</u> )

* <u>Gastroesophageal reflux disease</u> (<u>GERD</u>): Consider as alternate <u>maintenance therapy</u> for <u>NERD</u>, or in addition to <u>PPIs</u> to control nighttime symptoms

* <u>Gastritis</u>

* <u>Zollinger-Ellison syndrome</u>





### <u>Relative contraindications</u>




* <u>Pregnancy</u>, since <u>cimetidine</u> is known to cross the <u>placental barrier</u>

* Nursing mothers

* Children





> [!NOTE]
> Drink a glass of H<sub>2</sub>O before dining: H2 blockers need to be taken before dinner.
