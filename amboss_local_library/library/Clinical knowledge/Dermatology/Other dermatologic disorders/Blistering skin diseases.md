## Summary

This article provides an <u>overview of blistering skin diseases</u>, including <u>bullous impetigo</u>, <u>staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome</u> (<u>SSSS</u>), <u>Stevens-Johnson syndrome</u> (<u>SJS</u>)/<u>toxic epidermal necrolysis</u> (TEN), <u>epidermolysis bullosa acquisita</u>, <u>bullous pemphigoid</u>, <u>pemphigus vulgaris</u>, and <u>dermatitis herpetiformis</u>.




For more information on each specific condition, see the respective articles.

---

| <u>Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome</u> (<u>SSSS</u>) | <br>* 6 months–5 years of age<br><br> | <br><br>* Initially: <u>fever</u> and diffuse or localized <u>erythema</u> that begins periorally<br><br>* After 24–48 hours<br><br>* Flaccid, easily ruptured <u>blisters</u> that break to reveal moist, red <u>skin</u> beneath (resembles <u>scalding</u>)<br><br>* No <u>mucosal</u> involvement<br><br>* Positive <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> |  | <br><br>* Hospitalization<br><br>* IV <u>antibiotics</u><br><br>* <u>Nafcillin</u>, <u>oxacillin</u><br><br>* <u>Vancomycin</u> (in settings with high <u>MRSA</u> <u>prevalence</u>)<br><br>* <u>NSAIDs</u><br><br>* Supportive case (esp. <u>fluid resuscitation</u>)<br><br>* <u>Emollients</u><br><br>* Dressing of areas where <u>skin</u> has sloughed off<br><br><br> |  |
| <u>Stevens-Johnson syndrome</u> (<u>SJS</u>)/<u>toxic epidermal necrolysis</u> (TEN) | <br>* All ages<br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Adverse drug reaction</u> (most common)<br><br>* <u>Sulfasalazine</u><br><br>* Oxicam <u>NSAIDs</u><br><br>* <u>Antiepileptics</u> (e.g., <u>phenytoin</u>, <u>phenobarbital</u>)<br><br>* <u>Antibiotics</u>: <u>sulfonamides</u> (e.g., <u>TMP/SMX</u>), <u>aminopenicillins</u><br><br>* Infections: <u>mycoplasma pneumonia</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Prodromal</u> phase (1–3 weeks after trigger exposure/infection)<br>* High <u>fever</u>, <u>malaise</u>, <u>sore throat</u>, <u>myalgia</u>, and/or <u>arthralgia</u><br><br>* Mucocutaneous lesions (1–3 days after <u>prodromal</u> phase)<br><br>* Cutaneous<br><br>* Painful, <u>erythematous</u>/<u>purpuric</u> <u>macules</u><br><br>* May have a targetoid appearance<br><br>* <u>Bullae</u> and/or <u>vesicles</u><br><br>* <u>Mucosal</u><br><br>* Oropharyngeal: <u>stomatitis</u>, <u>mucositis</u>, pharyngeal involvement<br><br>* Ocular: severe <u>conjunctivitis</u>, <u>corneal ulcers</u>, <u>anterior uveitis</u><br><br>* Urogenital: <u>urethritis</u>, ulcerative <u>vaginitis</u>, vulvar <u>bullae</u><br><br>* Positive <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> |  | <br><br>* Discontinue causative drug<br><br>* Supportive therapy and <u>wound management</u><br><br>* <u>Antibiotic therapy</u> (in infection/<u>sepsis</u>)<br><br>* Regular dermatological, ophthalmological, and urological/gynecological evaluation<br><br><br> |
| <u>Epidermolysis bullosa acquisita</u> | <br>* 40–60 years of age<br><br> | <br>* <u>Autoantibodies</u> against type VII <u>collagen</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Subepidermal <u>blistering</u><br><br>* Noninflammatory form (most common)<br>* Tense <u>vesicles</u> and <u>blisters</u> on extensor surfaces (e.g., hands, knees, knuckles)<br><br>* Inflammatory form<br>* Tense <u>vesicles</u> and <u>bullae</u> on areas such as the trunk and <u>skin</u> folds)<br><br>* Negative <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> | <br> | <br><br>* Systemic <u>steroids</u><br><br>* <u>Immunosuppressants</u><br><br>* Avoid <u>skin</u> trauma<br><br><br> |
| <u>Bullous pemphigoid</u> | <br>* > 60 years of age<br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Type II hypersensitivity reaction</u><br><br>* <u>Antihemidesmosome antibodies</u> (<u>IgG</u>)<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Prodromal</u> stage: <u>urticarial</u> lesions<br><br>* Large, tense, subepidermal <u>blisters</u> on normal, <u>erythematous</u>, or erosive <u>skin</u> weeks to months after <u>prodromal</u> stage<br><br>* Intensely pruritic, possibly hemorrhagic, lesions, heal without <u>scar</u> formation<br><br>* Distributed on palms, soles, lower legs, groin, and <u>axillae</u><br><br>* Oral involvement is rare<br><br>* Negative <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> |  | <br><br>* First-line: high-dose <u>topical steroids</u> (e.g., <u>clobetasol</u>, <u>betamethasone</u>)<br><br>* Second-line: <u>systemic glucocorticoids</u> and <u>immunosuppressants</u> (e.g., <u>methotrexate</u>, <u>azathioprine</u>)<br><br><br> |
| <u>Pemphigus vulgaris</u> | <br>* 40–60 years of age<br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Type II hypersensitivity reaction</u><br><br>* <u>IgG antibodies</u> against <u>desmoglein</u> 3 and <u>desmoglein</u> 1<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Initially affects the oral <u>mucosa</u> before extending to parts of the body exposed to pressure<br><br>* Progression in stages<br><br>* First, spontaneous onset of painful flaccid, <u>intraepidermal</u> <u>blisters</u><br><br>* Then, rupture and confluence of lesions → development of <u>erosions</u> and crusts → reepithelialization with <u>hyperpigmentation</u> but without scarring<br><br>* <u>Pruritus</u> is typically absent<br><br>* Positive <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> |  | <br><br>* First-line: <u>Rituximab</u> and <u>systemic glucocorticoids</u> (e.g., <u>prednisone</u>)<br><br>* <u>Systemic glucocorticoids</u> plus <u>immunosuppressive therapy</u> (e.g., <u>azathioprine</u>)<br><br>* Topical treatment<br><br><br> |
| <u>Dermatitis herpetiformis</u> | <br>* 15–40 years of age<br><br> | <br><br>* Most likely genetic predisposition to autoimmune reaction<br><br>* Associated with <u>celiac disease</u> and <u>sensitivity</u> to <u>potassium iodide</u> (e.g., contrast medium)<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Tense, grouped subepidermal <u>vesicles</u>, <u>papules</u>, and/or <u>bullae</u> (herpetiform appearance)<br><br>* Intensely pruritic<br><br>* Bilateral, symmetrical distribution (e.g., <u>elbows</u>, knees, buttocks, shoulders, scalp)<br><br>* No <u>mucosal</u> involvement<br><br>* Negative <u>Nikolsky sign</u><br><br><br> |  | <br><br>* First-line: <u>dapsone</u><br><br>* <u>Gluten</u>-free diet<br><br>* <u>Topical steroids</u> to control severe <u>pruritus</u><br><br>* Low-<u>iodine</u> diet<br><br><br> |

> [!TIP]
