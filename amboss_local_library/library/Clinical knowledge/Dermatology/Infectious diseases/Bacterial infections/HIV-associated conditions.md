## Summary

As <u>HIV infection</u> progresses, immune function declines, and affected individuals become increasingly vulnerable to <u>opportunistic infections</u> and certain malignancies. Some of these conditions are typically seen when <u>CD4 counts</u> are less than 200 cells/μL; these are known as <u>AIDS-defining conditions</u>. With early detection of <u>HIV infection</u> and widespread use of <u>antiretroviral therapy</u> (<u>ART</u>), many of these conditions have become less common and more treatable. Patients with <u>HIV</u> are now more likely to develop non-<u>HIV</u>-associated conditions or complications of <u>ART</u>, such as <u>immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome</u> (IRIS), <u>hepatotoxicity</u>, or <u>nephrotoxicity</u>. However, even patients with well-controlled <u>HIV</u> remain at increased risk of some communicable and noncommunicable diseases, and the presentation of these conditions may be atypical. This article provides a system-based overview of <u>HIV</u>-associated conditions and their management.




See also “<u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>,” “<u>Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy</u>,” “<u>Bacillary angiomatosis</u>,” and “<u>Mycobacterium avium complex infections</u>.”

## AIDS-defining conditions

### Overview [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kNXmYA)




* <u>AIDS-defining conditions</u> are a set of potentially life-threatening conditions that indicate the progression of <u>HIV infection</u> to <u>AIDS</u>

* As the <u>CD4 count</u> declines, the <u>immune system</u> is weakened and many pathological processes may occur, such as:

* Development of malignancies e.g., <u>non-Hodgkin lymphomas</u>

* Rapid spread of opportunistic and nonopportunistic bacterial and <u>fungal infections</u> (e.g., <u>coccidioidomycosis</u>, <u>pneumocystis pneumonia</u>, <u>mycobacterial</u> infections)

* Reactivation of <u>latent infections</u> (e.g., <u>tuberculosis</u>, <u>herpes simplex</u> infections, <u>shingles</u>)

* <u>AIDS</u> (also known as <u>advanced HIV</u>) is defined as the development of an <u>AIDS-defining condition</u> or a <u>CD4 count</u> of < 200 cells/μL in <u>HIV</u>-infected patients. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Z5XZiA)

> [!TIP]
> Some <u>AIDS-defining conditions</u> are <u>opportunistic infections</u>, but the terms are not interchangeable!




### Management [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




* Optimize <u>antiretroviral therapy</u> (most effective measure).

* Treat <u>AIDS-defining condition</u> (see relevant sections below for details).

* Prevent further <u>opportunistic infections</u>.

* See “<u>Primary prevention of opportunistic infections in HIV</u>” and “<u>Vaccinations in HIV-infected individuals</u>.”

* Avoid <u>pathogen</u> exposure.





> [!TIP]
> Adequate <u>HIV treatment</u> (<u>ART</u>) is key to minimizing the risk of <u>opportunistic infections</u>.

### HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND)




* Definition: : neurocognitive impairment in patients with <u>HIV</u> that cannot be attributed to a cause other than <u>HIV infection</u>

* Etiology: thought to result from a combination of dissemination of <u>HIV</u> into the <u>CNS</u>  and the resultant immune activation [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Epidemiology</u>: common even in patients with well-controlled <u>HIV</u> (affecting up to 50% of individuals) [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LEXww-)

* Clinical features [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LEXww-)

* Early: mild impairment in attention, recall, and <u>executive function</u>

* Advanced: HIV-associated dementia (considered an <u>AIDS-defining condition</u>)

* Subcortical <u>dementia</u>: <u>memory</u> loss, <u>depression</u>, movement disorders, behavioral changes (e.g., <u>apathy</u>)

* Severe neurologic deficits: <u>altered mental state</u>, <u>aphasia</u>, gait disturbances [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KEXU9-)

* More common in patients with advanced or untreated <u>HIV</u>  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vXcA-a0)

* Due to <u>effectiveness</u> of <u>HAART</u>, elderly <u>HIV</u> patients more commonly develop non-<u>HIV dementia</u> such as <u>Alzheimer disease</u> than <u>HIV-associated dementia</u>.

* Diagnostics [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QEXuv-)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/oEX09-)

* Cognitive and <u>neuropsychological tests</u>  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LEXww-)
* Assess at least 5 cognitive domains: attention, language, <u>memory</u>, simple and complex motor skills, sensory-perceptual skills, and higher executive functioning.

* HAND: <u>mild cognitive impairment</u> (1 SD below mean) in ≥ 2 domains

* <u>HIV-associated dementia</u>: significant cognitive impairment (2 SDs below mean) in ≥ 2 domains

* Imaging: CT or <u>MRI</u> <u>brain</u> without and with IV contrast

* Diffuse <u>cerebral atrophy</u>; disproportionate to the patient's age

* Patchy symmetrical changes in the periventricular and deep <u>white matter</u>

* No <u>mass effect</u>, no contrast-<u>enhancement</u>

* Additional evaluation

* Rule out other <u>causes of dementia</u> (e.g., normal B<sub>12</sub>, <u>folate</u>, and <u>TSH</u> levels, negative <u>syphilis</u>).

* <u>Brain biopsy</u> [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KEXU9-)

* Performed only if there is diagnostic uncertainty

* <u>Histopathology</u> shows <u>giant cells</u> with multiple <u>nuclei</u> (formed through fusion of <u>HIV</u>-infected <u>monocytes</u>).

* Treatment: <u>antiretroviral therapy</u> (but avoid <u>efavirenz</u>)  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pEXL9-)[[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FXcg-a0)

### HIV-associated distal symmetric polyneuropathy (<u>HIV</u>–DSPN) [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mUWVdk0)




* Definition: axonal <u>peripheral neuropathy</u> in individuals with <u>HIV</u> that cannot be attributed to a cause other than <u>HIV infection</u>

* Etiology: damage to the nervous system by <u>HIV</u> or <u>ART</u> medications

* <u>Risk factors</u>

* In patients not treated with <u>ART</u>: low <u>CD4 count</u> and a high viral load

* In patients treated with <u>ART</u>

* Age > 40 years

* <u>Stavudine</u> use

* <u>Diabetes mellitus</u>

* Excessive substance use

* <u>Statin</u> use

* Clinical features

* Numbness, tingling, and <u>pain</u> in <u>stocking-glove distribution</u>

* Decreased pinprick and vibration sensation involving <u>distal</u> lower extremities

* Decreased/absent <u>ankle jerk reflex</u>

* <u>Proximal</u> spread of symptoms suggests <u>HIV-DSPN</u> progression.

* Diagnostics

* Laboratory testing to rule out non-<u>HIV</u>-related causes (e.g., <u>basic metabolic panel</u>, <u>HbA1c</u>)

* Typical clinical presentation and exclusion of other causes of <u>peripheral neuropathy</u> are sufficient for diagnosis.

* Treatment

* Management of reversible <u>risk factors</u> (e.g., initiate <u>ART</u> in untreated patients with <u>HIV</u>, optimize management in patients with <u>diabetes mellitus</u>)

* Symptomatic <u>pain management</u>

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u> (e.g., <u>gabapentin</u>, <u>pregabalin</u>, <u>lamotrigine</u>) are the preferred medication

* Topical <u>capsaicin</u> patches

* <u>Antidepressants</u> (e.g., <u>duloxetine</u>, <u>amitriptyline</u>) are preferred in patients with <u>depression</u>.

* <u>NSAIDs</u>, <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>opioids</u>

## Pulmonary complications

### <u>Reactivation tuberculosis</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




* <u>Epidemiology</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)

* In patients with <u>HIV</u>, <u>tuberculosis</u> (<u>TB</u>) is the leading cause of <u>morbidity</u> and mortality worldwide.

* Typically occurs at <u>CD4 counts</u> < 400.

* Clinical features: <u>Active TB</u> may be subclinical or asymptomatic in patients with <u>HIV</u>.

* <u>CD4 count</u> > 200: The presentation is similar to that of <u>HIV</u>-negative patients (e.g., <u>cough</u>, <u>fever</u>, weight loss, night sweats).

* <u>CD4 count</u> < 200

* May present with a rapidly progressive illness with <u>features of sepsis</u>, including high <u>fever</u>

* Higher risk of extrapulmonary or <u>disseminated TB</u>

* Diagnostics: : similar to that of <u>HIV</u>-negative patients (see “Diagnostics” in “<u>Tuberculosis</u>”)

* Treatment

* Start empiric treatment in patients with <u>advanced HIV</u> with clinical and radiographic features that suggest <u>active TB</u> (see “<u>Tuberculosis</u>” for details and dosages).

* Indications for treating as <u>LTBI</u>

* Positive <u>screening tests</u> without signs of active infection

* Close contact with a person with infectious <u>TB</u>, regardless of the results of <u>screening tests</u>

* Initiate <u>ART</u> 

* Within 2 weeks of starting <u>TB</u> therapy if <u>CD4 count</u> is < 50 and within 8 weeks in patients with higher <u>CD4 counts</u>

* <u>ART</u> regimens may need to be altered to minimize <u>drug interactions</u>.

* Monitor for IRIS in patients with <u>CD4 counts</u> < 100.

### <u>Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia</u> (<u>PCP</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




<u>Pneumocystis jirovecii</u> is a ubiquitous fungus that can cause <u>pneumonia</u> in patients with <u>AIDS</u>, especially those with <u>CD4 counts</u> < 200. The <u>incidence</u> has declined since the introduction of <u>ART</u> and the use of prophylaxis.




* Clinical features

* Subacute onset (e.g., 3–4 weeks) of <u>fever</u>, nonproductive <u>cough</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>, and <u>chest pain</u>

* <u>Spontaneous pneumothorax</u>

* Fulminant <u>pneumonia</u> is uncommon in patients with <u>HIV</u>.

* Management: Same as for <u>HIV</u>-negative patients; see “<u>Pneumocystis pneumonia</u>.”

* Characteristic diagnostic findings

* ↑ <u>Beta-D-glucan</u>

* <u>CXR</u>: diffuse bilateral infiltrates (ground-glass appearance)

* Treatment of choice: <u>trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole</u> (<u>TMP/SMX</u>) with or without <u>glucocorticoids</u>

* Special considerations in <u>HIV</u>

* Start <u>ART</u> (if not yet initiated) within 2 weeks of <u>PCP</u> diagnosis.

* Monitor for <u>adverse events</u> caused by <u>TMP-SMX</u>.

* <u>PCP prophylaxis</u>: See “<u>Primary prevention of opportunistic infections in HIV</u>.”

### <u>Community-acquired pneumonia</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




* Common; particularly at <u>CD4 counts</u> < 100

* Recurrent <u>pneumonia</u> (≥ 2 episodes within 1 year) is an <u>AIDS-defining illness</u>.

* Causative pathogens

* Common: <u>Streptococcus pneumoniae</u> and <u>Haemophilus influenzae</u>

* Less common: <u>Mycoplasma pneumonia</u>, <u>Chlamydia</u> spp., and <u>Staphylococcus aureus</u>

* Clinical features, diagnostics, and treatment are the same as for <u>HIV</u>-negative patients; see “<u>Pneumonia</u>” for further details.

* Prevention: <u>pneumococcal vaccine</u> and inactivated <u>influenza vaccine</u> are recommended; see “<u>Immunization schedule</u> for adults” for details.

* In patients not already on <u>ART</u>, initiate treatment as soon as possible.

## Gastrointestinal complications

### General principles




* GI symptoms are common in <u>HIV</u> and can occur at any <u>CD4 count</u>.

* Patients with well-controlled <u>HIV</u> are more likely to be experiencing common GI problems (i.e., similar to individuals without <u>HIV</u>).

* <u>Opportunistic infections</u>, <u>malignancy</u>, and <u>HIV wasting syndrome</u> are typically seen in patients with advanced <u>immunosuppression</u>.





### <u>Dyspepsia</u>, <u>dysphagia</u>, and <u>odynophagia</u> [[60]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqXgZz)[[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xYcEra0)




* Etiology

* <u>Advanced HIV</u>: <u>opportunistic infections</u> (e.g., <u>Candida</u>, <u>CMV</u>, <u>HSV</u>, <u>MAC</u>) or <u>malignancy</u> (e.g., <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>)

* Well-controlled <u>HIV</u>: Consider other causes (e.g., <u>GERD</u>); see “<u>Etiologies of esophagitis</u>” for details.

* <u>Odynophagia</u>: <u>esophagitis</u> or painful oropharyngeal lesions (see “<u>Mucosal and mucocutaneous complications in HIV infection</u>”)

* Management

* Start <u>ART</u> (if not already initiated).

* Mild <u>dysphagia</u>/<u>odynophagia</u>: Diagnostic studies are not routinely required; consider a trial of empiric therapy.

* <u>Advanced HIV</u>: trial of <u>fluconazole</u> DOSAGE for at least 2 weeks after resolution of symptoms.

* Well-controlled <u>HIV</u>: trial of <u>acid suppression therapy</u> with <u>PPIs</u> for 8 weeks

* Moderate to severe <u>dysphagia</u>/<u>odynophagia</u> or failure of empiric therapy: endoscopy for examination of <u>mucosa</u> and <u>biopsy</u>

* Characteristic findings in <u>advanced HIV</u>: See “<u>Infectious esophagitis</u>.”  [[62]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_EX5y-)

* Characteristic findings in well-controlled <u>HIV</u>: See “Initial diagnostics” in “<u>Esophagitis</u>.”

* Targeted treatment depends on the underlying etiology: See “<u>Infectious esophagitis</u>” for details and dosages.

### <u>Diarrhea</u> [[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xYcEra0)




<u>Diarrhea</u> is the most common GI complaint in patients with <u>HIV</u> and may occur at any <u>CD4 count</u>.




* Etiology

* Infectious (common in <u>advanced HIV</u>): e.g., <u>MAC</u>, <u>cryptosporidiosis</u>, <u>cystoisosporiasis</u>, <u>CMV</u>

* <u>Malignancy</u>: e.g., <u>lymphoma</u>, <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>

* <u>Idiopathic</u>: e.g., <u>HIV enteropathy</u>

* Medication-related (particularly <u>protease inhibitors</u>)

* Diagnostics [[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xYcEra0) 

* Stool analysis for infectious organisms

* Consider <u>blood culture</u> for suspected <u>MAC infection</u>.

* Upper and lower GI endoscopy with <u>biopsy</u> for patients with:

* Negative stool tests

* Suspected noninfectious etiology

* Treatment

* Infectious <u>diarrhea</u>: directed <u>antimicrobial therapy</u>

* <u>ART</u>-related <u>diarrhea</u>: Consider an alternative <u>ART</u> regimen, if possible.

* <u>Chronic diarrhea</u> secondary to <u>HIV enteropathy</u> [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AYcR7a0)

* Symptomatic management e.g., oral rehydration, <u>antidiarrheal agents</u>

* Low-fat, <u>lactose</u>-free diet

* Refractory cases: Consider <u>antisecretory agents</u>, e.g., <u>octreotide</u>, in consultation with a specialist.  [[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yYcd7a0)

Linear <u>ulcers</u> on endoscopy and intracellular <u>inclusions</u> (owl's <u>eye</u> appearance) on <u>histology</u> are characteristic diagnostic findings of <u>CMV infection</u>. ### HIV wasting syndrome [[66]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yQcdAX0)[[67]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_Qc5AX0)




* <u>CD4 count</u>: variable (typically seen in <u>advanced HIV</u>)

* Clinical features: <u>unintentional weight loss</u> of ≥ 10% and any of the following for ≥ 30 days 

* <u>Diarrhea</u>

* <u>Fever</u>

* Fatigue (<u>weakness</u>)

* Management

* Optimize nutrition (high-protein high-calorie diet).

* <u>Antiemetics</u> for <u>nausea</u>/<u>vomiting</u>

* Treat concurrent <u>gastrointestinal infections</u> (e.g., <u>CMV colitis</u>).

* Consider <u>appetite stimulants</u>: e.g., <u>megestrol acetate</u> or <u>dronabinol</u>





### <u>Protozoal</u> infections




See "<u>Cystoisosporiasis</u>" and "<u>Cryptosporidiosis</u>" in "<u>Infectious gastroenteritis</u>."




### AIDS cholangiopathy [[68]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8qXOZz)




* Etiology: <u>opportunistic infections</u> of the <u>biliary tract</u> (e.g., <u>Cryptosporidium</u>, <u>CMV</u>, <u>microsporidia</u>)

* <u>Epidemiology</u>: associated with advanced <u>immunosuppression</u>, often at a <u>CD4 count</u> < 100

* Clinical presentation: <u>right upper quadrant</u> <u>pain</u> with <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, and <u>diarrhea</u>

* Diagnostics 

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> (may be normal in ∼ 25% of patients) 

* Marked elevation of <u>alkaline phosphatase</u> and <u>GGT</u>

* Mild-moderate elevation of <u>AST</u> and <u>ALT</u>

* Normal or elevated <u>bilirubin</u>

* Imaging: most commonly <u>ultrasound</u>, but <u>MRCP</u> or <u>ERCP</u> may also be used

* Dilated <u>common bile duct</u> (<u>CBD</u>)

* Beaded appearance of the <u>biliary tract</u> may be seen.

* Intra- and extrahepatic ductal dilatation

* <u>Cholangiography</u>: stricture of the <u>distal</u> <u>CBD</u>

* <u>Liver biopsy</u>: consider if there is diagnostic uncertainty; findings resemble those of <u>sclerosing</u> <u>cholangitis</u>.

* Treatment

* <u>Analgesics</u>

* <u>Management of acute cholangitis</u>, if present, and <u>opportunistic infections</u>

* Initiate <u>ART</u> (if not already started).

* Consider <u>ursodeoxycholic acid</u> in patients with marked <u>cholestasis</u>.

* Consider endoscopic sphincterotomy for symptomatic relief.





### Miscellaneous [[69]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/N5X-4A)




* <u>Malignancy</u>: including <u>anal cancer</u> due to <u>HPV</u>, <u>lymphoma</u>, and <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>

* <u>Liver</u> disease: Multiple etiologies can cause <u>liver</u> disease in <u>HIV</u>. [[70]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hbccta0)

* <u>Hepatitis B</u> or C <u>coinfection</u>

* <u>CMV hepatitis</u>

* <u>Hepatotoxic</u> effects of <u>ART</u>

* <u>Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease</u>

* <u>Acute pancreatitis</u>: most commonly due to medication use, although infections and <u>hypertriglyceridemia</u> may also be responsible [[71]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3bcSta0)

## Oncological complications

### Overview [[82]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7rX4iz)




* <u>Malignancy</u> is more common at low <u>CD4 counts</u>

* Etiology is typically multifactorial.

* <u>Immunosuppression</u>

* Oncogenic <u>viruses</u>, e.g.:

* <u>HHV8</u>: <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>, <u>primary effusion lymphoma</u>, multicentric <u>Castleman disease</u>

* <u>EBV</u>: <u>primary CNS lymphoma</u>, <u>Burkitt lymphoma</u>, immunoblastic <u>lymphoma</u>

* <u>HPV</u>: <u>invasive cervical cancer</u>, oral and <u>pharyngeal cancers</u>, penile, vulval, and <u>anal cancer</u>

* <u>Hepatitis B</u> and <u>hepatitis C</u>: <u>hepatocellular carcinoma</u>

* <u>Chronic inflammation</u> secondary to <u>HIV infection</u> itself

* Increased <u>prevalence</u> of <u>carcinogenic</u> <u>risk factors</u>, e.g., smoking

* Management [[82]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7rX4iz)[[83]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HrXKiz)

* Similar to the management in <u>HIV</u>-negative patients (see dedicated articles for details).

* In addition, <u>ART</u> should be initiated or continued.





> [!TIP]
> Patients with <u>HIV</u> also develop incidental cancers (e.g., bowel, <u>breast</u>); encourage patients to attend all age-appropriate screening!




> [!TIP]
> Early diagnosis and <u>treatment of HIV</u> with <u>ART</u> is the most important step in preventing <u>HIV</u>-associated malignancies.




#### AIDS-defining malignancies [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kNXmYA)[[82]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7rX4iz)




Include cancers that are associated with profound <u>immunosuppression</u> and are categorized as <u>AIDS-defining illnesses</u>




* <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>

* <u>Invasive cervical cancer</u>

* <u>Non-Hodgkin lymphoma</u>  [[84]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/srXtiz)

* <u>Primary CNS lymphoma</u>

* <u>Burkitt lymphoma</u>

* Immunoblastic <u>lymphoma</u>

* Other aggressive <u>B-cell lymphomas</u> (e.g., <u>diffuse large B-cell lymphoma</u>, plasmablastic <u>lymphoma</u>, <u>primary effusion lymphoma</u>, multicentric <u>Castleman disease</u>)





#### Non-AIDS-defining malignancies [[82]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7rX4iz)




Include cancers that are found at higher rates among individuals with <u>HIV</u> but which are not classified as <u>AIDS-defining conditions</u>




* <u>Hodgkin lymphoma</u>  [[84]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/srXtiz)

* <u>Lung cancer</u>

* <u>Hepatocellular carcinoma</u>

* <u>Head and neck carcinoma</u>

* <u>Vulvar cancer</u>

* <u>Penile cancer</u>

* <u>Anal cancer</u>

### Multicentric <u>Castleman disease</u> [[85]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WYcPLa0)[[86]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3SdSz60)[[87]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I3dYPp0)




* Clinical features: systemic symptoms (e.g., night sweats, <u>fevers</u>), <u>hepatosplenomegaly</u>, <u>generalized lymphadenopathy</u>, systemic inflammatory response with <u>multi-organ failure</u> [[87]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I3dYPp0)

* Treatment (in consultation with a specialist) consists of <u>ART</u> and may include the following options:  [[87]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I3dYPp0)

* <u>Rituximab</u>-based regimen (can worsen coexisting <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>)

* <u>Antiviral therapy</u> (IV <u>ganciclovir</u> or oral <u>valganciclovir</u> with or without <u>zidovudine</u>)

* Adjunctive <u>corticosteroids</u>

* <u>Monoclonal antibodies</u>





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Rituximab</u> can exacerbate <u>Kaposi sarcoma</u>.




### Primary effusion lymphoma (<u>PEL</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)[[88]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-acDMa0)




<u>PEL</u> is a rare subtype of <u>non-Hodgkin</u> <u>B-cell lymphoma</u> most commonly seen in <u>HIV</u>-positive patients <u>coinfected</u> with <u>HHV-8</u>. Patients are also frequently <u>coinfected</u> with <u>EBV</u>.




* Clinical features: large effusions affecting the <u>peritoneal</u>, pleural, or <u>pericardial spaces</u>

* Treatment: <u>ART</u> in combination with <u>chemotherapy</u> (in consultation with a specialist)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)[[89]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZYcZna0)

* Prognosis: generally poor

## Prevention of opportunistic infections

### <u>Primary prevention</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




To avoid the risk of <u>opportunistic infections</u>, which increases with lower <u>CD4 counts</u>, the following primary preventive measures should be taken for all patients with <u>HIV</u>, preferably in consultation with infectious disease specialists




* Initiate <u>ART</u>.

* Screen all patients for <u>latent TB</u> (see “<u>Pulmonary complications in HIV</u>”)

* Ensure <u>vaccinations</u> are up-to-date (see “<u>Vaccinations for HIV-infected individuals</u>” for details).

* Discontinue primary prophylaxis in individuals who respond well to <u>ART</u> with an increase in <u>CD4 count</u>; Restart primary prophylaxis if <u>CD4 counts</u> fall below the threshold for that specific infection.

* Primary prophylaxis is not recommended for the following due to low <u>incidence</u> of infection, high risk of drug-related adverse effects, and high risk of developing drug resistance:

* <u>Bartonella</u> spp.

* <u>Candida</u> spp.

* <u>Cryptosporidium</u>

* <u>Cytomegalovirus</u>





> [!WARNING]
> The use of live attenuated <u>influenza vaccines</u> is contraindicated in patients living with <u>HIV</u>.

### <u>Secondary prevention</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SSdy_60)




* After completion of treatment for certain <u>opportunistic infections</u>, secondary prophylaxis of opportunistic infections (chronic <u>maintenance therapy</u>) should be initiated immediately and maintained until immune reconstitution occurs on <u>ART</u> to prevent a recurrence.

* Secondary prophylaxis should be discontinued once <u>CD4 counts</u> remain above the respective thresholds for > 3–6 consecutive months.

* Consult infectious disease specialists to determine when to initiate and discontinue <u>secondary prophylaxis of opportunistic infections</u>.
