# Restless legs syndrome

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Sleep-wake disorders > Restless legs syndrome*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z30rOf)

---

## Summary

Restless legs syndrome (RLS; also referred to as Willis-Ekbom disease) is a common <u>sleep</u>-related movement disorder characterized by a strong urge to move the legs that is triggered and/or worsened with rest and is usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations (e.g., <u>pain</u>, pins and needles, itching, tickling, crawling sensation). The urge to move the legs increases in the evenings and at night and is typically relieved by movement. Primary RLS is <u>idiopathic</u> but often associated with a positive <u>family history</u>. <u>Secondary RLS</u> is less common and results from a variety of underlying conditions (e.g., <u>iron deficiency</u>, <u>uremia</u>, <u>Parkinson disease</u>) and drugs (e.g., <u>H1 antihistamines</u>, <u>antidepressants</u>). Diagnostic studies (e.g., <u>iron studies</u>, <u>polysomnography</u>) can help exclude causes of <u>secondary RLS</u> and alternative diagnoses. Treatment depends on the duration and severity of symptoms and includes medications such as <u>alpha-2-delta calcium channel ligands</u> and treating underlying conditions (e.g., with <u>iron</u> supplementation).

## Epidemiology

* RLS affects up to 15% of the general US population. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tja6-4)

* Sex: <u>♀</u> > <u>♂</u>

* Peak <u>incidence</u>: 30–40 years of age (often misdiagnosed as <u>growing pains</u> in childhood) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1jX2zB)




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

## Etiology

* Primary RLS:  (common): <u>idiopathic</u>, but is familial in up to 77% of cases  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/djXozB)

* Secondary RLS: caused by an underlying condition or drug

* Chronic conditions include: 

* <u>Iron deficiency</u> with or without <u>anemia</u>, <u>vitamin deficiency</u>

* <u>Kidney failure</u> (<u>uremia</u>)

* <u>Peripheral neuropathy</u> (e.g., in <u>diabetes mellitus</u>)

* Inflammatory conditions: <u>celiac disease</u>, <u>rheumatoid arthritis</u>, <u>inflammatory bowel disease</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/a_aQ5M)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y_an5M)

* Psychiatric: <u>depression</u>, <u>anxiety disorders</u>

* Neurological: <u>Parkinson disease</u>, <u>multiple sclerosis</u>, <u>polyneuropathies</u>, spinal cord diseases

* Associated drugs include: ;  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VjXGzB)

* <u>H1 antihistamines</u>

* <u>Antidepressants</u> (e.g., <u>TCAs</u>, <u>SSRIs</u>, <u>SNRIs</u>)

* <u>Dopamine antagonists</u> (<u>neuroleptics</u>, <u>metoclopramide</u>)

* <u>Lithium</u>

* <u>Beta blockers</u>

* <u>Pregnancy</u>  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WjXPzB)

## Pathophysiology

* The pathophysiology of RLS remains unclear.

* Studies suggest that abnormal <u>dopamine</u> pathways in the <u>brain</u> and impaired <u>iron</u> <u>homeostasis</u> (leading to <u>iron deficiency</u> in the <u>substantia nigra</u>) are the most prominent pathophysiological mechanisms involved. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/b_aH5M)

## Clinical features

* Main features [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sMWtJN0)

* A recurrent urge to move the legs that is:

* Typically relieved by movement

* Triggered and/or worsened with rest

* Worse in the evening and at night (may occur exclusively at night)

* The urge to move the legs is often accompanied by uncomfortable sensations (e.g., <u>pain</u>, pins and needles, itching, tickling, or crawling sensations).

* Other features

* <u>Periodic limb movements of sleep</u> (<u>PLMS</u>) or periodic limb movements of wakefulness (PLMW) are common.  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sMWtJN0)

* Other <u>sleep disorders</u> may be present, e.g., <u>insomnia</u>.

* Patients with <u>secondary RLS</u> may have clinical features of the underlying disease (e.g., <u>signs of Parkinson disease</u> or <u>kidney failure</u>).

## Diagnosis

### General principles [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)




* RLS is mainly a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>.

* For all patients:

* Perform a complete <u>neurological examination</u>.

* Obtain <u>iron studies</u>.

* Obtain further diagnostic studies to:

* Assess for underlying conditions (i.e., causes of <u>secondary RLS</u>)

* Rule out alternative diagnoses (e.g., <u>akathisia</u>, <u>polyneuropathy</u>)

* Diagnostic criteria can help confirm the diagnosis.

### Diagnostic studies [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)




#### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




* <u>Iron studies</u> to assess for <u>iron deficiency</u>, i.e. either:  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)

* <u>Ferritin</u> < 75 ng/mL and <u>transferrin saturation</u> < 45% [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* <u>Transferrin saturation</u> < 20% in patients with acute or <u>chronic inflammation</u>  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* <u>CBC</u> to assess for <u>anemia</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)

* Consider further studies based on clinical suspicion, e.g.:

* <u>Kidney function tests</u> to assess for <u>uremia</u>

* <u>HbA1c</u>, <u>folate</u>, and <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> levels if there are <u>signs of peripheral neuropathy</u>





#### Advanced testing




* <u>Polysomnography</u>

* To assess for <u>PLMS</u> and PLMW if diagnosis is uncertain

* To rule out other <u>sleep disorders</u>, e.g., <u>obstructive sleep apnea</u>

* <u>Electromyography</u> and <u>nerve conduction studies</u>: to rule out alternative diagnoses, e.g., <u>polyneuropathy</u> or <u>radiculopathy</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)





> [!TIP]
> 30% of patients with <u>iron deficiency</u> have symptoms of RLS. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)

## Treatment

Treatment is mainly symptomatic. Pharmacotherapy is based on the severity and frequency of symptoms and the presence of comorbidities.




### General principles [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)




* Treat underlying and comorbid conditions.

* <u>Iron</u> supplementation for patients with <u>iron</u>-deficiency

* <u>Cognitive behavioral therapy</u>, e.g., for concomitant <u>depression</u>

* Management of coexisting <u>sleep disorders</u>

* Consider discontinuing or adjusting medications that may cause or aggravate RLS symptoms.

* Encourage lifestyle modifications.

* Consider a trial period of avoiding exacerbating factors, e.g., coffee and <u>alcohol</u>.

* <u>Sleep hygiene counseling</u>

* <u>Regular physical activity</u>

* Activities to distract from symptoms, e.g., video games, crosswords

* Consider stimulation techniques, e.g., vibrating pad, <u>acupuncture</u>, magnetic or electrical stimulation.

* Consider pharmacotherapy in patients with inadequate response to <u>iron</u> supplementation and <u>supportive care</u>.





#### <u>Iron</u> supplementation [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)




* Provide oral <u>iron</u> supplementation;  e.g., ferrous sulfate DOSAGE with <u>vitamin C</u> DOSAGE for patients with serum <u>ferritin</u> â¤ 75 ng/mL and <u>transferrin saturation</u> < 45%  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* IV <u>iron</u> supplementation, e.g., ferric carboxymaltose DOSAGE, may be indicated for patients with: [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PtWWdM0)

* Moderate or severe chronic persistent RLS and either:

* Serum <u>ferritin</u> levels 76–100 ng/mL  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* Need for rapid increase in <u>iron stores</u>

* Intolerance or inadequate absorption of <u>oral iron</u>

* Inadequate improvement after 3 months of <u>oral iron</u> supplementation

* Monitor serum <u>ferritin</u> levels every 3–6 months.

* Safe <u>ULN</u> of serum <u>ferritin</u>: 300 ng/mL

* Consider stopping <u>iron</u> supplementation if <u>ferritin</u> levels > 100 ng/mL in the absence of ongoing reasons for <u>iron deficiency</u>.





### Pharmacotherapy [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)




#### Intermittent RLS




* Consider intermittent pharmacotherapy if <u>supportive care</u> is unsuccessful.

* Options include:

* <u>Carbidopa/levodopa</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* <u>Benzodiazepines</u> (e.g., <u>clonazepam</u>) or <u>benzodiazepine</u> <u>receptor</u> <u>antagonists</u> (e.g., <u>temazepam</u>)

* Low-<u>potency</u> <u>opioids</u>, e.g., <u>codeine</u>, <u>tramadol</u>





#### Chronic persistent RLS  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)




* <u>Alpha-2-delta ligands</u> (e.g., <u>gabapentin</u> enacarbil DOSAGE, <u>pregabalin</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE, or <u>gabapentin</u>): first-line for most patients, unless contraindicated  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PtWWdM0)

* Low-dose <u>nonergot dopamine agonists</u> (e.g., <u>pramipexole</u>:  DOSAGE, <u>ropinirole</u> DOSAGE, <u>rotigotine</u> DOSAGE)   [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PtWWdM0)

* Second-line for most patients

* First-line for patients with any of the following:

* Very severe RLS or <u>PLMS</u>

* Associated moderate or severe <u>depression</u>, <u>obesity</u>, conditions that have caused <u>respiratory failure</u>, and history of <u>substance use disorder</u>

* Compromised <u>kidney function</u>





#### Refractory RLS  (or RLS associated with a severe <u>pain disorder</u>)




* Consider either:

* Combination therapy with <u>alpha-2-delta ligands</u> and <u>dopamine agonists</u>

* Low-dose <u>opioids</u>, e.g., <u>codeine</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)

* Reassess <u>iron studies</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> For pregnant patients, nonpharmacological interventions are the mainstay of treatment because most pharmacological agents are contraindicated. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AzcRvU0)




> [!WARNING]
> Close follow-up with <u>risk mitigation for opioid prescribing</u> is required for patients taking <u>opioids</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zMWrrN0)
