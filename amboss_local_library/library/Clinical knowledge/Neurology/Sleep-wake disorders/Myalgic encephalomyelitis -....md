# Myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Sleep-wake disorders > Myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YG0nBh)

---

## Summary

Myalgic <u>encephalomyelitis</u>/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a multisystem condition characterized by severe fatigue that is not relieved by rest, <u>postexertional malaise</u>, and unrefreshing <u>sleep</u>. The cause is not completely understood, but possible factors include genetic predisposition, history of infections, and immune abnormalities. The diagnosis is based on clinical features and confirmed using diagnostic criteria. Other <u>causes of fatigue and malaise</u> should be excluded based on clinical evaluation and/or diagnostic testing. There is currently no curative treatment. Management is focused on alleviating symptoms (e.g., improving quality of <u>sleep</u>), educating patients on energy management, and providing <u>supportive care</u>. In most patients, symptoms improve with management, but complete resolution is rare.

---

## Clinical features

* The clinical presentation of ME/CFS is variable; the most common features include: [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)

* New-onset, unexplained severe fatigue that is not relieved by rest

* <u>Myalgia</u> and/or <u>arthralgia</u>

* <u>Headaches</u>

* <u>Flu-like symptoms</u>

* Postexertional malaise (highly specific feature): Symptoms are triggered or exacerbated 12–48 hours after a period of physical activity or <u>stress</u>.

* <u>Sleep</u> disturbances (e.g., unrefreshing <u>sleep</u>, <u>hypersomnolence</u>, and/or <u>insomnia</u>)

* Cognitive impairment (e.g., <u>short-term memory</u> impairment, decreased attention span, <u>brain fog</u>)

* Symptoms of orthostatic intolerance (e.g., <u>dizziness</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>)

* Sensory hypersensitivity

* Symptoms of comorbid conditions (e.g., <u>anxiety</u>, <u>depressed mood</u>, diffuse musculoskeletal <u>pain</u>)

* <u>Physical examination</u> is typically normal.  [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)





> [!TIP]
> Clinical features of ME/CFS are typically exacerbated by physical activity or <u>stress</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/78d4L70)




* Perform a detailed <u>clinical evaluation of fatigue</u>.

* Consider other <u>causes of fatigue and malaise</u>.

* There are no specific diagnostic studies to confirm ME/CFS.

* Obtain <u>diagnostic studies for malaise and fatigue</u> to rule out alternative causes based on clinical suspicion.

* Initial studies include <u>CBC</u>, <u>BMP</u>, <u>TFTs</u>, and <u>LFTs</u>.

* Advanced studies may be considered by specialists.

* Confirm the diagnosis using the <u>diagnostic criteria for ME/CFS</u>.

* Reassess the diagnosis if symptoms are not moderate to severe at least 50% of the time.





> [!TIP]
> A thorough clinical evaluation is crucial, as other potentially treatable <u>causes of fatigue and malaise</u> may manifest similarly to ME/CFS. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/78d4L70)




### 2015 Institute of Medicine diagnostic criteria for CFS [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2g1TuT0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/P9dWnH0)




* All of the following:

* Profound fatigue

* New onset (not lifelong)

* Not alleviated by rest

* Not due to excessive exertion

* Substantially impairs academic, professional, leisure, and/or social function for > 6 months

* <u>Postexertional malaise</u>

* Unrefreshing <u>sleep</u>

* AND ≥ 1 of the following:

* Cognitive impairment

* Orthostatic intolerance (e.g., <u>postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome</u> or <u>postural hypotension</u>)





> [!TIP]
> Approx. 50% of patients with <u>postacute COVID-19 syndrome</u> meet the <u>diagnostic criteria for ME/CFS</u>. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)

## Management

### General principles [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)




* There is currently no curative treatment.

* Therapy focuses on symptom management and <u>supportive care</u> to maximize quality of life.

* Educate patients on self-management strategies.

* Identify and treat comorbid conditions (e.g., <u>fibromyalgia</u>, <u>mood disorders</u>, <u>irritable bowel syndrome</u>).

* Consider referral to a specialist ME/CFS clinic for patients with severe or complex features. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)





> [!WARNING]
> Approach to exercise should be carefully planned based on the patient's symptoms and energy limits to avoid <u>postexertional malaise</u> and sustained functional deterioration. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/78d4L70)




### Patient education [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/78d4L70)




* Self-management strategies (e.g., activity pacing)

* Goal: to balance periods of activity and rest to avoid <u>postexertional malaise</u> and reduce the risk of deterioration

* Patient instructions

* Determine an individual activity baseline (i.e., physical, cognitive, emotional, and social) that minimizes symptoms.

* Avoid exceeding the activity baseline.

* Schedule rest periods throughout the day (e.g., before challenging activities).

* Consider using activity diaries and/or tracking devices (e.g., mobile <u>heart</u>-rate monitor) to monitor activity and symptoms.

* Response to symptom deterioration: Instruct patients to decrease <u>energy expenditure</u> until symptoms return to baseline.

* Lifestyle changes, e.g.:

* <u>Sleep hygiene</u>

* Healthy balanced diet

* Adequate fluid intake





> [!TIP]
> Symptom deterioration is often triggered by infections and/or exertion and can occur despite good energy management.




### Symptom management and <u>supportive care</u> [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)




Provide individualized care and consider specialist referral for patients with complex or severe features.




* Fatigue: See “<u>Management of malaise and fatigue</u>.”

* <u>Sleep disorders</u>: Consider <u>pharmacotherapy for insomnia</u> (e.g., <u>melatonin</u>, <u>doxepin</u>, <u>suvorexant</u>).

* Sensory hypersensitivity: Recommend exposure reduction (e.g., noise-canceling headphones, tinted glasses).

* Cognitive impairment

* Perform a <u>cognitive assessment</u> and consider referral to <u>neurology</u>.

* Refer for <u>occupational therapy</u>.

* Orthostatic intolerance

* Refer for cardiovascular evaluation (e.g., <u>tilt table test</u>).

* See “<u>Nonpharmacological management of noncardiac syncope</u>” and “<u>Medications for noncardiac syncope</u>” for more information.

* <u>Pain management</u>

* <u>Arthralgia</u> or <u>myalgia</u>: Consider <u>oral analgesics</u> (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>, <u>NSAIDs</u>), <u>SSRIs</u>, or <u>pregabalin</u>.

* <u>Neuropathic pain</u>: Consider <u>adjuvant analgesics</u> (e.g., <u>pregabalin</u>, <u>tricyclic antidepressants</u>).

* <u>Dizziness</u> or <u>vertigo</u>

* Perform a <u>clinical evaluation for vertigo</u> and consider <u>vestibular rehabilitation therapy</u>.

* See also “<u>Persistent postural-perceptual dizziness</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> Intolerance to pharmacological treatment is common in patients with ME/CFS. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r8dfL70)
