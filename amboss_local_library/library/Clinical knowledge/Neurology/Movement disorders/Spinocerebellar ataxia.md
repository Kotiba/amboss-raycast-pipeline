## Summary

<u>Spinocerebellar</u> <u>ataxia</u> (<u>SCA</u>) refers to a group of progressive <u>neurodegenerative diseases</u> of genetic origin. Currently, more than 30 types have been identified, most of which are <u>autosomal dominant</u>, such as SCA3. <u>Trinucleotide repeat expansions</u> in a disease-associated protein (e.g., CAG repeats in ataxin-1) are commonly the underlying genetic anomaly. Patients usually present between the ages of 30–50 with slowly progressive symptoms of <u>cerebellar ataxia</u>, including incoordination of gait, hand, speech, and <u>eye movements</u>. The diagnosis is reached on the basis of <u>family history</u> and <u>neurological examination</u>, but <u>neuroimaging</u> and molecular <u>genetic testing</u> help identify the specific cause and type of <u>SCA</u>. To date, there is no effective treatment. Therapy is directed towards alleviating symptoms. The prognosis depends on individual genetic properties, but most patients progressively develop severe, irreversible disability, while retaining full mental capacity, within 15 years of symptom onset.

## Clinical features

* Features common to all types of <u>SCA</u>:

* <u>Cerebellar ataxia</u>

* Progressive <u>gait ataxia</u> (usually broad-based)

* Progressive <u>limb ataxia</u>;  including <u>tremor</u>

* <u>Nystagmus</u>

* Dyssynergia , <u>dysmetria</u>, <u>dysdiadochokinesia</u>, and/or <u>dysarthria</u> may be present.

## Diagnosis

The diagnosis is based on <u>family history</u> and <u>neurological examination</u>. Further tests help identify the specific cause and type of <u>SCA</u>.




* <u>Neuroimaging</u> (CT/<u>MRI</u>): <u>cerebellar</u> <u>atrophy</u> is a finding common to all SCAs

* Molecular <u>genetic testing</u>: precise identification of <u>SCA</u> type
