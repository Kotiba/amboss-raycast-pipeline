# Alcohol intoxication

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/380Sm3)

## Summary

<u>Alcohol</u> intoxication is a temporary condition in which excessive consumption of <u>alcohol</u> alters consciousness, <u>cognition</u>, perception, judgment, affect, and/or behavior. Diagnosis is usually clinical, but diagnostic studies are indicated in clinical uncertainty or to identify alternative diagnoses and/or complications. Management is supportive: Most patients only require observation, but severely intoxicated patients may require <u>management of agitation</u> or <u>respiratory support</u>. Patients with a history of regular <u>heavy alcohol use</u> may require treatment for <u>alcohol-related complications</u> (e.g., <u>Wernicke encephalopathy</u>). Patients may be discharged when they are hemodynamically stable, motor and cognitive function has improved, and there is minimal risk of harm to themselves and others. Prior to discharge, patients should undergo <u>screening for unhealthy alcohol use</u> and appropriate follow-up provided if screening is positive.




For <u>poisoning</u> of unknown cause, see “<u>Approach to the poisoned patient</u>.”

## Pathophysiology

* Mechanism: <u>GABA-A receptor</u> <u>agonist</u>

* Absorption: The majority of <u>alcohol</u> consumed is absorbed by the <u>proximal</u> <u>small intestine</u>; only a small amount of <u>alcohol</u> is absorbed by the oral, esophageal, and/or gastric <u>mucosa</u>. ## Clinical features

* The manifestation, peak severity, and duration of symptoms depend on individual factors, e.g. : [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K9cUoe0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LLWwyN0)

* Genetics (e.g., <u>ALDH2 deficiency</u>) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* <u>Alcohol</u> absorption (e.g., <u>alcohol</u> concentration, rate of ingestion)

* <u>Alcohol metabolism</u> (e.g., history of <u>liver</u> disease or <u>gastric bypass</u>, <u>heavy drinking</u>) [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S6Wy4m0)

* Concurrent use of <u>recreational drugs</u>

* In individuals who do not have a history of long-term or <u>heavy alcohol use</u>, clinical features generally correlate with <u>blood alcohol concentration</u> (<u>BAC</u>). [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K9cUoe0)

## Management

Most patients can be managed with simple observation in the emergency department. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### Approach [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Perform an ABCDE survey to assess hemodynamic and respiratory stability. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OMWIKN0)

* Check <u>vital signs</u> and <u>POC glucose</u>; <u>treat hypoglycemia</u> if present.  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OMWIKN0)

* Assess for concomitant conditions.

* Look for signs of trauma and maintain a low threshold for imaging, as intoxication can reduce clinical features of trauma. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* Administer <u>naloxone for opioid overdose</u> if co-ingestion is suspected.

* Provide <u>supportive care for alcohol intoxication</u> when appropriate, e.g.: [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l01vT20)

* <u>Respiratory support</u>

* <u>Management of dehydration and hypovolemia</u>

* Prophylactic dose of <u>thiamine</u> supplementation (for <u>Wernicke encephalopathy</u>)

* <u>Electrolyte repletion</u>

* <u>Manage agitation</u> as needed to facilitate care. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OMWIKN0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l01vT20)

* Start with <u>de-escalation techniques</u>. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K9cUoe0)

* For refractory <u>agitation</u>, use <u>calming medications</u> (e.g., <u>benzodiazepines</u> or <u>haloperidol</u>).

* Monitor patients for associated respiratory <u>depression</u>;  and initiate <u>airway management for agitated patients</u> if required.

* Regularly review patients; obtain <u>diagnostic studies for alcohol intoxication</u> if:

* There is diagnostic uncertainty

* The patient does not respond to treatment of intoxication

* Assess patients for indications for hospital admission and admit if present; consult specialists when appropriate.

* Ensure all patients have undergone <u>screening for unhealthy alcohol use</u> prior to discharge.





> [!WARNING]
> Do not assume <u>agitation</u> is caused by <u>alcohol</u> alone until other <u>causes of agitation</u>, such as injury or illness, have been excluded. [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NEc-DV0)




> [!TIP]
> Methods for <u>gastrointestinal decontamination</u> and <u>enhanced elimination</u> are generally discouraged because of the rapid absorption of <u>alcohol</u>, risk of complications (e.g., <u>upper airway</u> trauma with induction of emesis), and poor <u>efficacy</u>. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l01vT20)
