# Sedative-hypnotic drug overdose

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Toxic and metabolic disorders > Sedative-hypnotic drug overdose*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Q80um3)

---

## Summary

<u>Sedative-hypnotic drug</u> overdose and/or <u>poisoning</u> can be intentional or unintentional. Affected individuals usually present with a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>, but the severity and features vary based on the specific agent, the amount taken, and the presence of any co-intoxicants. Diagnosis is primarily clinical because <u>drug testing</u> for <u>sedative-hypnotics</u> has limited utility in management. Diagnostic studies to exclude alternative diagnoses and/or confirm the presence of co-intoxicants are usually obtained. Treatment is primarily supportive with an emphasis on <u>airway management</u> and <u>respiratory support</u> while the drug is metabolized. <u>Flumazenil</u> can be used as an <u>antidote</u> for isolated <u>benzodiazepine poisoning</u> in <u>benzodiazepine</u>-naive patients but is often avoided because of the risk of adverse effects. Symptomatic patients generally require admission for further monitoring and treatment.




See “<u>Sedative-hypnotic drugs</u>” for more information on specific agents, their clinical uses, mechanisms of action, and adverse effects.

---

## Overview

The <u>sedative-hypnotic</u> class of drugs comprises several agents that have similar effects at therapeutic levels, but the severity of overdose symptoms varies widely depending on the specific medication.




* <u>Benzodiazepine overdose</u>

* Typically manifests with <u>lethargy</u>, <u>somnolence</u>, and slurred speech

* Treatment includes <u>supportive care</u> and, in some cases, <u>flumazenil</u>.

* <u>Barbiturate overdose</u>

* Typically manifests with profound <u>CNS depression</u> and respiratory <u>depression</u>

* Treatment often includes <u>endotracheal intubation</u> and <u>mechanical ventilation</u>.

* <u>Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotic overdose</u>

* Typically manifests with <u>oversedation</u>

* Treatment is <u>supportive care</u>.

* <u>Gamma-hydroxybutyrate overdose</u> (<u>GHB overdose</u>)

* Manifestations vary from euphoria to profound <u>CNS depression</u>, <u>seizures</u>, and respiratory <u>depression</u>.

* Patients may rapidly cycle between different clinical presentations.

* Treatment is supportive and may involve intermittent <u>respiratory support</u> with <u>bag-mask ventilation</u>.

* <u>Xylazine intoxication</u>

* Most often seen alongside other <u>recreational drug</u> intoxication (e.g., <u>fentanyl</u>).

* Typically manifests with a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u> similar to <u>opioid overdose</u> but unresponsive to <u>naloxone</u>.

* Treatment is supportive and often involves <u>airway management</u> and <u>rescue breathing</u>.





> [!TIP]
> All <u>sedative-hypnotic drug</u> overdoses manifest with some features of <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>, but the severity of symptoms varies with the specific medication.

## Clinical features

### Sedative-hypnotic toxidrome [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/26bT4u)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)




The severity of symptoms varies with the specific drug and quantity taken.




* <u>CNS depression</u>

* Reduced <u>level of consciousness</u>: drowsiness, <u>stupor</u>, or <u>coma</u>

* <u>Ataxia</u>

* <u>Nystagmus</u>

* Slurred speech

* <u>Hypotonia</u> and/or <u>hyporeflexia</u>

* <u>Respiratory failure</u>

* <u>Upper airway</u> obstruction: snoring, obstructive <u>apnea</u>  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Hl1KyS0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jqW_ym0)

* Decreased <u>respiratory drive</u>: decreased <u>respiratory rate</u> and/or <u>tidal volume</u>  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MqWM_m0)

* <u>Hypotension</u>

* <u>Hypothermia</u>





> [!WARNING]
> The manifestations of <u>sedative-hypnotic</u> overdose are nonspecific. Always consider alternative <u>critical causes of altered mental status</u> as well as <u>causes of respiratory failure</u>.

## Diagnosis

### <u>Clinical diagnosis</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




Establish the diagnosis based on:




* Presence of a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>

* Suspected exposure to a <u>sedative-hypnotic drug</u>

* <u>Toxicological history and physical examination</u>





> [!WARNING]
> Do not delay initial <u>management of sedative-hypnotic drug overdose</u> for diagnostic testing or confirmation.




### Supportive diagnostic studies [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




The following are used to assess for complications and rule out alternative diagnoses.




* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* <u>CMP</u>, <u>TFTs</u>: to evaluate for alternative or associated <u>causes of AMS</u>

* <u>VBG</u> or <u>ABG</u>: to evaluate the severity of <u>respiratory failure</u>

* <u>Acetaminophen level</u>: to rule out an overdose of this commonly co-ingested medication

* <u>Drug tests</u> for <u>sedative-hypnotic drugs</u> have limited utility in clinical management.  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* <u>ECG</u>: : to look for signs of cardiotoxicity from commonly co-ingested medications

* Imaging studies: if clinically indicated

* <u>CXR</u>: to evaluate for suspected <u>aspiration pneumonia</u> or <u>acute lung injury</u>

* CT head: to evaluate for suspected <u>head injury</u> or for alternative <u>causes of AMS</u>





> [!WARNING]
> Consider testing for co-ingested <u>CNS depressants</u> (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, <u>opioids</u>) if the patient has significant respiratory <u>depression</u>, <u>hypotension</u>, and/or prolonged <u>coma</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zpWrHm0)

## Management

### Initial management [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Follow the <u>ABCDE approach in poisoning</u> and initiate resuscitation (e.g., <u>oxygen therapy</u>, <u>IV fluid resuscitation</u>) as needed.

* Perform a <u>toxicological risk assessment</u> and obtain diagnostic studies.

* Call the local Poison Control Center: In the US, the Poison Help line is 1-800-222-1222.

* Provide <u>supportive care for the poisoned patient</u>;  (e.g., <u>treat hypoglycemia</u>, <u>replete electrolytes</u>).

* Treat concurrent drug intoxications if indicated, e.g., <u>psychiatric drug poisoning</u>.





### <u>Respiratory support</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* <u>Secure the airway</u> in patients with reduced <u>level of consciousness</u> or <u>airway obstruction</u>

* <u>Airway opening maneuvers</u> and/or <u>recovery position</u>: relieve most mild or moderate <u>airway obstruction</u> due to transient <u>CNS depression</u>

* <u>Bag mask ventilation</u> (<u>BMV</u>) provides temporary ventilation if:

* <u>Antidote</u> is indicated, e.g., <u>flumazenil</u> for severe <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u>.

* Respiratory <u>depression</u> is expected to be short-lived, e.g., <u>GHB overdose</u>.

* <u>Endotracheal intubation</u>: may be required for <u>poisoning</u> with long-acting agents with no known <u>antidote</u>, e.g., <u>barbiturate overdose</u>

* Monitor for respiratory <u>depression</u> with <u>pulse oximetry</u>, <u>capnography</u>, and serial examinations.

* Provide <u>mechanical ventilation</u> if the patient develops <u>respiratory failure</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Supportive cardiorespiratory care with frequent reevaluation of respiratory and neurologic status is the cornerstone of <u>sedative-hypnotic</u> overdose management. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zpWrHm0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)




> [!WARNING]
> Monitor respiratory status with <u>capnography</u> because <u>supplemental oxygen</u> can mask worsening <u>hypoventilation</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### <u>Antidotes</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Consider <u>flumazenil</u> only in patients with severe isolated <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u> who are not <u>benzodiazepine</u>-dependent. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)

* Consider <u>naloxone for opioid overdose</u> in patients with concurrent <u>opioid</u> use and respiratory suppression.





### <u>GI decontamination</u> and <u>enhanced elimination</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




Consider based on causative agent and patient factors.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vGXAa-)




* Not recommended for <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* May be considered with expert consultation for <u>barbiturate overdose</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJWR9m0)





### Disposition [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Asymptomatic patients: may be medically cleared if no symptoms develop within 6 hours of ingestion

* Mildly symptomatic patients: Observe until mental status returns to normal and the risk of respiratory <u>depression</u> has resolved (agent-specific).

* Severely symptomatic patients: Admit to <u>ICU</u> for ongoing care.

* All patients: Consult <u>psychiatry</u> if overdose was intentional.

## Benzodiazepine overdose

### General principles [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* <u>Benzodiazepines</u> have a wide <u>therapeutic index</u> and are therefore safer than <u>barbiturates</u>.

* Overdose is rarely life-threatening unless other respiratory or <u>CNS depressants</u> have been coingested (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, <u>opioids</u>, <u>barbiturates</u>, <u>antihistamines</u>).

* Older adults are at particularly high risk because of <u>pharmacokinetic</u> changes and potential <u>drug interactions</u>. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/s1YtRL)

* See “<u>Benzodiazepines</u>” for information on specific agents, their clinical uses, mechanisms of action, and adverse effects.





### Clinical features [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




<u>Benzodiazepine overdose</u> manifests with a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>.




* Mild <u>CNS depression</u> (e.g., <u>lethargy</u>, <u>somnolence</u>, slurred speech, cognitive impairment) and <u>ataxia</u> are most common. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)

* Respiratory <u>depression</u> (uncommon in isolated <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u>)

* <u>Hypotonia</u> and <u>hyporeflexia</u>

* <u>Vital signs</u> are often normal (<u>hypotension</u> is uncommon). [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Benzodiazepine overdose</u> most commonly manifests with mild <u>CNS</u> symptoms, and <u>vital signs</u> are often normal. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)




> [!WARNING]
> Severe symptoms (e.g., <u>coma</u>, respiratory suppression, <u>hypotension</u>) suggest a very large ingestion and/or the co-ingestion of another intoxicant. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### Management [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/RqWlym0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Follow the <u>approach to sedative-hypnotic drug overdose</u>.

* <u>Supportive care</u> is the mainstay of treatment.

* <u>Endotracheal intubation</u> is seldom required for pure <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u>.

* <u>GI decontamination</u> is usually not recommended.

* <u>Flumazenil</u> administration may be considered in select cases.





> [!WARNING]
> Avoid <u>flumazenil</u> in patients on chronic <u>benzodiazepine</u> therapy because of the risk of precipitating <u>withdrawal symptoms</u> and <u>seizures</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### Flumazenil [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)




<u>Flumazenil</u> is a specific <u>antidote</u> to <u>benzodiazepines</u>, but its risks often outweigh its benefits. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)




#### Mechanism of action [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)




* <u>Flumazenil</u> is a <u>competitive antagonist</u> to <u>benzodiazepines</u> at the <u>GABA</u> <u>receptor</u>.

* It rapidly reverses <u>CNS depression</u>.

* It does not reliably reverse respiratory <u>depression</u>.





#### Adverse effects [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bJWHsm0)




* <u>Seizures</u>, tachyarrythmias: especially in patients with proconvulsive or proarrythmic co-ingestions  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)

* Acute <u>benzodiazepine withdrawal</u>: in patients with <u>benzodiazepine dependence</u>

* Other: aggressive behavior, <u>agitation</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, abdominal cramping





#### Indications [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nqW7_m0)




* Severe <u>benzodiazepine</u>-induced <u>CNS depression</u> in <u>benzodiazepine</u>-naive patients who meet all the following criteria:

* Absence of other neurologic findings

* Absence of <u>ECG</u> abnormalities

* Presence of <u>normal vital signs</u>

* Severe <u>benzodiazepine</u>-induced respiratory <u>depression</u> (controversial)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* Reversal of <u>oversedation</u> with a <u>benzodiazepine</u> (mostly in perioperative settings)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Flumazenil</u> is not routinely recommended for reversal of sedative overdose in the emergency department. Expert consultation is advised. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




#### Contraindications [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Chronic <u>benzodiazepine</u> use

* History of <u>seizure disorder</u>

* Suspected co-ingestion of proconvulsive or proarrythmic substances





#### Administration [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Dose: <u>flumazenil</u> (for <u>benzodiazepine overdose</u>) DOSAGE

* Carefully monitor for resedation and recurrent respiratory <u>depression</u>: Readministration may be necessary.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Flumazenil</u> can increase the risk of <u>seizures</u>, <u>supraventricular arrhythmias</u>, and/or <u>benzodiazepine withdrawal</u>. Be prepared to manage these complications. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bJWHsm0)

## Barbiturate overdose

### General principles [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zpWrHm0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* <u>Barbiturates</u> have a <u>narrow therapeutic index</u> making them less safe than other <u>sedative-hypnotics</u>.

* Overdose is often caused by dose escalation due to rapid tolerance.

* See “<u>Barbiturates</u>” for information on specific agents, their clinical uses, mechanisms of action, and adverse effects.





### Clinical features [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LqWw_m0)




<u>Barbiturate overdose</u> manifests with a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>. Manifestations of severe toxicity include:




* <u>Respiratory failure</u>: due to severe <u>hypoventilation</u> or <u>respiratory arrest</u>

* Profound CNS depression: e.g., <u>stupor</u>, <u>coma</u>

* Cardiovascular <u>depression</u>: <u>hypotension</u>, often with normal or increased <u>heart rate</u>

* <u>Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema</u> or <u>acute lung injury</u>





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Barbiturate</u> toxicity can manifest with potentially fatal <u>respiratory failure</u> and/or <u>cardiogenic shock</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




### Management [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Follow the <u>approach to sedative-hypnotic drug overdose</u>.

* Aggressive <u>supportive care</u> is the mainstay of treatment.

* <u>Endotracheal intubation</u> and <u>mechanical ventilation</u> are often required.

* No specific <u>antidotes</u> are available.

* <u>Enhanced elimination</u>: Consider with expert consultation. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJWR9m0)

* <u>Multidose activated charcoal</u>: for severe <u>phenobarbital</u> or <u>primidone</u> toxicity [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJWR9m0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KFXUQ-)

* <u>Urine alkalinization</u> (with <u>sodium bicarbonate</u>): for severe <u>phenobarbital</u> toxicity  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJWR9m0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6FXjQ-)

* Charcoal <u>hemoperfusion</u> or <u>hemodialysis</u>: for life-threatening <u>barbiturate</u> toxicity  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJWR9m0)





> [!WARNING]
> Monitor all patients with <u>pulse oximetry</u> and <u>capnography</u>; rapid shallow respirations frequently mask clinically significant <u>hypoventilation</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

## Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotic (Z-drug) overdose

### General principles [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqWgZ50)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8qWOZ50)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uqWpZ50)




* <u>Z-drugs</u> (e.g., <u>zolpidem</u>, <u>zaleplon</u>, and <u>zopiclone</u>) have a better safety profile than <u>benzodiazepines</u>.

* Serious toxicity is more likely when <u>Z-drugs</u> are co-ingested with other <u>CNS depressants</u>.

* See “<u>Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics</u>” for information on specific agents, clinical use, mechanisms of action, and adverse effects.





### Clinical features [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqWgZ50)




<u>Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotic overdose</u> causes a <u>sedative-hypnotic toxidrome</u>.




* Sedation is the most common symptom.

* <u>Coma</u>, respiratory <u>depression</u>, and cardiovascular instability are rare.





### Management [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqWgZ50)




* Follow the <u>approach to sedative-hypnotic drug overdose</u>.

* <u>Supportive care</u> is the mainstay of treatment.

* <u>Respiratory support</u> is usually unnecessary.

* <u>Gastric decontamination</u> is typically not indicated.

* <u>Flumazenil</u> may be considered with caution for isolated <u>nonbenzodiazepine hypnotic</u> overdose.  [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqWgZ50)





> [!WARNING]
> Nonbenzodiazepine overdose typically manifests with sedation only. Consider co-ingestion in patients with more severe manifestations. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FqWgZ50)
