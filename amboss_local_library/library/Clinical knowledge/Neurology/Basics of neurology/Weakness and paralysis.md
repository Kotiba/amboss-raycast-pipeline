## Summary

<u>Weakness</u> is a common symptom that can occur in a wide variety of conditions. In medical settings, “<u>weakness</u>” is best used to refer to decreased strength specifically attributable to neuromuscular abnormalities, rather than to systemic abnormalities of energy and metabolism. Degree of <u>neurological weakness</u> ranges from <u>paresis</u> (mild to moderate) to <u>paralysis</u> (severe or complete). The approach to patients with <u>weakness</u> requires careful clinical evaluation, including a detailed history and <u>physical examination</u> focusing on <u>neurological examination</u> and <u>muscle strength grading</u>. Specific patterns (e.g., acute vs. chronic, <u>upper motor neuron</u> vs. <u>lower motor neuron</u>) can help narrow down the etiology. Aggravating and alleviating factors, associated symptoms, and exposure history can often provide clues to a specific diagnosis. Diagnostic testing is guided by clinical evaluation and includes routine <u>laboratory studies</u>, targeted testing for specific neuromuscular disorders, <u>neuroimaging</u>, and <u>electrodiagnostic studies</u>. Specialist evaluation, e.g., by a <u>neurologist</u>, is often required. The management of <u>weakness</u> or <u>paralysis</u> varies significantly depending on the underlying cause and acuity of presentation and ranges from immediate <u>respiratory support</u> to <u>supportive care</u>, <u>rehabilitation</u>, and long-term pharmacotherapy.




See “<u>Malaise and fatigue</u>” for a more general approach not limited to neuromuscular causes.
