## Summary

Fibromuscular <u>dysplasia</u> (FMD), a disease that primarily affects young to middle-aged women, is characterized by the <u>proliferation</u> of <u>connective tissue</u> and muscle fibers within the arterial vessel walls. The resulting stenosis impairs <u>perfusion</u> of the affected organ, causing <u>ischemia</u>. The symptoms of fibromuscular <u>dysplasia</u> vary depending on the site and the degree of stenosis of FMD. The renal, <u>internal carotid</u>, and <u>vertebral arteries</u> are predominantly involved. Carotid and <u>vertebral artery</u> involvement may present with <u>transient ischemic attack</u> (<u>TIA</u>) and/or <u>stroke</u>, while patients with renal FMD usually present with <u>secondary hypertension</u> and <u>chronic renal insufficiency</u>. <u>Bruits</u> at the costovertebral angle and the carotid region are characteristic findings of renal and carotid <u>artery</u> involvement respectively. In rare cases, patients may present with <u>mesenteric</u> <u>ischemia</u> and/or <u>peripheral artery disease</u> as a result of <u>splanchnic</u> or peripheral arterial involvement. The “string of beads” sign, a characteristic finding on <u>angiography</u>, distinguishes FMD from other causes of arterial occlusion. All patients with renal FMD should be treated with <u>ACE inhibitors</u> and/or <u>ARBs</u>, while those with carotid <u>artery</u> involvement should be placed on <u>stroke</u> prophylaxis (low-dose <u>aspirin</u> therapy). <u>Balloon angioplasty</u> without <u>stenting</u> is the definitive treatment.

## Pathophysiology

Fibromuscular <u>dysplasia</u> (FMD) is an <u>idiopathic</u>, non-inflammatory, non-atherosclerotic, developmental condition that primarily affects small and medium-sized <u>muscular arteries</u>.




### <u>Histopathology</u>




The most commonly encountered <u>histology</u> is <u>medial</u> <u>fibroplasia</u>.




### Pathophysiology




* FMD results in <u>ischemia</u> by one or more of the following mechanisms:

* Stenosis

* Formation of <u>saccular aneurysms</u>  → <u>aneurysmal</u> rupture

* <u>Arterial dissection</u>   → arterial occlusion

* Formation of <u>intravascular</u> <u>thrombi</u> → embolization

* <u>Renal artery stenosis</u> → ↓ renal <u>perfusion</u> → compensatory activation of the <u>renin</u>–angiotensin–<u>aldosterone</u> system → <u>secondary hypertension</u>





### Disease localization




* <u>Renal artery</u> (renal FMD; ∼ 75–80% of cases) 

* Usually bilateral <u>renal artery stenosis</u>

* Second most common cause of <u>renal artery stenosis</u> after <u>atherosclerosis</u>.

* Accounts for 30–50% of cases of <u>renal artery stenosis</u> among children and 5–10% of cases among adults.

* Carotid and <u>vertebral artery</u> involvement (extracranial <u>cerebrovascular FMD</u>; ∼ 65–75% of cases and often bilateral)

## Clinical features

The symptoms of fibromuscular <u>dysplasia</u> are nonspecific and vary depending on the site of FMD, the degree of stenosis, and the underlying pathology (e.g., <u>arterial dissection</u>).




* Renal FMD

* Clinical features of <u>renal artery stenosis</u>

* <u>Secondary hypertension</u>

* Abdominal <u>bruit</u>

* Symptoms of <u>chronic kidney disease</u>

* Flank or abdominal <u>pain</u>

* Cerebrovascular FMD

* <u>Headache</u>, <u>neck pain</u>, <u>pulsatile tinnitus</u>

* <u>TIA</u>, <u>amaurosis fugax</u>, <u>stroke</u>, <u>Horner's syndrome</u>

* Cervical <u>bruit</u>

## Diagnosis

* Imaging

* Imaging modalities

* Best initial tests for renal FMD: <u>duplex ultrasonography</u> and/or <u>CT angiography</u> (see “Diagnostics” in <u>renal artery stenosis</u>)

* Best initial tests for <u>cerebrovascular FMD</u>: <u>CT angiography</u>

* <u>Gold standard</u>: <u>digital subtraction angiography</u> (<u>DSA</u>)

* Finding

* Common finding: “string of beads” sign

* Less commonly: a single, circumferential/tubular stenotic lesion

* Laboratory tests: serum <u>creatinine</u>

## Differential diagnoses

* <u>Atherosclerosis</u>

* Patients with <u>atherosclerosis</u> are usually older, male, and have <u>risk factors</u> such as <u>obesity</u> and/or <u>cigarette smoking</u>.

* On <u>angiography</u>, <u>atherosclerosis</u> affects the <u>proximal</u> segments and ostia of <u>arteries</u>, while FMD affects middle and <u>distal</u> segments of the <u>artery</u>.

* <u>Vasculitis</u> (e.g., <u>giant cell</u> <u>temporal arteritis</u>; , <u>PAN</u>)




The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.

## Treatment

* <u>Renal artery</u> FMD: see “Therapy” in <u>renal artery stenosis</u>

* <u>Cerebrovascular FMD</u>

* <u>Antiplatelet drugs</u> (e.g., <u>aspirin</u>) for <u>stroke</u> prophylaxis is recommended for all patients.

* Patients who are symptomatic: <u>percutaneous transluminal angioplasty</u>
