# Locked-in syndrome

## Summary

Locked-in syndrome (LIS) is a rare condition caused by bilateral damage to the <u>ventral</u> <u>pons</u>, most often due to a <u>stroke</u>. LIS is characterized by <u>quadriplegia</u> and <u>bulbar palsy</u> or <u>pseudobulbar palsy</u>, caused by the interruption of the corticospinal and <u>corticobulbar tracts</u> in the <u>pons</u>. The only remaining voluntary muscle movements include vertical <u>eye</u> movement and blinking. Consciousness, awareness, <u>cognition</u>, and sensation are preserved. Diagnosis of <u>pontine</u> damage is made on a CT or <u>MRI</u> of the <u>brain</u>. Preserved <u>cognition</u> is diagnosed via <u>EEG</u> and <u>neuropsychological testing</u>. Management in most patients includes <u>tracheostomy</u>, <u>mechanical ventilation</u>, placement of a <u>feeding tube</u>, and <u>physiotherapy</u>. Patients learn to communicate through blinking and/or <u>eye movements</u> and the help of computer programs/speech synthesizers. Some patients may recover a certain degree of motor control, speech, and <u>swallowing</u> ability.

---

## Etiology

* <u>Ventral</u> <u>pontine</u> damage/injury

* Bilateral <u>ventral</u> <u>pontine stroke</u> (most common)

* <u>Ischemic</u> <u>stroke</u> (<u>thrombotic</u>/embolic occlusion of the <u>basilar artery</u>)

* <u>Hemorrhagic stroke</u>

* Direct trauma to the <u>ventral</u> <u>pons</u>

* <u>Pontine demyelination</u> (e.g., <u>multiple sclerosis</u> affecting the <u>ventral</u> <u>pons</u>, <u>central pontine myelinolysis</u>)

* <u>Brain tumor</u> or <u>brain abscess</u> affecting the <u>ventral</u> <u>pons</u>

* <u>Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis</u> (postinfectious or postimmunization)

* Other diseases

* <u>Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis</u>

* Guillain-Barré syndrome

* <u>Myasthenia gravis</u>





> [!NOTE]
> To remember that locked-in syndrome is caused by the damage to the <u>ventral</u> (i.e., basilar) part of the <u>pons</u>, think of someone locked in the basement.

## Clinical features

Locked-in syndrome is typically preceded by a <u>loss of consciousness</u> and subsequent <u>coma</u> lasting for days or weeks. The following symptoms are detected on regaining consciousness:




* <u>Paralysis</u> of voluntary muscles

* <u>Paralysis</u> of all 4 limbs and torso (<u>quadriplegia</u>)

* Caused by interruption of the bilateral <u>corticospinal tracts</u> as they pass through the <u>ventral</u> <u>pons</u>.

* Characterized by <u>spasticity</u>, ↑ deep <u>tendon reflexes</u>, positive bilateral <u>Babinski's sign</u>

* <u>Bulbar palsy</u> or <u>pseudobulbar palsy</u> 

* Anarthria: a severe form of <u>dysarthria</u> characterized by the inability to speak due to impaired neuromuscular control of the muscles used for speech

* <u>Dysphagia</u> (inability to swallow)

* <u>Horizontal gaze palsy</u>: caused by damage to the center for horizontal gaze and the VI<sup>th</sup><u>cranial</u> nerve <u>nucleus</u>, which both lie in the <u>pons</u>

* Respiratory abnormalities

* <u>Cheyne-Stokes</u> breathing, <u>apnea</u>, loss of voluntary control of breathing

* Often requires <u>tracheostomy</u> and <u>mechanical ventilation</u>

* Preservation of the following functions

* Normal consciousness, language comprehension, <u>cognition</u>, and ability to make decisions (the <u>cerebral cortex</u> and <u>reticular activating system</u> are undamaged/preserved)

* Vertical <u>eye movements</u> and voluntary blinking

* Cutaneous sensation





> [!NOTE]
> Patients with LIS can only communicate by blinking and vertical <u>eye movements</u>!




> [!NOTE]
> Patients with locked-in syndrome due to basilar <u>artery</u> occlusion are blocked like a basalt rock!

## Diagnosis

* CT/<u>MRI</u> of the <u>brain</u>: : indicated in all patients to identify the underlying cause

* <u>EEG</u>

* Indicated in all patients to rule out <u>brain death</u>

* Used to measure visual/auditory evoked potentials → nearly normal <u>EEG</u> in LIS

* <u>Lumbar puncture</u>: indicated if an infectious etiology or <u>Guillain-Barré syndrome</u> is suspected

* <u>Neuropsychological testing</u>

* Performed once the patient is stable to assess <u>cognition</u>

* Patients communicate with <u>eye movements</u> or blinking in response to the test questions → normal or near-normal <u>cognition</u>





> [!NOTE]
> Demonstration of preserved <u>cognition</u>, vertical <u>eye movements</u>, and blinking in a <u>quadriplegic</u>, <u>anarthric</u> patient is diagnostic of LIS!
