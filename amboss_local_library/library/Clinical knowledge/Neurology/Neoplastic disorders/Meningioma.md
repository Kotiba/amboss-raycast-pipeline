# Meningioma

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Neoplastic disorders > Meningioma*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/I30YPf)

---

## Summary

Meningiomas are almost always benign, slow-growing <u>brain tumors</u> that arise from arachnoid cap cells of the <u>arachnoid villi</u>. As meningiomas may remain asymptomatic for long periods of time, they are often an incidental finding. <u>Tumor</u> compression can lead to a wide variety of neurological symptoms (i.e., <u>headaches</u>, <u>seizures</u>, <u>paresthesias</u>) that are generally specific to the structure(s) being compromised. Contrast imaging typically shows an enhanced round <u>tumor</u> with well-defined margins that often resembles a snowball. Management depends on the location and <u>grade of the tumor</u>, as well as patient-specific factors such as age, comorbidities, and accompanying symptoms. Treatment generally consists of surgical intervention, <u>radiotherapy</u>, or a combination of both. In some cases (e.g., asymptomatic elderly patients, or those with slow-growing meningiomas), a "<u>watch and wait</u>" approach with regular <u>tumor</u> monitoring may be safer than invasive therapy.

## Etiology

* Mostly <u>idiopathic</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5oXic_)

* Exposure to <u>ionizing radiation</u>: <u>radiotherapy</u> for head and neck tumors, dental <u>x-rays</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aN0Q-T)

* Multiple meningiomas may develop in patients with <u>neurofibromatosis type II</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bN0H-T)

## Diagnosis

### <u>MRI</u> (imaging modality of choice)




* Plain <u>MRI</u> findings [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/toXXd_)

* Round, sharply demarcated space-occupying lesion with radiological features of an extra-axial <u>tumor</u>

* <u>Dural tail sign</u>

* T1: <u>isointense</u> or <u>hypointense</u>

* T2: <u>isointense</u> or <u>hyperintense</u>

* Contrast <u>MRI</u> findings [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/toXXd_)

* Significant homogenous <u>enhancement</u> of the meningioma

* Sunburst or spoke-wheel appearance  [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8oXOd_)

* Spinal meningioma: <u>ginkgo</u>-leaf sign  [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FoXgd_)

* The enhanced but distorted spinal cord takes the shape of the leaf, while the stretched non-enhancing <u>dentate ligament</u> forms the "stem" of the leaf.

* Seen on an axial view of the spinal cord after <u>contrast enhancement</u>

* En plaque meningioma: a rare benign subtype that is characterized by diffuse carpet-like growth along the dural layer

### <u>CT scan</u> [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uoXpd_)




* <u>CT scans</u> performed to investigate unexplained <u>headaches</u> or <u>seizures</u> are usually the first to pick up incidental meningiomas.

* Findings

* <u>Hyperdense</u> or <u>isodense</u> well-demarcated extra-axial mass

* Possible calcifications

## Pathology

### Gross findings




* Encapsulated, round, grayish-white <u>tumor</u>

* Firm to hard consistency

* Cross-sectional surface: gray, granular





### Microscopic findings




* <u>Mesenchymal</u> origin (arachnoid cap cells)

* Whorls of meningothelial cells (onion peel arrangement)   [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/woXhV_)

* <u>Psammoma bodies</u>  [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BMXzIA)

* Increased vascularity  [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9oXNV_)

## Treatment

* Surgical resection: first-line treatment [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BMXzIA)

* Preoperative embolization in the case of highly vascular tumors [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9oXNV_)

* <u>Skull base</u> meningiomas have a high recurrence risk . [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CoXqV_)

* <u>Radiotherapy</u> [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BMXzIA)

* In the case of inoperable tumors

* Postoperatively, if the <u>tumor</u> was incompletely resected

* As an <u>adjuvant therapy</u> in the case of grade II and III meningiomas

* Small tumors (usually < 3 cm): <u>stereotactic radiosurgery</u> (e.g., <u>gamma knife</u>) [[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XN09-T)

* <u>Active surveillance</u>: consider in a slow-growing asymptomatic <u>tumor</u> in an elderly patient [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aN0Q-T)
