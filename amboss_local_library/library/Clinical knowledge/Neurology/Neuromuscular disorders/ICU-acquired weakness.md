# ICU-acquired weakness

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Neuromuscular disorders > ICU-acquired weakness*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7D04fR)

## Summary

<u>ICU</u>-acquired <u>weakness</u> (<u>ICU</u>-AW) is generalized limb <u>weakness</u> that develops during critical illness without an alternative cause; it encompasses <u>critical illness polyneuropathy</u> (<u>CIP</u>), <u>critical illness myopathy</u> (CIM), and critical illness polyneuromyopathy. <u>ICU</u>-AW affects approximately half of all critically ill patients. <u>Risk factors</u> include severe illness (e.g., <u>sepsis</u>), <u>iatrogenic</u> factors (e.g., prolonged <u>mechanical ventilation</u>, early <u>parenteral nutrition</u>, certain medications including <u>glucocorticoids</u>), and <u>hyperglycemia</u>. Patients typically present with diffuse, symmetrical, flaccid limb <u>weakness</u>; <u>weaning</u> from <u>mechanical ventilation</u> may be prolonged if the respiratory muscles are affected. Clinical features often overlap, but <u>CIP</u> is characterized by <u>distal</u> sensory disturbances and reduced or absent <u>deep tendon reflexes</u>, whereas CIM is characterized by muscle <u>atrophy</u> and typically affects <u>proximal</u> muscles more than <u>distal</u> muscles. The diagnosis is based on clinical features and confirmed on <u>electrodiagnostic studies</u>. Additional diagnostic testing is required to rule out alternative causes. Management focuses on treating the underlying critical illness, addressing contributing <u>iatrogenic</u> factors, and ensuring early mobilization with physical and <u>occupational therapy</u>. Most patients make a full recovery, usually over weeks to months.

## Clinical features

<u>CIP</u> and CIM often have overlapping clinical features, but characteristic features help distinguish them; features of both conditions may coexist in critical illness polyneuromyopathy. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Bd2-H0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0BdezH0)




* General features of <u>ICU</u>-AW

* Diffuse, symmetrical, flaccid limb <u>weakness</u>

* Respiratory muscle <u>weakness</u> may be present.

* Facial muscles are typically spared. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-lVDzu0)

* Characteristic features of <u>CIP</u> 

* Reduced or absent <u>deep tendon reflexes</u>

* <u>Distal</u> sensory disturbance (e.g., <u>dysesthesia</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZNVZ-u0)

* Typically affects <u>distal</u> muscles more than <u>proximal</u> muscles [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZNVZ-u0)

* Characteristic features of CIM

* Normal or reduced <u>deep tendon reflexes</u>

* Muscle <u>atrophy</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZNVZ-u0)

* Typically affects <u>proximal</u> muscles more than <u>distal</u> muscles [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1Bd2-H0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZNVZ-u0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>ICU</u>-AW is a common cause of prolonged <u>weaning</u> from <u>mechanical ventilation</u> in critically ill patients with <u>sepsis</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0BdezH0)
