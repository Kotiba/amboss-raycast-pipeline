## Summary

Myotonic syndromes are a heterogeneous group of inherited disorders with similar pathological mechanisms. Myotonic syndromes are subdivided into <u>dystrophic myotonic syndromes</u> and <u>nondystrophic myotonic syndromes</u>. <u>Nondystrophic myotonic syndromes</u> are channelopathies and include <u>Thomsen disease</u>, <u>Becker disease</u>, and Eulenberg disease. The channelopathies are <u>autosomal dominant</u> or <u>autosomal recessive</u> conditions caused by defective ion channels in the <u>skeletal muscle</u> <u>sarcolemma</u>. All three diseases manifest with <u>myotonia</u>, muscle stiffness, and <u>weakness</u>. <u>Thomsen disease</u> and <u>Becker disease</u> are furthermore characterized by muscular <u>hypertrophy</u>. <u>Myotonic dystrophies</u>, the most prevalent myotonic syndromes, are one of the most common forms of adult-onset <u>muscular dystrophy</u>. Both types, <u>myotonic dystrophy type I</u> (DM1, <u>Curschmann-Steinert</u> disease) and <u>myotonic dystrophy</u> type II (<u>DM2</u>, <u>proximal myotonic myopathy</u>), are <u>autosomal dominant</u> conditions with CTG <u>trinucleotide repeat</u> and CCTG tetranucleotide repeat expansions respectively. Type I is a severe (often life-threatening) form of disease, while type II is usually mild. Both present with <u>skeletal muscle</u> <u>weakness</u> and <u>myotonia</u>, muscle <u>pain</u>, <u>heart</u> conduction defects, <u>cataracts</u>, testicular <u>atrophy</u>, and frontal balding. <u>Electromyography</u> may confirm <u>myotonia</u> that is not identified during <u>clinical examination</u>; however, genetic tests usually confirm the diagnosis. As no curative therapy exists, treatment is symptomatic. Except for DM1, patients with myotonic syndromes have a normal lifespan.

## Dystrophic myotonic syndromes

### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FfYgLo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8fYOLo)




* One of the most common forms of adult-onset <u>muscular dystrophy</u>

* DM1 <u>prevalence</u> in US: ∼ 5 per 10,000 births [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PI1W1R0)

* Congenital DM1: most commonly inherited through the mother [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QI1ucR0)





### Etiology




* <u>Autosomal dominant</u> inheritence

* Type 1: <u>CTG</u> trinucleotide repeat expansion in the <u>DMPK</u> <u>gene</u> → changes in myotonin protein <u>kinase</u> expression

* Type 2: CCTG tetranucleotide repeat expansion of the ZNF9 <u>gene</u> (CNBP <u>gene</u>)

> [!NOTE]
> <u>Myotonic dystrophy type I</u> is caused by a CTG <u>nucleotide</u> repeat expansion and results in Cataracts, Toupee (premature <u>hair</u> loss in men), and Gonadal <u>atrophy</u>.




### Diagnostics [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FfYgLo)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QPauek)




* <u>Electromyography</u> (<u>EMG</u>): allows for the identification of <u>myotonia</u>

* Genetic diagnostics: (<u>confirmatory test</u>) detection of <u>trinucleotide repeat expansion</u> mutation in <u>leukocytes</u>

* <u>Biopsy</u>: to distinguish between an inflammatory and a metabolic cause of the <u>myopathy</u>

* Other supportive tests

* ↑ CK, <u>IgG</u> and <u>IgM</u> <u>hypogammaglobulinemia</u>, ↑ <u>FSH</u>, and ↓/normal <u>testosterone</u>

* <u>ECG</u>: to exclude <u>cardiac arrhythmias</u> and other abnormalities

* <u>MRI</u>: signs of <u>global</u> <u>atrophy</u>





### Treatment [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FfYgLo)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8fYOLo)




* Incurable

* Symptomatic treatment (e.g., <u>analgesia</u>, <u>physiotherapy</u>, walking aids, pacemaker, medical therapy for <u>myotonia</u> )

* Regular monitoring (i.e., of respiratory compromise, <u>dysphagia</u>)

* <u>Genetic counseling</u> and testing





### Prognosis




* The course of DM1 is chronic progressive. Cardiac complications reduce <u>life expectancy</u>.

* <u>DM2</u> has a milder disease course.

### Diagnostics




* Percussion myotonia: percussion of a muscle results in <u>myotonia</u> with delayed muscle relaxation (e.g., tapping the <u>thenar eminence</u> with a reflex hammer causes the thumb to <u>abduct</u>)

* <u>EMG</u>: repetitive discharges with oscillating frequency and amplitude (crescendo-decrescendo sound )

* <u>Genetic testing</u>: confirms the diagnosis





### Treatment [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fjXk-B)




* Mild cases

* Usually no treatment necessary

* Avoid precipitating factors, e.g., strenuous exercise.

* Severe cases

* <u>Sodium channel blocker</u> (e.g., <u>mexiletine</u>, <u>carbamazepine</u>, tocainide)

* <u>Acetazolamide</u>





### Prognosis




* Incurable

* Varying degree of life-long disability but no effect on <u>life expectancy</u>

* No symptom progression throughout the course of the disease
