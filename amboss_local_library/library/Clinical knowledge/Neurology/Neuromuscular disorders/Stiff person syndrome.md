## Summary

Stiff person syndrome (SPS) is a rare disorder that manifests with progressive muscle stiffness and rigidity. It is caused by an autoimmune process in which <u>antibodies</u> are formed against <u>glutamic acid decarboxylase</u> (<u>GAD</u>) or another protein in the <u>gamma-aminobutyric</u> acid (<u>GABA</u>) synthesis pathway. This leads to reduced levels of <u>GABA</u>, which then causes loss of <u>CNS</u> inhibition and increasing muscle stiffness. SPS is associated with autoimmune disorders (particularly type 1 <u>diabetes mellitus</u>). Less commonly, it occurs as a paraneoplastic process. Treatment of SPS consists of symptomatic treatment with <u>benzodiazepines</u> and <u>muscle relaxants</u> as well as immune modulation therapy with IVIG and <u>immunosuppressants</u>. Prognosis is poor, with most patients experiencing progressive gait disorders and disability.
