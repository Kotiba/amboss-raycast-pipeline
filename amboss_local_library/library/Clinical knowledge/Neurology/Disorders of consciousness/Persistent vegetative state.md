# Persistent vegetative state

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Disorders of consciousness > Persistent vegetative state*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Yi0nJf)

---

## Summary

Persistent vegetative state (<u>PVS</u>) is a clinical condition in which function of the cortex is impaired while function of the <u>brainstem</u> is preserved for a period of more than one month. <u>Traumatic brain injury</u> and diffuse cerebral <u>hypoxia</u> are the most common etiologies. Patients in <u>PVS</u> may appear awake but are not aware or conscious, and they are unable to communicate with others or purposefully interact with their environment. However, they are still able to breathe on their own, <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles are preserved, and autonomic function is at least partially retained. <u>PVS</u> is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>, and it is important to differentiate it from <u>coma</u> (no <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles) and <u>brain death</u> (no <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles or <u>brainstem</u> function). No definitive treatment exists. Although most patients remain in a vegetative state for years, a few may recover spontaneously. A vegetative state is declared permanent when recovery is very unlikely (i.e., <u>PVS</u> due to trauma lasting more than 1 year or <u>PVS</u> due to a nontraumatic cause lasting more than 3 months).

---

## Etiology

* <u>Traumatic brain injury</u>

* Diffuse cerebral <u>hypoxia</u>

* Other causes of severe damage to the cerebral hemispheres and/or <u>thalamus</u> (e.g., <u>neurodegenerative diseases</u>, <u>CNS</u> infections, metabolic disturbances, toxins, developmental <u>malformations</u> of the <u>CNS</u>)

## Pathophysiology

Widespread damage to the <u>white matter</u> of both cerebral hemispheres and/or the <u>thalamus</u> bilaterally, without damage to the <u>brainstem</u>. This results in reduction of cortical function with preserved <u>brainstem</u> control of respiration, cardiac function, <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles, and reflexes.

## Clinical features

* Usually preceded by <u>comatose</u> state requiring intensive care and gradual recovery of autonomic function

* But lack of recovery of higher mental function after 1 month and the following features:

* Intact <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles with periods of wakefulness

* Loss of perceptual and self-awareness

* Lack of purposeful response to external stimuli (e.g., tactile, auditory, visual, etc.)

* Inability to communicate and lack of apparent language comprehension

* Disconjugate <u>eye movements</u> with loss of fixation

* Occasional nonpurposeful limb movements and spontaneous smiling, crying, <u>grunting</u>, or screaming

* Autonomic function and reflexes retained to variable degrees

* Sufficient cardiovascular, respiratory, and <u>thermoregulatory</u> function

* Sufficient gastrointestinal and renal function but bowel and <u>bladder incontinence</u>

* Variable preservation of reflexes (e.g., <u>pupillary</u>, corneal, vestibulo-ocular, gag, <u>cough</u>)





References:[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y4Yn36)

## Diagnosis

* <u>Clinical diagnosis</u> based on ≥ 1 month of intact <u>sleep</u>-wake cycles, loss of perception and self-awareness, and autonomic functions retained to variable degrees

* <u>Neuroimaging</u>

* CT/<u>MRI</u>: nonspecific diffuse or multifocal abnormalities involving the gray and <u>white matter</u>; decrease in <u>cerebral blood flow</u>

* PET: diffuse reduction of the <u>glucose</u> metabolic rate of the <u>brain</u>

* <u>EEG</u>: diffuse generalized polymorphic delta and/or <u>theta activity</u>

## Differential diagnoses

| Disorders of consciousness |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | Persistent vegetative state | <u>Coma</u> | <u>Locked-in syndrome</u> | <u>Brain death</u> |
| Perceptual and self-awareness | Absent | Absent | Present | Absent |
| <u>Sleep</u>-wake cycles | Present | Absent | Present | Absent |
| <u>Motor function</u> | No purposeful movement | No purposeful movement | <u>Quadriplegia</u> and bulbar or <u>pseudobulbar palsy</u>; <u>eye</u> movement preserved | None or only reflex spinal movements |
| <u>Brainstem</u> reflexes | Variable | Variable | Variable | Absent |
| Respiratory function | Normal | Depressed, variable | Variable | Absent |
