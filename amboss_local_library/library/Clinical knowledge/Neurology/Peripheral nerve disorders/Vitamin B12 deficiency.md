# Vitamin B12 deficiency

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Neurology > Peripheral nerve disorders > Vitamin B12 deficiency*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6R0jLf)

---

## Summary

<u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> (<u>cobalamin</u>) plays an essential role in enzymatic reactions responsible for <u>red blood cell</u> (<u>RBC</u>) formation and myelination of the nervous system. <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency is caused by insufficient dietary intake, <u>malabsorption</u>, or increased demand and can result in <u>megaloblastic anemia</u> and various neurological disturbances. Patients typically present with <u>signs of anemia</u> (e.g., fatigue) and/or neurological manifestations (e.g., <u>paresthesia</u>, <u>ataxia</u>, and neuropsychiatric disorders). <u>Pernicious anemia</u> is the most common type of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency and is caused by <u>autoimmune gastritis</u> (<u>AIG</u>). <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency in <u>AIG</u> is due to <u>mucosal</u> <u>atrophy</u>, <u>achlorhydria</u>, and loss of <u>intrinsic factor</u>, which is crucial for <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> absorption. <u>Laboratory studies</u> often show <u>macrocytic anemia</u>, with or without <u>pancytopenia</u>, and circulating <u>megaloblasts</u>. Serum <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> level may support the diagnosis but has low <u>sensitivity</u> and <u>specificity</u>. If levels are normal, diagnosis is confirmed based on elevated <u>methylmalonic acid</u> (MMA) and/or <u>homocysteine</u> levels. Additional studies (e.g., <u>autoantibodies</u> for <u>pernicious anemia</u>, <u>EGD</u> with <u>biopsy</u>) may be required to establish the underlying cause. Treatment of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency consists of oral or parenteral supplementation. Depending on the underlying cause, long-term <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub> replacement</u> may be needed.

## Etiology

* Insufficient dietary intake [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)

* <u>Vegan diet</u>: <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency typically only develops after years of strictly avoiding all animal products (unlike <u>folate deficiency</u>, which occurs within a few months of insufficient intake).

* <u>Malnutrition</u>

* Chronic <u>heavy alcohol use</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* <u>Malabsorption</u>

* Gastric: ↓ <u>intrinsic factor</u> and <u>achlorhydria</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* <u>Atrophic gastritis</u>: autoimmune; , resulting in <u>pernicious anemia</u> (most common type of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency; ), or nonautoimmune, e.g., <u>Helicobacter pylori infection</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* <u>Gastrectomy</u> (partial or total), <u>gastric bypass</u>, <u>bariatric surgery</u>

* <u>Proton pump inhibitors</u> (<u>PPIs</u>)

* <u>H<sub>2</sub> receptor blockers</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* <u>Pancreas</u>: <u>chronic pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Jejunum</u>: ↑ <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> demand [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)
* Bacterial overgrowth;  or <u>parasites</u>, including <u>fish tapeworm</u> (<u>Dibothriocephalus latus</u>)

* <u>Terminal ileum</u>: ↓ uptake of <u>intrinsic factor</u>-<u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> complex [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* Inflammation; : <u>celiac disease</u>; , <u>tropical sprue</u>, <u>Crohn disease</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* <u>Ileal</u> resection and/or reconstructive <u>surgery</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)

* Other [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* <u>Pregnancy</u> or <u>breastfeeding</u> due to increased <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> demand [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* Inherited disorders of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> absorption (e.g., transcobalamin deficiency) or inherited metabolic disorders [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* Drug-induced deficiency, e.g., due to <u>metformin</u>, <u>nitrous oxide</u> misuse, <u>PPIs</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>Malabsorption</u>, <u>autoimmune gastritis</u> (<u>pernicious anemia</u>), <u>gastrectomy</u>, <u>gastric bypass</u>, <u>nitric oxide</u> misuse, and inherited disorders can cause severe <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

## Pathophysiology

For information on sources, absorption, transport, and storage of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>, see “<u>Vitamins</u>.”




### <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency




<u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> is a water-soluble <u>cofactor</u> for enzymes involved in <u>DNA synthesis</u> (i.e., <u>methionine synthase</u>) and <u>odd-chain fatty acid</u> metabolism (i.e., <u>methylmalonyl-CoA mutase</u>), and deficiency leads to enzymatic dysfunction. Enzymatic dysfunction has the following consequences: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)




* Methionine synthase demethylates <u>5-methyltetrahydrofolate</u>, producing <u>tetrahydrofolic acid</u>, a <u>cofactor</u> in <u>purine</u> synthesis, and also converts <u>homocysteine</u> to <u>methionine</u>

* ↓ <u>Tetrahydrofolic acid</u> → ↓ <u>DNA synthesis</u> → impaired <u>hematopoiesis</u>

* Large, nucleated <u>hematopoietic</u> cells, including <u>megaloblasts</u>, are produced, which undergo <u>apoptosis</u> or are <u>phagocytosed</u> by <u>macrophages</u>

* <u>Pancytopenia</u> (including <u>megaloblastic anemia</u>)

* ↓ <u>Methionine</u> → neuropathy  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4MX3KA)

* ↑ <u>Homocysteine</u> → <u>endothelial</u> damage (increases risk for cardiovascular disease)

* Can cause secondary <u>folate deficiency</u>

* <u>Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase</u> (converts <u>methylmalonyl-CoA</u> to <u>succinyl CoA</u>)

* Accumulation of <u>methylmalonyl-CoA</u> and its precursor <u>propionyl-CoA</u>, as well as their associated <u>odd-chain fatty acids</u>, which cannot be completely metabolized

* <u>Propionyl CoA</u> replaces <u>acetyl CoA</u> in neuronal membranes → <u>demyelination</u> → neurological manifestations





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Folate deficiency</u> also leads to low levels of <u>tetrahydrofolic acid</u>, causing <u>megaloblastic anemia</u>.




### Pernicious anemia




The pathophysiology of <u>AIG</u> has not been clearly defined but is associated with: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FWdgMK0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)




* Antiparietal cell antibodies: target <u>gastric parietal cells</u> → ↓ acid production and <u>atrophic gastritis</u> and ↓ <u>intrinsic factor</u> production → ↓ <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> absorption in <u>terminal ileum</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* Autoreactive <u>T cells</u> target gastric proton pump (<u>H+/K+ ATPase</u>) and are likely the main mediators of inflammation in <u>AIG</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* Anti-intrinsic factor antibodies bind <u>intrinsic factor</u>, blocking the <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> binding site and most <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> absorption; a small amount of <u>passive diffusion</u> occurs throughout the intestinal tract. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* See also “Pathophysiology” in “<u>Atrophic gastritis</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> <u>AIG</u> causes chronic destruction of <u>gastric parietal cells</u>, leading to <u>mucosal</u> <u>atrophy</u>, <u>achlorhydria</u>, and loss of <u>intrinsic factor</u>. <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency is a late manifestation of <u>AIG</u>, termed <u>pernicious anemia</u>.




> [!TIP]
> <u>AIG</u> is associated with other autoimmune diseases (e.g., <u>hypothyroidism</u>, <u>vitiligo</u>) and an increased risk of <u>gastric cancer</u>.

## Clinical features

* Signs of <u>anemia</u> (e.g., fatigue, <u>pallor</u>) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tk06MT)

* Mild <u>scleral icterus</u> and/or <u>jaundice</u>

* Neurological disturbances are generally symmetrical

* <u>Peripheral neuropathy</u>: tingling, numbness, pins-and-needles sensation, coldness (especially in the lower extremities)

* Subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord (<u>SCD</u>): symmetrical <u>demyelination</u> of the <u>spinal cord tracts</u> occurs in <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency due to insufficient <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>-dependent <u>fatty acid synthesis</u> and production/maintenance of <u>myelin</u>

* It manifests with the following symptoms:

* <u>Paresthesia</u>, impaired <u>proprioception</u>, loss of vibratory sensation, tactile sensation, and position discrimination due to <u>demyelination</u> of the <u>dorsal columns</u>

* <u>Spastic paresis</u> due to <u>demyelination</u> of the <u>lateral corticospinal tracts</u> (<u>axons</u> of <u>upper motor neurons</u>)

* Gait abnormalities (spinal <u>ataxia</u>) resulting from the damage of <u>spinocerebellar tracts</u> and <u>dorsal columns</u>

* Long-term deficiency can lead to irreversible neurological damage.

* Neuropsychiatric disease;  (e.g., reversible <u>dementia</u>, <u>depression</u>, <u>paranoia</u>) [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Tk06MT)

* Worsening <u>vision</u>

* <u>Autonomic dysfunction</u>: <u>impotence</u> and incontinence

* <u>Glossitis</u>





> [!NOTE]
> The Spinocerebellar tracts, <u>lateral</u> Corticospinal tracts, and Dorsal columns are affected in Subacute Combined Degeneration.




> [!TIP]
> Always consider <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency when evaluating patients with <u>dementia</u>.

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)




Consider <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency in patients with <u>risk factors</u>, clinical features, and/or supportive laboratory findings (e.g., <u>macrocytic anemia</u>).




* Obtain initial studies and <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> levels.

* Consider additional confirmatory studies (e.g., in patients with normal <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> levels).

* Obtain additional studies if the cause is not immediately evident (e.g., low <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> intake or <u>malabsorption</u>).





> [!TIP]
> Consider screening for <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency in individuals taking <u>metformin</u> or with a <u>vegan diet</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)




> [!TIP]
> Do not wait for diagnostic confirmation to initiate treatment if there is a strong clinical suspicion for <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)




### Initial workup [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)




* <u>CBC</u>

* <u>Macrocytic anemia</u>: ↓ <u>Hb</u>, ↑ <u>MCV</u>, ↑ <u>MCH</u>, ↓ <u>reticulocyte index</u>  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* <u>Pancytopenia</u> may occur.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* <u>Blood smear</u>: : oval <u>macrocytes</u>, circulating <u>megaloblasts</u>, > 5% <u>hypersegmented neutrophils</u>

* <u>Folate</u> level: <u>Folate deficiency</u> causes a similar hematologic presentation to <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* <u>Hemolysis</u> markers: <u>Bilirubin</u> and <u>LDH</u> levels may be elevated.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* Patients with <u>peripheral neuropathy</u>: See “<u>Diagnostics for polyneuropathy</u>.”  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)





### Diagnostic confirmation [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)




Diagnosis is confirmed based on circulating <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> and elevated MMA and/or <u>homocysteine</u> levels. There is a lack of consensus on <u>cutoff values</u> and many laboratories use a combination of biomarkers for diagnosis.




* Serum <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> level (first-line test) : deficiency confirmed if levels are low  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* Serum MMA and/or <u>homocysteine</u> levels

* Indication: normal serum <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> levels (serum MMA and <u>homocysteine</u> testing have higher <u>sensitivity</u> than serum <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> testing)

* Findings: Elevation confirms the diagnosis.

* MMA is elevated in <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency and normal in <u>folate deficiency</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* <u>Homocysteine</u> is elevated in both <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> and <u>folate deficiency</u>.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)





#### Schilling test




* Description

* A test historically used to assess <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> uptake and determine the cause of deficiency. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ok0ILT)

* This test is now obsolete but the principles are useful for understanding the causes of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency.

* Stages

* Consists of four stages that assess impaired <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> uptake

* Patients ingest radiolabeled <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> and urinary excretion is measured.

* Urinary excretion of 8–40% (varies between laboratories) of radioactive <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> within 24 hours is considered normal. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kMXmKA)

| Stage 2 | <br>* Oral radiolabeled <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> PLUS <u>intrinsic factor</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Normal excretion: <u>Malabsorption</u> due to lack of <u>intrinsic factor</u> is the most likely cause.<br><br>* Excretion remains below expected: Perform stage 3.<br><br><br> |

### Additional studies [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)




Consider the following additional studies based on clinical suspicion of <u>malabsorption</u> as the cause of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)




* <u>Autoantibodies</u> for <u>pernicious anemia</u> (against <u>intrinsic factor</u> or <u>parietal cells</u> ; ) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Presence of serum <u>autoantibodies</u> supports the diagnosis.

* Consider testing even if <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency is not confirmed if there is strong clinical suspicion. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* <u>EGD</u> with <u>biopsy</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FWdgMK0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* Recommended in patients with <u>autoantibodies</u> indicating <u>pernicious anemia</u>

* Consider if the cause is unclear, e.g., in patients without <u>autoantibodies</u> if there is strong clinical suspicion.

* See also "<u>Autoimmune gastritis</u>."

* Testing for <u>malabsorption</u>: See “<u>Diagnosis of malabsorption</u>.” [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Autoantibodies</u> may not be present in all patients with <u>pernicious anemia</u>.




> [!WARNING]
> Individuals with newly diagnosed <u>pernicious anemia</u> require <u>EGD</u> with <u>biopsies</u> to evaluate for <u>autoimmune gastritis</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FWdgMK0)

> In contrast to <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency, <u>folate deficiency</u> is generally not associated with neurological symptoms.




### Other causes of <u>macrocytic anemia</u>



* <u>Myelodysplastic syndrome</u>

* <u>CBC</u> may show <u>macrocytic</u> (but not megaloblastic) <u>anemia</u>

* Most patients have other concurrent hematological alterations (e.g., <u>neutropenia</u>, <u>thrombocytopenia</u>).




### Other causes of neuropathy




* <u>Multiple sclerosis</u>

* <u>Friedreich ataxia</u>

* Charcot-Marie-<u>Tooth</u> disease

* Infectious diseases (e.g., <u>syphilis</u>/<u>tabes dorsalis</u>, <u>HIV</u> <u>myelopathy</u>, herpes <u>virus</u> <u>myelitis</u>)

* Other deficiencies (e.g., <u>copper deficiency</u>, <u>vitamin E deficiency</u>)

* <u>Neoplasms</u> (e.g., <u>astrocytoma</u>, <u>ependymoma</u>)





The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.

## Treatment

### <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub> replacement</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)




Do not wait for diagnostic confirmation to initiate treatment in patients with severe neurological deficits, if there is a strong clinical suspicion of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency, and/or in pregnant individuals.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)




* <u>Cyanocobalamin</u> IM (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vTYAso)

* Preferred route for patients with any of the following:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yidd8p0)

* Neurological deficit

* Severely low cell counts on <u>CBC</u>

* Very low serum <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> levels

* Patients sometimes develop itch, <u>flu-like symptoms</u>, and/or <u>anaphylaxis</u>.

* <u>Cyanocobalamin</u> PO

* High dose (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Effective alternative to IM route even in patients with <u>pernicious anemia</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* Suitable for patients with good therapy adherence [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)

* Low dose (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE: for subclinical deficiency or preemptively for individuals with a low-<u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> diet (e.g., <u>vegan diet</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Sublingual or nasal routes: not recommended [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9idNFp0)





#### Follow-up [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)




* Response to therapy [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Monitor clinical response.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Repeat <u>CBC</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)

* Duration of <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> replacement therapy [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/widhFp0)

* Reversible causes: Treat the underlying cause and stop replacement once clinical features stabilize or resolve.

* Irreversible causes (e.g., in <u>pernicious anemia</u>): Continue indefinitely and counsel the patient on the importance of ongoing <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> replacement therapy.  [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9idNFp0)





> [!TIP]
> Consider alternative diagnoses if there is an insufficient response to treatment. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CidqFp0)




### Additional management [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9idNFp0)




* Management of the underlying condition: See “<u>SIBO</u>,” “<u>Pancreatic</u> insufficiency,” and “<u>Crohn disease</u>.”

* <u>Supportive care</u>, e.g.:

* Avoidance of <u>PPIs</u> in patients with <u>pernicious anemia</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BidzFp0)

* Neurological <u>rehabilitation</u>: See “<u>Treatment of polyneuropathy</u>.”

* <u>RBC</u> <u>transfusion</u> for <u>anemia</u>-related cardiac symptoms, e.g., <u>angina pectoris</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xidEFp0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9idNFp0)

> In combined <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> and <u>folate deficiency</u>, <u>folate supplementation</u> without <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> replacement may exacerbate neurological symptoms, likely due to ongoing damage from untreated <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u> deficiency.
