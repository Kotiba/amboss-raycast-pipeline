## Summary

<u>Upper respiratory tract</u> infections (URTIs) involve structures at or above the <u>vocal cords</u>, e.g., the nasal cavity, sinuses, <u>pharynx</u>, and/or <u>larynx</u>. URTIs include the <u>common cold</u>, <u>acute rhinosinusitis</u>, <u>pharyngitis</u>, <u>laryngitis</u>, <u>influenza</u>, <u>croup</u>, and <u>COVID-19</u>. Most URTIs are caused by <u>viruses</u> such as the <u>rhinovirus</u>. URTIs more commonly occur in the fall and winter and are especially common in children. Typical clinical manifestations include <u>rhinorrhea</u>, nasal congestion, <u>sore throat</u>, and <u>cough</u>. Diagnosis is generally clinical, although diagnostic testing may be performed to assess for specific infections (e.g., <u>influenza</u>, <u>COVID-19</u>, <u>group A streptococcal pharyngitis</u>). In most cases, treatment is supportive, with symptoms typically resolving within 14 days. <u>Antibiotics</u> are not indicated unless there is a confirmed bacterial infection.

## Epidemiology

* More commonly occur in the fall and winter [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zeWrYk0)

* Higher <u>incidence</u> in children than adults [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ObWIFP0)

* Children: 6–10 per year

* Adults: 2–4 per year




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

## Etiology

Most URTIs are caused by <u>viruses</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bHbHKE)




### Viral




* <u>Rhinovirus</u>

* <u>Coronavirus</u>

* <u>Influenza virus</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ObWIFP0)

* <u>Respiratory syncytial virus</u> (<u>RSV</u>)

* <u>Adenovirus</u>

* <u>Parainfluenza virus</u>

* <u>Human metapneumovirus</u>

* <u>Epstein-Barr virus</u>

* <u>Enterovirus</u>





### Bacterial




* <u>Streptococcus pneumoniae</u>

* <u>Streptococcus pyogenes</u>

* <u>Haemophilus influenzae</u>

* <u>Mycoplasma pneumoniae</u>

* <u>Moraxella catarrhalis</u>





> [!TIP]
> Many of the pathogens that cause URTI can also cause <u>lower respiratory tract infections</u> (<u>LRTIs</u>). [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zeWrYk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bHbHKE)

## Diagnosis

Most URTIs can be <u>diagnosed clinically</u>. Consider diagnostic studies in selected patients.




### Nonspecific <u>laboratory studies</u>




* Routine studies: to support the diagnosis and/or determine disease severity

* <u>CBC</u> may show:

* <u>Lymphopenia</u>, e.g., in <u>COVID-19</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/P_cWoU0)

* <u>Lymphocytosis</u>, e.g., in <u>pertussis</u>, <u>influenza</u>, <u>infectious mononucleosis</u>  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IfcYnb0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZoXZ0_)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K2WUik0)

* <u>ESR</u> and <u>CRP</u> may be elevated. [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/62Wjik0)

* <u>BMP</u> and <u>liver chemistries</u> may show signs of <u>end-organ damage</u> in severe disease (e.g., <u>severe COVID-19</u>).

* <u>Blood cultures</u>: Consider in severe disease (e.g., <u>epiglottitis</u>) to evaluate for concurrent <u>bacteremia</u>.

* <u>ABG</u>: Obtain in patients with <u>signs of respiratory distress</u>. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A71RoR0)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k2Wm3k0)





### Disease-specific <u>laboratory studies</u>




Consider specific studies based on clinical suspicion to support treatment and/or disposition decisions.




* <u>Rapid strep test</u>: : suspected <u>acute bacterial tonsillopharyngitis</u>

* <u>Heterophile antibody test</u>: : suspected <u>infectious mononucleosis</u> [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K2WUik0)

* Disease-specific <u>NAATs</u> (e.g., <u>PCR</u>) in patients with:

* <u>Indications for influenza testing</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IfcYnb0)

* <u>COVID-19</u> symptoms and/or known exposure [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H_XKp00)

* <u>Suspected pertussis</u>

* <u>Risk factors for severe RSV infection</u>

* <u>Respiratory viral panel</u>

* Can differentiate between different viral etiologies of URTIs (e.g., <u>influenza</u>, <u>parainfluenza</u>, <u>RSV</u>)

* Not routinely indicated

* May be considered in certain patients, e.g.:

* Those with an atypical presentation

* If there is diagnostic uncertainty in suspected <u>croup</u>

### Imaging




Consider imaging to support the diagnosis, determine disease severity, and/or evaluate for complications.




* Chest imaging (<u>CXR</u> or <u>CT scan</u>): to evaluate for <u>pneumonia</u>  [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/42W33k0)[[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ua1pm20)

* Neck imaging

* <u>Anterior</u>-<u>posterior</u> <u>x-ray</u> neck: to evaluate for <u>croup</u> [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Od1IJf0)

* <u>Lateral</u> <u>x-ray</u> neck: to evaluate for <u>epiglottitis</u> in children [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PzYWG7)

* CT neck with IV contrast [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nfb75G)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pd1LIf0)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y71doR0)

* Adults: to rule out differential diagnoses in patients with suspected <u>epiglottitis</u>

* Children: to rule out differential diagnoses and/or assess for congenital abnormalities in <u>croup</u>

* CT maxillofacial with or without IV contrast: in patients with <u>red flags for rhinosinusitis</u>

## Complications

Most URTIs are mild and <u>self-limited</u> and rarely cause complications. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zeWrYk0)




* <u>LRTIs</u>

* <u>Otitis media</u>

* Secondary bacterial infection

* See also the respective articles for complications of specific causes of URTI, e.g.:

* <u>Sinusitis complications</u>

* <u>Pertussis complications</u>
