## Summary

Nasal papillomas are benign <u>epithelial tumors</u> of the nasal cavity which mainly affect males between 40–60 years of age. The exact etiology is unknown, but <u>human papillomavirus infection</u>, smoking, and <u>chronic sinusitis</u> are predisposing factors for the development of nasal papillomas. There are three types of nasal papillomas. <u>Inverted papilloma</u> is the most common, arises from the <u>lateral</u> nasal wall and has the highest risk of malignant degeneration. Fungiform and oncocytic nasal papillomas are less common. Patients often present with unilateral nasal obstruction, <u>anosmia</u>, and intermittent <u>epistaxis</u>. On examination, nasal papillomas appear dull gray/pink and are <u>friable</u>. CT or <u>MRI</u> delineate the extent of the papilloma; diagnosis is established on <u>biopsy</u>. All nasal papillomas should be completely excised. Although benign, nasal papillomas are locally aggressive, have a malignant potential, and a high recurrence rate if incompletely excised.
