## Summary

Bruxism is involuntary and repetitive <u>teeth</u> grinding and/or <u>jaw</u> clenching due to increased masticatory muscle activity either while asleep (<u>sleep bruxism</u>) or awake (<u>awake bruxism</u>). The exact cause of bruxism remains unknown, but it is believed to be multifactorial, with potential factors including genetic predisposition, <u>sleep</u> architecture, psychological stressors and disorders (e.g., <u>sleep disorders</u>, <u>anxiety disorder</u>), neurological changes and disorders (e.g., due to stimulatory drug use, <u>brain</u> trauma, <u>autonomic nervous system</u> dysfunction). Bruxism is considered primary if it cannot be associated with a medical condition or a certain substance and secondary if it can be associated with a psychiatric or neurological condition (e.g., <u>sleep disorders</u>, <u>cerebral palsy</u>) or the use of a particular substance (esp. stimulants, e.g., <u>SSRIs</u>, <u>anxiolytics</u>, <u>dopaminergic</u> drugs, <u>amphetamine</u>, <u>caffeine</u>). Clinical features of bruxism include abraded <u>teeth</u>, <u>tongue</u> indentations, recessed <u>gums</u>, <u>linea alba</u> in the cheek, <u>headaches</u>, locking of the <u>jaw</u>, <u>temporomandibular joint</u> <u>pain</u>, and facial <u>pain</u>. Diagnosis is based upon self-report, third-party observation (e.g., family member), and <u>physical examination</u>. <u>Electromyography</u> of the masticatory muscle and <u>polysomnography</u> (for <u>sleep bruxism</u>) showing increased muscle activity may support the diagnosis.Treatment includes patient education (e.g., <u>stress</u> management, cognitive behavioral training) and, in secondary bruxism, treatment of any underlying condition, cessation of substance use, and/or adjustment of medication regime. An <u>occlusal splint</u> may be used to relax the <u>jaw</u> and protect the <u>teeth</u> while sleeping. If left untreated, bruxism can lead to severe <u>tooth</u> damage, such as <u>dental attrition</u> and abfraction.

## Definitions

* General: involuntary and repetitive <u>teeth</u> grinding and/or <u>jaw</u> clenching due to increased masticatory muscle activity [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q21CiT0)

* Sleep bruxism: bruxism that occurs while asleep (mainly during <u>NREM sleep</u> stage 2)

* Awake bruxism: bruxism that occurs during wakefulness

* Primary bruxism: no association with any medical condition

* Secondary bruxism: associated with medical conditions (e.g., <u>parasomnias</u>), medication (e.g., <u>antidepressants</u>), and substance use (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, <u>caffeine</u>)

---

## Etiology

The exact causes and pathophysiology of bruxism are unknown, but the following associations have been identified.




* Primary bruxism

* Psychological (e.g., <u>stress</u>, <u>anxiety</u>)

* Positive <u>family history</u>

* Secondary bruxism

* <u>Sleep disorders</u> (e.g., <u>parasomnias</u>)

* Neurological disorders (e.g., <u>cerebral palsy</u>, <u>Huntington disease</u>)

* Medication (e.g., <u>methylphenidate</u>, <u>dextroamphetamine</u>, <u>antidepressants</u>, <u>antipsychotics</u>)

* <u>Recreational drugs</u>;  (e.g., <u>amphetamines</u>, <u>cocaine</u>, <u>alcohol</u>, tobacco, <u>caffeine</u>)

---

## Clinical features

* <u>Teeth</u> grinding and/or <u>jaw</u> clenching (often noticed by a sleeping partner or family member in <u>sleep bruxism</u>)

* Tenderness of the <u>jaw</u> muscle

* Oral lesions

* Cheek and/or lip biting: <u>canker sores</u>, bite line (<u>linea alba</u>) on the cheeks

* <u>Tongue</u> biting: <u>tooth</u> indentations on the <u>tongue</u>, scalloped <u>tongue</u>

* <u>Temporomandibular joint</u> <u>pain</u>

* Limited <u>jaw</u> opening

* Masticatory muscle <u>hypertrophy</u>

---

## Treatment

* All types

* Patient education and behavior modification

* <u>Stress</u> management

* <u>Cognitive-behavioral therapy</u>

* Treatment of underlying conditions (e.g., <u>anxiety disorder</u>, <u>substance use disorder</u>)

* <u>Sleep hygiene</u>

* <u>Biofeedback training</u>

* Avoidance of substances associated with bruxism (see “Etiology” above)

* Medication (rarely used): <u>clonidine</u>, L-dopa, <u>clonazepam</u>

* <u>Sleep bruxism</u>: <u>occlusal splint</u> (mouthguard) to relax the <u>jaw</u> and protect the <u>teeth</u> while sleeping
