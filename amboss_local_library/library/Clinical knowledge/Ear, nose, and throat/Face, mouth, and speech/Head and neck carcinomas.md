# Head and neck carcinomas

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Ear, nose, and throat > Face, mouth, and speech > Head and neck carcinomas*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ts068h)

---

## Summary

Head and neck <u>carcinomas</u> arise in the squamous cells of the <u>mucosal</u> <u>epithelium</u> in the nasal cavity, <u>paranasal sinuses</u>, <u>oral cavity</u>, <u>pharynx</u>, <u>larynx</u>, and <u>trachea</u>. Other nonsquamous cell <u>carcinomas</u> arise in <u>salivary glands</u>, sinuses, muscles, and nerves of the head and neck. Excluding <u>nonmelanoma skin cancer</u>, <u>oral cavity</u> <u>malignancy</u> is the most common head and neck <u>carcinoma</u>. Nasal cavity, <u>oral cavity</u>, and <u>larynx</u> cancers are generally associated with tobacco and/or <u>alcohol</u> use, whereas <u>pharyngeal carcinomas</u> are typically caused by infections with <u>human papillomavirus</u> and <u>Epstein-Barr virus</u>. Clinical features depend on the site and etiology of the primary <u>tumor</u>. Treatment usually involves <u>surgery</u> followed by <u>radiotherapy</u> and/or <u>chemotherapy</u>. Cancers of the <u>brain</u>, <u>eye</u>, <u>ears</u>, <u>esophagus</u>, <u>thyroid gland</u>, and <u>skin</u> of the head and neck are generally not classified as head and neck cancers and are discussed in other articles.




<u>Oral cavity carcinoma</u>, <u>pharyngeal carcinoma</u>, and <u>laryngeal carcinoma</u> are discussed in more detail in their respective articles.

## Nasal cavity and paranasal sinus carcinomas

### Definition




Tumors arising in the nasal cavity and/or <u>paranasal sinuses</u>.




### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/srctid0)




* Peak <u>incidence</u>: ≥ 60 years of age

* <u>Prevalence</u>: < 3% of tumors in the respiratory tract

* Sex: <u>♂</u> > <u>♀</u>

* Most common <u>malignant tumors</u>: <u>cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma</u>, <u>adenocarcinoma</u>, <u>neuroendocrine tumors</u>, <u>mucosal melanoma</u>





### Etiology [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QhYudK)




* <u>Nasal cavity carcinoma</u>

* Chronic and/or occupational inhalation exposure to tobacco, wood dust, glues, and adhesives

* Associated with <u>HPV infection</u>

* <u>Paranasal sinus carcinoma</u>

* Chronic and/or occupational inhalation exposure to <u>air pollution</u>, tobacco, wood dust, leather, textiles, and formaldehyde

* Associated with <u>HPV infection</u>, <u>EBV infection</u>, and sinonasal tract <u>lymphomas</u>





### Clinical features




* Most common sites: nasal cavity, followed by the <u>maxillary sinus</u>

* Unilateral nasal obstruction

* <u>Epistaxis</u>

* <u>Chronic sinusitis</u>

* <u>Hyposmia</u>/<u>anosmia</u>

* Advanced disease: a triad of facial asymmetry, palpable or visible <u>tumor</u> in the <u>oral cavity</u>, and visible intranasal <u>tumor</u>





![Paranasal sinuses](../../../../media/l26zt8idisx1956j1jef2jed.jpg)

Paranasal sinuses




### Diagnostics




* <u>Nasal endoscopy</u>

* <u>Biopsy</u> and histopathological examination

* Imaging: CT and <u>MRI</u>





### Treatment




* Resectable tumors: surgical resection and <u>radiotherapy</u> (pre- or postoperative)

* Unresectable tumors: <u>radiation therapy</u> combined with <u>chemotherapy</u>





### Complications



* Involvement of adjacent structures (e.g., <u>eye</u> <u>orbits</u>, sinuses, <u>palate</u>) causing <u>neuralgias</u>, facial <u>pain</u>, impaired <u>vision</u>, ocular symptoms (e.g., <u>exophthalmos</u>, <u>diplopia</u>), and/or <u>dentalgia</u>




### Prognosis [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eDcxWe0)




The five-year relative survival rate for nasal cavity and paranasal sinus <u>carcinomas</u> is ∼ 80%.




> [!TIP]
> A patient with unilateral difficulty breathing through the nose may have a <u>malignant tumor</u>.

## Lip carcinoma

### Definition




A <u>malignant tumor</u> of the <u>lips</u>.




### <u>Epidemiology</u>




* Peak <u>incidence</u>: ∼ 66 years of age [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dHco6d0)

* Sex: <u>♂</u> > <u>♀</u>





### Etiology [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7cfMd0)



* <u>Risk factors</u>

* Tobacco use, <u>alcohol</u> use, prolonged UV exposure

* Associated with <u>HPV infection</u>




### Histological subtype [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/r7cfMd0)




* <u>SCC</u> (most common)

* <u>Basal cell carcinoma</u>





### Clinical features




* Infiltrative or <u>exophytic</u> lesion of the <u>lips</u>

* <u>Ulcer</u> lesion

* <u>Pain</u>, numbness, and/or bleeding of the lip

* Advanced stage: loss of sensation around the chin and/or invasion of the premaxilla and nasal cavity

## Tracheal carcinoma

### Definition




A <u>malignant tumor</u> of the <u>trachea</u>.




### Classification




* Primary <u>tracheal carcinomas</u>

* Uncommon: approx. 0.2% of all respiratory malignancies in the US [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R7clld0)

* Main subtypes: <u>SCC</u> (∼ 85%) and <u>adenoid cystic carcinoma</u> (∼ 15%)

* Very rare: <u>mucoepidermoid carcinoma</u>, <u>neuroendocrine tumors</u>, <u>pleomorphic adenoma</u>

* Usually malignant in adults and benign in children

* Secondary <u>tracheal carcinomas</u> (most common): may arise from hematogenous or lymphatic sites of <u>metastasis</u>, or by direct extension from adjacent structures (e.g., <u>thyroid</u>, <u>esophagus</u>)





### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R7clld0)




* Peak <u>incidence</u>: 60–70 years of age

* Sex: <u>♂</u> > <u>♀</u>





### Etiology




* <u>Risk factors</u>: <u>tobacco smoking</u>, inhalation exposure to hydrocarbons

* Direct invasion of the <u>trachea</u> from <u>carcinoma</u> of the <u>lung</u>, <u>esophagus</u>, <u>larynx</u>, or <u>thyroid gland</u>

* Hematogenous spread from <u>carcinoma</u> of the <u>breast</u>, <u>colon</u>, <u>kidney</u>, and <u>melanoma</u>





### Clinical features [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VDcGWe0)




* Usually mimics other conditions

* Only symptomatic if obstructing the <u>tracheal</u> lumen

* Symptoms vary based on the location of the <u>tumor</u> and histological subtype.

* <u>Airway obstruction</u>: <u>wheezing</u>, <u>stridor</u>, <u>cough</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>, and <u>hemoptysis</u>

* Invasion of adjacent structures (e.g., <u>larynx</u>, <u>pharynx</u>): <u>dysphagia</u>, <u>hoarseness</u> (due to recurrent nerve palsy)





### Diagnostics




* Usually a diagnosis of exclusion, after lack of response to treatment for alternative diagnoses (e.g., <u>COPD</u>, <u>bronchitis</u>)

* Imaging: <u>chest x-ray</u> or CT to rule out other conditions

* <u>Bronchoscopy</u> with <u>biopsy</u> (<u>confirmatory test</u>)

* <u>SCC</u>: <u>exophytic</u> or ulcerous lesions that can be multifocal (up to 10%) or arise as an intraluminal <u>nodule</u>

* <u>Adenoid cystic carcinoma</u> (<u>ACC</u>): polypoid lesions in the <u>trachea</u> or main stem <u>bronchi</u>

* <u>Mucoepidermoid carcinoma</u>: polypoid lesions from bronchial glands in the central <u>airways</u>





### Treatment [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/k7cmNd0)




* <u>SCC</u>

* Localized <u>carcinoma</u>: complete surgical resection with postoperative radiation or <u>chemoradiotherapy</u>

* Unresectable, nonmetastatic <u>carcinoma</u>: <u>chemoradiotherapy</u>

* <u>ACC</u>: <u>radiotherapy</u> or <u>chemoradiotherapy</u>

* <u>Mucoepidermoid carcinoma</u>: surgical resection

* See “<u>Airway management in head and neck cancer</u>” if sedation and/or <u>intubation</u> is required.





### Prognosis




The five-year overall survival rate for <u>ACC</u> is approx. 80% and approx. 10% for <u>SCC</u>. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VDcGWe0)
