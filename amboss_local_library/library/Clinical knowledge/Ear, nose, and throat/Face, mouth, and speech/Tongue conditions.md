# Tongue conditions

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Ear, nose, and throat > Face, mouth, and speech > Tongue conditions*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9j0NcT)

---

## Summary

The <u>tongue</u>'s appearance holds a wealth of information about a patient's general health status, and specific changes (e.g., <u>strawberry tongue</u>) can provide valuable clues in the diagnosis of diseases such as <u>scarlet fever</u>, <u>candidiasis</u>, <u>Kawasaki syndrome</u>, nutritional deficiencies, and certain cancers. Accordingly, the ability to identify <u>tongue</u> findings may become crucial in making the right diagnosis and initiating the proper treatment early in the course of a disease.

## Overview

### <u>Tongue</u> conditions




| Common <u>tongue</u> conditions |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | Features | Etiology | Treatment |
| <br><u>Leukoplakia</u> <br> | <br>* Persistent white <u>plaques</u> that cannot be scraped off<br><br> | <br><br>* Not fully understood<br><br>* Associated with <u>alcohol</u> and tobacco use<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Cryotherapy</u><br><br>* <u>Laser ablation</u><br><br>* Surgical removal<br><br><br> |
| <br><u>Oral cavity cancer</u> and tongue carcinoma <br> | <br><br>* <u>Tongue</u> and mouth <u>pain</u><br><br>* Infiltrative or <u>exophytic</u> lesions: nonhealing, white or red <u>ulcers</u> on <u>tongue</u> and in <u>oral cavity</u><br><br>* Accompanied by bleeding in the mouth, <u>halitosis</u>, <u>pain</u>, and <u>lymphadenopathy</u><br><br>* Advanced stages<br><br>* <u>Dysarthria</u>, <u>dysphagia</u>, <u>odynophagia</u><br><br>* Nerve involvement: <u>ipsilateral</u> <u>tongue</u> deviation (<u>hypoglossal nerve</u>), numbness in the chin, lower lip, and/or mandibular <u>teeth</u> (inferior alveolar nerve), altered <u>tongue</u> sensation (<u>lingual nerve</u>)<br><br><br> | <br>* Exposure to <u>carcinogens</u> (e.g., <u>alcohol</u>, tobacco, <u>HPV</u>)<br><br> | <br><br>* Surgical excision<br><br>* <u>Radiation therapy</u><br><br><br> |
| <br>Oral <u>lichen planus</u>  <br> | <br><br>* Thin, gray-white lines that form a reticular pattern (<u>Wickham striae</u>)<br><br>* Painful, <u>atrophic</u>, and erosive lesions may be present.<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Not fully understood<br><br>* Associated with dental materials, drugs (e.g., <u>NSAIDs</u>, <u>ACE inhibitors</u>), infection, <u>autoimmunity</u> (e.g., <u>PBC</u>), and <u>IBD</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KAbUkw)<br><br><br> | <br>* <u>High potency topical corticosteroids</u> PLUS <u>antifungal</u> therapy<br><br> |
| <u>Oral candidiasis</u>/<u>oral thrush</u> | <br>* White lesions that can be scraped off, resulting in red, inflamed, or bleeding areas<br><br> | <br>* <u>Candida</u> species<br><br> | <br>* Topical <u>antifungal agent</u> (e.g., <u>clotrimazole</u>, <u>nystatin</u>)<br><br> |
| Atrophic glossitis | <br>* Painful, <u>erythematous</u>, <u>edematous</u> <u>tongue</u> with a smooth appearance<br><br> | <br><br>* Nutritional deficiencies (e.g., <u>iron deficiency</u>, <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency</u>, <u>folate deficiency</u>, <u>riboflavin deficiency</u>, <u>niacin deficiency</u>)<br><br>* Infection (e.g., <u>syphilis</u>, <u>candida</u>)<br><br>* <u>Amyloidosis</u><br><br>* <u>Celiac disease</u><br><br>* <u>Protein-calorie malnutrition</u><br><br>* <u>Sjogren syndrome</u><br><br><br> | <br>* Treatment of the underlying condition<br><br> |
| <br>Erythroplakia [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hAbcjw)<br> | <br>* A red patch on the <u>tongue</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Not fully understood<br><br>* Associated with tobacco and <u>alcohol</u> use<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Tobacco and <u>alcohol</u> use cessation<br><br>* Carbon dioxide laser excision<br><br><br> |
| Oral hairy leukoplakia | <br><br>* Benign, painless, irregularly shaped white <u>plaques</u> with feathery or hairy appearance<br><br>* Most commonly located on the <u>lateral</u> <u>tongue</u>, but can also occur on the floor of the mouth, <u>palate</u>, or buccal <u>mucosa</u><br><br>* Patches cannot be scraped off (in contrast to <u>oral thrush</u>)<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>EBV</u><br><br>* <u>Immunosuppression</u> (e.g., patients with a history of <u>organ transplantation</u>)<br><br>* <u>Immunocompromise</u> (e.g., <u>HIV</u>-positive individuals)<br><br><br> | <br>* Treatment of the underlying condition<br><br> |
| <u>Herpetic gingivostomatitis</u> | <br><br>* <u>Erythema</u>, painful <u>erosions</u>, and intact <u>vesicles</u> on perioral <u>skin</u> and oral <u>mucosa</u>, especially inner cheek, <u>soft palate</u>, and <u>tongue</u><br><br>* Accompanied by <u>pharyngitis</u> and <u>cervical lymphadenopathy</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>HSV-1</u><br><br>* Predominantly affects children<br><br><br> | <br>* Treatment of <u>HSV-1</u> infection (oral <u>acyclovir</u>)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pAbLkw)<br><br> |
| <br><u>Lingual thyroid</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Kc1UWf0)<br> | <br><br>* An abnormal mass of <u>ectopic</u>, <u>thyroid</u> tissue at the base of the <u>tongue</u><br><br>* <u>Dysphagia</u> for solids, <u>dysphonia</u>, <u>hypothyroidism</u> (in individuals with hypoactive <u>thyroid</u> tissue)<br><br>* Occasional bleeding, <u>upper airway</u> obstruction<br><br><br> | <br>* Failure in descent of the <u>thyroid gland</u> to its normal position during <u>embryogenesis</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Supplemental <u>thyroxine</u>: for symptomatic, hypothyroid patients<br><br>* Surgical excision of the mass<br><br>* Ablative <u>radioiodine therapy</u><br><br><br> |

### <u>Tongue</u> findings




| Common <u>tongue</u> findings |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finding | Features | Associated conditions and factors | <br>Treatment <br> |
| <br>Geographic tongue (<u>benign migratory glossitis</u>)<br> | <br><br>* Solitary, irregular patches of red and white <u>tongue</u> discoloration that resemble a geographic map<br><br>* Lesions migrate and patients experience exacerbations and remissions over time<br><br>* Caused by loss of filiform <u>lingual papillae</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Atopy</u><br><br>* <u>Psoriasis</u><br><br>* <u>Reactive arthritis</u><br><br><br> | <br>* <u>Topical steroid</u> gels, topical <u>anesthetic agents</u>, or <u>antihistamine</u> mouth rinses may reduce <u>pain</u> or discomfort in patients with increased <u>sensitivity</u> to hot or spicy food.<br><br> |
| <br>Median rhomboid glossitis [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gAbFQw)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SAbyQw)<br> | <br>* Painless area of reddish discoloration located in the middle third of the <u>tongue</u>, <u>ventral</u> to the <u>foramen cecum</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Smoking<br><br>* Use of <u>corticosteroid</u> inhalers<br><br>* <u>HIV infection</u><br><br>* <u>Candida</u> infection<br><br><br> | <br>* Topical <u>antifungals</u> (e.g., <u>nystatin</u>, <u>fluconazole</u>, <u>clotrimazole</u>) in severe cases<br><br> |
| <br>Fissured tongue [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hAbcjw)<br> | <br><br>* One or more deep grooves located on the dorsum of the <u>tongue</u><br><br>* Possibly <u>halitosis</u> and discoloration if food gets trapped in the fissures and causes inflammation<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Associated with <u>Melkersson-Rosenthal syndrome</u><br><br>* <u>Psoriasis</u><br><br>* <u>Acromegaly</u><br><br>* <u>Down syndrome</u><br><br><br> | <br>* In case debris causes inflammation, it may be necessary to rinse or brush the affected area.<br><br> |
| <br>Black hairy tongue [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hAbcjw)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3AbSjw)<br> | <br>* Brown, black, or yellowish discoloration of the <u>tongue</u> with fuzzy appearance<br><br> | <br><br>* Smoking<br><br>* Coffee/tea consumption<br><br>* Poor oral hygiene<br><br>* <u>Penicillin</u> and <u>tetracycline</u> use<br><br>* Seen in patients on a <u>gastrostomy tube</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* Brushing affected area with toothbrush<br><br>* Practicing good oral hygiene<br><br>* Avoiding <u>alcohol</u> and tobacco<br><br><br> |
| White <u>tongue</u> | <br>* Temporary white or gray accumulation of debris on the dorsum of the <u>tongue</u><br><br> | <br>* Accumulation of dead cells, food residue, and bacteria, promoted by:<br><br>* Poor oral hygiene<br><br>* <u>Dehydration</u><br><br>* Mouth breathing<br><br>* Smoking, <u>alcohol</u> consumption<br><br> | <br><br>* Practicing good oral hygiene<br><br>* Avoiding <u>alcohol</u> and tobacco<br><br><br> |
| <u>Strawberry tongue</u> | <br>* Painful, bright red swollen <u>tongue</u> with <u>papillary</u> <u>hyperplasia</u> resembling a strawberry/raspberry<br><br> | <br>* <u>Scarlet fever</u><br><br> | <br>* <u>Treatment of scarlet fever</u><br><br>* Oral <u>penicillin V</u> or <u>amoxicillin</u><br><br>* In the case of <u>penicillin</u> <u>allergy</u>, <u>cephalosporins</u>, <u>macrolides</u>, or <u>clindamycin</u> can be used.<br><br> |
| <br>* <u>Kawasaki syndrome</u><br><br> | <br>* Treatment of <u>Kawasaki syndrome</u><br><br>* IVIG, high-dose<br><br>* Oral <u>aspirin</u><br><br>* IV <u>glucocorticoids</u><br><br> |  |  |
| <u>Tongue</u> <u>fasciculations</u> | <br>* Involuntary twitching of the <u>tongue</u><br><br> | <br>* <u>Lower motor neuron lesion</u> (e.g., <u>amyotrophic lateral sclerosis</u>)<br><br> | <br>* Treatment of the underlying condition<br><br> |
| <u>Telangiectasias</u> | <br>* Dilated small <u>superficial</u> <u>blood vessels</u> on the <u>tongue</u><br><br> | <br>* <u>Osler-Weber-Rendu syndrome</u><br><br> | <br>* No specific treatment for the <u>tongue</u> abnormality<br><br> |
| Bluish-black patches | <br>* Bluish-black discoloration of the <u>tongue</u> and <u>mucous membranes</u><br><br> | <br>* <u>Adrenal insufficiency</u><br><br> | <br>* Treatment of the underlying condition<br><br> |
| Macroglossia | <br><br>* Enlarged <u>tongue</u> with scalloped <u>lateral</u> margins<br><br>* May result in cosmetic and/or functional impairment<br><br>* <u>Dyspnea</u> and <u>obstructive sleep apnea</u><br><br>* <u>Dysphagia</u><br><br>* <u>Dysphonia</u><br><br>* <u>Sialorrhea</u><br><br><br> | <br><br>* Congenital disorders<br><br>* <u>Trisomy 21</u><br><br>* Muscular <u>hypertrophy</u> (e.g., <u>Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome</u>, <u>hemihyperplasia</u>)<br><br>* Vascular <u>malformations</u> (e.g., <u>lymphangioma</u> or <u>hemangioma</u>)<br><br>* <u>Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B</u><br><br>* <u>Glycogen storage disease</u> type 2<br><br>* <u>Neurofibromatosis</u><br><br>* Acquired conditions<br><br>* <u>Myxedema</u> and hypothyroid disorders<br><br>* <u>Amyloidosis</u><br><br>* <u>Acromegaly</u><br><br>* <u>Angioedema</u> (e.g., due to <u>ACE inhibitors</u>)<br><br>* Tumors (e.g., <u>squamous cell carcinoma</u> of the <u>tongue</u>)<br><br><br> | <br><br>* Mild cases: <u>speech therapy</u><br><br>* Severe cases: <u>surgery</u> (reduction glossectomy)<br><br>* Treatment of the underlying acquired cause (e.g., <u>radiotherapy</u> for tumors)<br><br><br> |
| Squamous cell papilloma | <br>* Solitary <u>exophytic</u> lesions with a <u>verrucous</u> surface<br><br> | <br>* <u>Human papillomavirus</u> (mostly <u>HPV</u>-6 and <u>HPV</u>-11)<br><br> | <br><br>* Surgical excision<br><br>* <u>Laser ablation</u><br><br><br> |
