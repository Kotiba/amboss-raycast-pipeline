# Cerumen impaction

## Summary

<u>Cerumen</u> impaction is a buildup of <u>cerumen</u> in the <u>external auditory canal</u> (<u>EAC</u>) that is symptomatic and/or prevents clinical evaluation of the auditory canal, <u>tympanic membrane</u> (TM), or the audiovestibular system. While anyone can develop <u>cerumen</u> impaction, <u>risk factors</u> include anatomical <u>ear</u> abnormalities, regular insertion of objects into <u>the ear</u> canal (including <u>hearing aids</u>), dermatological conditions (e.g., <u>eczema</u>, seborrheic dermatosis), and susceptible populations (i.e., young children, individuals with cognitive impairment, and older adults). Symptoms include localized <u>ear</u> discomfort, audiovestibular symptoms, and, in some individuals, nonspecific symptoms such as speech delay and behavioral changes. <u>Cerumen removal</u> is indicated for all individuals with <u>cerumen</u> impaction. Treatment options include <u>manual cerumen removal</u>, <u>EAC irrigation</u>, and <u>cerumenolytics</u>; all options are equally effective and may be used alone or in combination. Individuals should be assessed for <u>risk factors for cerumen removal complications</u>, with management tailored accordingly. Prevention involves patient education on <u>ear</u> hygiene; in individuals with recurrent <u>cerumen</u> impaction, <u>cerumen removal</u> regimens may be considered.
