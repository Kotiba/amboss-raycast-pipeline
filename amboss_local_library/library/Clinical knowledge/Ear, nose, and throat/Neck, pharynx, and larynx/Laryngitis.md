# Laryngitis

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Ear, nose, and throat > Neck, pharynx, and larynx > Laryngitis*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yj0d1T)

---

## Summary

Laryngitis is inflammation of the <u>larynx</u>. The main symptom is an altered voice (e.g., <u>hoarseness</u>). Acute laryngitis is commonly caused by viral <u>upper respiratory tract infection</u> (<u>URTI</u>) or vocal strain and is typically <u>self-limited</u>, with complete resolution within three weeks. Laryngitis is considered chronic if symptoms last longer than three weeks. Common <u>causes of chronic laryngitis</u> include gastroesophageal or <u>laryngopharyngeal reflux</u>, smoking, and <u>postnasal drip</u>. Acute laryngitis with no <u>red flags of hoarseness</u> is <u>diagnosed clinically</u> without any further evaluation, especially if the patient's history is consistent with viral <u>URTI</u> or vocal strain. Individuals with <u>red flags</u> of any duration and individuals with <u>dysphonia</u> and/or <u>hoarseness</u> that lasts for four weeks or longer require <u>laryngoscopy</u> to evaluate for alternative diagnoses. Further evaluation is guided by history, <u>physical examination</u>, and <u>laryngoscopy</u>. Laryngitis is treated with supportive measures (e.g., voice rest, hydration, and avoidance of <u>airway</u> irritants) and, in the case of chronic laryngitis, treatment of the underlying cause.

---

## Definitions

* Acute laryngitis: inflammation of the <u>larynx</u>, which contains the <u>vocal fold</u> <u>mucosa</u>, for < 3 weeks

* Chronic laryngitis: inflammation of the <u>larynx</u> for ≥ 3 weeks

## Etiology

### Etiology of acute laryngitis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rJWfvm0)




* Viral <u>URTIs</u> (most common cause) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* Bacterial <u>URTIs</u>

* <u>Superinfections</u> may occur following a viral infection.

* <u>Laryngeal diphtheria</u> (rare)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* Vocal strain, e.g., from <u>coughing</u> or overuse of <u>vocal cords</u>





### Etiology of chronic laryngitis [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)




* Most common: <u>Gastroesophageal reflux disease</u> (<u>GERD</u>) or <u>laryngopharyngeal reflux</u> (<u>LPR</u>) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xlWE_l0)

* Smoking

* Adverse effects of certain medications  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)

* <u>Postnasal drip</u>, e.g., from recurrent <u>URTIs</u> or <u>allergic rhinitis</u>

* Chronic infections of the <u>larynx</u> (e.g., <u>fungal infections</u>, <u>tuberculosis</u>)  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Systemic autoimmune diseases (rare) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* <u>Granulomatosis with polyangiitis</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kNWmYN0)

* <u>Rheumatoid arthritis</u>

* <u>Sarcoidosis</u>

## Clinical features

* Voice changes: <u>dysphonia</u>, <u>hoarseness</u>, loss of voice [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Sore or dry throat [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)

* Additional symptoms depend on the underlying cause. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)

* <u>Fever</u>: viral <u>URTIs</u>, bacterial infections [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* <u>Cough</u>: viral <u>URTIs</u>, <u>allergic rhinitis</u>, <u>airway</u> irritants

* <u>Enlarged lymph nodes</u>: infection

* <u>Odynophagia</u>: <u>GERD</u>, <u>LPR</u>, <u>URTI</u>

* Throat clearing: <u>postnasal drip</u>, <u>GERD</u>, <u>LPR</u>, <u>inhaled corticosteroids</u>

* <u>Globus sensation</u>: <u>GERD</u>, <u>LPR</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AlWRzl0)

## Management

### Approach




* Initiate immediate <u>airway management</u> in patients with <u>clinical features of airway obstruction</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* Obtain a thorough <u>medical history</u> and <u>physical examination</u>.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* If history and/or <u>physical examination</u> suggest a specific underlying cause, order appropriate tests, as indicated.

* Urgently refer patients to otolaryngology for <u>laryngoscopy</u> for any of the following:

* <u>Red flags of hoarseness</u>

* <u>Hoarseness</u> and/or <u>dysphonia</u> ≥ 4 weeks

* Professional voice users, e.g., professional singers, teachers  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Recommend <u>supportive care for laryngitis</u> to all patients.

* Initiate <u>management of acute laryngitis</u> or <u>management of chronic laryngitis</u> as appropriate.

* Follow-up; if symptoms do not improve, consider:

* Referral to otolaryngology

* <u>Diagnostic studies for laryngitis</u>

* Reevaluating for <u>differential diagnoses of laryngitis</u>





> [!TIP]
> The majority of patients with acute laryngitis can be <u>diagnosed clinically</u> and do not require diagnostic studies. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)




### Supportive care for laryngitis




* Vocal rest: Avoid shouting, using a loud voice, and forced whispering.

* <u>Analgesia</u> as needed

* <u>Mucosal</u> hydration

* Drink water.

* Increase <u>humidity</u> (e.g., cool mist humidifier) in dry environments.

* Avoid medications that dry the <u>airway</u> <u>mucosa</u>.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Avoid exposure to <u>airway</u> irritants, e.g., via <u>smoking cessation</u>.





### Management of acute laryngitis [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)




* Acute laryngitis is primarily a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>; consider diagnostic studies only: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* If certain bacterial infections are suspected  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HJWKvm0)

* To exclude <u>differential diagnoses of laryngitis</u>

* Typically <u>self-limited</u> and resolves in 1–3 weeks with <u>supportive care</u> only  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HJWKvm0)

* Follow up within a few weeks to ensure resolution.





> [!TIP]
> <u>Antibiotics</u> are ineffective in improving symptoms in adults with acute laryngitis. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HJWKvm0)




### Management of chronic laryngitis [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)




* Treat the underlying cause.

* Consider changing offending medications (e.g., <u>ACE inhibitors</u>, <u>inhaled corticosteroids</u>).

* For all other <u>causes of chronic laryngitis</u>, confirm the diagnosis before initiating treatment, e.g.:

* Suspected <u>GERD</u> or <u>LPR</u>:

* Perform <u>laryngoscopy</u> to look for evidence of reflux.

* Start <u>GERD treatment</u> with <u>acid suppression medications</u>.

* <u>Allergic rhinitis</u>:

* Perform <u>diagnostics for allergic rhinitis</u>.

* If diagnosis is confirmed, initiate <u>management of allergic rhinitis</u>.

* Consider referral to speech pathology for adjunctive voice therapy.

* Refer patients with <u>hoarseness</u> and/or <u>dysphonia</u> lasting ≥ 4 weeks for <u>laryngoscopy</u>.

* Reassess response to treatment at appropriate intervals.





> [!WARNING]
> Do not initiate treatment with <u>proton pump inhibitors</u>, <u>antibiotics</u>, or <u>corticosteroids</u> without first confirming the <u>cause of chronic laryngitis</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)




### Diagnostic studies for laryngitis




Diagnostic studies are usually performed by otolaryngology.




#### <u>Laryngoscopy</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)




* <u>Laryngoscopy</u> is used to assess for characteristic changes of laryngitis.

* <u>Biopsy</u> can be performed on lesions or for tissue culture.  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VqWGxm0)

* Consider laryngeal swabs in chronic laryngitis. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VqWGxm0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eqWxxm0)

* Minimally invasive and easy to obtain

* High <u>false negative rate</u> [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/VqWGxm0)





##### Indications




* Red flags of hoarseness, e.g.: [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* <u>Signs of respiratory distress</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>, and/or <u>stridor</u>

* Laryngeal trauma, e.g., recent <u>endotracheal intubation</u> or <u>surgery</u> of the head, neck, or chest  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* History of neck radiation [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* Neck mass

* History of <u>tobacco use disorder</u>

* Neurological symptoms, e.g., <u>dysarthria</u>

* <u>Dysphagia</u>

* <u>Unintentional weight loss</u>

* <u>Hoarseness</u> and/or <u>dysphonia</u> lasting ≥ 4 weeks

* <u>Hoarseness</u> in professional voice users, e.g., professional singers, teachers  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Suspected <u>GERD</u> or <u>LPR</u>

* Diagnostic uncertainty





> [!TIP]
> Children with <u>inspiratory stridor</u> consistent with typical <u>croup</u> do not require <u>laryngoscopy</u> or referral to otolaryngology. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pd1LIf0)




##### Modalities




* <u>Mirror laryngoscopy</u>

* <u>Fiberoptic laryngoscopy</u>

* <u>Direct laryngoscopy</u>





##### Potential findings in laryngitis [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AlWRzl0)




* Laryngeal inflammation with <u>erythema</u> and <u>edema</u>, including <u>hyperemia</u> of the inflamed <u>vocal folds</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BlWz_l0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Additional features include:

* Mucus: in <u>LPR</u>, <u>allergic rhinitis</u> [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_lW5zl0)

* <u>Pus</u>: in bacterial infections [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)

* <u>Posterior</u> commissure <u>hypertrophy</u>: in <u>GERD</u> and <u>LPR</u>

* <u>Ulcerations</u>, <u>nodules</u>, and <u>granulomas</u>: in <u>GERD</u>, <u>LPR</u>, and chronic tuberculous laryngitis [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JCXsGZ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Cobblestone appearance</u> of the <u>posterior</u> pharyngeal wall: in <u>GERD</u>, <u>LPR</u>, and <u>allergic rhinitis</u> [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-mWDRN0)





#### Imaging [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/zmWrRN0)




* Indications

* Inconclusive <u>laryngoscopy</u>

* <u>Laryngoscopy</u> finding of <u>vocal cord paralysis</u> or laryngeal mass

* Modalities: <u>contrast-enhanced CT</u> or <u>MRI</u>  [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/17W2k50)[[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W7WPk50)

## Differential diagnoses

* <u>Upper airway foreign body aspiration</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hHccJd0)

* Trauma: <u>endotracheal intubation</u>, injury, <u>hematoma</u>

* Acute infection of: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Subglottic</u> structures: <u>laryngotracheitis</u> (<u>croup</u>)

* <u>Supraglottic</u> structures: <u>epiglottitis</u>, <u>pharyngitis</u>

* <u>Retropharyngeal abscess</u>

* <u>Inhalation injury</u>

* Differential diagnoses of hoarseness:

* <u>Laryngeal cancer</u>

* <u>Benign laryngeal lesions</u>, e.g., polyp, cyst, <u>laryngeal papillomatosis</u>

* <u>Spasmodic dysphonia</u>

* <u>Vocal fold paralysis</u>

* Neuromuscular and neurological diseases, e.g., <u>Parkinson disease</u>, <u>myasthenia gravis</u>

* See also “<u>Differential diagnoses of stridor</u>.”




The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.
