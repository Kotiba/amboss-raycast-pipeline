## Summary

Anal stenosis is a narrowing of the <u>anal canal</u>, typically caused by fibrous <u>scar</u> tissue. It develops in 6% of patients within a year following surgical <u>hemorrhoidectomy</u>. Other causes include <u>inflammatory bowel disease</u> (<u>IBD</u>), <u>anal cancer</u>, infections, <u>radiation therapy</u>, or trauma. Key clinical features include painful or difficult defecation, <u>constipation</u>, <u>overflow diarrhea</u>, and narrow stool caliber. Diagnosis is primarily based on history and <u>physical examination</u>, with <u>biopsy</u> or imaging reserved for suspected <u>malignancy</u> or <u>IBD</u>. Treatment ranges from symptom management with dietary changes and <u>laxatives</u> to anal dilation and surgical therapy such as sphincterotomy or anoplasty. In refractory cases, fecal diversion may be necessary. Prevention during anorectal <u>surgery</u> is crucial and relies on preserving viable <u>anoderm</u> tissue.
