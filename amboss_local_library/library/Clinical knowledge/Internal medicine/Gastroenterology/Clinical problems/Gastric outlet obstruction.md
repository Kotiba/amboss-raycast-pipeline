# Gastric outlet obstruction

## Summary

Gastric <u>outlet obstruction</u> (GOO) is a mechanical blockage at the <u>pylorus</u> or <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u> that prevents normal gastric emptying. Malignancies such as <u>pancreatic</u> and <u>gastric cancer</u> are the most common causes, while <u>peptic ulcer disease</u> (<u>PUD</u>) is the leading benign etiology. GOO manifests with <u>postprandial</u> nonbilious <u>vomiting</u>, early satiety, epigastric <u>pain</u>, and weight loss. A characteristic <u>physical examination</u> finding is a <u>succussion splash</u> after ≥ 3 hours of <u>fasting</u>. Diagnostic evaluation includes <u>cross-sectional imaging</u> (e.g., <u>contrast-enhanced CT</u>) and <u>esophagogastroduodenoscopy</u> (<u>EGD</u>). Initial management focuses on <u>fluid resuscitation</u>, <u>electrolyte repletion</u>, and <u>gastric decompression</u> using a <u>nasogastric tube</u>. Subsequent management is cause-specific and may include endoscopic therapy or surgical bypass (e.g., <u>gastrojejunostomy</u>).

## Etiology

### Malignant [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZX1Z920)




* <u>Pancreatic cancer</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZX1Z920)

* <u>Gastric cancer</u>

* <u>Duodenal</u> cancer

* <u>Biliary tract cancer</u> (e.g., <u>cholangiocarcinoma</u>, <u>ampullary cancer</u>)

* <u>Lymphoma</u>

* <u>Metastases</u> (e.g., from <u>colon</u> or <u>ovarian cancer</u>)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Malignancy</u> (e.g., <u>pancreatic cancer</u>, <u>gastric cancer</u>) is the most common cause of GOO.




### Benign [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZX1Z920)




* <u>PUD</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZX1Z920)

* Strictures due to:

* <u>Chronic pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Crohn disease</u>

* <u>Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs</u> (<u>NSAIDs</u>)

* <u>Caustic ingestion</u>

* Postsurgical scarring or adhesions

* Radiation

* Mechanical obstruction (intraluminal or extrinsic) due to:

* <u>Acute pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Pancreatic pseudocyst</u>

* <u>Gallstone</u> obstruction (<u>Bouveret syndrome</u>)

* <u>Gastric bezoar</u>

* Foreign bodies

* Gastrointestinal polyps or <u>benign tumors</u> (e.g., <u>gastric leiomyoma</u>)

* Gastic <u>volvulus</u>

* <u>Eosinophilic gastritis</u>

* <u>Ladd band</u>

* <u>Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis</u>

* <u>Tuberculosis</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>PUD</u> may cause GOO via two mechanisms: inflammation and <u>edema</u> in acute <u>ulcers</u>, and scarring and <u>fibrosis</u> from chronic <u>ulcers</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LOYwG6)

## Pathophysiology

Inflammation, <u>edema</u>, scarring, <u>fibrosis</u> (e.g., from <u>PUD</u>), or luminal encroachment/extrinsic compression (e.g., from <u>malignancy</u>) → mechanical or functional obstruction at the <u>distal</u> <u>stomach</u>, <u>pylorus</u>, or <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u> → failed or delayed passage of gastric contents → gastric distention resulting in <u>nausea</u>, nonbilious <u>vomiting</u>, early satiety, and weight loss

## Clinical evaluation

### Focused history [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




* Symptoms that suggest GOO

* <u>Postprandial</u> <u>nausea</u>, nonbilious <u>vomiting</u>

* Early satiety

* Epigastric <u>pain</u>

* <u>Bloating</u> or abdominal distention

* <u>Constitutional symptoms</u> (e.g., weight loss)

* Medications (e.g., <u>NSAIDs</u>)

* <u>Past medical history</u>: <u>PUD</u>, <u>gallstone disease</u>, prior <u>malignancy</u>





### Focused examination [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




* General examination: <u>signs of significant dehydration</u> and <u>malnutrition</u>

* <u>Abdominal examination</u>

* Succussion splash: a splashing sound created by the movement of gastric contents

* Elicited by placing a stethoscope over the epigastrium of a patient in the <u>supine position</u> and gently rocking the upper abdomen

* Physiological immediately after a meal; pathological if present after ≥ 3 hours of <u>fasting</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)

* Palpable abdominal mass or <u>lymphadenopathy</u>





> [!TIP]
> GOO should be presumed malignant until proven otherwise. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)

## Diagnostics

### Imaging [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




* <u>Abdominal x-ray</u>

* Initial screening modality for suspected <u>intestinal obstruction</u>

* Findings

* Enlargement of the gastric bubble

* Dilatation of the <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u>

* Reduced <u>distal</u> bowel gas

* <u>Cross-sectional imaging</u> (e.g., <u>contrast-enhanced CT</u> or <u>MRI</u>)

* Preferred imaging modality to evaluate <u>intestinal obstruction</u> and identify the underlying cause

* Findings

* Gastric distention with retained contents

* Transition point at <u>pylorus</u> or <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u>

* Features of the underlying cause (e.g., infiltrative mass in malignant GOO)

* <u>Upper gastrointestinal series</u>

* Targeted study to localize the level of obstruction and assess gastric emptying

* Findings

* <u>Delayed gastric emptying</u>

* Narrowed or irregular <u>pylorus</u> or <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u>

* Partial or complete obstruction

* Avoid oral contrast if <u>EGD</u> is planned, as it may interfere with the procedure.





### <u>EGD</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




* Preferred modality to confirm the diagnosis

* Allows for direct visualization of the gastric outlet and enables <u>biopsies</u> of suspicious lesions

* Findings

* Retained gastric contents

* Narrowed <u>pylorus</u> or <u>proximal</u> <u>duodenum</u> with difficulty advancing the endoscope

* Features of the underlying cause (e.g., <u>edema</u>, <u>ulceration</u>, scarring, mass lesion)

* <u>Endoscopic ultrasound</u> (<u>EUS</u>): assists in evaluating the underlying cause and obtaining <u>biopsies</u>





> [!TIP]
> A <u>nasogastric tube</u> should be inserted before <u>EGD</u> to empty the <u>stomach</u> and reduce <u>aspiration</u> risk. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LhVwe80)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gb1FG20)




Nonspecific findings may support the diagnosis.




* <u>BMP</u>: <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities (e.g., <u>hypokalemic</u>, <u>hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis</u> from <u>vomiting</u>)

* Decreased <u>albumin</u>
