## Summary

Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD), also referred to as upper endoscopy, is a procedure in which a flexible fiber-optic endoscope is passed through the mouth and <u>oropharynx</u> to visualize the <u>mucosa</u> of the <u>esophagus</u>, <u>stomach</u>, and, sometimes, the <u>duodenum</u>. It is commonly used to diagnose and manage upper gastrointestinal (GI) disorders, monitor precancerous syndromes (e.g., <u>Barrett esophagus</u>), and guide endoscopic percutaneous <u>feeding tube</u> placement. Contraindications include <u>GI perforation</u> and postsurgical <u>upper GI bleeding</u> (<u>UGIB</u>). Complications include bleeding, <u>esophageal perforation</u>, and infection (e.g., <u>bacteremia</u>, <u>aspiration pneumonia</u>).
