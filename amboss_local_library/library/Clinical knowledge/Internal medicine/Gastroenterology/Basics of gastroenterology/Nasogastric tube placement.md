## Summary

Nasogastric (NG) tube placement is a common procedure in which a flexible tube is inserted through the nose into the <u>stomach</u>. It is performed for many indications, including <u>ileus</u>, <u>gastric bleeding</u>, <u>bowel obstruction</u>, and <u>enteral feeding</u>. Multiple types of NG tubes exist and the appropriate size varies based on age. Before insertion, steps should be taken to ease patient discomfort and ensure the correct length of tube is used. Proper NG tube placement can be confirmed at bedside via <u>auscultation</u> and <u>aspiration</u> and/or using radiographic confirmation. Complications include <u>iatrogenic</u> injury (e.g., <u>esophageal perforation</u>, bronchial perforation, <u>pneumothorax</u>), <u>epistaxis</u>, and <u>gastric bleeding</u>.
