## Summary

An internal <u>hernia</u> is the protrusion of <u>visceral</u> contents through a congenital or acquired defect in the <u>peritoneum</u> or <u>mesentery</u> within the <u>abdominal cavity</u>. Internal <u>hernias</u> have an <u>incidence</u> of < 1% and are significantly less common than external <u>hernias</u>. Patients with a history of <u>Roux-en-Y</u> <u>gastric bypass</u> or <u>liver transplant</u> are especially at risk of internal <u>hernia</u> formation. <u>Small bowel</u> loops are the most common content of an internal <u>hernia</u>. For this reason, the typical clinical presentation is that of a mechanical <u>small bowel obstruction</u> (i.e., colicky abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>constipation</u>, abdominal distention). <u>Contrast-enhanced CT scan</u> is the imaging modality of choice in most cases, but surgical intervention is often required for definitive diagnosis and treatment. Incarceration or strangulation of internal <u>hernias</u> carries a high <u>mortality rate</u>; rapid diagnosis and surgical repair is therefore imperative.
