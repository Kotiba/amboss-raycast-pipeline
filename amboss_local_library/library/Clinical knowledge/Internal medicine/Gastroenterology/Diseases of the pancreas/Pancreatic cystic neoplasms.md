# Pancreatic cystic neoplasms

## Summary

<u>Pancreatic</u> cystic <u>neoplasms</u> are a group of tumors with varying malignant potential. <u>Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms</u> (<u>IPMNs</u>) are <u>mucin</u>-producing tumors with malignant potential that arise from the <u>pancreatic duct</u> <u>epithelium</u>; malignant potential varies. <u>Mucinous cystic neoplasms</u> (<u>MCNs</u>) are also <u>mucin</u>-secreting tumors with malignant potential that almost exclusively affect women > 40 years of age and are defined by a subepithelial ovarian-type stroma. <u>Serous cystadenomas</u> are <u>benign tumors</u> with an extremely low risk of malignant transformation that are also more common in women. <u>Pancreatic</u> cystic <u>neoplasms</u> are often found incidentally. Diagnosis involves imaging (e.g., <u>MRI</u>, <u>MRCP</u>) and <u>endoscopic ultrasound</u> (<u>EUS</u>) with <u>fine-needle aspiration</u> (<u>FNA</u>) for cyst fluid analysis; elevated <u>CEA</u> levels suggest a <u>mucinous</u> cyst (e.g., an <u>IPMN</u> or <u>MCN</u>). Management is based on the type of <u>neoplasm</u>. Surgical resection is recommended for branch duct <u>IPMNs</u> with high-risk features, main duct and mixed-type <u>IPMNs</u>, and often for <u>MCNs</u>. Postoperative surveillance is required for resected <u>IPMNs</u> due to the risk of recurrence and concomitant <u>pancreatic cancer</u>. Asymptomatic <u>serous cystadenomas</u> typically do not require treatment.
