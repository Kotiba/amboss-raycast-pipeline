## Summary

A <u>biliary stricture</u> is an abnormal narrowing of the <u>bile</u> ducts that can obstruct <u>bile</u> flow. Etiologies are broadly categorized as malignant (e.g., <u>pancreatic cancer</u>, <u>cholangiocarcinoma</u>) or benign (e.g., postsurgical changes, <u>chronic pancreatitis</u>, <u>primary sclerosing cholangitis</u>). Patients may be initially asymptomatic or present with symptoms such as <u>jaundice</u>, <u>pruritus</u>, fatigue, or symptoms of <u>acute cholangitis</u>. Diagnosis includes findings of <u>cholestasis</u>, <u>biliary strictures</u> and/or obstruction, and histopathologic confirmation. Management focuses on biliary drainage to alleviate symptoms and facilitate further treatment.




<u>Secondary sclerosing cholangitis</u> is a chronic <u>cholestatic</u> biliary disease characterized by <u>biliary strictures</u> resulting from identifiable causes, which can lead to biliary <u>cirrhosis</u>.




<u>Primary sclerosing cholangitis</u> is discussed separately.
