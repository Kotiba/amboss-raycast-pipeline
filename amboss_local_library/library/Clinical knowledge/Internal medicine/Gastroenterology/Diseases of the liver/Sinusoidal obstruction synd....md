## Summary

Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS), also known as hepatic veno-occlusive disease (VOD), is a serious and potentially fatal complication most commonly seen after <u>hematopoietic cell transplantation</u> (HCT). This condition is characterized by hepatic <u>venule</u> obstruction that leads to sinusoidal congestion and hepatic <u>ischemia</u>. <u>Fulminant liver failure</u> may occur in severe cases. Key causes include <u>allogeneic HCT</u> and <u>myeloablative</u> <u>conditioning</u> regimens. Pre-existing <u>liver</u> disease is a <u>risk factor</u>. The classic clinical presentation is a triad consisting of painful <u>hepatomegaly</u>, <u>jaundice</u>, and fluid retention with weight gain, typically appearing within 21 days of HCT. Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on established criteria, and is supported by laboratory findings (e.g., <u>hyperbilirubinemia</u>) and imaging studies (e.g., <u>Doppler ultrasound</u>). Management focuses on <u>supportive care</u>, including careful <u>fluid balance</u> and avoidance of <u>hepatotoxic</u> agents. For moderate to severe cases, <u>defibrotide</u> is the <u>standard of care</u>. Prophylaxis with <u>ursodeoxycholic acid</u> is recommended for all patients undergoing HCT. The prognosis is highly dependent on the severity of the disease and the timeliness of intervention, with severe cases involving <u>multi-organ dysfunction</u> carrying a <u>mortality rate</u> exceeding 80%.

## Clinical features

* Typical presentation [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UYVboG0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TYV6oG0)

* <u>Hepatomegaly</u>

* <u>Right upper quadrant</u> <u>pain</u>

* <u>Jaundice</u> (due to <u>hyperbilirubinemia</u>)

* Weight gain (due to fluid retention)

* <u>Ascites</u>

* Timing of onset [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mbVV8G0)

* Classical SOS/VOD: occurs ≤ 21 days after HCT [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2YVToG0)

* Late-onset SOS/VOD: occurs > 21 days after HCT

## Diagnosis

The diagnosis is based on clinical criteria, supported by laboratory and imaging findings, after excluding other causes of <u>liver</u> dysfunction.




### Supportive diagnostic studies [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mbVV8G0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)




#### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




* Total <u>bilirubin</u> ≥ 2 mg/dL [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)

* Other biomarkers that may support the diagnosis include:

* ↑ <u>D-dimer</u>

* ↓ <u>Antithrombin III</u>

* ↑ <u>von Willebrand factor</u>





#### Imaging




* <u>Doppler ultrasound</u> findings may include:

* Reduction or reversal of <u>portal venous</u> flow

* Monophasic or attenuated hepatic venous waveforms

* Increased portal pulsatility

* Increased hepatic <u>artery</u> resistance index (> 0.8) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)

* <u>Hepatomegaly</u> and/or <u>splenomegaly</u>

* <u>Ascites</u>

* Diffuse hepatic <u>hyperechogenicity</u>

* Elastography: <u>Liver</u> stiffness measurement can be used to assess for hepatic congestion.





#### <u>Biopsy</u>




* Transjugular <u>liver biopsy</u> is the <u>gold standard</u> for diagnosis.

* Rarely performed due to the high risk of bleeding





### Diagnostic criteria [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mbVV8G0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)




#### Probable SOS/VOD [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mbVV8G0)




Diagnosed if ≥ 2 of the following criteria are present:




* Total <u>bilirubin</u> ≥ 2 mg/dL

* Painful <u>hepatomegaly</u>

* Weight gain > 5% from baseline

* <u>Ascites</u>

* <u>Ultrasound</u> and/or elastography findings that suggest SOS/VOD





#### Clinical SOS/VOD [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mbVV8G0)




Diagnosed in patients with <u>bilirubin</u> ≥ 2 mg/dL and at least two of the following:




* Painful <u>hepatomegaly</u>

* Weight gain > 5%

* <u>Ascites</u>





#### Proven SOS/VOD




Diagnosis is confirmed with at least one of the following:




* Histological confirmation via <u>biopsy</u>

* <u>Hepatic venous pressure gradient</u> ≥ 10 mm Hg

## Differential diagnoses

* <u>Budd-Chiari syndrome</u>

* Isolated acute hepatic <u>graft-versus-host disease</u> (GVHD)

* Other <u>causes of acute liver failure</u>

* Hepatic fungal infiltration

* <u>Acute viral hepatitis</u>

* <u>Cholangitis lenta</u>

* Drug-induced <u>liver</u> disease

* <u>Constrictive pericarditis</u> and right <u>congestive heart failure</u>

* Persistent <u>tumor</u> infiltration of the <u>liver</u>

* <u>Parenteral nutrition</u>-associated <u>liver</u> injury

* <u>Hemolysis</u>




The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.

## Management

### Pharmacotherapy [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)




* Agent: <u>Defibrotide</u> DOSAGE

* Indications [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UYVboG0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fYVkoG0)

* <u>Standard of care</u> for severe or very severe SOS/VOD

* Consider for patients with moderate SOS/VOD.

* Early administration is crucial to improve outcomes.

* Duration: at least 21 days and until complete resolution of symptoms [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)

* Monitoring [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)

* Monitor for signs of bleeding.

* Monitor <u>coagulation parameters</u> (e.g., <u>prothrombin time</u>, <u>aPTT</u>, <u>fibrinogen</u>), <u>liver function</u>, and renal function.





### <u>Supportive care</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UYVboG0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fYVkoG0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)




* Careful management of fluid and <u>sodium</u> balance 

* Twice-daily weight monitoring

* <u>Sodium</u> restriction

* Strict monitoring of intake and output

* <u>Diuretics</u> for <u>fluid overload</u>

* Avoidance of <u>hepatotoxic</u> and <u>nephrotoxic medications</u>

* Symptom management

* <u>Opioid analgesics</u> for <u>pain</u>

* Drainage of <u>ascites</u> or <u>pleural effusions</u>

* Management of complications, e.g.:

* <u>Renal replacement therapy</u> (e.g., <u>hemodialysis</u>) for <u>renal failure</u>

* Early transfer to an <u>intensive care unit</u> for <u>multi-organ dysfunction</u> management

* <u>Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt</u> (<u>TIPS</u>) for portal pressure reduction

* Consider maintaining higher <u>transfusion</u> thresholds (e.g., <u>platelets</u> > 30,000/mm<sup>3</sup>, <u>hemoglobin</u> > 8.5 g/dL). [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/eYVxLG0)
