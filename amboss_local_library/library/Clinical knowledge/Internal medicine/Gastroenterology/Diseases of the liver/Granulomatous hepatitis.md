## Summary

Granulomatous <u>hepatitis</u> is a condition characterized by the presence of <u>liver</u> <u>granulomas</u> and is detected in 1–15% of <u>liver</u> <u>biopsies</u>. Granulomatous <u>hepatitis</u> is <u>idiopathic</u> in approx. 10–36% of patients. Nonidiopathic causes are broad and include infectious diseases (e.g., <u>tuberculosis</u>), systemic conditions (e.g., <u>sarcoidosis</u>), and drugs. In Western countries, granulomatous <u>hepatitis</u> is most commonly caused by noninfectious conditions; in Middle Eastern and Asian countries, it is most commonly caused by infectious diseases. Patients are typically asymptomatic, but nonspecific symptoms (e.g., <u>fever</u>, fatigue, abdominal <u>pain</u>) may be present. <u>Liver biopsy</u> is required for diagnostic confirmation and to identify <u>granuloma</u> characteristics. Management differs based on the underlying cause. Complications include <u>fibrosis</u>, <u>cirrhosis</u>, and <u>portal hypertension</u>.
