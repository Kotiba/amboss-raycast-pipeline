## Summary

Hepatolithiasis is the formation of biliary stones within the intrahepatic <u>bile</u> ducts. It is <u>endemic</u> in East and Southeast Asia but rare in Western countries. Hepatolithiasis typically affects individuals aged 30–70 years; <u>prevalence</u> is higher in women. The etiology is complex, involving a cycle of <u>cholestasis</u>, biliary infection, and stricture formation, with <u>risk factors</u> including anatomical abnormalities, infections, and metabolic factors. Symptoms include abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>fever</u>, and <u>jaundice</u>, but many patients are asymptomatic. Diagnosis primarily involves imaging: Abdominal <u>ultrasound</u> is the initial modality and <u>MRCP</u> provides more detailed findings. Management for symptomatic patients is multidisciplinary and aims to remove stones, resolve strictures, and prevent complications such as recurrent <u>acute cholangitis</u>, <u>liver abscess</u>, <u>secondary sclerosing cholangitis</u>, and intrahepatic <u>cholangiocarcinoma</u>. Treatment options range from endoscopic procedures and <u>lithotripsy</u> to surgical interventions such as hepatectomy and, in severe cases, <u>liver transplantation</u>. The prognosis is worsened by advanced age, prolonged <u>jaundice</u>, and the development of <u>cirrhosis</u> or <u>cholangiocarcinoma</u>.
