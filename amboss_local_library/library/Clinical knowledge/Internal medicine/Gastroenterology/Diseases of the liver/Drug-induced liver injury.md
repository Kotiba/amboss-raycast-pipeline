# Drug-induced liver injury

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Gastroenterology > Diseases of the liver > Drug-induced liver injury*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HF0Kj3)

---

## Summary

Drug-induced <u>liver</u> injury (DILI) is most commonly caused by <u>antibiotics</u>, <u>anticonvulsants</u>, over-the-counter medications (e.g., <u>NSAIDs</u>, <u>acetaminophen</u>), and herbal or dietary supplements. Patients with DILI may be asymptomatic or present with a range of symptoms, from <u>nausea</u> and fatigue to <u>signs of acute liver failure</u>. Diagnosis requires a thorough clinical assessment of current and prior medication use. <u>Laboratory studies</u> show <u>elevated liver enzymes</u> with a pattern of injury that can be determined as hepatocellular, <u>cholestatic</u>, or mixed, based on the <u>R-value for liver injury</u>. DILI is a diagnosis of exclusion; other diagnoses such as viral <u>hepatitis</u> or biliary disease must be ruled out with <u>laboratory studies</u> and abdominal imaging (e.g., <u>ultrasound</u>). Management primarily includes immediate discontinuation of the offending agent with symptomatic and adjunctive therapies (e.g., <u>N-acetylcysteine</u>) as indicated.

## Etiology

Common causative agents of DILI include: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m2WVRk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




* <u>Analgesics</u>: e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u> (in acute overdose or chronic overuse), <u>NSAIDs</u>

* Antimicrobials

* <u>Antibiotics</u>: e.g., <u>macrolides</u>, <u>trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole</u>, <u>nitrofurantoin</u>, <u>amoxicillin/clavulanate</u>, <u>fluoroquinolones</u>, <u>doxycycline</u>, <u>dapsone</u>, <u>cephalosporins</u>

* <u>Antituberculosis therapy</u>: e.g., <u>isoniazid</u>, <u>rifampin</u>, <u>pyrazinamide</u>

* <u>Antifungals</u>: e.g., <u>terbinafine</u>, <u>itraconazole</u>, <u>ketoconazole</u>

* Antiretrovirals: e.g., <u>efavirenz</u>, <u>abacavir</u>, <u>didanosine</u>

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u>: e.g., <u>valproic acid</u>, <u>carbamazepine</u>, <u>phenytoin</u>

* Cardiovascular medications: e.g., <u>statins</u>, <u>amiodarone</u>, <u>methyldopa</u>, <u>fibrates</u>, <u>labetalol</u>, <u>ACE inhibitors</u>

* Immune modulators: e.g., <u>methotrexate</u>, <u>leflunomide</u>, <u>azathioprine</u>, <u>sulfasalazine</u>, <u>immune checkpoint inhibitors</u> (<u>ICIs</u>), <u>interferon alpha</u> and <u>interferon beta</u>, <u>TNF inhibitors</u> (e.g., <u>etanercept</u>)

* Antithyroid medication: e.g., <u>propylthiouracil</u>, <u>methimazole</u>

* Other drugs: inhaled <u>anesthetics</u> (e.g., <u>halothane</u>), <u>disulfiram</u>, <u>allopurinol</u>, <u>anabolic steroids</u>, <u>danazol</u>, <u>tolcapone</u>, <u>dopamine antagonists</u> (e.g., <u>chlorpromazine</u>, <u>phenothiazine</u>)

* Herbal and dietary supplements : e.g., ashwagandha, green tea extract, Chinese <u>skullcap</u>, kratom, garcinia cambogia, Polygonum multiflorum





> [!TIP]
> Consult a comprehensive database of substances associated with <u>liver</u> injury if necessary (see “Tips & links”).

## Clinical features

Patients may be asymptomatic or develop symptoms within days to a year after drug exposure. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L2WwRk0)




* <u>Nausea</u>

* Fatigue

* Abdominal <u>pain</u>

* <u>Features associated with cholestasis</u> (e.g., <u>jaundice</u>, <u>pruritus</u>)

* <u>Signs of acute liver failure</u> (e.g., <u>ascites</u>, <u>encephalopathy</u>)

* Immunoallergic symptoms, e.g., <u>fever</u>, <u>rash</u> (see “<u>Nonimmediate drug hypersensitivity reactions</u>”)

## Diagnosis

DILI is diagnosed if <u>liver chemistry</u> findings are abnormal, there is a history of exposure to a causative medication, and alternative diagnoses have been excluded. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




### Approach [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m2WVRk0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KUWUVk0)




* Clinical assessment, including a detailed medication history

* Initial <u>laboratory studies</u> to confirm clinically significant <u>liver</u> injury, i.e., either:

* <u>AST</u> and/or <u>ALT</u> > 5× <u>ULN</u> and/or <u>ALP</u> > 2× <u>ULN</u> on 2 separate measurements

* Total <u>bilirubin</u> > 2.5 mg/dL and any elevation of <u>AST</u>, <u>ALT</u>, or <u>ALP</u>

* <u>INR</u> > 1.5 and any elevation of <u>AST</u>, <u>ALT</u>, or <u>ALP</u>

* Calculate the <u>R-value for liver injury</u>.

* Additional studies to rule out alternative diagnoses depending on the pattern of injury

* Consult hepatology if the cause remains unclear.





> [!TIP]
> A thorough medication history should include prescription and over-the-counter medications, herbal remedies, and dietary supplements, especially within the last 6 months.




### Initial <u>laboratory studies</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m2WVRk0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LfWw5k0)




* <u>Liver chemistries</u>: Findings depend on the pattern of injury.

* Normal or ↑ <u>ALT</u> and/or <u>AST</u>

* Normal or ↑ <u>ALP</u>

* Normal or ↑ <u>bilirubin</u>

* Normal or ↑ <u>albumin</u>

* <u>Coagulation panel</u>: normal or ↑<u>INR</u>





> [!TIP]
> Elevated serum <u>albumin</u> and elevated <u>INR</u> are signs of severe <u>liver</u> injury. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)




### Additional studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m2WVRk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




#### Additional <u>laboratory studies</u>




* Hepatocellular or mixed injury patterns 

* All patients

* <u>Viral hepatitis panel</u>

* <u>ANA</u>, <u>ASMA</u>: to evaluate for <u>autoimmune hepatitis</u>

* Select patients based on clinical suspicion

* <u>HSV</u>, <u>EBV</u>, and <u>CMV</u> <u>PCR</u>  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/L2WwRk0)

* Serum <u>ceruloplasmin</u>: to assess for <u>Wilson disease</u>

* <u>Iron studies</u>: to assess for <u>hemochromatosis</u>

* Serum and/or urine drug or toxin levels (e.g., <u>acetaminophen</u>)

* <u>α1-antitrypsin</u>: to evaluate for <u>AATD</u>

* <u>Cholestatic injury pattern</u>: <u>AMA</u> to assess for <u>primary biliary cholangitis</u>





#### Imaging [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/LfWw5k0)




* <u>Ultrasound</u> abdomen

* Obtain in all patients.

* Used to assess for <u>cirrhosis</u>, <u>biliary obstruction</u>, or other <u>parenchymal</u> focal lesions

* Advanced abdominal imaging (e.g., CT or <u>MRI</u> abdomen, <u>MRCP</u>): Consider based on clinical suspicion, especially in patients with a <u>cholestatic injury pattern</u>.





#### <u>Liver biopsy</u>




* Not routinely required

* May be considered to:

* Increase diagnostic certainty  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)

* Assess for alternative diagnoses, e.g., <u>autoimmune hepatitis</u>

## Treatment

For patients with <u>acute liver failure</u>, start treatment immediately: See “<u>Management of acute liver failure</u>.”




### General principles [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)




* Immediately discontinue the offending agent(s).

* Consider pharmacological treatment, e.g., <u>N-acetylcysteine</u> or <u>glucocorticoids</u>.

* Initiate inpatient care for patients with <u>dehydration</u>, <u>encephalopathy</u>, and/or <u>coagulopathy</u>.

* Provide symptomatic management as needed.

* Determine severity based on prognostic scores (e.g., <u>MELD</u>). 

* Consult hepatology for patients with severe disease.

* Refer to a transplant center if necessary.

* Report suspected cases of DILI to the <u>FDA</u> via MedWatch (see “Tips & Links”).





> [!TIP]
> Immediately stop any potential causative agents in patients with DILI.




### Drug-specific therapy [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m2WVRk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




Very few specific drugs provide a clear benefit.




* <u>Acetaminophen toxicity</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/xTWEGk0)

* Start <u>N-acetylcysteine</u>: IV <u>NAC protocol</u> DOSAGE or oral <u>NAC protocol</u> DOSAGE. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/drWoT50)

* Consider <u>single-dose activated charcoal</u> for <u>acute acetaminophen overdose</u>.

* <u>Valproate toxicity</u>: Consider L-carnitine in patients with <u>hyperammonemia</u>.

* <u>Leflunomide</u> toxicity: <u>Cholestyramine</u> may be considered in patients with persistent <u>cholestasis</u> to <u>enhance elimination</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Consider <u>N-acetylcysteine</u> for at least 3 days in all patients with <u>acute liver failure</u> related to DILI. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)




### <u>Glucocorticoids</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




Evidence on the use of <u>glucocorticoids</u> in DILI is sparse.




* May be considered in patients with, e.g.:

* Severe immune-mediated <u>drug hypersensitivity reactions</u> (e.g., <u>DRESS</u>)

* Findings of <u>autoimmunity</u>

* <u>Hepatotoxicity</u> due to <u>ICIs</u> or <u>tyrosine kinase inhibitors</u>

* Agents include:  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)

* <u>Methylprednisolone</u> DOSAGE for severe <u>ICI</u>-related DILI [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/v7WAn50)

* <u>Prednisone</u> DOSAGE for all other patients [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PTWWIk0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/l81v5i0)





### Symptomatic treatment




* <u>Management of nausea and vomiting</u>

* <u>Management of cholestasis-associated pruritus</u>

* <u>Management of dehydration</u>





### Follow up [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)




* Frequently check <u>liver chemistries</u> for improvement after drug discontinuation.

* Consider other causes if:

* Peak <u>ALT</u> declines < 50% in 30–60 days

* Peak <u>ALP</u> or <u>bilirubin</u> decline < 50% or < 2× <u>ULN</u> in 180 days





> [!TIP]
> Recovery from <u>cholestatic</u> DILI is typically slower than recovery from hepatocellular DILI. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/52WiRk0)
