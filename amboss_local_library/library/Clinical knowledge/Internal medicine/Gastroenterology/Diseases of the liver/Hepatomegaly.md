## Summary

Hepatomegaly is an abnormal enlargement of the <u>liver</u> (e.g., <u>liver</u> span > 16 cm in the <u>midclavicular line</u> on <u>ultrasound</u>). Causes are broad and include metabolic (e.g., <u>metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease</u>, <u>alcohol-associated liver disease</u>), infectious (e.g., viral <u>hepatitis</u>), congestive (e.g., <u>right-sided heart failure</u>), <u>neoplastic</u>, and infiltrative conditions. Patients may be asymptomatic or present with features of the underlying disorder (e.g., <u>right upper quadrant</u> discomfort, <u>jaundice</u>, or signs of <u>volume overload</u>). Initial evaluation includes history and <u>physical examination</u>, <u>liver chemistries</u> and <u>liver synthetic function tests</u>, and <u>RUQ</u> <u>ultrasound</u> to assess <u>liver</u> size and <u>parenchymal</u> appearance. Further testing (e.g., viral <u>serologies</u>, <u>iron studies</u>, advanced imaging) is guided by <u>risk factors</u> and initial results, while <u>biopsy</u> is reserved for unclear or high-risk cases. Management focuses on treating the underlying etiology.
