## Summary

Peliosis hepatis is a rare vascular condition that is characterized by multiple small, blood-filled cystic cavities within the <u>liver</u>. It is often <u>idiopathic</u> but can be caused by medications (e.g., <u>anabolic–androgenic steroids</u>, <u>oral contraceptives</u>), infections (especially <u>Bartonella henselae</u> in <u>immunocompromised individuals</u>), and malignancies. Most patients are asymptomatic, with the diagnosis frequently made based on an incidental finding on imaging. If symptoms occur, they are often nonspecific, such as upper abdominal <u>pain</u> or fatigue. Diagnosis can be challenging due to variable appearances on imaging, with <u>MRI</u> being the most sensitive modality. A definitive diagnosis often requires a <u>liver biopsy</u>, which has a high risk of bleeding. Management focuses on treating the underlying cause, which may lead to regression of the lesions. Interventional or surgical management is reserved for symptomatic patients or those with severe complications, such as spontaneous rupture and <u>hemoperitoneum</u>.
