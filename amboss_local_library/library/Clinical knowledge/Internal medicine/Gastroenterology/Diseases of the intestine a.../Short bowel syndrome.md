# Short bowel syndrome

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Gastroenterology > Diseases of the intestine and appendix > Short bowel syndrome*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/n907LR)

---

## Summary

Short bowel syndrome is a malabsorptive condition caused by the surgical removal of a significant portion of the <u>small intestine</u>. Clinical features include <u>chronic diarrhea</u>, <u>dehydration</u>, weight loss, and <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities. Diagnosis is confirmed by a comprehensive nutritional assessment in patients with an estimated residual <u>small bowel</u> length ≤ 200 cm. Nutritional management involves individualized dietary modifications, fluid management with <u>oral rehydration solutions</u>, and <u>specialized nutrition support</u>. Pharmacological treatments include <u>antidiarrheals</u>, <u>antisecretory agents</u>, and agents to support absorption (e.g., teduglutide). Complications can arise from the condition itself or from treatment; complications include catheter-related infections, <u>metabolic bone disease</u>, and <u>kidney stones</u>.

---

## Etiology

* Short bowel syndrome is caused by the surgical resection of a significant portion of the <u>small bowel</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gYVFoG0)

* Common underlying conditions leading to resection include: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gYVFoG0)

* <u>Mesenteric</u> <u>ischemia</u>

* <u>Crohn disease</u>

* <u>Malignancy</u>

---

## Clinical features

* Common signs and symptoms [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* Chronic <u>diarrhea</u>

* <u>Dehydration</u>

* <u>Electrolyte</u> abnormalities

* Weight loss

* Consequences of <u>terminal ileum</u> resection, e.g.: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency</u>

* <u>Bile acid diarrhea</u>

---

## Management

The <u>management of short bowel syndrome</u> is also applicable to patients with other causes of chronic <u>intestinal failure</u>.




* Multidisciplinary management, including intestinal <u>rehabilitation</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* Diet optimization under specialist guidance

* Frequent meals (e.g., 5–6 meals per day)

* Recommended diet depends on anatomy (e.g., whether the <u>colon</u> is intact).

* Consider <u>oral rehydration solutions</u>.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* <u>Total parenteral nutrition</u> may be necessary immediately after <u>bowel surgery</u> and in patients with <u>intestinal failure</u>.

* Pharmacological treatment [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* <u>Antidiarrheal agents</u> (e.g., <u>loperamide</u>, <u>diphenoxylate-atropine</u>, tincture of opium)

* <u>Antisecretory agents</u> (e.g., <u>PPIs</u>, H₂ <u>receptor</u> blockers, <u>octreotide</u>, <u>clonidine</u>)

* Agents to support absorption (e.g., <u>glucagon</u>-like <u>peptide</u> 2 analogs such as teduglutide)

* Monitoring for nutritional deficiencies and complications (e.g., <u>intestinal failure</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)

* Surgical management is reserved for advanced disease (e.g., <u>intestinal failure-associated liver disease</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uaVpmG0)

* <u>Autologous</u> gastrointestinal reconstructive <u>surgery</u>

* Intestinal <u>transplantation</u>





> [!TIP]
> Patients without a functional <u>colon</u> often lose large amounts of water, increasing the risk of <u>dehydration</u>, <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities, and <u>acute kidney injury</u>. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vSdAYp0)
