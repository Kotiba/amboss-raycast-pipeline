## Summary

<u>Small bowel</u> <u>adenocarcinoma</u> is a rare <u>malignancy</u> of the <u>gastrointestinal tract</u> that most commonly occurs in the <u>duodenum</u>. The median age at diagnosis is 66 years. <u>Risk factors</u> include <u>heavy alcohol use</u>, smoking, certain medical conditions (e.g., <u>IBD</u>, <u>celiac disease</u>), and <u>familial cancer syndromes</u> (e.g., <u>Lynch syndrome</u>). The clinical presentation is often nonspecific; symptoms include intermittent crampy abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>gastrointestinal bleeding</u>, and weight loss. Diagnosis requires a <u>biopsy</u>, which is typically obtained via endoscopy, and is supported by <u>laboratory studies</u> and <u>cross-sectional imaging</u>. Management for localized disease is primarily surgical resection, often followed by <u>adjuvant chemotherapy</u> based on the stage and molecular features. For unresectable or <u>metastatic</u> disease, treatment involves systemic therapy; the specific regimen is chosen based on <u>mismatch repair</u> or <u>microsatellite instability</u> status.
