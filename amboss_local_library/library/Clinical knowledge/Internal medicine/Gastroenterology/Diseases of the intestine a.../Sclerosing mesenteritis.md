## Summary

<u>Sclerosing</u> mesenteritis is an uncommon <u>idiopathic</u> autoimmune condition characterized by inflammation, <u>fat necrosis</u>, and <u>fibrosis</u> of the bowel <u>mesentery</u>. Many patients are asymptomatic; in symptomatic patients, abdominal <u>pain</u> is most common. The diagnosis is increasingly made incidentally via CT imaging, which characteristically shows intestine displaced by a hazy <u>mesenteric</u> fat mass, also known as “misty <u>mesentery</u>.” A presumptive diagnosis is often possible; <u>biopsy</u> is indicated if there are features concerning for <u>malignancy</u> or <u>IgG4-related disease</u>. Treatment of symptoms typically involves immunomodulatory drugs (e.g., <u>tamoxifen</u>, <u>glucocorticoids</u>). <u>Surgery</u> is reserved for patients with <u>bowel obstruction</u> and is not curative. Most patients show no disease progression after 2 years of follow-up.
