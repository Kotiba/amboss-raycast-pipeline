## Summary

Appendiceal cancer is a rare <u>malignancy</u> of the <u>appendix</u> that is most commonly discovered incidentally on <u>cross-sectional imaging</u> or after routine <u>appendectomy</u>. There are several distinct histological subtypes, including appendiceal <u>neuroendocrine tumors</u>, <u>mucinous</u> <u>adenocarcinoma</u>, <u>colonic</u>-type <u>adenocarcinoma</u>, and the more aggressive <u>goblet cell</u> and signet ring <u>adenocarcinomas</u>. While many patients are asymptomatic, clinical presentation ranges from <u>carcinoid syndrome</u> to <u>pseudomyxoma peritonei</u> from <u>mucinous</u> tumors with <u>peritoneal</u> spread. Diagnosis is confirmed by <u>histopathology</u>, and further workup involves cross-sectional CT or <u>MRI</u> imaging, <u>colonoscopy</u>, and serum <u>tumor markers</u> (e.g., <u>CEA</u>, <u>CA 19-9</u>, and <u>CA-125</u>). Management is guided by a multidisciplinary team and depends on the <u>tumor</u>'s <u>histology</u> and stage. Localized disease is typically treated surgically with an <u>appendectomy</u> or a <u>right hemicolectomy</u>, sometimes with <u>adjuvant chemotherapy</u>. For disease with <u>peritoneal</u> involvement, treatment often involves a combination of systemic <u>chemotherapy</u> and <u>cytoreductive surgery</u>.

## Appendiceal mucinous adenocarcinoma

### Definition



* A subtype of appendiceal <u>adenocarcinoma</u> characterized by infiltrative growth of invasive glands with > 50% of the lesion composed of extracellular <u>mucin</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8YVOIG0)




### <u>Epidemiology</u>



* Most common type of <u>epithelial</u> appendiceal <u>malignancy</u>, accounting for 35–40% of cases [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tYVXIG0)




### Clinical features




* Frequently asymptomatic [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tYVXIG0)

* Patients with <u>peritoneal</u> spread may present with <u>mucinous</u> <u>ascites</u> (e.g., <u>pseudomyxoma peritonei</u>). [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FYVgIG0)





### Management




* Localized disease: <u>appendectomy</u> alone or conservative resection (e.g., appendiceal cuff resection or partial cecectomy) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tYVXIG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8YVOIG0)

* <u>Metastatic</u> disease (e.g., <u>peritoneal</u> involvement): <u>cytoreductive surgery</u> with or without <u>hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FYVgIG0)
