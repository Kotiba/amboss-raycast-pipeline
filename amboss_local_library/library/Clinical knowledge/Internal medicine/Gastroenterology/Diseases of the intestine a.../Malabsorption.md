## Summary

Malabsorption is the impaired absorption of <u>nutrients</u> caused by functional or structural alterations of the intestinal <u>mucosa</u> or inadequate breakdown of food in the intestinal lumen (i.e., <u>maldigestion</u>). Malabsorption can lead to <u>malnutrition</u>, which, in children, can manifest with delayed and inadequate physical development. Adults typically present with <u>chronic diarrhea</u>, weight loss, <u>steatorrhea</u>, and signs of nutritional deficiencies. Diagnosis is primarily based on clinical evaluation. Diagnostic testing, including <u>laboratory studies</u>, is aimed at identifying the underlying cause, which is the focus of management, e.g., adherence to a <u>gluten</u>-free diet for patients with <u>celiac disease</u>.

## Definitions

* Malabsorption

* Impaired absorption of <u>nutrients</u> due to functional or structural alterations of the intestinal <u>mucosa</u>

* Often used as an umbrella term for both malabsorption and <u>maldigestion</u> in clinical practice

* Maldigestion: impaired breakdown of food in the intestinal lumen





> [!TIP]
> Malabsorption is commonly used as an umbrella term for both malabsorption and <u>maldigestion</u>, despite distinct underlying pathological mechanisms.

## Etiology

Disorders can cause malabsorption through multiple pathways simultaneously.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)




* <u>Mucosal</u> damage [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* <u>Celiac disease</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* Infections, e.g., <u>tropical sprue</u>, <u>Whipple disease</u>, <u>giardiasis</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* <u>Inflammatory bowel disease</u>, e.g., <u>Crohn disease</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)

* <u>Lactase deficiency</u>

* <u>Chronic radiation enteritis</u>

* <u>Chronic mesenteric ischemia</u>

* <u>Intestinal lymphangiectasia</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)

* <u>HIV enteropathy</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)

* <u>Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth</u> (<u>SIBO</u>)  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* <u>Short bowel syndrome</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* Hormonally active tumors (e.g., <u>gastrinoma</u>, <u>VIPoma</u>)

* <u>Maldigestion</u>

* <u>Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* <u>Bile acid</u> deficiency, due to:  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)

* Hepatobiliary disorders, e.g., <u>cholestasis</u>, <u>cirrhosis</u>, <u>primary sclerosing cholangitis</u>

* <u>Bile acid</u> malabsorption, e.g., after <u>ileum</u> resection or due to <u>Crohn disease</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Sncys10)

* Medications, e.g., <u>orlistat</u>, <u>acarbose</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7Mc4J10)

* Gastric resection  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qSdC0p0)





> [!TIP]
> Malabsorption can be caused by insufficient <u>nutrient</u> absorption and/or insufficient breakdown of <u>nutrients</u>.

## Subtypes and variants

"<u>Short bowel syndrome</u>" is described in detail in a separate article.




### Carbohydrate malabsorption [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qidCsp0)




* Definition: the inability to efficiently absorb nutritional <u>carbohydrates</u> (e.g., <u>lactose</u>, fructose, <u>sorbitol</u>) in the <u>small intestine</u>, either due to primary (e.g., <u>lactase deficiency</u>) or secondary causes (e.g., <u>SIBO</u>, <u>Crohn disease</u>)

* Symptoms can often be managed by removing the specific type of <u>carbohydrate</u> from the diet.

* See also:

* <u>Lactose intolerance</u> (common)

* <u>Fructose malabsorption</u>, including <u>hereditary fructose intolerance</u>





### Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uSdpYp0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)




* Definition: the inability of the <u>pancreas</u> to release <u>pancreatic enzymes</u> necessary for digestion, including <u>amylase</u>, <u>lipases</u>, and <u>proteases</u> (e.g., <u>trypsinogen</u>, chymotrypsinogen, <u>elastase</u>), which can lead to malabsorption of fats, <u>fat-soluble vitamins</u> (A, D, E, K), <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>, <u>carbohydrates</u>, <u>proteins</u>, <u>folate</u>, <u>thiamine</u>, <u>zinc</u>, <u>calcium</u>, and <u>magnesium</u>

* Etiology

* <u>Chronic pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Cystic fibrosis</u>

* Obstruction, e.g., due to <u>pancreatic cancer</u>

* <u>Pancreatic resection</u>

* Severe <u>necrotizing pancreatitis</u>

* Shwachman-Diamond syndrome: a rare <u>autosomal recessive</u> condition and the second most common cause of <u>exocrine pancreatic insufficiency</u> in children, after <u>cystic fibrosis</u>

* Clinical features of exocrine pancreatic insufficiency

* <u>Steatorrhea</u> (with or without <u>diarrhea</u>)

* Weight loss

* <u>Secondary protein-energy malnutrition</u>

* Signs of nutritional deficiencies

* Diagnostics [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* <u>Stool elastase</u> test (preferred initial test): ↓ <u>fecal elastase-1</u> (< 100 mcg/g)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* Consider abdominal imaging (CT, <u>MRI</u>, or <u>endoscopic ultrasound</u>) to evaluate for <u>pancreatic</u> disease.

* Additional supportive findings include:

* ↓ <u>Bicarbonate</u> and ↓ <u>pH</u> in the <u>duodenum</u>

* If performed, normal <u>D-xylose absorption test</u>

* Management [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uSdpYp0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* Prescribe <u>pancreatic enzyme</u> replacement therapy with food, e.g., <u>pancrelipase</u> DOSAGE; adjust as needed. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* <u>H<sub>2</sub> receptor blocker</u> or <u>PPI</u> for patients receiving non-<u>enteric-coated</u> <u>pancreatic enzyme</u> replacement therapy  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* <u>Fat-soluble vitamin</u> supplementation [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* Dietary modifications (e.g., high-protein diet) under specialist guidance [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FSdgYp0)

* Monitoring for nutritional deficiencies and complications of deficiencies

## Clinical features

### General symptoms




Symptoms vary based on the cause and severity of the condition but are often mild and nonspecific.




* <u>Chronic diarrhea</u> (most common)

* <u>Steatorrhea</u>

* Weight loss

* Fatigue

* Abdominal distention, flatulence

* Additional symptoms in children include delayed and inadequate physical development.





### Signs of nutritional deficiencies




* <u>Vitamin deficiency</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Clinical features of vitamin D deficiency</u>, e.g., <u>osteoporosis</u>

* <u>Clinical features of vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency</u>, e.g., fatigue, <u>pallor</u>, <u>peripheral neuropathy</u>

* See “<u>Water-soluble vitamins</u>” and “<u>Fat-soluble vitamins</u>” for an overview of symptoms of other <u>vitamin deficiencies</u>.

* <u>Trace element</u> deficiency, e.g.:

* <u>Zinc deficiency</u>: <u>alopecia</u>, <u>diarrhea</u>, <u>periorificial</u> and/or <u>acral dermatitis</u>

* <u>Iron deficiency anemia</u>: fatigue, <u>pallor</u>

* Protein deficiency: <u>edema</u>, <u>cachexia</u>

* <u>Electrolyte</u> deficiency, e.g.:

* <u>Clinical features of hypokalemia</u>

* <u>Clinical features of hypocalcemia</u>

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




Suspect malabsorption in patients with <u>chronic diarrhea</u>, <u>steatorrhea</u>, and/or nutritional deficiencies.




* Obtain a focused <u>patient history</u>, including:

* Symptom duration and triggers, e.g., specific foods

* <u>Risk factors</u> for potentially causative diseases, e.g., <u>alcohol</u> consumption

* History of abdominal <u>surgery</u> (including <u>bariatric surgery</u>)

* Consider routine <u>laboratory studies</u> to assess for diagnostic clues for nutritional deficiencies.

* Obtain focused testing based on clinical suspicion.





> [!TIP]
> <u>Diarrhea</u> can have various causes, but the presence of nutritional deficiencies should raise suspicion for malabsorption, as <u>nutrients</u> are almost exclusively absorbed in the <u>small bowel</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




Broad diagnostic testing is not routinely necessary, as the differential diagnosis can usually be narrowed based on <u>patient history</u>.




* Routine studies: may indicate certain deficiencies

* <u>CBC</u>

* <u>Macrocytic</u> <u>anemia</u> in <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency</u> and/or <u>folate deficiency</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Microcytic</u> <u>anemia</u> in <u>iron deficiency</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>CMP</u>

* ↓ <u>Electrolytes</u>

* ↓ Total protein, ↓ <u>albumin</u>

* Abnormal <u>cholesterol</u> levels

* ↑ <u>ALP</u> and ↓ <u>calcium</u> in <u>vitamin D deficiency</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Coagulation panel</u>: ↑ <u>PT</u> in <u>vitamin K deficiency</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Testing for specific deficiencies (based on previous findings), e.g.:

* <u>Fat-soluble vitamins</u>, including <u>vitamin D</u>, <u>vitamin K</u>, <u>β-carotene</u> (indirect marker of <u>vitamin A deficiency</u>)

* <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>, <u>folate</u>

* <u>Iron</u>





#### Stool studies




* Evaluation for <u>steatorrhea</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HMcKJ10)

* <u>Screening test</u>: <u>Sudan III stain</u>

* <u>Gold standard test</u>: <u>72-hour fecal fat test</u>

* Evaluation for specific <u>causes of malabsorption</u>

* <u>Stool culture</u> ± microscopy: to assess for pathogens, e.g., <u>Giardia duodenalis</u>, <u>Escherichia coli</u>

* <u>Fecal elastase-1</u>: to evaluate for <u>exocrine pancreatic insufficiency</u>

* Stool <u>pH</u>: <u>pH</u> < 6 supports <u>carbohydrate malabsorption</u>. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HMcKJ10)





### <u>Small bowel</u> evaluation




Diagnostic testing in the absence of a clear clinical suspicion focuses on the <u>small bowel</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




#### Endoscopy




* Small bowel biopsy [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Indications include:

* <u>Steatorrhea</u>

* <u>Chronic diarrhea</u>

* Abnormalities on <u>small bowel follow-through</u>

* Can be used to diagnose <u>mucosal</u> diseases, e.g., <u>Whipple disease</u>, <u>amyloidosis</u>, <u>Crohn disease</u>, <u>celiac disease</u>

* See “<u>Differential diagnosis of malabsorption</u>” for specific findings.

* <u>Small bowel aspirate</u>: to evaluate for <u>SIBO</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HMcKJ10)





#### Other




These tests have limited clinical relevance because of widespread access to endoscopy.




* Small bowel follow-through [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Findings may be nonspecific, e.g., dilation, <u>diverticula</u>, strictures

* Rarely, findings may be specific, e.g., <u>string sign</u> in <u>Crohn disease</u>

* Cannot be used to rule out diseases

* D-xylose absorption test: may be used to assess the absorptive function of the upper <u>small intestine</u>

* Physiological background [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Intestinal absorption of <u>D-xylose</u> requires intact <u>mucosa</u> in the <u>proximal</u> <u>small intestine</u>, but not digestive enzymes.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Most of the absorbed <u>D-xylose</u> is eliminated by the <u>kidneys</u> without being metabolized.

* Method

* After overnight <u>fasting</u>, 25 g of <u>D-xylose</u> is administered orally. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Urine is collected over 5 hours, and a venous blood sample is collected after 1 hour. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* ↓ <u>D-xylose</u> levels (in urine and serum) indicates either: ;  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Damage to the <u>duodenal</u> and/or <u>jejunal</u> <u>mucosa</u> (e.g., <u>celiac disease</u>, <u>Whipple disease</u>)

* <u>Small intestine bacterial overgrowth</u>

* Adequately elevated <u>D-xylose</u> levels suggest a different cause of malabsorption.





### Further testing for underlying diseases




Obtain further testing based on clinical suspicion.




* <u>Hydrogen breath tests</u>:  to assess for malabsorption of individual <u>carbohydrates</u> (e.g., <u>lactose malabsorption</u>; , <u>fructose malabsorption</u>) or <u>SIBO</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Glucose hydrogen breath test</u>

* <u>Lactulose breath test</u>

* <u>Serologic studies</u>: : e.g., <u>tTG</u> <u>IgA</u> to assess for <u>celiac disease</u>

* <u>Mesenteric</u> <u>angiography</u>: if <u>chronic mesenteric ischemia</u> is suspected

## Treatment

* Identify and treat the underlying disease.

* Optimize diet under specialist guidance based on the underlying disease, e.g.:

* Calorie- and protein-enriched diet

* Supplementation of fluid and <u>nutrients</u>, including <u>vitamins</u> and <u>trace elements</u>

* Specific dietary restrictions, e.g., <u>gluten</u> in <u>celiac disease</u>, dairy in <u>lactose intolerance</u>

* <u>Total parenteral nutrition</u> may be temporarily necessary in severe cases, e.g., after extensive intestinal resection or in <u>intestinal failure</u>

* Symptomatic treatment as needed, e.g., <u>antidiarrheal agents</u>

* Follow-up includes:

* Monitoring for nutritional deficiencies

* Monitoring for complications, e.g., <u>intestinal failure</u>, <u>osteoporosis</u>

## Complications

* <u>Intestinal failure</u>

* <u>Dehydration</u>

* Complications of severe deficiencies, e.g.:

* <u>Protein-energy malnutrition</u>

* <u>Osteoporosis</u>

* <u>Coagulopathy</u>

* <u>Peripheral neuropathy</u>

* In children: <u>failure to thrive</u>





### Protein-losing enteropathy [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0jde_p0)




* Definition: a condition characterized by massive loss of protein that can occur as a complication of several gastrointestinal disorders

* Etiology

* Erosive or ulcerative conditions (e.g., <u>Crohn disease</u>, <u>Clostridioides difficile infection</u>)

* Increased intestinal permeability to <u>proteins</u> (e.g., <u>celiac disease</u>, <u>SIBO</u>)

* Impaired lymphatic circulation (e.g., <u>intestinal lymphangiectasia</u>, <u>Whipple disease</u>)

* Clinical features

* <u>Peripheral edema</u> and effusions

* Nutritional deficiencies

* Infections

* Gastrointestinal symptoms (e.g., <u>diarrhea</u>, <u>steatorrhea</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>vomiting</u>)

* Symptoms of the underlying disease

* Diagnostics

* Nonselective <u>hypoproteinemia</u>: <u>hypoalbuminemia</u> and ↓ <u>immunoglobulins</u>

* Exclusion of alternative causes of <u>hypoproteinemia</u>, <u>diarrhea</u>, and/or malabsorption

* Diagnostic confirmation: ↑ 24-hour fecal <u>alpha-1 antitrypsin</u>  [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0jde_p0)

* Additional diagnostics: Obtain as needed to determine the underlying cause and assess for complications.

* Treatment

* Treatment of the underlying disease

* <u>Supportive care</u>, e.g.:

* Correction of <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities

* <u>Vitamin</u> and <u>trace element</u> supplementation

* <u>Nutritional support</u>

* Management of <u>peripheral edema</u> (e.g., with <u>compression stockings</u>)

* <u>IVIg</u> and <u>antibiotic prophylaxis</u> for patients with <u>immunodeficiency</u> and recurrent infections
