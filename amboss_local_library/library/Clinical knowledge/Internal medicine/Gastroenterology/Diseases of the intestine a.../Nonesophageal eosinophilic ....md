## Summary

Nonesophageal eosinophilic gastrointestinal disorders (nonEoE EGIDs) are chronic, immune-mediated conditions characterized by <u>eosinophil</u>-predominant inflammation of the <u>stomach</u>, i.e., <u>eosinophilic gastritis</u> (EoG), <u>small intestine</u>, i.e., <u>eosinophilic enteritis</u> (EoN), or <u>colon</u>, i.e., <u>eosinophilic colitis</u> (EoC) in the absence of other causes of GI <u>eosinophilia</u>. The etiology of EoG and EoN is not fully understood but is believed to involve a <u>Th2</u> inflammatory response to <u>allergens</u> and is associated with a personal or <u>family history</u> of <u>atopic disease</u>. The cause of EoC is unknown, but contributing factors may include <u>food allergies</u>, intestinal dysbiosis, and genetic predisposition. Clinical features depend on the depth of infiltration (<u>mucosal</u>, muscularis, or serosal/subserosal) and commonly include abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, and <u>diarrhea</u>. Muscularis or serosal/subserosal involvement may manifest with obstruction or <u>ascites</u>. Diagnosis requires endoscopic <u>biopsy</u> of the affected segments that reveals eosinophilic infiltration. Other causes of gastrointestinal <u>eosinophilia</u>, such as parasitic infections and <u>inflammatory bowel disease</u>, must be excluded. Management involves dietary therapy (e.g., empiric elimination of common food <u>antigens</u>) in consultation with a dietician. First-line pharmacological treatment for inducing remission consists of <u>glucocorticoids</u> (e.g., oral <u>prednisone</u> or <u>enteric</u>-release <u>budesonide</u>).




Eosiniphilic <u>esophagitis</u> is described in "<u>Esophagitis</u>."
