# Cyclical vomiting syndrome

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Gastroenterology > Diseases of the stomach > Cyclical vomiting syndrome*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bE0H83)

---

## Summary

Cyclical <u>vomiting</u> syndrome (<u>CVS</u>) is characterized by severe, recurrent, <u>self-limited</u> episodes of <u>bilious</u> or nonbilious <u>vomiting</u>. <u>CVS</u> may affect individuals of any age but is most common in children and women. Etiology is likely multifactorial, involving genetic, environmental, and neurohormonal factors. <u>CVS</u> is often associated with a personal or <u>family history</u> of <u>migraines</u>, and common triggers include psychological or physiological <u>stress</u>, certain foods, and hormonal changes such as <u>menstruation</u> or <u>pregnancy</u>. Diagnosis is clinical and involves ruling out other causes through a <u>workup of nausea and vomiting</u>. Treatment focuses on managing acute episodes with <u>abortive therapies for CVS</u> and <u>supportive care</u>, including hydration and <u>antiemetics</u>. Prevention of future episodes includes lifestyle modifications and, in some patients, prophylactic medications. Early intervention in the <u>prodromal</u> phase can help minimize the severity of symptoms.

## Etiology

* Likely multifactorial (genetic, environmental, neurohormonal factors) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x4dElJ0)

* Often associated with a personal or <u>family history</u> of <u>migraines</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x4dElJ0)

* Common triggers include: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B4dzlJ0)

* Psychologic <u>stress</u>

* Physiologic <u>stress</u> (e.g., infection, <u>fasting</u>,;  <u>sleep deprivation</u>)

* Certain foods (e.g., dairy, soy, chocolate, red wine)

* Hormonal changes (e.g., <u>menstruation</u>, <u>pregnancy</u>)

## Clinical features

Recurrent, <u>self-limited</u> attacks of severe <u>vomiting</u> (<u>bilious</u> or nonbilious) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B4dzlJ0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x4dElJ0)




* <u>Prodromal</u> phase (minutes to hours)

* Abdominal <u>pain</u> and <u>nausea</u>

* Autonomic symptoms (e.g., <u>pallor</u>, <u>diaphoresis</u>)

* Irritability, sense of impending doom

* Emetic phase (hours to days; typically < 1 week)

* Recurrent intense <u>vomiting</u> and retching

* Abdominal <u>pain</u>, <u>headaches</u>, <u>diarrhea</u>, and/or intense thirst

* Relief with <u>sleep</u>, stimuli avoidance, and/or hot showers or baths  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B4dzlJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)

* Recovery phase (hours to days): gradual resolution of <u>vomiting</u>, improvement in oral intake

* Interepisodic phase (weeks to months): usually asymptomatic, though <u>dyspepsia</u> and <u>nausea</u> may occur





> [!TIP]
> The emetic phase of <u>CVS</u> often begins in the early morning. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/B4dzlJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)




> [!TIP]
> <u>Prodromal</u> and emetic symptoms typically follow a consistent, recurrent pattern. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x4dElJ0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)




* <u>CVS</u> is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> based on <u>Rome IV criteria for cyclical vomiting syndrome</u>.

* Obtain routine <u>diagnostics for nausea and vomiting</u> to rule out other <u>causes of nausea and vomiting</u>.

* Consider <u>advanced diagnostics for nausea and vomiting</u> if anatomic abnormalities or other underlying conditions are suspected.





> [!TIP]
> Inquire about cannabinoid use to screen for <u>cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome</u>, a common <u>CVS mimic</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)




### Rome IV criteria for cyclical vomiting syndrome [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/V_cGMU0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)




#### Adults [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pSWL0O0)




All of the following criteria must be met:




* Stereotypical episodes of acute <u>vomiting</u> with a duration of < 1 week

* ≥ 3 episodes in the past year and ≥ 2 episodes in the past 6 months, occurring ≥ 1 week apart

* Asymptomatic intervals between episodes (mild symptoms may be present)

* Symptoms present for the past 3 months with onset ≥ 6 months prior





#### Children [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y6YdnJ)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A6YRnJ)




All of the following criteria must be met:




* Stereotypical episodes of unrelenting <u>nausea and vomiting</u>, lasting hours to days

* ≥ 2 episodes within a 6-month period

* Asymptomatic intervals (weeks to months) between episodes

* Symptoms cannot be attributed to another cause.





### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




Obtain basic laboratory tests to identify potential causes or complications of recurrent <u>vomiting</u>, e.g.:




* <u>CBC</u>

* <u>CMP</u>

* Serum <u>lipase</u>

* <u>Urinalysis</u>

* <u>Beta-HCG test</u> for female patients





### Additional studies




* <u>Esophagogastroduodenoscopy</u> (<u>EGD</u>): to exclude obstructive lesions  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)

* <u>Gastric emptying scintigraphy</u>: not routinely indicated  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)





> [!WARNING]
> Avoid repeating <u>EGDs</u> and other advanced studies in patients with no evidence of anatomical anomalies or other underlying conditions on initial evaluation. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w4dhlJ0)
