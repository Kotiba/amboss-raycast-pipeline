# Helicobacter pylori infection

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Gastroenterology > Diseases of the stomach > Helicobacter pylori infection*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/K80Un3)

---

## Summary

<u>Helicobacter pylori</u> is a <u>gram-negative bacteria</u> that colonizes the <u>stomach</u>. Infection most commonly occurs in childhood and persists for life if left untreated. <u>H. pylori</u> infection is often asymptomatic but may cause <u>dyspepsia</u> and is associated with chronic <u>gastritis</u>, gastric and <u>duodenal</u> <u>peptic ulcer disease</u> (<u>PUD</u>), <u>gastric cancer</u>, and gastric <u>mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue</u> (<u>MALT</u>) <u>lymphoma</u>. Diagnosis is confirmed using noninvasive tests (e.g., <u>urea breath test</u>) and/or tests on gastric <u>biopsy</u> samples obtained via <u>EGD</u> (e.g., <u>rapid urease test</u>). To optimize <u>sensitivity</u> and <u>specificity</u>, <u>PPIs</u> and <u>antibiotics</u> should be withheld before performing most diagnostic tests. All patients who test positive should be treated with a combination of acid blockers (<u>PPIs</u> or <u>vonoprazan</u>) and <u>antibiotics</u> to eradicate the infection, followed by eradication confirmation testing ≥ 4 weeks after completion of treatment.

---

## Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>H. pylori</u> is a flagellated, gram-negative, S-shaped, <u>microaerophile bacteria</u>. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ruWfsM0)

* Routes of transmission [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ruWfsM0)

* Fecal-oral (most likely route)

* Contaminated water

* Person-to-person within families

---

## Clinical features

* Acute Infection [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ruWfsM0)

* Often asymptomatic

* Mild to moderate <u>dyspepsia</u> and/or nonspecific abdominal symptoms

* Chronic infection [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ruWfsM0)

* Children: often asymptomatic; complications are rare.

* Adults: symptoms secondary to complications of <u>H. pylori</u> infection, e.g., <u>PUD</u>

## Diagnosis

### General principles




* Prior to testing, withhold medications that can interfere with test results (whenever possible).

* <u>PPIs</u> for 1–2 weeks  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZEYZ8I)

* <u>Antibiotics</u> and/or <u>bismuth</u> for ≥ 4 weeks [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZEYZ8I)

* Choose a noninvasive test (e.g., <u>urea breath test</u>) if there are no <u>indications for EGD</u>.





> [!TIP]
> <u>PPIs</u> should be discontinued 1–2 weeks prior to most <u>H. pylori</u> testing modalities to minimize rates of <u>false-negative</u> results. However, some types of testing, e.g., <u>histology</u>, are not affected by recent <u>PPI</u> treatment. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)




### Indications for testing [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)




* GI conditions

* <u>PUD</u>: active disease or a history of disease without prior eradication

* Uninvestigated <u>dyspepsia</u>: See “<u>Approach to dyspepsia</u>” for details.

* Early <u>gastric malignancy</u>: e.g., <u>MALT lymphoma</u>

* <u>Functional dyspepsia</u>

* History of or high risk for <u>gastric cancer</u>, e.g., individuals with certain <u>endogenous</u> <u>risk factors for gastric cancer</u>

* Other

* Before starting long-term treatment with <u>low-dose aspirin</u>

* Long-term use of <u>NSAIDs</u>

* Unexplained <u>iron deficiency anemia</u>

* <u>Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura</u>

* Adults who share a household with individuals with <u>H. pylori</u>





### Tests




#### Initial tests




* Noninvasive: Order one of the following if <u>EGD</u> is not indicated.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MuYM7I)

* Urea breath test: The labeled carbon isotope in a breath sample is quantified.

* H. pylori stool antigen test: detects <u>H. pylori</u> <u>antigens</u> via an <u>enzyme immunoassay</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uK0pRS)

* Invasive: Order one or both of the following tests (performed on a gastric <u>biopsy</u> sample) for patients with <u>indications for EGD</u>.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)

* <u>Histology</u> (<u>gold standard</u>)

* Staining and direct microscopic identification

* Typical microscopic appearance of <u>H. pylori</u>: curved, <u>gram-negative rods</u> with multiple <u>flagella</u>

* <u>Sensitivity</u> and <u>specificity</u> are not affected by recent <u>PPI</u> treatment.

* Rapid urease test: detects the amount of <u>ammonia</u> produced by <u>H. pylori</u> during <u>urea</u> <u>hydrolysis</u>

> [!TIP]
> <u>Urea breath test</u> or <u>H. pylori stool antigen test</u> can be used for the initial diagnosis of infection and/or confirmation of eradication.




> [!WARNING]
> Do not order an <u>EGD</u> solely to diagnose <u>H. pylori</u> infection.




#### Other tests [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)




The following studies are not widely used and should be ordered in consultation with a GI specialist.




* Serum <u>IgG antibodies</u> against <u>H. pylori</u>

* <u>Sensitivity</u> and <u>specificity</u> are not affected by recent <u>PPI</u> treatment.

* <u>Antibodies</u> may still be detected after eradication.

* Poor <u>positive predictive value</u> if the <u>prevalence</u> of infection is less than approx. 20%  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/uK0pRS)

* <u>PCR</u>

* Sensitive and specific, but ordered infrequently because of the high cost

* May be performed on gastric <u>biopsy</u>, saliva, or stool samples

* <u>Culture and sensitivity testing</u>: useful if <u>antibiotic</u> resistance is suspected

## Treatment

### General principles [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)




* All patients who test positive for <u>H. pylori</u> infection should be offered <u>H. pylori</u> eradication therapy.

* Recommended regimen: <u>optimized bismuth quadruple therapy</u>

* Consider <u>antibiotic sensitivity testing</u> if results will guide treatment decisions, e.g., in patients with:

* <u>Penicillin</u> <u>allergy</u>

* Previous treatment with <u>macrolides</u> or <u>quinolones</u> (for any reason)

* Previous treatment of <u>H. pylori</u> infection





### Initial pharmacotherapy [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)




* Optimized bismuth quadruple therapy: 10–14 days (preferred)  

* <u>PPI</u> at standard dose twice daily (e.g., <u>omeprazole</u> DOSAGE) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>bismuth</u> (e.g., <u>bismuth</u> subcitrate DOSAGE or <u>bismuth subsalicylate</u> DOSAGE) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>tetracycline</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>metronidazole</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* Rifabutin triple therapy: 14 days   [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* <u>Rifabutin</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>omeprazole</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>amoxicillin</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* <u>Potassium-competitive acid blocker</u> therapy (<u>PCAB</u> therapy): 14 days 

* <u>PCAB</u> dual therapy: <u>vonoprazan</u> PLUS <u>amoxicillin</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PCAB triple therapy: <u>vonoprazan</u> PLUS <u>clarithromycin</u> PLUS <u>amoxicillin</u> DOSAGE  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)





> [!NOTE]
> Bismuth binds to the affected <u>mucosa</u>, providing physical protection from acids, and stimulates gastric <u>HCO3-</u> secretion, which helps to restore the <u>mucosal</u> <u>pH</u> gradient and is hence used to treat <u>peptic ulcers</u> and <u>H. pylori</u> infection (as a part of <u>Bismuth quadruple therapy</u>).




### Treatment of persistent infection [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)




* Prescribe a <u>salvage regimen</u> with different <u>antibiotics</u> than those used as first-line therapy.

* Preferred: <u>optimized bismuth quadruple therapy</u> for 14 days for patients who have not received it before [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* Alternatives (all for 14 days)

* <u>Rifabutin triple therapy</u>

* Levofloxacin triple therapy: consider for <u>levofloxacin</u>-sensitive <u>H. pylori</u> strains

* <u>PPI</u> at standard dose [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>levofloxacin</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PLUS <u>amoxicillin</u> DOSAGE OR <u>metronidazole</u> DOSAGE [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* PPI clarithromycin triple therapy: consider for <u>clarithromycin</u>-sensitive <u>H. pylori</u> strains [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* <u>PPIs</u> at standard or double dose twice daily (e.g., <u>omeprazole</u> DOSAGE) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)

* PLUS <u>clarithromycin</u> DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)

* PLUS <u>amoxicillin</u>  DOSAGE OR <u>metronidazole</u> DOSAGE [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5K0iSS)

* <u>PCAB triple therapy</u>: Consider for <u>clarithromycin</u>-sensitive <u>H. pylori</u> strains. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* Perform <u>antibiotic sensitivity testing</u> if regimens containing <u>clarithromycin</u> or <u>levofloxacin</u> are considered.

* Refer patients with unconfirmed <u>penicillin</u> <u>allergy</u> for <u>allergy testing</u>, e.g., <u>graded challenge testing</u>.

* Refer to a GI specialist for guidance.





> [!WARNING]
> Do not prescribe regimens containing <u>clarithromycin</u> or <u>levofloxacin</u> unless <u>sensitivity</u> has been proven. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

## Complications

<u>H. pylori</u> infection is associated with an increased risk of several conditions, e.g.:




* <u>PUD</u>

* <u>Dyspepsia</u>

* Chronic <u>gastritis</u>

* <u>Gastric cancer</u>

* <u>MALT lymphoma</u>

* <u>Iron deficiency anemia</u>





We list the most important complications. The selection is not exhaustive.

## Follow-up

### Eradication confirmation testing [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)




* Indication: : all patients post <u>H. pylori eradication therapy</u>

* Timing

* Hold <u>PPIs</u> for 1–2 weeks before testing. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* Test ≥ 4 weeks after completion of treatment. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/i4dJQJ0)

* Modalities

* <u>Urea breath testing</u>

* <u>Stool antigen assay</u>

* Endoscopic <u>biopsy</u> samples with rapid <u>urea</u> testing (only if endoscopy is indicated for other reasons)
