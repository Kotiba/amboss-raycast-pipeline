# Microscopic polyangiitis

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bt0zf3)

---

## Summary

Microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) is an <u>ANCA-associated small-vessel vasculitis</u>. Typical clinical features include pulmonary <u>vasculitis</u>, <u>pauci-immune glomerulonephritis</u>, and palpable <u>purpura</u>. <u>ANCAs</u> (most commonly <u>MPO-ANCAs</u>) are present in up to 75% of patients with MPA. CT chest is indicated in all patients with pulmonary symptoms and often reveals <u>ground-glass opacifications</u> or nodular lesions. <u>Biopsy</u> is required to confirm the diagnosis; <u>histopathology</u> findings include <u>fibrinoid necrosis</u> without <u>granulomas</u>. Management involves <u>immunosuppressive agents</u> (e.g., <u>glucocorticoids</u> plus a <u>glucocorticoid-sparing agent</u> such as <u>rituximab</u> or <u>methotrexate</u>). <u>Plasmapheresis</u> may be considered for patients with severe disease.

---

## Definitions

* Necrotizing <u>vasculitis</u> of small vessels, typically with pulmonary, renal, and <u>skin</u> involvement [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U30b3f)

* Manifestations are similar to <u>granulomatosis with polyangiitis</u>.

* The <u>nasopharynx</u> is usually not affected.

---

## Clinical features

* <u>Constitutional symptoms</u>

* Renal (∼ 90%): <u>pauci-immune glomerulonephritis</u>;  with <u>hypertension</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fgXkux)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)

* <u>Lungs</u> (∼ 50%): pulmonary <u>vasculitis</u>;  → diffuse alveolar hemorrhage → <u>hemoptysis</u>

* <u>Skin</u> (∼ 40%): palpable <u>purpura</u>, <u>nodules</u>, <u>necrosis</u>

* Gastrointestinal: abdominal <u>pain</u> (∼ 50%), <u>GI bleeding</u> (∼ 25%)

* Neurological (∼ 70%): <u>mononeuritis multiplex</u>, <u>distal</u> symmetric <u>polyneuropathy</u>





> [!TIP]
> Clinical features are very similar to those of <u>granulomatosis with polyangiitis</u> (<u>GPA</u>), but MPA spares the vessels in the <u>upper respiratory tract</u> (no <u>sinusitis</u> or <u>rhinitis</u>). [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)

---

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis is primarily clinical and confirmed by <u>biopsy</u>. Chest imaging is required for all patients with lower respiratory symptoms.




* <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AZXRV9)

* <u>Inflammatory markers</u>: ↑ <u>ESR</u>, ↑ <u>CRP</u>

* <u>CBC</u>: <u>leukocytosis</u>, <u>thrombocytosis</u>, <u>anemia</u>

* <u>Serology</u>: <u>ANCA</u> (positive in 50–75% of patients)   [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AZXRV9)

* <u>MPO-ANCA</u> in > 50% of patients

* <u>PR3-ANCA</u> in ∼ 25% of patients

* <u>Urinalysis</u>: <u>proteinuria</u>, <u>microscopic hematuria</u>, <u>erythrocyte casts</u>

* CT chest

* Indication: patients with lower respiratory symptoms

* Findings: <u>ground-glass opacifications</u>  (> 90% of patients), nodular lesions

* <u>Biopsy</u> of involved organ: confirms the diagnosis ;  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nEX7w-)

* Absence of <u>granulomas</u>

* <u>Fibrinoid necrosis</u> with <u>neutrophil</u> infiltration





> [!NOTE]
> Microscopic PolyAngiitis has MyeloPeroxidase Antibodies (i.e., p<u>ANCA</u>).




> [!TIP]
> The absence of <u>granulomas</u> on <u>histopathology</u> plus <u>pANCA</u> positivity helps differentiate MPA from <u>GPA</u>, as they have very similar clinical features. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AZXRV9)

## Treatment

### General principles  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9EXNB-)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nEX7w-)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x01ER20)




* Consult rheumatology and other specialties (e.g., nephrology, pulmonology) as required.

* Goal of pharmacotherapy (e.g., <u>glucocorticoids</u> PLUS <u>cyclophosphamide</u>) is induction of remission

* <u>Plasmapheresis</u> is only considered for patients with severe <u>end-organ damage</u>.





### Pharmacotherapy [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x01ER20)




* Induction of remission: for patients with active disease 

* Nonsevere disease

* Oral <u>glucocorticoids</u>: e.g., <u>prednisone</u>

* PLUS a <u>glucocorticoid-sparing agent</u>: e.g., <u>methotrexate</u> (preferred), <u>cyclophosphamide</u>, <u>rituximab</u>, OR <u>azathioprine</u>

* Severe disease 

* High-dose <u>glucocorticoids</u>: e.g., <u>methylprednisolone</u> pulses OR <u>prednisone</u>

* PLUS a <u>glucocorticoid-sparing agent</u>: e.g., <u>rituximab</u> (preferred) OR <u>cyclophosphamide</u>

* <u>Maintenance of remission</u>: indicated for patients who presented with severe disease

* Slowly taper <u>glucocorticoids</u> to the minimum effective dose.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x01ER20)

* <u>Glucocorticoid-sparing agents</u>: e.g., <u>rituximab</u> (preferred), <u>methotrexate</u>, OR <u>azathioprine</u>

* Duration of treatment: typically ≥ 18 months





### <u>Plasmapheresis</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x01ER20)



* Indications  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x01ER20)

* <u>Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis</u> at risk of <u>end-stage renal disease</u>

* Concomitant <u>anti-GBM disease</u>

* Severe <u>diffuse alveolar hemorrhage</u> refractory to other therapies




### <u>Supportive care</u>




* <u>Prevent complications of glucocorticoid therapy</u>.

* Monitor for <u>adverse effects of immunosuppressants</u>.

* Consider <u>pneumocystis pneumonia prophylaxis</u>.
