## Summary

<u>Connective tissue</u> is an important biological tissue composed of an <u>extracellular matrix</u> that binds, anchors, and supports organs. There are various types of <u>connective tissue</u>, all of which consist of varying combinations of fibers, cells, and intercellular substance. Over 200 conditions, which may be inherited or autoimmune, affect <u>connective tissue</u>, and they are collectively known as <u>connective tissue</u> diseases (CTDs). Inherited CTDs are caused by mutations that affect one of the two fibers (<u>collagen</u> and <u>fibrillin</u>). Autoimmune CTDs have no clear etiology, but the <u>incidence</u> is higher in women and among genetically predisposed individuals. As the name suggests, in autoimmune CTDs, the <u>immune system</u> develops <u>antibodies</u> against components of <u>connective tissue</u>. Individual conditions can affect a vast range of bodily structures (including <u>cartilage</u>, <u>blood vessels</u>, bone, <u>tendons</u>, and organs) and thus present with a wide array of clinical findings.

## Inherited connective tissue diseases

* <u>Ehlers-Danlos</u> syndrome

* <u>Marfan syndrome</u>

* <u>Osteogenesis imperfecta</u>

* <u>Alport syndrome</u>

* Loeys-Dietz syndrome [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C7YqLq)

* <u>Autosomal dominant inheritance</u>

* Caused by mutations in TGFβ <u>receptor</u>

* Features shared with <u>Marfan syndrome</u>: <u>marfanoid habitus</u>, increased risk of ascending <u>aortic aneurysms</u> and <u>aortic dissection</u>

* Distinct features: <u>hypertelorism</u>, <u>bifid uvula</u>, <u>cleft palate</u>, easy <u>bruising</u> and <u>keloids</u>, arterial <u>tortuosity</u> (winding course of <u>arteries</u>)

* Kniest syndrome

* <u>Autosomal dominant inheritance</u>

* Caused by mutations in COL2A1 <u>gene</u> leading to impaired production of <u>type 2 collagen</u>

* Clinical features

* <u>Short stature</u>

* Ocular disorders (e.g., <u>myopia</u>, <u>cataracts</u>)

* <u>Hearing</u> impairment

## Clinical manifestations of connective tissue disorders

Clinical features vary greatly among individual diseases. The table below provides a general overview of the more common features.




| Overview of manifestations of <u>connective tissue</u> disorders |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Features | Autoimmune CTDs | Inherited CTDs |
| General | <br>* <u>Fever</u>, weight loss, fatigue<br><br> | <br>* Fatigue, <u>weakness</u><br><br> |
| Musculoskeletal | <br>* <u>Polyarthritis</u>, <u>myositis</u><br><br> | <br>* <u>Joint hypermobility</u>, curved <u>spine</u>, brittle bones, bone deformities, growth abnormalities (short or <u>tall stature</u>)<br><br> |
| <u>Skin</u> | <br>* <u>Erythema</u>, <u>Raynaud's phenomenon</u><br><br> | <br>* Hyperextensive, fragile <u>skin</u><br><br> |
| Other organ manifestations | <br><br>* <u>Heart</u>: <u>pericarditis</u><br><br>* <u>Lungs</u>: scarring (<u>pulmonary fibrosis</u>), <u>pleurisy</u><br><br>* <u>Kidneys</u>: nephritis (e.g., <u>glomerulonephritis</u>)<br><br>* <u>CNS</u>: depressive moods<br><br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Heart</u>: valve <u>weakness</u>/prolapse<br><br>* <u>Lungs</u>: <u>weakness</u>, difficulty breathing, tendency to develop <u>emphysema</u><br><br>* <u>Blood vessels</u>: <u>aneurysms</u>, ruptures<br><br>* <u>Eye</u> abnormalities<br><br><br> |

### Non-autoimmune




| Overview of inherited and acquired <u>connective tissue</u> disorders |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Condition | Etiology | Pathophysiology | Clinical features (examples) |
| <u>Marfan syndrome</u> | <br>* <u>Autosomal dominant</u> disease caused by mutation of <u>fibrillin-1 gene</u> (<u>FBN1</u>) on <u>chromosome</u> 15<br><br> | <br>* Defective <u>fibrillin</u> → defective <u>elastin</u> → defective <u>connective tissue</u> throughout the body<br><br> | <br><br>* <u>Tall stature</u> with disproportionately long extremities; <u>joint hypermobility</u><br><br>* <u>Arachnodactyly</u><br><br>* <u>Aortic aneurysm</u> and dissection<br><br>* <u>Lens subluxation</u> superiorly and temporally<br><br>* <u>Heart valve</u> defects (<u>mitral valve prolapse</u>)<br><br>* <u>Skin</u> hyperelasticity<br><br><br> |

| <br>Menkes disease [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3YlRK) | <br>* X-linked recessive <u>neurodegenerative disease</u> caused by a mutation in the <u>gene</u> ATP7A (coding for <u>Menkes</u> protein, <u>copper</u>-transporting ATPase 1)<br><br> | <br>* Defective <u>copper</u>-transporting ATPase 1 → impaired <u>copper</u> absorption and transport → reduced activity of <u>copper</u>-dependent enzymes (e.g., <u>lysyl oxidase</u>, <u>tyrosinase</u>)→ defective <u>collagen</u> crosslinking and fibril synthesis<br><br> | <br><br>* Sparse, fragile, kinky <u>hair</u><br><br>* <u>Hypopigmented</u> <u>skin</u> and <u>hair</u><br><br>* <u>Failure to thrive</u><br><br>* Developmental retardation<br><br>* <u>Epilepsy</u><br><br>* <u>Hypotonia</u><br><br>* Increased risk of <u>cerebral aneurysm</u><br><br>* Decreased <u>copper</u> and <u>ceruloplasmin</u> levels<br><br><br> |
| <u>Osteogenesis imperfecta</u> | <br>* Rare, <u>autosomal dominant</u> inherited bone disorder due to mutation in <u>COL1A1</u> or <u>COL1A2</u> <u>genes</u><br><br> | <br>* Mutation in type I <u>collagen</u> <u>gene</u> → decreased synthesis of <u>type 1 collagen</u><br><br> | <br><br>* Brittle bones and frequent and/or multiple <u>fractures</u> from minimal trauma<br><br>* Growth retardation<br><br>* Skeletal deformities<br><br>* <u>Blue sclera</u><br><br>* Progressive <u>hearing loss</u> (due to abnormal <u>ossicles</u>)<br><br><br> |
| <u>Scurvy</u> | <br>* <u>Vitamin C</u> deficiency<br><br> | <br>* Defect in <u>hydroxylation</u> of <u>proline</u> and <u>lysine</u> residues in <u>procollagen</u> → production of abnormal <u>collagen</u> with decreased tensile strength<br><br> | <br><br>* Swollen <u>gums</u><br><br>* <u>Mucosal</u> bleeding<br><br>* Poor <u>wound healing</u><br><br>* Curly body <u>hair</u><br><br>* <u>Follicular hyperkeratosis</u><br><br>* <u>Hemarthrosis</u><br><br>* Generalized <u>weakness</u>/fatigue<br><br><br> |





> [!NOTE]
> A for Absent <u>copper</u> and B for a Bunch of <u>copper</u>: In <u>Menkes disease</u>, the ATP7A <u>gene</u> is defective, resulting in decreased <u>copper</u> levels. In <u>Wilson disease</u>, the ATP7B <u>gene</u> is defective, which leads to high <u>copper</u> levels.




> [!NOTE]
> <u>Ehlers-Danlos</u> syndrome and <u>Menkes disease</u> are both associated with reduced <u>lysyl oxidase</u> activity, resulting in defective <u>collagen</u> cross-linking and fibril synthesis.
