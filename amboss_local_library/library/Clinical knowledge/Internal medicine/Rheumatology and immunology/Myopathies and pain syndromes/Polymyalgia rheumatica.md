# Polymyalgia rheumatica

## Summary

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is a common inflammatory disease that predominantly affects individuals > 50 years of age and occurs more often in women than men. Patients typically present with <u>acute pain</u> and stiffness in the <u>shoulder girdle</u>, <u>pelvic girdle</u>, and/or neck, and <u>constitutional symptoms</u>. Diagnosis is clinical. Findings from <u>laboratory studies</u> (i.e., elevated <u>inflammatory markers</u>, negative <u>creatine kinase</u>, and absence of <u>autoantibodies</u>) and imaging such as <u>ultrasound</u> further support the diagnosis. Oral <u>glucocorticoids</u> are the mainstay of treatment. All patients should be monitored for features of <u>giant cell arteritis</u> (<u>GCA</u>), which is commonly associated with PMR.

## Etiology

* Unknown

* Possible contributing factors are:

* Genetic predisposition (e.g., <u>human leukocyte antigen</u> <u>HLA-DR4</u>)

* Association with <u>GCA</u>: Approx. 20% of individuals with PMR also have <u>GCA</u>. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)

## Clinical features

* Systemic features [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)

* <u>Constitutional symptoms</u>: <u>fever</u>, weight loss, night sweats

* <u>Fatigue and malaise</u>

* <u>Depressed mood</u>

* <u>Symptoms of anemia</u>

* Musculoskeletal features: <u>shoulder girdle</u>, neck, and <u>pelvic girdle</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)

* <u>Pain</u>: acute onset, bilateral usually, worse at night

* Morning stiffness (> 45 minutes) [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)

* Muscular <u>atrophy</u> and <u>weakness</u>: not directly caused by PMR but resulting from reduced activity due to <u>pain</u> and stiffness

* <u>Features of GCA</u>: <u>GCA</u> and PMR share clinical features and may occur simultaneously.

## Diagnosis

### General principles [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XaY9Qn)




* PMR is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>.

* The <u>2012 EULAR/ACR classification criteria for PMR</u> help to distinguish it from other inflammatory conditions but are not diagnostic.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/O7dINr0)

* <u>Laboratory studies</u> help to exclude alternative diagnoses.

* Imaging studies (e.g., <u>ultrasound</u>) may be requested in consultation with specialists to support the diagnosis.

* Refer patients with atypical clinical presentations (e.g., peripheral <u>arthritis</u>, age < 60 years) to rheumatology.

* A rapid response (< 7 days) to low to medium doses of <u>glucocorticoids</u> helps confirm the diagnosis.





> [!TIP]
> All patients should be examined for <u>features of GCA</u>.




### <u>Laboratory studies</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)




* <u>CBC</u>: <u>thrombocytosis</u>, <u>normocytic anemia</u>

* <u>Inflammatory markers</u>: ↑ <u>ESR</u>;  and/or ↑ <u>CRP</u>; normal levels of both suggest a different diagnosis.

* <u>Serology</u>: absent <u>autoantibodies</u> (including <u>rheumatoid factor</u>)

* <u>Muscle enzymes</u>: normal levels of <u>creatine kinase</u> and other enzymes





> [!TIP]
> Normal <u>creatine kinase</u> levels and the absence of <u>autoantibodies</u> help differentiate PMR from other rheumatic diseases (e.g., <u>polymyositis</u>, <u>dermatomyositis</u>, <u>rheumatoid arthritis</u>).




### Imaging [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AJdR9I0)




* <u>Ultrasound</u> (e.g., shoulder and/or hip)

* Indications: to help support the diagnosis (not routinely required)

* Findings (often bilateral)

* Subdeltoid <u>bursitis</u>

* <u>Biceps</u> <u>tenosynovitis</u>

* <u>Trochanteric bursitis</u>, glenohumeral <u>synovitis</u>, and hip <u>synovitis</u> (less common)

* 18F-<u>FDG PET-CT scan</u>: considered in atypical presentations to rule out alternative diagnoses (e.g., <u>GCA</u>, <u>malignancy</u>)

## Differential diagnoses

* <u>Giant cell arteritis</u>

* <u>Rheumatoid arthritis</u>

* <u>Polymyositis</u> and <u>dermatomyositis</u>

* <u>Fibromyalgia</u>

* <u>Paraneoplastic syndromes</u> associated with <u>lung cancer</u>, e.g., <u>Stiff person syndrome</u>

* <u>Thyroid disorders</u>

* <u>Statin-induced myopathy</u>

* <u>Rotator cuff disease</u>




The differential diagnoses listed here are not exhaustive.

## Treatment

Patients at high risk for <u>side effects of glucocorticoid therapy</u>, relapse, or prolonged therapy should be referred to rheumatology.




### Pharmacological treatment [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fUXkcx)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XaY9Qn)




* <u>Glucocorticoids</u> (low to medium dose)

* Indicated for all patients with PMR

* Oral <u>prednisone</u> DOSAGE or IM <u>methylprednisolone</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XaY9Qn)

* Dose adjustment is based on treatment response.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XaY9Qn)

* Other <u>immunosuppressive agents</u> 

* <u>Methotrexate</u>: may be used as a <u>glucocorticoid-sparing agent</u>

* <u>Sarilumab</u>: may be used in patients with relapse during tapering of <u>glucocorticoids</u> [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CJdqwI0)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Glucocorticoids</u> typically provide rapid symptomatic relief for PMR.




### <u>Supportive care</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8IdOer0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XaY9Qn)




Patients with PMR are almost exclusively older adults, with an increased <u>prevalence</u> of comorbidities and risk of complications.




* Refer patients to <u>physiotherapy</u> for a tailored program to maintain muscle mass and function.

* Counsel patients on preventing complications, e.g.:

* <u>ASCVD prevention</u>

* Recommended <u>vaccines</u>

* <u>Side effects of glucocorticoid therapy</u>
