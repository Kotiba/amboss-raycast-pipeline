## Summary

The bony <u>pelvis</u> (<u>pelvic girdle</u>) is composed of the two <u>hip bones</u>, the <u>sacrum</u>, and the <u>coccyx</u>. These bones are firmly connected by the <u>pubic symphysis</u> anteriorly and the sacrococcygeal and <u>sacroiliac joints</u> posteriorly. Each <u>hip bone</u> consists of three fused bones: the <u>ilium</u>, <u>ischium</u>, and <u>pubis</u>. In female individuals, the <u>pelvis</u> additionally accommodates the <u>birth canal</u> and therefore is larger and wider than in male individuals. The <u>pelvic girdle</u> transmits body weight from the <u>axial skeleton</u> to the lower extremities and provides attachment to a large number of muscles and <u>ligaments</u>. The <u>pelvic cavity</u> is the space within the <u>pelvic girdle</u>; it contains abdominal and <u>pelvic</u> organs, which are protected by the <u>pelvic girdle</u>. Located in the space within <u>pelvic girdle</u> is the <u>pelvic cavity</u>, which provides protection for abdominal and <u>pelvic</u> organs. Anatomical spaces of the <u>pelvic cavity</u> (e.g., <u>rectouterine pouch</u>, <u>rectovesical pouch</u>) vary between male and female individuals because of their different reproductive organs. Located inferior to the <u>pelvic cavity</u> is the <u>pelvic floor</u>, a complex fibromuscular structure that prevents <u>pelvic organ prolapse</u>, helps maintain fecal and urinary continence, and separates the <u>perineal region</u> from the <u>pelvic cavity</u>. Openings in the <u>pelvic floor</u> allow for the passage of the <u>rectum</u>, <u>vagina</u>, and <u>urethra</u>. <u>The gluteal region</u> consists of the muscles that form the buttocks, which can be divided into a <u>superficial</u> and a deep muscle layer. The <u>superficial</u> <u>gluteal muscles</u> <u>abduct</u> and medially rotate the thigh, while the deep <u>gluteal muscles</u> are responsible for <u>lateral</u> rotation of the thigh. The <u>hip joint</u> is located between the head of the <u>femur</u> and the <u>acetabulum</u> of the <u>pelvis</u> on each side. It connects the trunk to the lower extremities and supports dynamic and static body weight. The arterial supply of the femoral head is ensured by the <u>foveolar artery</u> and branches of the <u>deep femoral artery</u>. <u>Movements of the hip joint</u> include <u>flexion</u>, extension, <u>lateral</u> and <u>medial</u> rotation, <u>abduction</u>, <u>adduction</u>, and circumflexion.

## Overview

### The <u>pelvis</u> (<u>pelvic girdle</u>)




* Characteristics

* A ring-like structure of the <u>axial skeleton</u> that connects the <u>vertebral column</u> to the lower extremities (e.g., <u>femur</u>)

* Largely immobile; provides stability and supports proper transfer of weight from the <u>vertebral column</u> to the lower extremities, especially in the upright position

* The shape of the <u>pelvis</u> differs significantly between male and female individuals.

* Bones 

* The <u>hip bones</u> (os coxae): <u>ilium</u>, <u>ischium</u>, <u>pubis</u>

* <u>Sacrum</u>

* <u>Coccyx</u>

* <u>Joints</u>: <u>lumbosacral joint</u>, <u>sacroiliac joint</u>, <u>sacrococcygeal joint</u>, <u>pubic symphysis</u>, <u>hip joint</u>

* <u>Ligaments</u> and <u>foramina of the pelvis</u> 

* A number of <u>ligaments</u> (e.g., <u>iliolumbar ligament</u>, sacroiliac <u>ligament</u>) stabilize and support the <u>joints of the pelvis</u>.

* The <u>foramina of the pelvis</u> (e.g., sciatic foramina, vascular and <u>muscular lacuna</u>) allow the passage of nerves, muscles, <u>blood vessels</u>, and <u>lymphatics</u>.

* <u>Pelvic cavity</u>: the space within the <u>pelvic girdle</u>

* Allows passage of gastrointestinal and urogenital structures into the <u>pelvis</u>, as well as <u>arteries</u> <u>veins</u>, muscles, and nerves

* Bounded inferiorly by the <u>pelvic floor</u>, which holds the organs in place

* <u>Pelvic spaces</u>: anatomical spaces of the <u>pelvic cavity</u> 

* In female individuals: <u>rectouterine pouch</u>, <u>vesicouterine pouch</u>

* In male individuals: <u>rectovesical pouch</u>

* In both male and female individuals: <u>pararectal fossa</u>, <u>ischiorectal fossa</u>, <u>retropubic space of Retzius</u>

* <u>Birth canal</u>

* <u>Pelvic</u> form: gynecoid, anthropoid, android, plateylpelloid

* <u>Pelvic regions</u>: <u>pelvic inlet</u>, <u>pelvic cavity</u>, <u>pelvic outlet</u>

* <u>Pelvic planes</u>: plane of the <u>pelvic inlet</u>, planes of the greatest and least <u>pelvic</u> dimension, plane of the anatomical outlet

* <u>Soft tissue</u> structures: inner portion (lower uterine segment, <u>cervix</u>, <u>vagina</u>, <u>vulva</u>), outer portion (<u>pelvic floor</u>)

* <u>Pelvic floor</u>: complex fibromuscular structure that holds the <u>pelvic</u> organs in place; separates the <u>pelvic cavity</u> superiorly from the <u>perineal region</u> inferiorly 

* Layers of the <u>pelvic floor</u>: <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>, <u>urogenital diaphragm</u>, <u>superficial perineal layer</u>

* <u>Perineal region</u>: an anatomical region inferior to the <u>pelvic floor</u>

* <u>Urogenital triangle</u>: consists of the <u>perineal membrane</u> and the deep and <u>superficial perineal pouch</u>

* <u>Anal triangle</u>: consists of the <u>anal canal</u>, the external sphincter muscle, and the <u>ischiorectal fossae</u>

* <u>Perineum</u>: <u>superficial</u> area between the <u>anus</u> and external genitalia

* Vessels, nerves, and <u>lymphatics</u>

* <u>Arteries</u>: mainly derived from the <u>internal iliac artery</u>

* <u>Veins</u>: follow the course of the <u>arteries</u> and are named accordingly

* Nerves: Innervation is mainly provided by autonomic nerves and the <u>pudendal nerve</u>.

* <u>Lymphatics</u>: travel along the iliac vessels

* <u>The gluteal region</u>: the group of muscles that form the buttocks

* <u>Superficial</u> <u>gluteal muscles</u>: <u>gluteus maximus</u>, <u>gluteus medius</u>, <u>gluteus minimus</u>, <u>tensor fasciae latae</u>

* Deep <u>gluteal muscles</u>: <u>piriformis</u>, <u>obturator internus</u>, <u>superior gemellus</u>, <u>inferior gemellus</u>, <u>quadratus femoris</u>

* <u>Embryology</u>: <u>Development of the pelvis</u> starts around week 4 of <u>embryonic development</u> and originates from the <u>paraxial mesoderm</u>.

### The <u>hip joint</u>




* Definition: a <u>joint</u> between the head of the <u>femur</u> and the <u>acetabulum</u> of the <u>pelvis</u>

* Function

* Connects the <u>axial skeleton</u> to the lower extremities

* Supports static (e.g., standing) and dynamic (e.g., walking, running) weight

* Type of <u>joint</u>: ball-and-socket <u>synovial joint</u>

* Arterial supply: <u>medial femoral circumflex artery</u>, <u>lateral femoral circumflex artery</u>, <u>foveolar artery</u>

* Movements  

* <u>Flexion</u> and extension, <u>lateral</u> and <u>medial</u> rotation, <u>abduction</u>, <u>adduction</u>

* The combination of these movements results in <u>circumduction</u>.

* <u>Articular capsule</u>: fibrous capsule that attaches to the margin of the <u>acetabulum</u>, the <u>transverse acetabular ligament</u>, and the neck of the <u>femur</u>

### Pelvic inlet




* Definition: plane that separates the abdominal and <u>pelvic</u> cavities

* Location: superior rim of the <u>pelvic cavity</u> (upper <u>pelvic</u> aperture)

* Boundaries

* <u>Anterior</u>: <u>pectineal line (pubis)</u>, pubic crest, superior margin of the <u>pubic symphysis</u>

* <u>Posterior</u>: sacral promontory, <u>anterior</u> border of the sacral ala

* <u>Lateral</u>: <u>arcuate line (ilium)</u>

* Linea terminalis: the combination of the <u>pectineal line (pubis)</u>, sacral promontory, and <u>arcuate line (ilium)</u>; forms the boundary of the <u>pelvic inlet</u>

* Diameters: transverse, oblique, conjugate (anteroposterior), anatomical conjugate, diagonal, straight, median

* Structures passing through: <u>ureter</u>, <u>spermatic cord</u>, <u>round ligament of the uterus</u>, <u>suspensory ligament of the ovary</u>, median sacral vessels, gonadal vessels, iliolumbar vessels, <u>lumbosacral trunk</u>, <u>sympathetic trunk</u>, <u>obturator nerve</u>

### Pelvic outlet




* Definition: inferior opening of the <u>pelvis</u>

* Location: lower <u>pelvic</u> aperture

* Boundaries

* <u>Anterior</u>: <u>pubic symphysis</u>, ischiopubic ramus, <u>inferior pubic ligament</u>

* Inferior: <u>urogenital diaphragm</u>, <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>

## Joints, ligaments, and foramina of the pelvis

### Joints of the pelvis




* Lumbosacral joint 

* Located between the L5 <u>vertebra</u> and the sacral promontory

* Stabilized by <u>iliolumbar ligaments</u>

* <u>Symphysis</u> <u>joint</u> with two <u>facet joints</u>

* Site of most possible movement of the <u>lumbar spine</u>

* Sacroiliac joint 

* Located between the articular surfaces of the <u>ilium</u> and <u>sacrum</u>

* Stabilized by the <u>anterior</u> and <u>posterior sacroiliac ligaments</u>

* Synovial <u>plane joint</u>

* Transmits weight from the <u>vertebral column</u> to the <u>hip bones</u>

* Inflammation results in <u>sacroiliitis</u>.

* Sacrococcygeal joint 

* Located between the <u>sacrum</u> and <u>coccyx</u>

* Stabilized by <u>lateral</u>, <u>anterior</u>, and <u>posterior</u> sacrococcygeal <u>ligaments</u>

* <u>Cartilaginous joint</u>

* Pubic symphysis

* Located between the pubic bones in the midline of the body

* <u>Fibrocartilaginous</u> or <u>cartilaginous joint</u>

* Compensates shear forces while walking

* Enables <u>childbirth</u> by partially separating the pubic bones

* <u>Hip joint</u>

* Formed by the articulation of the <u>femur</u> and the <u>acetabulum</u>

* <u>Ball and socket joint</u>

* For more details, see “<u>Hip joint</u>” below.

### Ligaments of the pelvis




* Iliolumbar ligament 

* <u>Anterior</u> attachment between the <u>transverse processes</u> of L5 and the <u>ilium</u>

* Supports the <u>lumbosacral joint</u>

* Anterior sacroiliac ligament 

* <u>Anterior</u> attachment between the <u>ilium</u> and the <u>sacrum</u>

* Supports the <u>sacroiliac joint</u>

* Posterior sacroiliac ligament 

* <u>Posterior</u> attachment between the <u>ilium</u> and the <u>sacrum</u>

* Supports the <u>sacroiliac joint</u>

* Sacrospinous ligament 

* <u>Posterior</u> attachment between the <u>sacrum</u> and the <u>ischial spine</u>

* Supports and immobilizes the <u>sacrum</u> during weight transfer from the <u>vertebral column</u>

* Sacrotuberous ligament 

* <u>Posterior</u> attachment between the <u>sacrum</u> and the <u>ischial tuberosity</u>

* Supports and immobilizes the <u>sacrum</u> during weight transfer from the <u>vertebral column</u>

* Pubic <u>ligaments</u>

* Superior pubic ligament: attachment between the two <u>pubic tubercles</u>

* Inferior pubic ligament: attachment between the two pubic bones inferiorly

* Support and stabilize the <u>pubic symphysis</u>

* Obturator membrane 

* Spans the <u>obturator foramen</u>

* Perforated by the <u>obturator canal</u>





For information on the <u>ligaments of the hip joint</u>, see “<u>Hip joint</u>” below.

## Pelvic cavity and pelvic spaces

### Pelvic cavity




* Definition: the space within the <u>pelvic girdle</u>

* Structures passing through: parts of the <u>gastrointestinal tract</u> (e.g., <u>colon</u> and <u>rectum</u>), urogenital tract (e.g., <u>ureters</u>, <u>bladder</u>, <u>uterus</u>), major <u>blood vessels</u> (e.g., <u>iliac arteries</u> and their branches), nerves (e.g., <u>pudendal nerve</u>), muscles that attach to the <u>spine</u> and the lower limbs (e.g., <u>psoas</u> muscle and <u>iliacus muscle</u>)

* Openings

* Superior opening: formed by the <u>pelvic inlet</u>

* Inferior opening: formed by the <u>pelvic outlet</u>

* Boundary: <u>pelvic floor</u> (holds <u>pelvic</u> organs in place)





### Pelvic spaces




The <u>pelvic spaces</u> include <u>peritoneal spaces</u> located within the <u>pelvic cavity</u>  and extraperitoneal anatomical spaces of the <u>pelvic cavity</u>. They vary significantly between male and female individuals due to their different reproductive organs.




* In female individuals 

* Rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas) 

* <u>Peritoneal space</u> formed by an indentation in the layer of <u>peritoneum</u> covering the <u>uterus</u>, <u>vagina</u>, and the <u>anterior</u> <u>rectum</u>

* The lowest part of the <u>peritoneal cavity</u> in the upright position in female individuals, where <u>intraperitoneal</u> fluid (e.g., <u>ascites</u>, blood, <u>pus</u>, malignant cells) and <u>endometrial</u> implants preferentially collect

* Vesicouterine pouch

* <u>Peritoneal space</u> formed by the <u>peritoneal</u> cover of the fundus and body of the <u>uterus</u> posteriorly and the posterosuperior surface of the <u>bladder</u> anteriorly

* Bounded posterolaterally by the broad and round <u>ligaments</u> of the <u>uterus</u>

* In male individuals  
* Rectovesical pouch (<u>pouch of Douglas</u>)

* <u>Peritoneal space</u> formed by an indentation in the layer of <u>peritoneum</u> that covers the <u>posterior</u> <u>bladder</u> and <u>anterior</u> <u>rectum</u>

* The lowest part of the <u>peritoneal cavity</u> in the upright position in male individuals, where <u>intraperitoneal</u> fluid (e.g., <u>ascites</u>, blood, <u>pus</u>) preferentially collects

* In female and male individuals

* Pararectal fossa

* <u>Peritoneal space</u> formed by folds of <u>peritoneum</u> on either side of the <u>rectum</u> and the <u>lateral</u> wall of the <u>pelvis</u>

* Laterally continuous with the <u>rectovesical pouch</u> in male individuals and the <u>rectouterine pouch</u> in female individuals

* Ischiorectal fossae

* Extraperitoneal, fat-filled spaces inferior to the <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>

* Surround the <u>anal canal</u>

* <u>Lateral</u> walls contain the <u>pudendal nerve</u> on each side of the <u>Alcock canal</u>.

* Allow expansion of the <u>anal canal</u> during defecation

* Retropubic space: extraperitoneal, fat-filled space between the <u>pubis</u> and the <u>anterior</u> surface of the <u>bladder</u>

## Pelvic floor and perineal region

### Pelvic floor




* Supports the abdominal and <u>pelvic</u> <u>viscera</u>

* Prevents prolapse of the <u>pelvic</u> organs

* Assists in increasing <u>intraabdominal pressure</u>

* Plays an integral role in urinary continence and defecation

* For information on the <u>defecation process</u>, see “Function” in “<u>Large intestine</u>.”

* For information on the <u>urination</u> process, see “<u>Urinary bladder</u>” in “<u>Overview of the urinary tract</u>.”

* Contains two openings formed by a gap between the <u>levator ani muscles</u>

* Urogenital hiatus

* Allows passage of <u>urethra</u> and <u>vagina</u> (in female individuals)

* <u>Anterior</u> to the <u>anus</u>

* Rectal hiatus

* Allows passage of the <u>rectum</u>

* Centrally positioned in the <u>pelvic floor</u>, <u>posterior</u> to the <u>perineal body</u>

* Stabilized by the perineal body: <u>central tendon</u> of the <u>perineum</u>, located between the anal and <u>urogenital hiatus</u>





#### Layers of the <u>pelvic floor</u>




##### Pelvic diaphragm




* Description: innermost layer of the <u>pelvic floor</u>

* Components

* <u>Levator ani muscle</u>

* Broad muscular sheet that forms the majority of the musculature of the <u>pelvic floor</u>

* Can be divided into three separate muscles: <u>pubococcygeus</u>, <u>puborectalis</u>, <u>iliococcygeus</u>

* <u>Coccygeus muscle</u>: <u>posterior</u> to the <u>levator ani muscle</u>

* Surrounded by the superior and inferior <u>fascia</u> of the <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>

* Anococcygeal ligament: a fibrous raphe mainly formed by fibers of the <u>levator ani muscles</u> uniting in the midline; connected to the <u>coccyx</u> and located <u>posterior</u> to the <u>anus</u>

### Perineal region




* Definition: an anatomical region inferior to the <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>

* Boundaries

* <u>Anterior</u>: <u>pubic symphysis</u>

* <u>Posterior</u>: <u>coccyx</u>

* Superior: <u>pelvic diaphragm</u>

* Inferior: <u>skin</u>





#### Divisions of the <u>perineal region</u>




The <u>perineal region</u> can be divided by an imaginary line between the <u>ischial tuberosities</u>. This line forms two triangles within the <u>perineal region</u>, the <u>urogenital triangle</u> anteriorly and the <u>anal triangle</u> posteriorly. The contents of each triangle are listed below.




* Urogenital triangle

* Deep perineal pouch

* Space between the superior and <u>inferior fascia of the urogenital diaphragm</u> (<u>perineal membrane</u>)

* Contains the <u>deep transverse perineal muscle</u>, <u>urethral sphincter</u>, part of the <u>urethra</u>, and the <u>vagina</u> in female individuals

* Perineal membrane

* A triangular-shaped sheet of dense fibrous tissue

* Provides attachment to the muscles of the external genitalia

* Allows passage of the <u>urethra</u> and <u>vagina</u>

* Superficial perineal pouch

* Space between the <u>inferior fascia of the urogenital diaphragm</u> (<u>perineal membrane</u>) and the <u>Colles fascia</u>

* <u>Anal canal</u> and orifice

* <u>External anal sphincter</u> muscle

* <u>Ischiorectal fossae</u>

* Perineum

* A diamond-shaped, <u>superficial</u> area between the <u>anus</u> and external genitalia

* Bounded laterally by the <u>medial</u> thighs, anteriorly by the <u>mons pubis</u> (in female individuals) or the base of the <u>penis</u> (in male individuals), and posteriorly by the superior end of the <u>intergluteal cleft</u>

## Vessels, nerves, and lymphatics of the pelvis

### <u>Arteries</u>




#### Internal iliac artery




The blood supply of the <u>pelvis</u> is primarily derived from the <u>internal iliac artery</u>, which originates from the bifurcation of the <u>common iliac artery</u>. The <u>internal iliac artery</u> divides into the <u>anterior</u> trunk and the <u>posterior</u> trunk.




* <u>Anterior</u> trunk

* <u>Umbilical artery</u>

* <u>Artery</u> to the <u>ductus deferens</u>

* Superior vesical <u>arteries</u>: blood supply for the superior aspect of the <u>bladder</u>

* Inferior vesical artery: supplies the fundus of the <u>bladder</u> and lower part of the <u>ureter</u>; in male individuals also supplies the <u>prostate gland</u>, <u>seminal vesicles</u>, and <u>ductus deferens</u>

* Vaginal artery: supplies the <u>anterior</u> and <u>posterior</u> wall of the <u>vagina</u>

* Uterine artery (in female individuals)

* Superior branch: supplies the body and <u>fundus of the uterus</u>

* Vaginal branch: supplies the <u>cervix</u> of the <u>uterus</u> and the <u>vagina</u>

* <u>Anastomoses</u> with the <u>ovarian artery</u>

* Inferior gluteal artery: supplies <u>the gluteal region</u> and thigh

* Middle rectal artery

* Supplies the lower <u>rectum</u>, upper <u>anal canal</u>, and the <u>ureter</u>

* In male individuals: supplies the <u>prostate gland</u> and <u>seminal vesicles</u>

* In female individuals: supplies the <u>vagina</u>

* Obturator artery: supplies the <u>medial compartment of the thigh</u> and the head of the <u>femur</u> (via the <u>foveolar artery</u>)

* <u>Internal pudendal artery</u>: supplies the <u>perineum</u>, genitalia, <u>anal canal</u>, and erectile tissues

* <u>Posterior</u> trunk

* Iliolumbar artery

* Iliac branch: supplies the <u>ilium</u> and the <u>iliacus muscle</u>

* Lumbar branch: supplies the <u>quadratus lumborum muscle</u> and the <u>psoas major muscle</u>

* Lateral sacral arteries: supply the <u>skin</u> <u>posterior</u> to the <u>sacrum</u>

* Superior gluteal artery: supplies the muscles and <u>skin</u> of <u>the gluteal region</u>

#### Other <u>arteries</u>




* Median sacral artery

* Arises from the <u>posterior</u> wall of the aorta, <u>proximal</u> to the bifurcation

* Supplies the <u>coccyx</u>, <u>lumbar vertebrae</u>, and <u>sacrum</u>

* Superior rectal artery

* Continuation of the <u>inferior mesenteric artery</u>

* Supplies the <u>anal canal</u> <u>above the pectinate line</u> (upper ⅔ of the <u>rectum</u>)

* Ovarian artery

* Arises from the <u>abdominal aorta</u> inferior to the <u>renal arteries</u>

* Crosses the <u>proximal</u> end of the <u>external iliac artery</u>

* Supplies the <u>ovary</u> and the <u>lateral</u> third of the <u>fallopian tube</u> through the <u>suspensory ligament of the ovary</u>

* Forms an <u>anastomosis</u> with the ovarian branch of the uterine <u>arteries</u>





### <u>Veins</u>




The <u>veins</u> follow the course of the <u>arteries</u> and are named accordingly, and they drain the same structures supplied by the <u>arteries</u> (e.g., <u>internal iliac artery</u> and internal iliac vein).

### Nerves




#### Autonomic innervation




* <u>Parasympathetic</u> and <u>sympathetic</u> fibers

* <u>Superior hypogastric plexus</u>: distribution of fibers to the nerve plexus of the urogenital organs (e.g., <u>testicular plexus</u>, <u>ureteric plexus</u>); innervates <u>ovaries</u>, <u>testes</u>, <u>hindgut</u>, and the middle third of the <u>ureter</u>

* <u>Inferior hypogastric plexus</u> (<u>pelvic plexus</u>)

* Distribution of fibers to the nerve plexus of urogenital organs (e.g., <u>prostatic plexus</u>, <u>uterovaginal plexus</u>, <u>ovarian plexus</u>) and thereby innervates a majority of <u>viscera</u> within the <u>pelvic cavity</u> (e.g., <u>distal</u> third of the <u>ureter</u>, <u>ovaries</u>, <u>uterus</u>, upper <u>vagina</u>, <u>prostate</u>)

* Directly innervates <u>rectum</u>, <u>internal anal sphincter</u>, <u>bladder</u>, and <u>urethra</u>

* <u>Hypogastric nerve</u>: responsible for the emission of semen into the <u>posterior urethra</u> in male individuals

* <u>Parasympathetic</u> fibers: <u>pelvic splanchnic nerves</u> innervate the <u>hindgut</u> and <u>pelvic</u> organs (e.g., <u>bladder</u>)

* <u>Sympathetic</u> fibers: <u>sacral splanchnic nerves</u> <u>synapse</u> at the <u>inferior hypogastric plexus</u>





For additional information on the autonomic <u>innervation of the pelvis</u>, see “Nerves of the <u>abdominal cavity</u>” in “<u>Abdominal cavity</u>.”




#### Somatic innervation



* Pudendal nerve (<u>S2</u>–<u>S4</u>)
* Innervated structures

* Motor: external <u>urethral sphincter</u>, <u>external anal sphincter</u>, <u>levator ani</u>, <u>bulbospongiosus muscle</u>, <u>ischiocavernosus muscle</u>

* Sensory

* <u>Perineum</u>, <u>penis</u>/<u>clitoris</u>, <u>posterior</u> <u>scrotum</u>/labia, <u>anal canal</u>

* Responsible for <u>reflex erection</u>

* <u>Sympathetic</u>

* Fibers to the <u>penis</u>/<u>clitoris</u>

* Responsible for male <u>ejaculation</u>




For additional information on the lumbar and <u>sacral plexus</u> see ”<u>Thigh, knee, and popliteal fossa</u>” and for information on the innervation of the <u>gluteal muscles</u>, see “<u>The gluteal region</u>” below.




### <u>Lymphatics</u>




The <u>pelvic</u> <u>lymphatics</u> course along the internal iliac vessels and drain into the <u>lymph nodes</u> listed below.




* Inferior <u>mesenteric</u> and aortic <u>lymph nodes</u>: receive <u>lymph</u> from the upper part of the <u>rectum</u>

* <u>Paraaortic lymph nodes</u>: drain the <u>ovaries</u>, <u>fallopian tubes</u>, and the <u>fundus of the uterus</u>

* Internal and <u>external iliac lymph nodes</u>: drain the body and <u>cervix</u> of the <u>uterus</u>, <u>bladder</u>, <u>prostate</u>, and <u>rectum</u>

## Hip joint

### Articulating bones




* Acetabulum 

* Cup-like structure on the <u>lateral</u> side of the bony <u>pelvis</u> that articulates with the round femoral head

* Covered by the acetabular labrum: ring of <u>cartilage</u> that provides an articular surface for the femoral head

* Stabilized by capsular <u>ligaments</u> (<u>iliofemoral ligament</u>, <u>ischiofemoral ligament</u>, <u>pubofemoral ligament</u>)

* Surrounded by a fibrous capsule

* Attaches to the margin of the <u>acetabulum</u> and to the <u>transverse acetabular ligament</u>

* Attaches to the neck of the <u>femur</u>

* <u>Anterior</u>: intertrochanteric line (<u>anterior</u>) and the greater trochanter

* <u>Posterior</u>: intertrochanteric crest

* Encloses the femoral head and neck

* <u>Femur</u>: See “The <u>femur</u>” in “<u>Thigh, knee, and popliteal fossa</u>.”

### Ligaments of the hip joint




| Overview of the <u>ligaments of the hip joint</u> |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Description | Function |
| Iliofemoral ligament | <br><br>* Reinforces the fibrous capsule anteriorly<br><br>* Y-shaped <u>ligament</u> that runs from the <u>anterior</u> inferior iliac <u>spine</u> and the <u>acetabular</u> rim to the intertrochanteric line and the greater trochanter<br><br>* Strongest <u>ligament</u> in the human body<br><br><br> | <br>* Prevents excessive <u>lateral</u> rotation and hyperextension of the hip (in the standing position)<br><br> |
| Ischiofemoral ligament | <br><br>* Reinforces the fibrous capsule posteriorly<br><br>* Runs from the ischial region of the <u>acetabulum</u> to the neck of the <u>femur</u> <u>medial</u> to the greater trochanter<br><br><br> | <br>* Limits <u>medial</u> rotation and extension of the thigh<br><br> |
| Pubofemoral ligament | <br><br>* Runs from the pubic part of the <u>acetabular</u> rim and the superior pubic ramus to the lower part of the femoral neck<br><br>* Reinforces the fibrous capsule inferiorly<br><br><br> | <br>* Limits <u>abduction</u> and extension<br><br> |
| Transverse acetabular ligament | <br>* Fibrous structure on the lower part of the <u>acetabulum</u> that converts the <u>acetabular</u> notch into a foramen<br><br> | <br>* Allows passage of vessels and nerves into the <u>joint</u> space<br><br> |
| Round ligament of the head of femur | <br>* Originates within the <u>acetabular</u> fossa and attaches to the <u>fovea capitis femoris</u> (on the head of the <u>femur</u>)<br><br> | <br>* Forms a pathway for the <u>foveolar artery</u> to the head of the <u>femur</u><br><br> |

### Arterial supply of the hip joint




* Superior and inferior gluteal <u>arteries</u>

* <u>Deep femoral artery</u>: main branch of the <u>femoral artery</u>

* Medial femoral circumflex artery

* Arises from the posteromedial side of the <u>profunda femoris artery</u>

* Supplies the head and neck of the <u>femur</u>

* Lateral femoral circumflex artery

* Arises from the <u>lateral</u> side of the <u>profunda femoris artery</u>

* Supplies the head and neck of the <u>femur</u>

* Foveolar artery

* Most commonly arises from the <u>obturator artery</u> and less commonly from the <u>medial circumflex femoral artery</u>

* Passes through the <u>ligament</u> of the <u>femur</u> head

* Supplies the head of the <u>femur</u>

## Clinical significance

* <u>Sacroiliitis</u> (associated with e.g., <u>ankylosing spondylitis</u>, <u>inflammatory bowel disease</u>)

* <u>Pelvic organ prolapse</u>

* <u>Obturator hernia</u>

* <u>Femoral hernia</u>

* <u>Hip replacement</u>

* <u>Hip fractures</u>

* <u>Coxarthrosis</u>

* <u>Avascular necrosis of the femoral head</u> (due to insufficient supply by the <u>foveolar artery</u>)

* <u>Trendelenburg sign</u>: Injury to the <u>superior gluteal nerve</u> leads to <u>paralysis</u> of the <u>gluteus medius</u> and minimus that results in a positive <u>Trendelenburg sign</u> and a <u>Trendelenburg gait</u>.

* Pudendal nerve block: injection of a <u>local anesthetic</u> into the <u>pudendal canal</u> to block transmission within the <u>pudendal nerve</u>

* Most commonly used during <u>childbirth</u> prior to an <u>episiotomy</u> or during surgical procedures involving the <u>perineum</u>

* The <u>ischial spine</u> is used as a landmark for injection.

* <u>Pudendal nerve injury</u> (due to, e.g., <u>pelvic</u> trauma, <u>pelvic</u> <u>surgery</u>) → urinary and/or <u>fecal incontinence</u>

* Piriformis syndrome

* A condition caused by entrapment of the <u>sciatic nerve</u> due to trauma or strain of the <u>piriformis muscle</u>

* Manifests with buttock <u>pain</u> and <u>paresthesia</u> in the distribution of the <u>sciatic nerve</u>

* <u>Urinary incontinence</u>

* <u>Premature ejaculation</u>

* <u>Pouch of Douglas</u>

* In the upright position, the <u>pouch of Douglas</u> is the lowest part of the <u>peritoneal cavity</u>, where blood, <u>pus</u>, malignant cells, and <u>endometrial</u> implants preferentially collect.

* Important in the diagnosis of gynecological conditions and emergencies (e.g., <u>ectopic pregnancy</u>, <u>pelvic inflammatory disease</u>)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Kegel exercises</u> involve the contraction of the entire <u>levator ani</u>. These exercises especially strengthen the <u>pubococcygeus</u> muscles, which can treat <u>stress incontinence</u>, <u>urge incontinence</u>, and <u>pelvic organ prolapse</u> and assists in <u>ejaculation</u>.
