## Summary

Basic <u>calcium</u> <u>phosphate</u> (BCP) crystal deposition diseases are an uncommon form of <u>crystalline arthropathy</u>. BCP crystals include several different types of <u>calcium</u> <u>phosphate</u> crystals, such as partially carbonate-substituted <u>hydroxyapatite</u>, octacalcium <u>phosphate</u>, and tricalcium <u>phosphate</u>. These crystals are deposited in <u>joints</u> or in periarticular structures, causing BCP-associated <u>arthritis</u> (including <u>Milwaukee shoulder syndrome</u>) and <u>calcific tendinitis</u>. The underlying etiology of articular and periarticular BCP crystal deposition is unknown, but <u>risk factors</u> include female sex, <u>joint</u> trauma, and various metabolic abnormalities. The presentation and management of BCP-associated <u>osteoarthritis</u> are similar to that of age-related <u>osteoarthritis</u>. <u>Milwaukee shoulder syndrome</u> and acute <u>calcific tendinitis</u> manifest with <u>joint</u> <u>pain</u> and swelling, most commonly of the shoulder or other <u>large joints</u>. Diagnosis is based on clinical and radiological findings. <u>Synovial fluid analysis</u> may be helpful in excluding differential diagnoses (e.g., <u>acute gout</u>, <u>pseudogout</u>, <u>septic arthritis</u>) but rarely identifies the small, nonbirefringent BCP crystals. Management is primarily supportive and includes <u>pain</u> relief for acute episodes, physical dissolution of crystals by various methods (e.g., needle <u>aspiration</u>, <u>ultrasound therapy</u>), and <u>surgery</u> in severe cases.
