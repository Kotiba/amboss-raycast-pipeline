# Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vr0AQh)

## Summary

<u>Heparin</u>-induced <u>thrombocytopenia</u> (HIT; formerly called type 2 HIT) is an immune-mediated prothrombotic disorder characterized by a sudden drop in <u>platelet count</u> (typically > 50% from baseline) in a patient receiving <u>heparin-containing products</u>. It typically occurs within 5–10 days of <u>heparin</u> initiation, but it can occur earlier in patients with recent prior <u>heparin</u> exposure. The disorder is mediated by <u>platelet</u>-activating <u>autoantibodies</u> and frequently leads to significant thromboembolic complications. Bleeding, on the other hand, is rare. HIT should be suspected in any patient with recent <u>heparin</u> exposure who develops <u>thrombocytopenia</u>, venous or arterial <u>thrombosis</u>, <u>skin</u> <u>necrosis</u> at <u>heparin</u> injection sites, <u>DIC</u>, or an <u>anaphylactoid reaction</u> to <u>heparin</u>. HIT should be differentiated from the more common <u>nonimmune heparin-associated thrombocytopenia</u> (formerly called <u>type 1 HIT</u>), which is a transient and clinically insignificant mild to moderate <u>thrombocytopenia</u> that can be managed with serial monitoring of <u>platelet counts</u> and does not require discontinuation of <u>heparin</u>. <u>Empirical management of HIT</u> should be initiated immediately in any patient with an <u>intermediate or high pretest probability of HIT</u> (i.e., <u>4Ts score</u> ≥ 4) while confirmatory <u>HIT-specific diagnostic testing</u> is pursued. Management consists of the immediate cessation of <u>heparin</u> therapy and treatment with a <u>nonheparin anticoagulant</u> at least until the resolution of <u>thrombocytopenia</u> (longer if concurrent <u>thrombosis</u> is present). Lifetime avoidance of <u>heparin</u>-containing products is indicated following recovery from HIT.

## Definitions

<u>Heparin</u>-induced <u>thrombocytopenia</u> (HIT; formerly called type 2 HIT) is an <u>antibody</u>-mediated response to <u>heparin</u> leading to <u>thrombocytopenia</u> and an increased risk of <u>thrombosis</u>.




* Isolated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (<u>isolated HIT</u>): acute HIT in the absence of <u>thrombosis</u>

* Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia with thrombosis (<u>HITT</u>): acute HIT complicated by <u>thrombosis</u> and/or <u>thromboembolism</u>





> [!TIP]
> Unlike other drug-induced immune <u>thrombocytopenias</u>, HIT leads to <u>thrombosis</u> in 25-50% of affected patients.

---

## Etiology

* An immune-mediated <u>adverse drug reaction</u> to <u>heparin</u> (see “Pathophysiology”)

* Risk factors for HIT include: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FVXgwC)

* Female sex

* <u>Surgery</u> (involving the use of <u>cardiopulmonary bypass</u> in particular)

* Major trauma

* Use of <u>unfractionated heparin</u>

* Longer duration of <u>heparin</u> exposure (> 5 days)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Heparin</u> treatment, especially with <u>unfractionated heparin</u> (<u>UFH</u>), often causes <u>thrombocytopenia</u>. For this reason, a baseline check and regular monitoring of <u>platelet counts</u> are required.

---

## Pathophysiology

* <u>Heparin</u> and <u>platelet</u> factor 4 (PF4) form a complex → production of <u>IgG antibodies</u> against the <u>heparin</u>/PF4 complex → <u>IgG antibody</u>-<u>heparin</u>/PF4 immunocomplex binds on <u>platelet</u> surface → <u>platelet activation</u> and aggregation → consumption of <u>platelets</u> (<u>thrombocytopenia</u>) and arterial/venous <u>thrombosis</u>

* <u>Thrombocytopenia</u> also occurs due to <u>phagocytosis</u> of <u>IgG antibody</u>-<u>heparin</u>/PF4 immunocomplex-bound <u>platelets</u> by <u>macrophages</u> in the <u>spleen</u>, <u>liver</u>, and <u>bone marrow</u>.

## Clinical features

### Overview




* HIT is primarily a prothrombotic disorder, with venous and/or arterial <u>thrombosis</u> developing in 25–50% of affected patients.

* <u>Clinical features of thrombocytopenia</u> are uncommon because HIT rarely causes <u>platelet counts</u> to decrease < 20,000/mmÂ³.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)





> [!TIP]
> HIT more commonly manifests with symptoms of <u>thrombosis</u> than with bleeding or other <u>clinical features of thrombocytopenia</u>.




#### <u>Thrombotic</u> manifestations




* Venous <u>thrombosis</u> occurs most frequently; manifestations include:

* <u>Acute chest pain</u> and <u>dyspnea</u> from <u>PE</u>

* Calf swelling and <u>pain</u> from lower extremity <u>DVT</u>

* Arterial <u>thrombosis</u> occurs less commonly; manifestations include:

* Abdominal <u>pain</u> from <u>acute mesenteric ischemia</u>

* New-onset neurological deficits from <u>stroke</u>

* <u>Chest pain</u> from <u>myocardial infarction</u>

* Limb <u>pain</u> and <u>pallor</u> from <u>acute limb ischemia</u>





#### Other manifestations




* Localized <u>skin</u> <u>necrosis</u> at <u>heparin</u> injection sites (seen in 10–20% of cases) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)

* <u>Clinical features of DIC</u> (seen in up to 20% of cases)   [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)

* Bleeding at unusual sites (e.g., <u>adrenal hemorrhage</u>, cerebral bleed)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2nXTsA)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fnXksA)

* <u>Anaphylactoid reactions</u> from IV <u>heparin</u>

## Diagnostics

### Initial diagnostics




* <u>CBC</u>: <u>PLT</u> < 50% of the baseline count at <u>heparin</u> initiation 

* Timing of <u>PLT</u> decline: usually within 5–7 days of <u>heparin</u> initiation ; can occur rapidly (within 1–3 days)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/bnXH7A)

* Typical <u>PLT</u> <u>nadir</u>: 40,000–80,000 /mm³  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)

* <u>Coagulation studies</u>: ↑ <u>PTT</u>, ↑ <u>INR</u>, and ↓ <u>fibrinogen</u> may be seen in <u>DIC</u> or massive <u>thrombosis</u>.

* Tests to rule out differential diagnoses

* <u>Peripheral blood smear</u>: to exclude <u>pseudothrombocytopenia</u> and other findings consistent with an alternative diagnosis

* <u>Renal function tests</u>: may be abnormal in other <u>causes of thrombocytopenia</u> (e.g., <u>HUS</u> and <u>TTP</u>)

* <u>Liver chemistries</u>: may be abnormal in other <u>causes of thrombocytopenia</u> (e.g., chronic <u>alcohol abuse</u>, <u>cirrhosis</u>, <u>HELLP syndrome</u>)





> [!TIP]
> A rapidly falling <u>platelet count</u> in a patient with recent <u>heparin</u> exposure should raise concern for HIT, even if the <u>platelet count</u> is still within the normal range.




> [!TIP]
> HIT rarely causes <u>platelet counts</u> to decrease < 20,000/mmÂ³. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)

> [!WARNING]

### Confirmatory diagnostic studies (HIT-specific diagnostic tests) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/R3XlRB)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)




<u>Confirmatory testing</u> is indicated in patients with an <u>intermediate or high PTP of HIT</u> and is a two-stage process.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)




#### Step one: Order PF4 heparin immunoassay (e.g., <u>ELISA</u>).




* Goal: to detect <u>antibodies</u> against <u>heparin</u>/PF4 complexes

* Findings and further management

* Negative: HIT is unlikely (see “HIT ruled out/unlikely” in the “Management” section).

* Positive: HIT is possible; proceed to step two.

* Important consideration: In patients with a <u>high PTP of HIT</u>  and a strongly positive <u>heparin</u> immunoassay , the diagnosis of HIT may be established without further testing. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)





#### Step two: Order functional platelet activation assay (e.g., serotonin release assay).




* Goal: to identify clinically significant anti-<u>heparin</u>/PF4 <u>antibodies</u> (i.e., those that cause <u>platelet activation</u>)

* Findings and further management

* Negative: HIT is unlikely (see “Hit ruled out/unlikely” in the “Management” section).

* Positive: HIT is confirmed.

* Important consideration

* As false-negatives may occur, a diagnosis of HIT may still be considered in patients with a high <u>PTP</u> of HIT  and a strongly positive <u>heparin</u> immunoassay.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)

* Results may take days ; continue <u>empirical treatment of HIT</u> while awaiting results.





### Imaging




#### Screening for asymptomatic <u>deep vein thrombosis</u> (<u>DVT</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)




* Indication: confirmed or strongly suspected HIT

* Modalities

* All patients: bilateral lower extremity venous <u>compression ultrasonography</u>

* Patients with <u>central venous catheters</u>: upper extremity venous <u>compression ultrasonography</u> on side with catheter





#### Additional imaging based on clinical suspicion [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)




* Indications: suspected <u>thrombosis</u> or hemorrhage due to HIT

* Examples

* Abdominal imaging : in patients with <u>acute abdominal pain</u> or <u>hypotension</u>

* <u>CT pulmonary angiography</u> (<u>CTPA</u>) to rule out <u>pulmonary embolism</u> in patients with <u>acute chest pain</u>, <u>dyspnea</u>, and/or <u>hypoxia</u>

* <u>MRI</u> or CT head to rule out <u>cerebral venous thrombosis</u> or <u>stroke</u> in patients with new onset <u>headache</u>, <u>seizure</u>, and/or neurological deficit

## Treatment

Initiate <u>empirical management of HIT</u> in all patients with intermediate or high <u>PTP</u> for HIT, without waiting for diagnostic confirmation (see “Management” section above for initial steps). The key features of management include immediate discontinuation of <u>heparin</u> and preventing and treating complications such as <u>thrombosis</u>.




### Empirical management of HIT [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)[[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/J3XsjB)




* Stop all <u>heparin</u> exposure immediately (including <u>heparin</u> flushes and <u>heparin</u>-coated catheters).

* Patients on <u>vitamin K antagonists</u> (e.g., <u>warfarin</u>): Stop the medication and consider reversal with <u>vitamin K</u>. DOSAGE

* Consult <u>hematology</u>.

* Screen for associated <u>thrombosis</u> (see “Imaging” in “Diagnostics”).

* Initiate <u>nonheparin anticoagulation</u>; the choice of <u>anticoagulant</u> depends on the severity of illness and/or the presence of <u>thrombosis</u>.

* Consider <u>platelet transfusion</u> only for significant bleeding or patients at high risk of bleeding (e.g., during invasive procedures).





### <u>Nonheparin anticoagulation</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/33XSRB)




#### General principles




* Choice of <u>anticoagulant</u>

* Initiate <u>fondaparinux</u> or oral anticoagulation in clinically stable patients.

* Initiate <u>parenteral anticoagulation</u> before transitioning to oral therapy in the following instances:

* Critically ill patients

* Increased risk of bleeding

* Life- or limb-threatening <u>thrombosis</u> (e.g., <u>venous limb gangrene</u>, <u>massive pulmonary embolus</u>)

* Need for an urgent invasive procedure

* Dosing: A therapeutic dose should be used to counteract the large amounts of <u>thrombin</u> generated during HIT in all patients.

* Duration of <u>nonheparin anticoagulation</u>: The duration of therapy depends on the presence or absence of <u>thrombosis</u>.

* <u>HITT</u>: ≥ 3 months  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)

* <u>Isolated HIT</u> 

* Patients with <u>platelet</u> recovery (<u>platelets</u> â¥ 150,000/mmÂ³): maximum duration of 3 months

* Patients with persistent <u>thrombocytopenia</u> due to HIT: Continue <u>nonheparin anticoagulation</u> until <u>platelet</u> recovery (optimal duration unknown).





> [!NOTE]
> A significantly increased risk of bleeding is the main side effect of all <u>anticoagulants</u>.




#### Parenteral therapy  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)




* Intravenous <u>direct thrombin inhibitors</u>

* Preferred in critical illness and in patients with increased risk of bleeding or potential need for urgent procedure

* Options: <u>argatroban</u> DOSAGE   or <u>bivalirudin</u> DOSAGE

* Subcutaneous <u>synthetic heparin</u>: <u>fondaparinux</u> DOSAGE





#### Oral therapy [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/QhXudB)




* First line: <u>DOACs</u> 

* Can be started immediately in clinically stable patients

* In unstable patients, <u>DOACs</u> may be started as soon as the patient is stable (<u>platelet count</u> recovery not required), with no need for overlap with <u>parenteral anticoagulation</u>.

* Options: <u>rivaroxaban</u> DOSAGE, <u>apixaban</u> DOSAGE, or <u>dabigatran</u> DOSAGE  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)

* Second line: <u>warfarin</u> DOSAGE

* Should not be used in the acute <u>management of HIT</u>

* Transition to <u>warfarin</u> only once <u>platelet count</u> has recovered (usually to â¥ 150,000/mmÂ³).

* Overlap <u>warfarin</u> therapy with ongoing <u>anticoagulants</u> for at least 5 days and until the target <u>INR</u> (typically 2–3) is reached.  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/J3XsjB)





### Ongoing management




* Lifetime avoidance of <u>heparin-containing products</u>

* Ensure patient awareness and medical record documentation.

* Use nonheparin <u>anticoagulants</u> for <u>VTE</u> treatment/prophylaxis in all patients with a prior history of HIT. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/S3Xy3B)

* Patients who were on <u>VKA</u> before HIT: Restart <u>VKA</u> once the <u>platelet count</u> has recovered.

## Differential diagnoses

* Nonimmune heparin-associated thrombocytopenia (formerly called <u>type 1 HIT</u>)

* A transient and clinically insignificant mild to moderate <u>thrombocytopenia</u>, usually occurring within 5 days of <u>heparin</u> initiation

* Caused by a direct effect of <u>heparin</u> on <u>platelet activation</u>

* Not associated with an increased risk of <u>thrombosis</u>

* See also “<u>Differential diagnosis of platelet disorders</u>.”
