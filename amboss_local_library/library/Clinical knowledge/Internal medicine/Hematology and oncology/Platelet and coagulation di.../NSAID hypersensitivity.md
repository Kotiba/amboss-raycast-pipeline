## Summary

<u>NSAID</u> hypersensitivity refers to a range of <u>adverse reactions</u> to <u>NSAIDs</u> and includes the <u>pseudoallergic reactions</u> <u>aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease</u> (<u>AERD</u>), <u>NSAID</u>-exacerbated cutaneous disease (NECD), and <u>NSAID</u>-induced <u>urticaria</u>/<u>angioedema</u> (NIUA), and the <u>allergic reactions</u> single <u>NSAID</u>-induced <u>urticaria</u>/<u>angioedema</u> or <u>anaphylaxis</u> (SNIUAA), and single <u>NSAID</u>-induced <u>delayed hypersensitivity reaction</u> (SNIDHR). The underlying mechanism involves an imbalance between proinflammatory <u>leukotrienes</u> and anti-inflammatory <u>prostaglandins</u> due to <u>cyclooxygenase-1</u> (<u>COX-1</u>) inhibition (e.g., in <u>AERD</u>, NECD, and NIUA) or an immunologic <u>allergic reaction</u> (e.g., in SNIUAA and SNIDHR). Diagnosis is primarily based on history and clinical presentation and, in some cases, drug challenge testing. Treatment involves discontinuation of the offending <u>NSAID</u>, management of severe reactions (e.g., <u>anaphylaxis</u>), and desensitization for patients requiring continued <u>NSAID</u> use. Nasal polypectomy and/or <u>aspirin</u> desensitization may be necessary to manage symptoms in patients with <u>AERD</u>.

## Overview

### <u>Epidemiology</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gLdFCq0)




* <u>NSAID</u> hypersensitivity <u>prevalence</u>: up to 5.7% of the general population  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gLdFCq0)

* <u>Aspirin</u> hypersensitivity <u>prevalence</u>: up to 2% of the general population [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/cLda9q0)
