## Summary

Hepatic <u>angiosarcoma</u> is a rare, aggressive <u>malignancy</u> arising from the <u>liver</u> <u>endothelium</u>, predominantly affecting men over 60 years of age. While often <u>idiopathic</u>, it is associated with environmental exposures such as <u>vinyl chloride</u> and <u>arsenic</u>. Individuals present with nonspecific abdominal symptoms or acutely with <u>tumor</u> rupture and <u>intraperitoneal</u> hemorrhage. Diagnosis relies on imaging confirmed with <u>histopathology</u> findings. Management is often limited by advanced disease at the time of diagnosis. Surgical resection offers the best chance for survival but is only possible in a minority of cases. <u>Liver transplantation</u> is contraindicated due to high recurrence rates. For unresectable disease, treatment is palliative and may include <u>transarterial embolization</u> and <u>chemotherapy</u>. The overall prognosis is poor, with a median survival of approximately 6 months.

## Etiology

* Environmental and <u>iatrogenic</u> exposures [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hYVcKG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)

* <u>Vinyl chloride</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)

* Thorium dioxide

* <u>Arsenic</u>

* <u>Anabolic steroids</u>

* Radiation

* <u>Exogenous</u> <u>estrogens</u>

* <u>Idiopathic</u>  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hYVcKG0)

## Diagnostics

### Imaging [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3YVSKG0)




On imaging, hepatic <u>angiosarcoma</u> appears as a single heterogeneous mass, multiple masses, or infiltrative lesions.




* <u>Contrast-enhanced CT</u>

* Lesions are typically hypoenhancing on postcontrast phases.

* <u>Hyperdense</u> areas on noncontrast phases if hemorrhage is present

* <u>MRI</u>: heterogeneous lesions with signs of hemorrhage





### <u>Biopsy</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hYVcKG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)




* Required for definitive diagnosis

* <u>Laparoscopic</u> or <u>open biopsy</u> is preferred.  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/hYVcKG0)

* Histopathologic findings are heterogeneous.

* <u>Immunohistochemistry</u>: positive for <u>endothelial</u> markers, e.g.,

* Erythroblast transformation specific-related <u>gene</u> (<u>ERG</u>)

* <u>PECAM-1</u> (<u>CD31</u>)

* <u>CD34</u>

---

## Prognosis

* Median overall survival: ∼ 6 months [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)

* <u>Metastases</u> are often present at the time of diagnosis.

* High recurrence rate following resection

* Poor prognostic factors: older age, large <u>tumor</u> size, high <u>Ki-67 index</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYVyoG0)
