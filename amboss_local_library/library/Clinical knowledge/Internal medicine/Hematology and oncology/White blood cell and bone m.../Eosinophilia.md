## Summary

Eosinophilia is an elevation of <u>eosinophils</u> in the peripheral circulation (absolute count > 500/mmÂ³). Eosinophilia can be primary (e.g., due to a hematologic disorder), secondary (e.g., due to infection or inflammation), or <u>idiopathic</u>. Diagnosis involves a detailed history and examination and studies such as <u>CBC with differential</u>, a <u>peripheral blood smear</u> (<u>PBS</u>), and <u>inflammatory markers</u>. Additional studies to assess for tissue hypereosinophilia, secondary causes, and <u>end-organ damage</u> are chosen based on clinical presentation and suspected cause. Management is based on the underlying cause.

## Etiology

The following list includes common and/or significant <u>causes of eosinophilia</u>. It is not exhaustive. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C3dqkp0)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/w3dhkp0)




* Allergic conditions

* <u>Allergic rhinitis</u>

* <u>Asthma</u>

* Medications

* Medications that cause <u>drug hypersensitivity reactions</u> (e.g., <u>DRESS</u>)

* <u>Interleukin-2</u> therapy

* Dermatologic conditions

* <u>Atopic dermatitis</u>

* <u>Eczema</u>

* <u>Dermatitis herpetiformis</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HKYK3J)

* <u>Erythema multiforme</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tddX7K0)

* <u>Bullous pemphigoid</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HKYK3J)

* Infections

* Parasitic (e.g., <u>strongyloidiasis</u>, <u>ascariasis</u>, <u>scabies</u>)

* Fungal;  (e.g., <u>aspergillosis</u>, <u>coccidioidomycosis</u>, <u>histoplasmosis</u>)

* Bacterial (e.g., <u>scarlet fever</u>, <u>leprosy</u>, genitourinary infections, <u>chlamydial infections</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tddX7K0)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ecXxYC)

* Viral (e.g., <u>HIV</u>, <u>HTLV</u>-1) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HKYK3J)

* <u>Protozoan</u> (e.g., <u>isosporiasis</u>)

* <u>Neoplastic</u> disorders

* Solid tumors (e.g., <u>lung cancer</u>, colorectal <u>adenocarcinoma</u>) [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HKYK3J)

* <u>Leukemia</u> (e.g., <u>CML</u>, <u>AML</u>)

* <u>Lymphoma</u> (e.g., <u>Hodgkin lymphoma</u>, <u>non-Hodgkin lymphomas</u>)

* <u>T-cell</u> <u>lymphoproliferative disorders</u>

* <u>Myeloproliferative neoplasms</u> [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ecXxYC)

* <u>Polycythemia vera</u>

* <u>Myelofibrosis</u>

* Gastrointestinal disorders

* <u>Inflammatory bowel disease</u>

* <u>Chronic pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Celiac disease</u>

* <u>Eosinophilic esophagitis</u>

* Rheumatologic

* <u>Rheumatoid arthritis</u>

* <u>Systemic lupus erythematosus</u>

* Eosinophilic <u>fasciitis</u>

* Pulmonary syndromes

* <u>Sarcoidosis</u>

* <u>Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis</u>

* <u>Löffler syndrome</u>

* <u>Vasculitis</u>

* <u>Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis</u>

* <u>Polyarteritis nodosa</u>

* Other

* <u>Cholesterol embolization syndrome</u>

* <u>Primary adrenal insufficiency</u>

* Radiation exposure

* <u>Chronic graft-versus-host disease</u>

* Solid organ <u>transplant rejection</u> [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/HKYK3J)

* <u>Hyper-IgE syndrome</u>

* <u>IgG4-related disease</u>

* <u>Omenn syndrome</u>
