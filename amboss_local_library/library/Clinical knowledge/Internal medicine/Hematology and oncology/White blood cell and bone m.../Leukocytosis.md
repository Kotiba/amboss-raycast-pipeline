## Summary

Leukocytosis is an elevation in the <u>white blood cell</u> (<u>WBC</u>) count (> 11,000/mm<sup>3</sup>) and can be further defined by the primary <u>WBC</u> type that is elevated (i.e., <u>neutrophils</u>, <u>lymphocytes</u>, <u>eosinophils</u>, <u>monocytes</u>, <u>basophils</u>). <u>Neutrophilia</u> and <u>lymphocytosis</u> are the most common forms of leukocytosis and can be caused by numerous factors, including infection, hematologic abnormalities, <u>malignancy</u>, and autoimmune or inflammatory disorders. <u>Reactive leukocytosis</u> may also result from physiological causes. Diagnosis involves obtaining a <u>CBC with differential</u> and a <u>peripheral blood smear</u>. Further studies (e.g., infectious workup) should be obtained based on the suspected cause. Management depends on the underlying cause of leukocytosis.




<u>Eosinophilia</u> is discussed in a separate article.
