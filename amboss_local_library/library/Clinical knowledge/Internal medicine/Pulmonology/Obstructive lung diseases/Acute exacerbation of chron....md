# Acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Pulmonology > Obstructive lung diseases > Acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TI06Xh)

## Summary

Acute exacerbation of <u>chronic obstructive pulmonary disease</u> (AECOPD) is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> characterized by worsening respiratory symptoms within a period of 14 days. The most common trigger is a viral <u>upper respiratory tract infection</u> (<u>URTI</u>). <u>Cardinal symptoms of AECOPD</u> are worsening <u>dyspnea</u>, increased frequency and severity of <u>cough</u>, and increased volume and/or <u>purulence</u> of <u>sputum</u>. Testing is aimed at assessing severity, evaluating for underlying triggers, and identifying coexisting conditions (e.g., <u>pneumonia</u>). <u>Respiratory support</u> (e.g., <u>oxygen therapy</u>, <u>noninvasive positive pressure ventilation</u>) may be required to treat <u>hypoxemia</u> and <u>hypercapnia</u>. The mainstays of pharmacological therapy are <u>bronchodilators</u> and <u>systemic glucocorticoids</u>. <u>Antibiotics</u> should be considered in certain patients with <u>cardinal symptoms of AECOPD</u> (especially an increase in the <u>purulence</u> of <u>sputum</u>) and those who require <u>mechanical ventilation</u>.




See also “<u>COPD</u>” and “<u>Ventilation strategy for obstructive lung disease</u>.”

---

## Definitions

AECOPD is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u> based on the development of <u>cardinal symptoms of AECOPD</u> (e.g., acute worsening <u>dyspnea</u>, increase in the <u>purulence</u> of <u>sputum</u>) over ≤ 14 days. It is often accompanied by <u>tachypnea</u>, <u>tachycardia</u>, and increased local or systemic inflammation. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NUW-Wk0)

## Etiology

* Viral respiratory infections: most common cause of AECOPD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* <u>Rhinovirus</u>: most common [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3kbSnF)

* Others: <u>SARS-CoV-2</u>, <u>influenza</u>, <u>RSV</u>, <u>parainfluenza</u>, <u>metapneumovirus</u>, <u>adenovirus</u>

* Bacterial infections: e.g., <u>Haemophilus influenzae</u>, <u>Moraxella catarrhalis</u>, <u>Streptococcus pneumoniae</u> 
* <u>Risk factors</u> for <u>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</u> infection include:  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kkbmLF)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/OkbILF)

* Advanced <u>COPD</u>

* Previous hospitalizations

* <u>Systemic glucocorticoid</u> use

* Previous isolation of <u>P. aeruginosa</u>

* Additional triggers: drugs (e.g., <u>beta blockers</u>), <u>allergens</u>, <u>air pollution</u>, <u>stress</u>, <u>pulmonary embolism</u>

* <u>Risk factors</u>: previous history of exacerbations, advanced <u>GOLD grade</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)





> [!TIP]
> Obtain history of smoking and environmental exposure in all patients with AECOPD.[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

## Clinical features

* Cardinal symptoms of AECOPD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Worsening of <u>dyspnea</u>

* Increased frequency and severity of <u>cough</u>

* Increased volume and/or <u>purulence</u> of <u>sputum</u>

* Possible additional symptoms

* <u>Wheezing</u>, <u>tachypnea</u>

* Fatigue

* <u>Fever</u>, chills

* Signs of <u>AECOPD with life-threatening acute respiratory failure</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Severe <u>hypoxemia</u>

* <u>Increased work of breathing</u>, e.g., use of accessory respiratory muscles, <u>paradoxical breathing</u>

* <u>Altered mental status</u>

## Diagnosis

AECOPD is a <u>clinical diagnosis</u>; testing is aimed at assessing severity, identifying triggers, and ruling out complications and/or alternative diagnoses.




### Approach




* Obtain <u>pulse oximetry</u> to assess oxygenation.

* <u>Classify AECOPD severity</u> using:

* Focused clinical evaluation

* <u>Blood gas analysis</u>

* <u>CRP</u> level

* Consider chest imaging to rule out <u>pneumonia</u>.

* Obtain microbiological studies to identify triggers.

* Consider additional studies to exclude complications and/or <u>differential diagnoses of AECOPD</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Acute respiratory conditions (e.g., <u>pneumonia</u>, <u>pulmonary embolism</u>) can be a cause, consequence, or comorbidity of AECOPD or may have a similar manifestation. Determining the sequence of symptoms is essential to avoid misdiagnosis.




> [!WARNING]
> AECOPD can be life-threatening. Do not delay treatment for testing in patients who present with <u>respiratory failure</u> or <u>signs of respiratory distress</u>.




### Initial studies




#### Risk <u>stratification</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




The following tests can help <u>classify AECOPD severity</u>.




* <u>Pulse oximetry</u>: adequate for assessing oxygenation in most patients

* <u>Blood gas analysis</u>

* <u>ABG</u>: to assess for <u>hypoxemia</u>, <u>hypercapnia</u>, and/or <u>acidemia</u>

* Baseline elevation in <u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u> (chronic <u>hypercapnia</u>) with normal <u>pH</u> is common.

* A rise of <u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u> from baseline, <u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u>> 50 mm Hg, or a drop in <u>pH</u> may indicate <u>hypercapnic respiratory failure</u>.

* <u>VBG</u> is an acceptable alternative to <u>ABG</u> for estimating <u>pH</u> but not for assessing systemic oxygenation.

* Routine <u>laboratory studies</u>: <u>CRP</u> level â¥ 10 mg/L may indicate moderate or severe exacerbation.





> [!TIP]
> Elevated <u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u> with a normal <u>pH</u> in patients with known <u>COPD</u> is consistent with chronic <u>CO<sub>2</sub> retention</u>, while elevated <u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u> with a low <u>pH</u> suggests acute <u>respiratory failure</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Mn1MFh0)




#### Chest imaging [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/42W33k0)




* <u>CXR</u>

* Best initial test to rule out <u>pneumonia</u>

* Findings 

* See “<u>CXR findings of pneumonia</u>.”

* May show <u>CXR findings of COPD</u> (e.g., <u>barrel chest</u>)

* Chest CT: may be considered in addition to <u>CXR</u> in certain patients, e.g., those with <u>fever</u>, a history of <u>heart</u> disease, or severe <u>emphysema</u>

* Thoracic <u>ultrasound</u>: may be used to assess for <u>pulmonary edema</u>, <u>pneumothorax</u>, <u>pneumonia</u>, and <u>pleural effusion</u>  (see “<u>POCUS in acute heart failure</u>”)





#### Microbiological studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




* Testing for viral <u>URTIs</u>: Choose tests, e.g., <u>nasopharyngeal swab</u> for <u>respiratory viral panel</u> and/or <u>COVID-19 testing</u>, based on local infection patterns.  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lUWvWk0)

* <u>Sputum</u> <u>Gram stain</u> and culture  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Obtain in patients with any of the following:

* Need for <u>mechanical ventilation</u>

* History of frequent exacerbations

* Severe airflow obstruction (i.e., <u>GOLD grade</u> 3 or 4)

* May be used to guide <u>antibiotic therapy</u>





### Additional studies [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




Obtain additional studies based on clinical suspicion.




* <u>Pulmonary embolus diagnostics</u>, e.g., <u>D-dimer</u>, <u>CTA</u> chest

* <u>Acute heart failure diagnostics</u>, e.g., <u>BNP</u>/<u>NT-proBNP</u>, <u>echocardiography</u>

* <u>Diagnostics for acute coronary syndrome</u> and <u>cardiac arrhythmias</u>, e.g., <u>ECG</u>  , <u>troponin</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vWbAMs)

* <u>Diagnostics for dyspnea</u>, e.g., <u>CBC</u> to assess for <u>anemia</u>

* Serum <u>vitamin D</u> level: Obtain in all patients hospitalized with AECOPD.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)





> [!TIP]
> <u>Pulmonary function testing</u> is not routinely recommended during acute exacerbations. [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)




> [!TIP]
> Most patients with <u>COPD</u> have <u>baseline ECG</u> abnormalities; compare current tracing to prior <u>ECGs</u> to avoid misdiagnosis. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vWbAMs)




> [!WARNING]
> In patients presenting with respiratory exacerbation, consider <u>differential diagnoses of AECOPD</u> (e.g., <u>AHF</u>, <u>pulmonary embolism</u>), especially if there are no signs of infection.

## Management

Evaluate and treat patients simultaneously while assessing disease severity and the response to stabilization measures. Rapidly identify <u>indications for ICU admission in AECOPD</u> and any patients who require aggressive therapy.




### All patients




* <u>Oxygen therapy</u> as needed to maintain <u>SpO<sub>2</sub></u> 88–92%

* Start <u>pharmacotherapy for AECOPD</u>.

* Start <u>bronchodilators</u>.

* Consider <u>systemic glucocorticoids</u>.

* Consider indications for <u>antibiotic therapy in AECOPD</u>.

* Provide adjunct pharmacological agents if clinically indicated, e.g., <u>diuretics</u>.

* Consider <u>indications for hospital admission in AECOPD</u>, e.g., insufficient home/community support or severe symptoms.

* Continuously assess treatment response.





### Patients with <u>severe AECOPD</u>




* Perform <u>ABCDE survey</u>.

* Maximize <u>pharmacotherapy for AECOPD</u>.

* Assess for <u>signs of impending respiratory failure</u>.

* Obtain serial <u>ABGs</u>.

* If patients have <u>hypercapnia</u> and/or persistent <u>hypoxemia</u>:

* Titrate <u>oxygen therapy</u> to <u>target SpO<sub>2</sub></u>.

* Consider a trial of <u>NIPPV</u> or <u>high-flow nasal cannula therapy</u>.

* Consider <u>indications for intubation in AECOPD</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Intubation</u> in patients with <u>COPD</u> should be a last resort but should not be delayed in patients presenting with severe <u>respiratory distress</u> or who rapidly decline.

## Respiratory support

### <u>Oxygen therapy</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




* Provide <u>supplemental oxygen</u> for patients with <u>hypoxemia</u>.

* <u>Target SpO<sub>2</sub> range</u>: 88–92% [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Choose an <u>oxygen delivery</u> device.

* <u>Venturi mask</u>: generally preferred over standard <u>nasal cannula</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* <u>High-flow nasal cannula</u> (<u>HFNC</u>): may improve outcomes in patients with acute <u>hypoxemic respiratory failure</u>

* Check blood gases frequently to ensure appropriate level of oxygenation and monitor for <u>oxygen-induced hypercapnia</u> (<u>CO<sub>2</sub> narcosis</u>).





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Oxygen saturation</u> that is too high poses a risk of <u>oxygen-induced hypercapnia</u>.




### <u>Noninvasive positive pressure ventilation</u> (<u>NIPPV</u>) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




<u>NIPPV</u> is the preferred <u>ventilation strategy</u> for improving oxygenation and <u>acidosis</u> in AECOPD with acute <u>respiratory failure</u>.




* Indications for NIPPV in patients with AECOPD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* <u>Respiratory acidosis</u> (<u>PaCO<sub>2</sub></u> â¥ 45 mm Hg and arterial <u>pH</u> ≤ 7.35)

* Severe <u>dyspnea</u> with <u>signs of increased work of breathing</u> and/or <u>respiratory muscle fatigue</u>, e.g., use of <u>respiratory accessory muscles</u>

* Persistent <u>hypoxemia</u> despite <u>supplemental oxygen</u>

* Procedure

* <u>BiPAP</u> is most commonly used.

* See “<u>Mechanical ventilation</u>” for <u>contraindications for NIPPV</u> and typical <u>NIPPV</u> settings.





### <u>Invasive mechanical ventilation</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




<u>Intubation</u> and <u>mechanical ventilation</u> are especially high-risk and complication-<u>prone</u> procedures in AECOPD and should only be used as a last resort.




* Indications for intubation in AECOPD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* <u>NIPPV</u> is not tolerated or is inadequate, e.g., persistent <u>respiratory acidosis</u> or worsening mental status.

* Life-threatening <u>hypoxemia</u>

* Severe <u>hemodynamic instability</u>

* <u>Shock</u> refractory to fluids and <u>vasopressors</u>

* After respiratory and/or <u>cardiac arrest</u>

* Severe <u>cardiac arrhythmias</u>

* Impaired <u>airway</u> protection, e.g., in patients with:

* <u>Altered mental status</u>

* Persistent <u>vomiting</u>

* Large volume <u>aspiration</u>

* Persistent inability to clear respiratory secretions

* Important considerations

* Maximize <u>pharmacotherapy for AECOPD</u> to avoid <u>intubation</u> if possible.

* Ascertain <u>code status</u>.

* Initiate countermeasures for high <u>intubation</u> risk before <u>intubation</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Preoxygenation</u> with <u>NIPPV</u> and/or <u>apneic oxygenation</u>

* Consider <u>delayed sequence intubation</u>.

* See “<u>High-risk indications for mechanical ventilation</u>” for further examples of risks and preventive measures.

* See “<u>Ventilation strategy for obstructive lung disease</u>” for typical <u>ventilator settings</u>.

* Postintubation management

* Continue aggressive pharmacological treatment during <u>mechanical ventilation</u>.

* Monitor for <u>complications of mechanical ventilation</u> commonly seen with <u>COPD</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Intubation</u> and <u>mechanical ventilation</u> of patients with AECOPD carry a significant risk of periprocedural <u>cardiac arrest</u> due to rapid oxygen desaturation, <u>dynamic hyperinflation</u>, circulatory <u>shock</u>, and/or severe <u>respiratory acidosis</u>.




> [!WARNING]
> Always check for advanced directives and ascertain the patient's <u>code status</u> (e.g., <u>DNI order</u>) prior to <u>intubation</u>.

## Pharmacotherapy

### <u>Bronchodilators</u> for AECOPD [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




* Indication: : all patients with AECOPD

* Regimens (<u>off-label</u>) 

* <u>SABA</u> monotherapy, e.g., <u>albuterol</u> <u>MDI</u> DOSAGE  OR <u>albuterol</u> <u>nebulizer</u> DOSAGE  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kpYmpJ)[[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jcb_Xs)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Qn1uGh0)

* OR <u>SABA</u>/<u>SAMA</u> combination therapy, e.g., <u>albuterol</u> with <u>ipratropium bromide</u> <u>MDI</u> DOSAGE OR <u>ipratropium</u> <u>nebulizer</u> DOSAGE  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Routes of administration: <u>MDIs</u> and nebulizers are equally effective for drug delivery. [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jcb_Xs)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dn1oHh0)

* <u>MDI</u>: fewer adverse effects  and less aerosolization of airborne infectious pathogens than <u>nebulizer</u>  [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kcbmcs)

* <u>Nebulizer</u> [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4cb3cs)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/en1xHh0)

* Consider in patients who are unable to use an <u>MDI</u>.

* There is a lower risk of <u>O<sub>2</sub>-induced hypercapnia</u> with air-driven than oxygen-driven <u>bronchodilators</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Titrate dosage and frequency of medication to clinical effect and follow any local institutional protocols. Some sources recommend dosing <u>SABA</u> as frequently as every 20 minutes during <u>severe AECOPD</u>.[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Qn1uGh0)




### <u>Glucocorticoids</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ubbpvH)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ebb8vH)




Consider <u>glucocorticoids</u> in all patients with AECOPD.




* Route of administration

* Systemic: Oral and IV agents are equally effective but oral agents are preferred.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ebb8vH)

* <u>Inhaled corticosteroids</u> (<u>ICS</u>) may be considered as an alternative in <u>AECOPD without respiratory failure</u> or in patients for whom a systemic <u>steroid</u>-sparing approach is preferred.  [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/01be2s)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AkbRqF)

* Agents  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PpYWpJ)

* Systemic

* <u>Prednisone</u> PO  DOSAGE  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* <u>Methylprednisolone</u> IV  DOSAGE  or <u>methylprednisolone</u> PO DOSAGE  [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Ebb8vH)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AkbRqF)

* Inhaled: <u>budesonide</u> <u>nebulizer</u> DOSAGE [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/01be2s)

* Duration: 5 days  [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ubbpvH)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/01be2s)





### <u>Antibiotics</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ubbpvH)




There is no consensus on the routine use of <u>antibiotics</u> for the treatment of AECOPD. Some studies have shown an association with faster symptom resolution and a decreased risk of treatment failure. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




* Indications for empiric antibiotic therapy in AECOPD  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* All 3 <u>cardinal symptoms of AECOPD</u>

* Increased <u>sputum</u> <u>purulence</u> and ≥ 1 other cardinal symptom of AECOPD

* Use of <u>mechanical ventilation</u> (invasive or noninvasive)

* Route of administration: Oral route is preferred if possible.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Agents

* Choose based on local resistance patterns for common respiratory pathogens.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3ccSXY0)

* Consider <u>empiric antibiotic treatment</u>, e.g.:

* <u>Amoxicillin/clavulanate</u> DOSAGE

* <u>Azithromycin</u> DOSAGE

* <u>Doxycycline</u> DOSAGE

* For suspected <u>pseudomonal</u> infection , see “<u>Empiric antibiotics for community-acquired pneumonia</u>.”

* Duration: usually 5 days  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3ccSXY0)





> [!TIP]
> Following clinical stabilization, continue any previously prescribed <u>bronchodilators</u> for <u>COPD maintenance therapy</u> (e.g., <u>long-acting beta-agonists</u>) or start <u>maintenance therapy</u>.[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

## Supportive care

### Before stabilization [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




* Monitoring

* Continuous <u>pulse oximetry</u>

* Serial <u>blood gas</u> monitoring

* <u>Monitor fluid balance</u> and consider <u>diuretics</u> for <u>fluid overload</u>.

* Manage comorbidities, e.g., <u>heart failure</u>, <u>pulmonary embolism</u>.

* Provide <u>VTE prophylaxis</u> for hospitalized patients.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)





### After stabilization [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)




After an acute exacerbation, measures should be taken to prevent future exacerbations.




* Initiate or continue <u>nonpharmacological management of COPD</u>, e.g., <u>counseling on smoking cessation</u>.

* Initiate or continue <u>COPD maintenance therapy</u>, e.g., <u>bronchodilators</u>, <u>ICS</u>.

* For persistent exacerbations, consider <u>follow-up treatment in COPD</u> (e.g., long-term <u>antibiotics</u> or <u>dupilumab</u>).

* Supplement <u>vitamin D</u> in patients with confirmed severe <u>vitamin D deficiency</u> (< 10 ng/mL).  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)

* Ensure <u>recommended immunizations in COPD</u> are up-to-date.

* Educate patients on proper inhaler technique. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/An1RDh0)

* Describe and demonstrate when prescribing a new inhaler.

* Identify common errors, e.g., inadequate exhalation prior to inhalation.

* Recommend avoidance of triggers (e.g., <u>beta blockers</u>, indoor <u>air pollution</u>).





> [!TIP]
> Following clinical stabilization, continue any previously prescribed long-acting <u>bronchodilators</u> (i.e., <u>LABA</u> and/or <u>LAMA</u>) or consider starting them as indicated for <u>COPD maintenance therapy</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IEdYC70)
