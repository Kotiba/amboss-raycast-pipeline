## Summary

<u>Chest tube</u> placement or tube thoracostomy is a procedure in which a flexible tube is inserted between the <u>ribs</u> into the <u>thoracic cavity</u> to drain intrathoracic air, blood, or other fluid (e.g., <u>pleural effusion</u>, <u>empyema</u>), allowing for <u>lung</u> re-expansion. <u>Relative contraindications</u> include <u>coagulopathy</u> and multiple pleural adhesions. <u>Chest tubes</u> are most commonly placed in the 5<sup>th</sup> <u>intercostal space</u>, between the <u>anterior</u> axillary and <u>midaxillary lines</u>. They should be inserted directly above the superior edge of the <u>rib</u> to avoid injuring the intercostal neurovascular bundle. A finger should be inserted into the <u>chest tube</u> tract to maintain patency prior to and during <u>chest tube</u> placement. Complications include intercostal vessel injury and <u>re-expansion pulmonary edema</u>.
