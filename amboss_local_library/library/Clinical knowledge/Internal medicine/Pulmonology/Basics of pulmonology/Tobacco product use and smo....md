# Tobacco product use and smoking cessation

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Pulmonology > Basics of pulmonology > Tobacco product use and smoking cessation*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mr0V3h)

---

## Summary

Tobacco use is the leading cause of preventable disease and <u>death</u> in the US. <u>Tobacco products</u> are available in multiple forms (e.g., cigarettes, snuff), including <u>nicotine products</u> that contain no tobacco (e.g., e-cigarettes, <u>gum</u>). <u>Tobacco products</u> are highly addictive, and their use is associated with a multitude of negative health outcomes. All individuals should be screened regularly for <u>tobacco product</u> use; those who screen positive should be offered assistance to quit. Management consists of providing advice on the health risks of continued use and offering both pharmacological and behavioral treatments for <u>smoking cessation</u>. Close follow-up is important, as relapse is common in the first days or weeks after an attempt to quit. For children, <u>adolescents</u>, and pregnant individuals who use <u>tobacco products</u>, <u>behavioral therapy</u> alone is recommended.

## Epidemiology

* Tobacco use is the leading cause of preventable disease and <u>death</u> in the US. [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SN1y0h0)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/GN1Bch0)

* 18.7% of adults currently use <u>tobacco products</u>.  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/DtW1fM0)

* <u>Prevalence</u> of <u>tobacco product</u> use is higher among: [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gN1F0h0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TN160h0)

* Men

* Individuals with psychiatric disorders

* Individuals with disabilities

* Lesbian, gay, and <u>bisexual</u> individuals

* Low-income individuals

* 68% of adult cigarette smokers report interest in quitting.  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/f8WkNM0)




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

## Pathophysiology

* <u>Nicotine</u> stimulates <u>nicotinic receptors</u> in the <u>central nervous system</u> and autonomic <u>ganglia</u>, leading to both <u>sympathetic</u> and <u>parasympathetic</u> effects. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FtWg2M0)

* Addiction develops from a combination of:

* Positive neuropsychiatric effects (see “<u>Clinical features of tobacco product use</u>”)

* Development of <u>nicotine dependence</u>: repeated exposure → <u>substance tolerance</u> → <u>nicotine withdrawal</u> in the absence of <u>nicotine</u> use

## Clinical features

### Clinical features of tobacco product use




* Neuropsychiatric effects: ↑ release of <u>neurotransmitters</u>, including <u>dopamine</u>, in the <u>brain</u> → activation of the <u>mesolimbic pathway</u> (reward pathway) → euphoria, reduced <u>anxiety</u>, increased alertness [[15]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UkWbMl0)

* Cardiovascular effects: : stimulation of <u>catecholamine</u> release → <u>tachycardia</u>, ↑ blood pressure [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1FW2SM0)

* Metabolic effects: ↑ <u>resting metabolic rate</u>, ↓ appetite → weight loss [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WFWPSM0)

* Signs of long-term use include:

* Staining of fingers or <u>teeth</u> [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IyWYgL0)[[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ryWfgL0)

* Clinical features of <u>complications of tobacco product use</u>





### Clinical features of nicotine intoxication [[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2N1T0h0)




* Mild toxicity: <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>

* Severe toxicity can lead to <u>cholinergic syndrome</u>.

* See also “<u>Nicotine poisoning</u>.”





### Clinical features of nicotine withdrawal [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TN160h0)[[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Nta-dm)




* Irritability, frustration, anger, restlessness, <u>anxiety</u>

* Cravings

* <u>Dysphoria</u>, <u>depressed mood</u>

* <u>Insomnia</u>

* Impaired concentration

* Increased appetite, weight gain

## Screening

### Indications




* Adults ≥ 18 years and pregnant individuals: Screen regularly at clinical encounters. [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)

* Children > 11 years of age: Screen at least annually.  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/o-10yj0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/j01_f20)





### Screening modality [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)




* Ask all patients about current and past use of <u>nicotine</u> and <u>tobacco products</u>, e.g.:

* As part of strategies incorporating both screening and intervention:

* <u>5 As model of behavior change</u>

* Ask Advise Refer or Ask Advise Connect  [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UFWbhM0)

* Via screening tools for substance use, e.g.: [[27]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/g-cF9U0)

* <u>TAPS tool</u>

* <u>NIDA quick screen</u>

* By routinely collecting information about <u>tobacco product</u> use status at every clinical encounter

* See also “<u>Tobacco product use in children and adolescents</u>” for <u>screening recommendations</u> in this age group.





> [!TIP]
> When <u>screening for tobacco product use</u>, ask broad questions that cover smoking, oral ingestion, and use of <u>nicotine products</u>. [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)




### Further management [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)




* Offer assistance with <u>smoking cessation</u> to <u>current smokers</u>.

* Initiate <u>preventive health care for current and former smokers</u>.

* Repeat screening for all patients at recommended intervals.

## Management

This section is focused on management of <u>tobacco smoking cessation</u>. Guidelines on treatment of patients who use other tobacco and <u>nicotine products</u> (e.g., chewing tobacco, e-cigarettes) are lacking, and treatment plans should be individualized through <u>shared decision-making</u>.




### Approach [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UFWbhM0)




* Initiate <u>preventive health care for current and former smokers</u>.

* Advise patients on the health benefits of <u>smoking cessation</u> and harms of continued use.

* Offer a combination of pharmacological and behavioral treatments for cessation to all patients.





> [!TIP]
> A combination of behavioral and pharmacological therapies to stop smoking increases the likelihood of treatment success. [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)




#### Patients not currently interested in quitting




* Offer <u>varenicline</u> to reduce <u>nicotine dependence</u>.  [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)

* Advise patients that they can return at any point for support with <u>smoking cessation</u>.

* There is no consensus on <u>harm reduction strategies</u> for <u>tobacco smoking</u>.  [[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KyWUTL0)





#### Patients interested in quitting




* Inquire about past attempts to quit, including their duration and any tools used.

* Assess the degree of <u>nicotine dependence</u> (e.g., number of cigarettes per day, timing of first cigarette).  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TN160h0)

* Encourage the selection of a specific quit date and help formulate a treatment plan.  [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)[[36]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fN1k0h0)

* Educate patients on expected <u>withdrawal symptoms</u> and duration.  [[37]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WAWPiL0)

* Discuss how to manage cravings.  [[38]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IAWYOL0)

* Arrange follow-up within 2–4 weeks to evaluate for barriers to quitting or problems with the treatment plan.  [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)





> [!TIP]
> Patients may either quit by gradually reducing smoking or by immediately stopping use. Regardless of the strategy, the goal should be complete cessation.

## Pharmacological treatment

### Approach [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wl1h_S0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TN160h0)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)




* Initiate a <u>first-line smoking cessation medication</u> through <u>shared decision-making</u>.  [[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)[[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_FW5kM0)

* <u>Varenicline</u> is associated with higher success rates than <u>bupropion</u> and <u>nicotine replacement therapy</u> (<u>NRT</u>).

* A combination of <u>NRT</u> products is more effective than a single form of <u>NRT</u> alone.

* For patients starting <u>varenicline</u> or <u>bupropion</u>, begin medication ≥ 1 week before the planned quit date.

* Follow up closely with patients to assess for side effects and <u>efficacy</u>.

* For patients experiencing side effects: Consider reducing the dose or changing to another <u>first-line smoking cessation medication</u>. [[40]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/EyW8SL0)

* For patients with a poor response, see “<u>Managing relapse during smoking cessation</u>.”

* Recommended treatment duration is usually 12–24 weeks to reduce the risk of relapse.  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wl1h_S0)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/tl1XAS0)





> [!WARNING]
> E-cigarettes are not <u>FDA</u>-approved for <u>smoking cessation</u> and are not currently recommended. Although studies show they are effective for quitting tobacco use, most patients continue to use e-cigarettes, and their long-term health consequences are unknown.[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[41]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZAWZRL0)[[42]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-yWD3L0)

* <u>Cytisine</u> is a partial <u>nicotine</u> <u>agonist</u> that is used for <u>smoking cessation</u> in some countries, but it is not currently available for use in the US. [[39]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_FW5kM0)

## Behavioral interventions for smoking cessation

The following interventions can improve <u>smoking cessation</u> success. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wl1h_S0)[[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UFWbhM0)




* Advice to quit from a <u>health care professional</u>

* Individual or group counseling (e.g., <u>CBT</u>, <u>behavioral therapy</u>)

* State tobacco quitlines (1-800-QUIT-NOW) : interactions (phone or text) with trained <u>tobacco cessation</u> counselors

* Support via smartphone apps, internet, or text messaging  [[47]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nyW7fL0)[[48]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/CyWqhL0)

* Individualized self-help materials (written, audio, or video)

* Patient incentives (vouchers or cash payments)





> [!TIP]
> There is insufficient evidence to support the use of <u>acupuncture</u>, hypnotherapy, or biomedical feedback (e.g., <u>spirometry</u> or <u>carbon monoxide</u> monitoring) for <u>smoking cessation</u>. [[26]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/UFWbhM0)

## Prevention

### <u>Primary prevention</u>




* Advise all individuals to abstain from <u>tobacco product</u> use.

* To help protect children and <u>adolescents</u>:

* Ask about <u>tobacco product</u> use among household members of children and <u>adolescents</u>. [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)

* Encourage household members who smoke to quit, referring for <u>smoking cessation</u> as indicated.

* For household members who do not wish to quit, recommend they stop smoking in their homes and cars.

* Educate children on the harms of <u>tobacco products</u>.  [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)[[50]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Fl1gAS0)





### Preventive health care for current and former smokers




* To facilitate early detection and management of <u>complications of tobacco product use</u>, educate patients on the importance of attending <u>adult health maintenance</u> visits and undergoing <u>cancer screening</u>.

* Additional screening may be recommended.

* <u>AAA screening</u>: a one-time screening in men 65–75 years of age who have ever smoked  [[51]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/4JY38J)[[52]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BzYzE7)

* Patients may require <u>lung cancer screening</u> depending on lifetime cigarette consumption in <u>pack years</u>. [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2gcTub0)

* Pack years: the number of cigarette packs (1 pack = 20 cigarettes) that an individual smokes or has smoked per day multiplied by the number of years of cigarette consumption

* <u>Lung cancer screening</u> is recommended annually for patients aged 50–80 years with a ≥ 20 <u>pack-year</u> smoking history.  [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2gcTub0)[[54]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/JfdsM60)[[55]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pfdLM60) [[53]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2gcTub0)

* Advise patients to remain up-to-date with <u>routine vaccinations</u>; catch-up <u>pneumococcal vaccination</u> is recommended if the primary series is incomplete (see “<u>Immunization schedule by medical indication</u>”).





> [!TIP]
> The <u>USPSTF</u> recommends against screening asymptomatic adults for <u>COPD</u>. The GOLD guideline recommends screening with <u>spirometry</u> in patients undergoing annual <u>lung cancer screening</u> with <u>low-dose CT scans</u> and patients with incidental imaging findings consistent with <u>COPD</u>. [[56]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WSWPAk0)[[57]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/nwW7jn0)




> [!TIP]
> <u>Tobacco product</u> use is a chronic condition requiring long-term management even after cessation. [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/TN160h0)

## Special patient groups

### Tobacco product use in children and adolescents [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T8W6NM0)




* Most <u>nicotine dependence</u> starts in childhood and <u>adolescence</u>; e-cigarettes are the most commonly used product.  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wtWhfM0)[[20]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2N1T0h0)

* <u>Nicotine</u> use impairs <u>adolescent</u> <u>brain development</u>.  [[58]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/T8W6NM0)





#### Screening tools




Validated screening tools for <u>tobacco product</u> use in this population include: [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)




* <u>Screening to Brief Intervention</u> (<u>S2BI</u>)

* <u>Brief Screener for Tobacco, Alcohol, and other Drugs</u> (<u>BSTAD</u>)





#### Management




Evidence for <u>smoking cessation</u> treatment in children and <u>adolescents</u> is limited.  [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)[[50]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Fl1gAS0)




* Offer behavioral treatment for <u>smoking cessation</u> for all patients. [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)

* Pharmacotherapy may be considered for <u>adolescents</u> with moderate to severe <u>nicotine dependence</u>. [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)





### Tobacco product use in pregnancy and postpartum




* <u>Tobacco products</u> are <u>teratogenic</u> (see “<u>Risks of tobacco product use during pregnancy</u>”).

* All patients should undergo <u>screening for tobacco product use</u> during <u>preconception care</u>, <u>prenatal care</u>, and the <u>postpartum visit</u>. [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)

* <u>Smoking cessation</u> before 15 weeks' <u>gestation</u> is recommended to reduce the risk of <u>perinatal</u> complications.  [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)





> [!TIP]
> Smoking during <u>pregnancy</u> is associated with an increased risk of obstetric complications and complications for the fetus and birthing parent. [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)




#### Management [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)




* Educate patients and household members on the risks of:

* <u>Tobacco product use during pregnancy</u> (including through <u>secondhand smoke</u>)

* <u>Secondhand smoke</u> exposure in the <u>neonatal period</u>  [[59]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mnWVFN0)

* Offer <u>behavioral interventions for smoking cessation</u> to all pregnant individuals who smoke.  [[22]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KvcUae0)[[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)

* Continue behavioral and psychosocial support in the <u>postpartum period</u>.  [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)

* In <u>breastfeeding</u> individuals, <u>NRT</u> may be considered after <u>shared decision-making</u>.  [[60]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9y1Nhj0)[[61]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kEWmDM0)[[62]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AKWRQm0)





> [!TIP]
> Because of the risk of <u>secondhand smoke</u> to the fetus and <u>neonate</u>, recommend <u>smoking cessation</u> for all household members. [[49]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/e8WxlM0)




> [!WARNING]
> Relapse to smoking is common in the first year postpartum. Continue to assess and manage <u>tobacco use disorder</u> after delivery. [[23]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8l1OAS0)

## Complications

* Cancer (e.g., <u>lung cancer</u>, <u>esophageal cancer</u>, <u>cervical cancer</u>, <u>oral cancer</u>) [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)

* Cardiovascular disease (including <u>coronary heart disease</u> and <u>stroke</u>)  [[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/vl1AAS0)

* Respiratory disease (e.g., <u>COPD</u>)

* <u>Diabetes</u>

* <u>Periodontal disease</u>

* Early <u>death</u>

* <u>Erectile dysfunction</u>

* <u>Age-related macular degeneration</u>

* <u>Postoperative complications</u> (e.g., <u>delayed wound healing</u>, <u>postoperative pulmonary complications</u>)

* Delayed and <u>nonunion</u> of <u>fractures</u> [[63]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/5-WiBL0)

* <u>E-cigarette or vaping product use associated lung injury</u> (<u>EVALI</u>) [[64]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/9uWNtM0)

* <u>Perinatal</u> complications: See “<u>Risks of tobacco product use during pregnancy</u>.”





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Tobacco smoke</u> contains > 7000 chemicals, including <u>carcinogens</u>, respiratory irritants, and other toxins. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FtWg2M0)




> [!TIP]
> The development of smoking-related complications can be a motivator for behavioral change and an opportunity to engage patients in <u>smoking cessation</u> treatment. [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wl1h_S0)
