## Summary

The respiratory system consists of a <u>conducting zone</u> (<u>anatomic dead space</u>; i.e., the airways of the mouth, nose, <u>pharynx</u>, <u>larynx</u>, <u>trachea</u>, <u>bronchi</u>, <u>bronchioles</u>, and <u>terminal bronchioles</u>) and a <u>respiratory zone</u> (<u>lung parenchyma</u>; i.e., <u>respiratory bronchioles</u>, alveolar ducts, alveolar sacs). The <u>conducting zone</u> is composed of nonrespiratory tissue and provides the passage for ventilation of the <u>respiratory zone</u>, where the O<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub> exchange takes place. The respiratory system is furthermore divided into an upper tract (structures from the <u>larynx</u> upwards) and a lower tract (structures below the <u>larynx</u>). The entire respiratory tract down to the <u>bronchioles</u> is covered in <u>ciliated epithelium</u>, which provides immunologic protection by helping clear the airways of dust and microorganisms. <u>Hyaline cartilage</u> in the form of C-shaped rings (<u>trachea</u>) and plates (<u>bronchi</u>) provides structural protection and integrity. <u>Gas exchange</u> takes place in the <u>alveoli</u> of the lungs. The right lung consists of 3 lobes (upper, middle, lower), while the left lung consists of 2 lobes (upper, lower) and the lingula, a structure that is homologous to the middle lobe of the right lung. The left lung shares its space with the <u>heart</u>, which it accommodates in the cardiac notch. The development of the lungs begins in the <u>embryonic period</u> and continues until approximately 8 years of age.

## Gross anatomy

### Overview




#### Zones




* <u>Conducting zone</u>: large airways and small airways (<u>intrapulmonary conducting system</u>; nonrespiratory tissue)

* <u>Respiratory zone</u>: <u>lung parenchyma</u> (site of O<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub> exchange)





![Cells and structures of lungs and airways](../../../../../media/r476v5gg3he1qdje5c0vi0cv.jpg)

#### Location




* <u>Upper respiratory tract</u>

* <u>Nasopharynx</u>

* <u>Larynx</u>

* <u>Lower respiratory tract</u>

* <u>Trachea</u>

* <u>Bronchi</u>, <u>bronchioles</u>

* Lungs: located in the <u>thoracic cavity</u> (protected by the <u>rib</u> cage)

* Between the lungs: <u>inferior mediastinum</u> (including <u>heart</u>, large vessels, <u>tracheal</u> bifurcation, <u>esophagus</u>)

* Superior to the lungs: <u>superior mediastinum</u>

* Inferior to the lungs: <u>diaphragm</u>

* <u>Anterior</u> to the lungs: <u>ribs</u>

* <u>Posterior</u> to the lungs: <u>vertebral column</u>, <u>ribs</u>

#### Function




* Ventilation (distribution of air in the airway)

* Respiration: <u>gas exchange</u> (absorption of O<sub>2</sub> into the blood and release of CO<sub>2</sub> into the air)

* Immune defense (ciliary clearance, <u>alveolar macrophages</u>, <u>goblet cells</u>)

* For <u>respiratory mechanics</u>, see <u>pulmonary function testing</u>.

* For more information, see “<u>Respiratory physiology</u>.”





### Intrapulmonary conducting system




#### Large airways




* Nose

* <u>Nasopharynx</u>

* Most superior portion of the <u>pharynx</u>; connecting the nasal cavity to the <u>oropharynx</u>

* Contains the <u>adenoids</u> and <u>eustachian tube</u> openings

* Neighboring structures

* Anteriorly: the nasal choanae

* Posteriorly: clivus, prevertebral musculature covering C1–C2

* Laterally: pharyngeal opening of the <u>eustachian tube</u>, <u>fossa of Rosenmuller</u>, <u>medial pterygoid</u> plates, and <u>superior pharyngeal constrictor muscles</u>

* <u>Larynx</u>

* Trachea

* Formed by a series of cartilages (15–20), joined together by annular <u>ligaments</u>.

* Bifurcates at the level of T4 into the left and the right main <u>bronchus</u> (tracheal carina)

* Neighboring structures

* <u>Posterior</u>: <u>esophagus</u>, <u>descending aorta</u>

* <u>Anterior</u>: <u>thyroid</u>, <u>ascending aorta</u>, <u>brachiocephalic trunk</u>, <u>superior vena cava</u>

* Bronchi  

* The right main (primary) <u>bronchus</u> divides into 3 lobar <u>bronchi</u> (secondary <u>bronchi</u>).

* The left main (primary) <u>bronchus</u> divides into 2 lobar <u>bronchi</u> (secondary <u>bronchi</u>).

* The lobar <u>bronchi</u> divide further into segmental bronchi (tertiary <u>bronchi</u>).

#### Small airways




* <u>Bronchioles</u>

* <u>Terminal bronchioles</u>





#### Function




* Conduction of air in and out of the respiratory tree

* <u>Anatomic dead space</u> (no <u>gas exchange</u>)

* Warms and humidifies air

* Mucociliary clearance: <u>ciliated epithelium</u> transports mucus, bacteria, and dust towards the throat, where it is either swallowed or expelled through the mouth





### <u>Respiratory zone</u>




#### Lungs




* Overview

* Paired organ of the respiratory system

* Located in the <u>thoracic cavity</u>

* Surrounded by the <u>visceral pleura</u>

* Hilum of the lung: the point of attachment of the lung to the lung root, which serves as the entry and exit site of the primary <u>bronchi</u>, <u>pulmonary arteries</u> and <u>veins</u>, <u>bronchial arteries</u> and <u>veins</u>, pulmonary nervous plexus, and <u>lymphatics</u> of each lung

* Bronchopulmonary segments 

* Segments of <u>lung tissue</u> that are supplied by a <u>segmental bronchus</u> and a segmental branch of the <u>pulmonary artery</u>.

* They are surrounded by a <u>connective tissue</u> septum (intersegmental septum), which encloses a branch of the <u>pulmonary vein</u>.

* <u>Lung parenchyma</u>

* <u>Respiratory bronchioles</u>

* Alveolar ducts

* Alveolar sacs, which contain several <u>pulmonary alveoli</u> surrounded by <u>capillaries</u>

* Pulmonary acinus: a part of the airway <u>distal</u> to a <u>terminal bronchiole</u>; each acinus contains multiple <u>respiratory bronchioles</u>, alveolar ducts, alveolar sacs, and <u>alveoli</u>.

### <u>Lymphatics</u>




* Lymphatic vessels drain the entire respiratory tree (lymphatic vessels are not present in the <u>pulmonary alveoli</u>)

* Intrapulmonary nodes → bronchopulmonary nodes → tracheobronchial nodes → paratracheal nodes → bronchomediastinal nodes and trunks → <u>thoracic duct</u> on the left and <u>right lymphatic duct</u> on the right





### Innervation (pulmonary plexus)




* Branches accompany the <u>blood vessels</u> and <u>bronchi</u> into the lung.

* <u>Parasympathetic</u> fibers from the <u>vagus nerve</u> (<u>M<sub>3</sub> receptors</u>) innervate <u>smooth muscle</u> and glands (bronchoconstriction, increase secretion)

* <u>Sympathetic</u> fibers (act on <u>ß<sub>2</sub>-receptors</u>): innervate <u>blood vessels</u>, <u>smooth muscle</u>, and glands (bronchodilation, <u>vasoconstriction</u>, decrease secretion)

### Respiratory zone [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H4YKk6)





#### Respiratory bronchioles




<u>Respiratory bronchioles</u> contain simple (non-ciliated) cuboidal and <u>squamous epithelium</u> and <u>smooth muscle</u>.




* Club cells (formerly known as <u>Clara cells</u>) 

* Maintain the integrity of the <u>respiratory epithelium</u> by secreting specialized immunomodulatory <u>proteins</u>, glycoproteins, and lipids.

* <u>Cytochrome P450</u>-dependent degradation of toxins

* Act as a reserve for <u>ciliated cells</u> to restore bronchiolar <u>epithelium</u> (<u>stem cell</u> properties) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PdbWJs)

* Nonciliated, secretory, cuboidal cells located in the terminal and the <u>respiratory bronchioles</u> of the lung

* Respiratory airway secretory cells (<u>RAS cells</u>) ;  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/SYWyoP0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yw1dlQ0)

* Low cuboidal cells located in the <u>distal airways</u> and <u>respiratory bronchioles</u>

* Act as progenitor cells for <u>type II pneumocytes</u>

* Secrete <u>proteins</u>, glycoproteins, and lipids essential for the integrity of the <u>respiratory epithelium</u>

* Chronic exposure to tobacco (e.g., patients with <u>COPD</u>) alters <u>gene expression</u> and the differentiation pathway of <u>type II pneumocytes</u>.





#### Pulmonary alveoli




<u>Pulmonary alveoli</u> are separated from each other by the interalveolar septum with <u>elastic fibers</u> and <u>capillaries</u>. Interalveolar pores connect adjacent <u>alveoli</u>.




* Type I pneumocytes: thin squamous cells that line the <u>alveoli</u> that allow for <u>transcellular</u> <u>gas exchange</u>

* Comprise 95% of the total alveolar area

* Connected to each other by <u>tight junctions</u>

* Form the blood-air barrier, together with the <u>endothelial</u> cells of the <u>capillaries</u> and the <u>basement membrane</u> between the two cells.

* Type II pneumocytes: cuboidal alveolar cells 

* Comprise 5% of the total alveolar area, but 60% of total number of cells

* Surfactant formation: <u>Type II pneumocytes</u> contain lamellar bodies, which secrete surfactant (surface-activating <u>lipoprotein</u> complex).

* <u>Surfactant</u> is mainly composed of the phospholipids dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (<u>DPPC</u> or lecithin) and phosphatidylglycerol.

* It reduces alveolar surface tension and thereby prevents the <u>alveoli</u> from collapsing.

* Lung regeneration and repair: <u>Type II pneumocytes</u> can proliferate to replace type I or <u>type II pneumocytes</u> following lung damage.

* Alveolar macrophages: <u>phagocytose</u> foreign materials and initiate the <u>immune response</u> by releasing <u>cytokines</u>  

* Produce alveolar elastase, which is degraded by <u>tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases</u> (<u>TIMPs</u>)

* Increased <u>elastase</u> activity is associated with the development of <u>emphysema</u>.

* Presence of hemosiderin-laden macrophages (or <u>heart failure cells</u>) may be a sign of <u>pulmonary edema</u> or alveolar hemorrhage (from <u>pulmonary infarction</u>, <u>vasculitis</u>, or <u>aspiration</u> of blood).





> [!TIP]
> <u>Pulmonary surfactant</u> produced by <u>type II pneumocytes</u> reduces the surface tension of the thin layer of water that covers the pulmonary <u>epithelium</u>, thereby preventing alveolar collapse at end-expiration, increasing compliance, and reducing the <u>work of breathing</u>!




> [!TIP]
> The presence on microscopy of <u>hemosiderin-laden macrophages</u> of <u>lung tissue</u> indicates alveolar hemorrhage or <u>pulmonary edema</u>.

## Function

The main function of the lung is the absorption of oxygen into the blood and the release of carbon dioxide into the air.




* For this purpose, the air must first reach the <u>alveoli</u> (see <u>pulmonary function testing</u> for <u>respiratory mechanics</u>).

* The distribution of air (ventilation) is adjusted to the <u>perfusion</u> of pulmonary vessels so that the <u>gas exchange</u> proceeds evenly.

* The <u>respiratory center</u> adjusts the breathing to the needs of the entire organism.

* For more information, see ”<u>Respiratory physiology</u>.”

| <br>Alveolar period<br>(week 36–8 years) | <br>* Development of:<br><br>* Mature <u>type II pneumocytes</u><br><br>* Definitive <u>alveoli</u> (separated by secondary septa) from terminal sacs<br><br> | <br><br>* Following <u>birth</u>, <u>alveoli</u> increase in number but not in size<br><br>* At <u>birth</u>: approx. 20–70 million <u>alveoli</u><br><br>* In adults: approx 300 million <u>alveoli</u><br><br>* In utero: increased <u>vascular resistance</u> due to <u>aspiration</u> of <u>amniotic fluid</u><br><br>* Postpartum: inspiration of air leads to a drop in <u>pulmonary vascular resistance</u><br><br><br> |
