# Cough

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Pulmonology > Clinical problems > Cough*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3L0Sxg)

## Summary

A cough is a protective mechanism that forcefully expels air from the <u>lungs</u> to clear secretions, foreign bodies, and irritants from the <u>airway</u>, and can be triggered by various conditions. A cough can be classified as acute, subacute, or chronic, in addition to productive (with <u>sputum</u> expectoration) or dry. The most common causes of <u>acute cough</u> are <u>upper respiratory tract infections</u> (<u>URTIs</u>), exacerbations of chronic conditions, and <u>pneumonia</u>. <u>Subacute cough</u> is often a sequela of a <u>URTI</u> (postinfectious cough) but can also be caused by <u>upper airway cough syndrome</u> (<u>UACS</u>) or <u>pertussis</u>. Common causes of <u>chronic cough</u> in adults include <u>UACS</u>, <u>asthma</u>, <u>gastroesophageal reflux disease</u> (<u>GERD</u>), <u>nonasthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis</u> (<u>NAEB</u>), and certain medications (e.g., <u>ACE inhibitors</u>, <u>sitagliptin</u>). The cause of an <u>acute cough</u> can often be determined clinically with a thorough <u>medical history</u> and <u>physical examination</u>. <u>Chronic cough</u> or the presence of <u>red flag symptoms</u> (including <u>dyspnea</u>, <u>fever</u>, <u>hemoptysis</u>, and weight loss) indicate that further investigation is required. Treatment depends on the underlying etiology and often includes symptomatic therapy.




See also “<u>Dyspnea</u>” and “<u>Chest pain</u>.”

## Pathophysiology

Cough is a protective mechanism that forcefully expels air from the <u>lungs</u> to clear secretions, foreign bodies, and irritants from the <u>airway</u>.




* Triggers: cough may be voluntary or a reflex to <u>airway</u> irritants/triggers

* Mechanical 

* Inhaled/aspirated solid or particulate matter (e.g., smoke, dust)

* Mucus

* Chemical

* <u>Gastric acid</u> (<u>GERD</u>)

* Inflammatory mediators: <u>bradykinin</u>, <u>prostaglandin E2</u>

* Thermal: cold air

* Cough reflex arc

* Irritation of cough <u>receptors</u> in the nose, sinuses, upper and <u>lower respiratory tract</u> (see the triggers above)

* Transmission along the <u>afferent pathway</u> via the <u>internal laryngeal nerve</u> of the <u>vagus nerve</u> (<u>CN X</u>) to the cough center in the medulla

* Generation of efferent signal in the medulla and initiation of cough via the vagus, phrenic, and spinal motor nerves

* Mechanism of <u>cough reflex</u>: initiation of the <u>cough reflex</u> arc leads to

* Rapid inspiration, closure of the <u>epiglottis</u> and <u>vocal cords</u> (which traps inhaled air in the <u>lungs</u>), and contraction of the <u>diaphragm</u>, expiratory, and abdominal muscles → rapid increase of intrathoracic pressure

* A sudden opening of the <u>vocal cords</u> and forceful expulsion of air from the <u>lungs</u>

## Clinical evaluation

A detailed history and <u>physical examination</u> are essential to help narrow down the possible <u>causes of cough</u> and guide initial investigations and management. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qacCla0)




### Focused history




* Duration of cough (weeks): Ascertaining the symptom duration is a recommended first step in evaluating adults presenting with cough (see “Classification”). [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qacCla0)

* Other cough characteristics

* Presence of <u>sputum</u> 

* Productive cough

* Nonproductive cough

* Onset

* Sudden

* Gradual

* Quality: classic cough presentations in children [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/pacLla0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8dcO7Y0)

* Brassy or barking cough

* <u>Staccato cough</u> (in <u>infants</u>)

* <u>Paroxysmal</u> cough

* Inspiratory whoop

* Aggravating factors

* Symptom variation depending on the weather and/or season

* <u>Supine position</u>

* Exercise

* Daytime or nighttime worsening of symptoms

* Associated symptoms

* Viral symptoms, e.g., <u>rhinorrhea</u>, <u>odynophagia</u>, <u>myalgia</u>, <u>fever</u>

* Allergic symptoms, e.g., itching or watery eyes, <u>rhinorrhea</u>, nasal congestion, throat clearing

* Posttussive <u>vomiting</u>

* <u>Chest pain</u> or <u>heartburn</u>





> [!TIP]
> Cough exacerbated by exercise and at nighttime is characteristic of <u>cough-variant asthma</u>.




> [!TIP]
> Coughing paroxysms, inspiratory whoop, and posttussive emesis are characteristic of <u>pertussis</u>. [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IYcYqa0)




### Other key historical features




* Comorbidities

* Smoking history

* Personal or <u>family history</u> of <u>atopy</u>

* History of chronic disease

* Cardiac conditions

* Respiratory conditions

* <u>Immunodeficiency</u>

* Neurological or developmental impairment

* <u>Vaccination</u> status: absent or incomplete <u>immunization</u>, e.g., against <u>Streptococcus pneumoniae</u>, <u>Haemophilus influenzae type b</u>, <u>pertussis</u>, <u>influenza</u>

* Medication

* <u>ACE inhibitors</u>  [[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PbcWFa0)

* <u>Sitagliptin</u> [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)

* <u>Beta blockers</u>

* Exposures

* Infectious contacts

* Radiation sources

* Lifetime <u>tobacco smoke</u> exposure

* <u>Occupational exposure</u> to irritants, e.g., chemicals, organic or inorganic dust  [[18]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/aYcQna0)

* Travel history [[19]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0Ycena0)

* Travel to <u>endemic</u> areas

* At-risk activities

* Means of transportation





### Focused <u>physical examination</u>




* <u>Pulmonary examination</u>

* <u>Cardiac examination</u>

## Acute and subacute cough

### Management [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PbcWFa0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qacCla0)




* Diagnostic studies are not routinely indicated (unless <u>red flags for cough</u> are present).

* Consider differentiating between the following groups, based on clinical suspicion:

* <u>Life-threatening causes of cough</u> and/or <u>red flags for cough</u> present: Identify and treat underlying cause immediately.

* Infectious causes:

* <u>Self-limiting</u> infectious (e.g., <u>URTI</u>, <u>acute bronchitis</u>) or postinfectious cause most likely: <u>supportive care</u>  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2XcTxa0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fXckxa0)

* Non-<u>self-limiting</u> infection (e.g., <u>pneumonia</u>, <u>TB</u>, <u>pertussis</u>) most likely: Confirm diagnosis and begin <u>targeted therapy</u>. [[17]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IYcYqa0)

* New onset or exacerbation of pre-existing condition: Identify and treat the underlying condition.

* > 3 weeks duration and not post-infectious or due to pre-existing condition: Manage as <u>chronic cough</u>.





> [!TIP]
> Diagnostic studies for acute or <u>subacute cough</u> are not routinely indicated in patients without <u>red flags for cough</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PbcWFa0)[[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qacCla0)




> [!TIP]
> Postinfectious cough is the most common cause of <u>subacute cough</u> and often resolves without treatment. If it is interfering with the patient's <u>sleep</u> and/or daily activities, consider the use of <u>antitussives</u>.

## Chronic cough

### Management




* Obtain <u>CXR</u> (if not already performed).  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8dcO7Y0)

* Perform sequential diagnostics and/or empiric treatment for the most common causes of <u>chronic cough</u>.

* Consider the four most common etiologies and start empiric therapy for what is clinically most likely (see “Most common causes of <u>chronic cough</u>”). [[16]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/qacCla0)

* If suboptimal response after 4–6 weeks, proceed to the next condition. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/6acjla0)

* If symptoms persist despite evaluation and treatment for the most common causes:

* Consult or refer to a specialist, e.g., pulmonology, otolaryngology.

* Consider further diagnostic testing for less common causes (see “Diagnostics”).

## Treatment

### General principles




* All patients

* Identify and treat the underlying cause.

* Consider symptomatic therapy/<u>supportive care</u>.

* Eliminate any known modifiable triggers, e.g.: 

* <u>Smoking cessation</u>

* Potential offending medications (e.g., <u>ACE inhibitors</u>, <u>NSAIDs</u>, <u>beta blockers</u>).

* Environmental and/or <u>occupational exposures</u>.

* Acute and <u>subacute cough</u>: See “Management of acute and <u>subacute cough</u>.”

* <u>Chronic cough</u>: See “Management of <u>chronic cough</u>.”





> [!TIP]
> <u>Antibiotics</u> are not recommended for the routine treatment of cough unless there is a proven indication, e.g., in <u>pneumonia</u> or <u>acute bacterial sinusitis</u>.




### Symptomatic treatment for cough  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2XcTxa0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fXckxa0)[[28]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rYcfqa0)[[29]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fhckWX0)




#### Expectorants




* Indication: : can be considered for productive coughs

* Mechanism of action

* Increase bronchial fluid to reduce viscosity of mucus (no cough suppression)

* The exact mechanism is unknown.

* Guaifenesin (immediate release DOSAGE or extended release DOSAGE)

* Side effects include <u>nausea</u> and <u>headache</u>

* Symptoms of overdose include <u>vomiting</u>, <u>altered mental status</u> (due to <u>depression</u> of the <u>central nervous system</u>)

* <u>Potassium iodide</u>

* Side effects include <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>salivary gland</u> swelling and tenderness

* Symptoms of overdose include <u>Jod-Basedow phenomenon</u>, <u>Wolff-Chaikoff effect</u>, <u>hyperkalemia</u>





#### Cough suppressants (<u>antitussives</u>)




* Indications

* There is little evidence for the therapeutic <u>effectiveness</u> of <u>cough suppressants</u>.  [[24]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/2XcTxa0)[[25]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fXckxa0)

* Can be considered for nocturnal dry cough [[30]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/8X1O-20)

* Centrally acting: suppress the <u>cough reflex</u> arc at the level of the <u>central nervous system</u> (see “<u>Opioids</u>“ for more information)

* <u>Dextromethorphan</u> (immediate release DOSAGE or extended release DOSAGE)

* Side effects include <u>constipation</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>dizziness</u>

* Symptoms of overdose include <u>hallucinations</u>, <u>altered mental status</u>

* <u>Codeine</u> (<u>off-label</u>) DOSAGE 

* Side effects include, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>constipation</u>, <u>dizziness</u>, sedation, <u>palpitations</u>, <u>pruritus</u>

* Symptoms of overdose: see <u>opioid intoxication</u>

* Peripherally acting: suppress peripheral triggers of the <u>cough reflex</u> arc by anesthetizing respiratory stretch <u>receptors</u>
* Benzonatate DOSAGE

* A <u>local anesthetic</u> with cough suppressing properties which is administered as capsules

* Side effects: e.g., <u>nausea</u>, <u>dizziness</u>, <u>headache</u>, <u>altered mental status</u>

* Symptoms of overdose: visual disturbances, <u>tremor</u>, <u>seizures</u>





> [!WARNING]
> <u>Antitussive medications</u> decrease coughing and, therefore, should only be used in nonproductive cough, as coughing promotes the expectoration of mucus. <u>Antitussives</u> are not indicated in productive coughs or coughs caused by an infection.




> [!WARNING]
> Avoid prescribing <u>opioids</u> as <u>antitussive medication</u> in patients with <u>risk factors</u> for or a history of <u>substance use disorders</u>.




#### Mucolytics (e.g., <u>N-acetylcysteine</u>)




* Liquefy mucus by reducing the <u>disulfide bonds</u> of mucoproteins.

* Indications include:

* Hyperviscous chronic bronchopulmonary diseases (e.g., <u>COPD</u>, <u>cystic fibrosis</u>)

* <u>Acetaminophen overdose</u> (<u>antidote</u>): <u>acetylcysteine</u> restores depleted hepatic <u>glutathione</u>

* Prophylaxis of contrast agent nephropathy





#### Chest <u>physiotherapy</u>




* Loosens and mobilizes <u>airway</u> mucus through physical percussion, vibrations, and <u>postural drainage</u>

* May be beneficial for patients with ineffective cough (e.g., neuromuscular disorders) and/or bronchopulmonary diseases with increased <u>sputum</u> viscosity (e.g., <u>cystic fibrosis</u>, <u>bronchiectasis</u>, <u>pneumonia</u>)





### <u>Supportive care</u>




* Rest and adequate hydration

* Avoid <u>lung</u> irritants, e.g., smoke, incense. [[31]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IMXYJA)[[32]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/wMXhIA)

* Nonpharmacological measures may be beneficial, e.g.:  [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/rbcfEa0)

* Nasal saline for nasal congestion

* Honey  [[33]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0mXeVA)[[34]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/amXQVA)[[35]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YmXnVA)

* A humidifier
