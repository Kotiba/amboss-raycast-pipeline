# Wheezing

## Summary

<u>Wheezing</u> is a high-pitched, continuous respiratory sound due to narrowing of the <u>airways</u> below the thoracic inlet. <u>Causes of wheezing</u> can be infectious (e.g., <u>URTIs</u>, <u>bronchiolitis</u>), allergic or inflammatory (e.g., <u>anaphylaxis</u>, <u>bronchopulmonary dysplasia</u>), structural (e.g., <u>lower airway foreign body aspiration</u>), or other (e.g., <u>pulmonary edema</u>, neuromuscular disorders). The <u>initial management of wheezing</u> includes <u>airway management</u>, treating rapidly reversible <u>causes of wheezing</u>, etiology-specific diagnostics, and managing the underlying cause. <u>Stridor</u>, <u>stertor</u>, and <u>rales</u> may mimic <u>wheezing</u> but are separate entities that imply an alternative pathology.




See also “<u>Dyspnea</u>,” “<u>Airway obstruction</u>,” and “<u>Respiratory failure and arrest</u>.”
