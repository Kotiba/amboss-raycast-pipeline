# Diabetic retinopathy

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Endocrinology > Disorders of glucose metabolism > Diabetic retinopathy*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/jt0_W3)

---

## Summary

Diabetic <u>retinopathy</u> is a vascular disease of the <u>retina</u> that is usually asymptomatic in the early stages but can lead to visual impairment and blindness as the disease progresses. The majority of cases of diabetic <u>retinopathy</u> are detected during screening examinations. Further tests (<u>optical coherence tomography</u>, <u>fluorescein angiography</u>) may be performed to determine severity or identify complications. Management for all patients includes optimization of <u>treatment for diabetes</u>, managing <u>risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease</u> (<u>ASCVD</u>), and referral to an ophthalmologist for further management and monitoring. Further treatment depends on the subtype (<u>nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy</u> or <u>proliferative diabetic retinopathy</u>), presence of <u>macular edema</u>, and severity of diabetic <u>retinopathy</u> and includes <u>panretinal photocoagulation</u> (<u>PRP</u>), intravitreal anti-<u>vascular endothelial growth factor</u> (<u>VEGF</u>) and <u>vitrectomy</u>.

## Epidemiology

* After 15 years, approx. 80% of patients with <u>type 1 diabetes</u> will develop <u>retinopathy</u>. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)

* Among patients > 30 years old with <u>type 2 diabetes</u>:  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)

* Patients not requiring <u>insulin</u>

* Approx. 25% will develop <u>retinopathy</u> within 5 years of diagnosis.

* Approx. 50% will develop <u>retinopathy</u> within 19 years.

* Patients requiring <u>insulin</u>

* Approx. 40% will develop <u>retinopathy</u> within 5 years of diagnosis.

* Approx. 85% will develop <u>retinopathy</u> within 19 years.

* Diabetic <u>retinopathy</u> is the most common cause of visual impairment and blindness in patients aged 20–74 years. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0_1e5j0)




Epidemiological data refers to the US, unless otherwise specified.

## Clinical features

* Early stages: usually asymptomatic [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/N-1-xj0)

* Patients may experience symptoms of associated complications, e.g., <u>macular edema</u>, <u>vitreous hemorrhage</u>, and <u>retinal detachment</u>. [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/m-1VBj0)

* Blurred <u>vision</u>

* <u>Floaters</u>

* <u>Photopsia</u>

* <u>Scotoma</u>

* Sudden, painless <u>vision loss</u>

* Later stages: significant visual impairment progressing to blindness

## Screening

* Modalities [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0_1e5j0)

* Dilated <u>comprehensive eye examination</u> performed by an ophthalmologist/optometrist

* Retinal photography

* Onset

* <u>Type 1 DM</u>: within 5 years of the onset of disease  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BA1zNj0)

* <u>Type 2 DM</u>: at the time of diagnosis [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)

* Interval: every year  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dp11730)





> [!TIP]
> Less frequent <u>retinopathy</u> screening (e.g., every 2 years) may be considered in consultation with an ophthalmologist or optometrist for patients with <u>diabetes</u> who have a normal initial <u>eye exam</u> and good glycemic control. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/0_1e5j0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/BA1zNj0)
