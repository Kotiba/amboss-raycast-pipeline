# Vitamin D deficiency

## Summary

<u>Vitamin D</u> deficiency is characterized by low serum <u>vitamin D</u> levels, although there is significant debate among professional societies over what level constitutes a deficiency. Causes include decreased cutaneous production of <u>vitamin D</u>, insufficient dietary intake, inadequate gastrointestinal absorption, impaired <u>vitamin D</u> metabolism (i.e., decreased activation, increased deactivation), and excessive losses. Patients are typically asymptomatic unless the deficiency is severe, in which case symptoms of complications (e.g., <u>rickets</u>, <u>osteomalacia</u>, <u>hypocalcemia</u>) may develop. <u>Screening for vitamin D deficiency</u> should be performed in individuals with conditions placing them at increased risk (e.g., those causing impaired metabolism or absorption of <u>vitamin D</u>), and may be considered in certain population groups (e.g., individuals with <u>obesity</u>, who are pregnant and/or lactating, or who have darker <u>skin</u> pigmentation). <u>25-hydroxyvitamin D</u> (<u>25-OHD</u>) is the best diagnostic marker for <u>vitamin D</u> deficiency. Additional diagnostic studies are performed if complications or underlying conditions are suspected. Management involves the administration of <u>vitamin D</u> in addition to treatment of any associated complications and/or underlying conditions. Ensuring adequate daily <u>vitamin D</u> intake, especially in patients at increased risk for <u>vitamin D</u> deficiency, can help prevent associated complications.

## Etiology

### Causes of impaired vitamin D synthesis [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_h15gg0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)




#### Inadequate cutaneous synthesis of <u>vitamin D<sub>3</sub></u>




* Inadequate exposure to UV radiation, e.g., due to:

* Living in sufficiently northern or southern latitudes (typically above or below 33 degrees)

* Residence in an institution

* Sunscreen use

* <u>Skin</u> covering for cultural or religious reasons

* Darker <u>skin</u> pigmentation  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z51r5h0)

* Older age  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_h15gg0)





> [!TIP]
> The most common cause of <u>vitamin D</u> deficiency is inadequate exposure to UV radiation. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)




#### Inadequate dietary intake




* Restrictive diets (e.g., <u>vegan diets</u>, avoidance of <u>vitamin-D</u> fortified foods such as dairy products)  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)

* <u>Infants</u>: <u>Breast milk</u> is low in <u>vitamin D</u>.  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/A31ROg0)





#### Causes of impaired metabolism of vitamin D




* Conditions

* <u>Liver</u>;  disease (e.g., <u>cirrhosis</u>)

* <u>Chronic kidney disease</u>

* <u>Hypoparathyroidism</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ZM1ZMh0)

* <u>Vitamin D-dependent rickets type 1</u>

* <u>Granulomatous diseases</u>, <u>lymphoma</u>, and <u>primary hyperparathyroidism</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/IL1Y_h0)

* Medications: <u>cytochrome P450</u> modulators, e.g.: ;  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YM1nMh0)

* <u>Anticonvulsants</u>: <u>phenytoin</u>, <u>carbamazepine</u>, <u>phenobarbital</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_h15gg0)

* Antimicrobials: <u>rifampin</u>, <u>HAART</u>, <u>antifungals</u> [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sdXtrC)

* <u>Glucocorticoids</u>

* <u>Cholestyramine</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Od1IJf0)





### Causes of impaired absorption of vitamin D [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yoYdeJ)




Malabsorptive conditions can cause multiple dietary deficiencies, especially in <u>fat-soluble vitamins</u> (A, D, E, and K). These include:




* <u>Cystic fibrosis</u>

* <u>Chronic pancreatitis</u>

* <u>Inflammatory bowel disease</u>

* <u>Celiac disease</u>

* <u>Bariatric surgery</u> (e.g., <u>Roux-en-Y gastric bypass</u>)

* <u>Radiation colitis</u>





### Other causes [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)




* <u>Obesity</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_h15gg0)

* <u>Premature birth</u>  [[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Od1IJf0)[[13]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/y31dOg0)

* <u>Nephrotic syndrome</u>

* Target-organ resistance: <u>vitamin D-dependent rickets type 2</u> [[14]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/H31KPg0)

## Clinical features

* Mild or moderate <u>vitamin D</u> deficiency

* Typically asymptomatic [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* May lead to <u>osteoporosis</u> and <u>fragility fractures</u> [[12]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/yoYdeJ)

* Severe, chronic <u>vitamin D</u> deficiency may cause: [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/isXJuz)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Clinical features of osteomalacia and/or rickets</u>

* <u>Symptoms of hypocalcemia</u>
