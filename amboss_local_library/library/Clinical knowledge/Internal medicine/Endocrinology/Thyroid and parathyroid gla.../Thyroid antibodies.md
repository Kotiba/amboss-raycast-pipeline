# Thyroid antibodies

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/YM0nMg)

---

## Summary

Antithyroid <u>antibodies</u> are <u>autoantibodies</u> that target one or more components of the <u>thyroid gland</u> and can act as markers to help diagnose autoimmune <u>thyroid</u> conditions. For example, <u>thyroid</u> stimulating <u>hormone</u> <u>receptor</u> <u>antibodies</u> (<u>TRAb</u>) are seen mostly in <u>Graves' disease</u>, while <u>thyroid peroxidase antibodies</u> (<u>TPOAb</u>) and <u>thyroglobulin antibodies</u> (<u>TgAb</u>) are typical for <u>Hashimoto's thyroiditis</u> (but may also be present in <u>Graves' disease</u>). However, elevated <u>thyroid</u> <u>antibodies</u> do not always indicate disease, as somewhat increased levels may also be present in healthy individuals.

---

## Pathophysiology

* <u>TSH receptor antibodies</u> (<u>TRAbs</u>): There are three types of <u>antibodies</u> that may have either a stimulating (most common), blocking, or neutral effect on the <u>TSH receptors</u>.

* In <u>Graves disease</u>: stimulating <u>TRAbs</u> (<u>thyroid stimulating immunoglobulin</u>, TSI) → ↑ <u>thyroid</u> function and growth → <u>hyperthyroidism</u> and diffuse <u>goiter</u>

* In <u>Hashimoto thyroiditis</u>: blocking <u>TRAbs</u> → competitively block the activity of <u>TSH</u> on the <u>receptor</u> → <u>hypothyroidism</u>

* Neutral <u>TRAbs</u> have no effect on the <u>thyrotropin</u> <u>receptors</u> and their clinical and physiological relevance remains unclear.

* <u>Thyroid peroxidase antibodies</u> (<u>TPOAb</u>)

* <u>TPO</u> functions as a <u>catalyst</u> to the organification and coupling reactions in <u>thyroxine</u> production. <u>TPOAbs</u> prevent it from doing so.

* In <u>Hashimoto thyroiditis</u>: <u>TPOAbs</u> cause a fall in <u>thyroid hormone production</u> → <u>hypothyroidism</u>

* In <u>Graves disease</u>: <u>TPOAbs</u> have a <u>complement</u> fixing and cytotoxic capacity and are not known to play a role in pathogenesis of <u>Graves' disease</u>, but are seen as a result of the autoimmune nature of the disease.

* <u>Thyroglobulin antibodies</u> (<u>TgAb</u>)

* <u>Thyroglobulin</u> (<u>Tg</u>) is a protein produced in <u>thyroid</u> cells, which is involved in the synthesis of <u>thyroid hormone</u> and is normally found only in low concentrations in the bloodstream.

* Destructive <u>thyroid</u> processes (i.e., <u>Hashimoto thyroiditis</u>; ) and/or rapid disordered growth of <u>thyroid</u> tissue (e.g., <u>Graves disease</u>, <u>follicular thyroid cancer</u>) → release of circulating free <u>Tg</u> into the bloodstream → <u>TgAb</u> induction
