# Management of bradycardia

## Summary

<u>Bradycardia</u> is generally defined as a <u>heart rate</u> of < 60/min. All patients require urgent evaluation with <u>ECG</u> and monitoring. Patients with <u>unstable bradycardia</u> require immediate stabilization, initially with IV <u>atropine</u>, followed by <u>transcutaneous pacing</u> and/or IV <u>chronotropic</u> medications (e.g., <u>dopamine</u>, <u>epinephrine</u>), and <u>transvenous pacing</u> for refractory <u>bradycardia</u>. Treatment should also address reversible underlying <u>causes of bradycardia</u>, e.g., <u>hypoxemia</u>, <u>acute coronary syndrome</u>, <u>electrolyte</u> disturbances, and <u>medication-induced bradycardia</u>. Stable patients may not require immediate intervention, but often require monitoring and investigations to determine the underlying etiology and risk of progression to <u>unstable bradycardia</u> or <u>sudden cardiac death</u>. Definitive management depends on the underlying <u>cause of bradycardia</u> and can include <u>permanent pacemaker</u> implantation for patients with nonreversible <u>bradyarrhythmias</u>.




See also “<u>Overview of cardiac arrhythmias</u>,” “<u>Sinus node dysfunction</u>,” “<u>Atrioventricular block</u>,” and “<u>Cardiac implantable electronic devices</u>.”

## Causes of bradycardia

### Acute management of reversible causes [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntY7VI)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/3DYSVr)




* Provide <u>oxygen therapy</u> and/or <u>mechanical ventilation</u> for <u>hypoxemic respiratory failure</u>.

* Identify and <u>treat acute coronary syndrome</u>.

* Check core temperature and provide <u>rewarming therapy</u> for <u>hypothermia</u>.

* Identify <u>opioid toxidrome</u> and treat <u>opioid toxicity</u> if needed.

* Screen for and treat <u>medication-induced bradycardia</u>.

* Obtain <u>laboratory studies</u> for metabolic causes (e.g., <u>electrolytes</u>, <u>TSH</u>) and treat urgent disturbances.

* See “<u>Acute management checklist for hyperkalemia</u>.”

* See “<u>Acute management checklist for myxedema coma</u>.”

* Treat <u>permanent pacemaker malfunctions</u>, if present.





### Medication-induced bradycardia [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntY7VI)




Identify medications that may cause <u>bradycardia</u> (e.g., <u>beta blockers</u>, <u>CCBs</u>, <u>digoxin</u>, <u>antiarrhythmics</u>).




#### <u>Stable bradycardia</u>




Management depends on the indication and individual patient risk. Consult the prescribing clinician if available.




* Noncritical medication: Consider temporary hold or permanent discontinuation.

* Critical medication: Consider dosage reduction or alternative agents under specialist guidance.





#### <u>Unstable bradycardia</u>




* Follow the <u>adult unstable bradycardia algorithm</u>.

* If indicated, consider adding <u>antidotes</u> for <u>cardiovascular drug poisoning</u> (e.g., <u>CCB poisoning</u>, <u>beta blocker poisoning</u>, <u>digoxin poisoning</u>), such as:

* <u>Calcium for drug poisoning</u>

* <u>High-dose euglycemic insulin therapy</u>

* <u>High-dose glucagon</u>

* <u>Digoxin immune Fab</u>

* <u>Intravenous lipid emulsion therapy</u>

* <u>Methylene blue for drug poisoning</u>
