# Sinus bradycardia

## Summary

Sinus <u>bradycardia</u> is a <u>sinus rhythm</u> with a <u>heart rate</u> < 60/minute. It can be physiological, affecting healthy individuals, or pathological due to <u>sinus node dysfunction</u> (<u>SND</u>). The diagnosis is confirmed by <u>ECG</u>. Patients with <u>unstable bradycardia</u> are managed according to the <u>ACLS</u> <u>unstable bradycardia</u> algorithm. Mild asymptomatic sinus <u>bradycardia</u> usually requires no further workup or treatment. Patients with symptoms or a <u>heart rate</u> < 50/minute are evaluated for <u>SND</u>, with management directed at the underlying cause. Select patients with <u>SND</u> are candidates for placement of a <u>permanent pacemaker</u>.




See also "<u>Management of bradycardia</u>" and "<u>Sinus node dysfunction</u>."

---

## Etiology

* Physiological sinus bradycardia: : caused by high <u>vagal</u> tone in healthy individuals;  (e.g., athletes, healthy young adults, or occurrence during <u>sleep</u>) [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntY7VI)

* Pathological sinus bradycardia: caused by <u>SND</u>

* Intrinsic conditions affecting the <u>sinoatrial node</u> (<u>SA node</u>), e.g., degeneration, <u>ischemia</u>, infiltration, and <u>fibrosis</u>

* <u>Acute coronary syndrome</u> and/or <u>chronic coronary disease</u>

* <u>Cardiomyopathies</u>

* Extrinsic factors affecting <u>SA node</u> conduction, such as <u>vagal</u> tone, medications, and metabolic abnormalities

* <u>Vasovagal response</u>

* <u>Beta blockers</u>, <u>calcium channel blockers</u>, <u>sedative-hypnotic drugs</u>, cholinergic agents

* <u>Electrolyte</u> abnormalities

* <u>Hypoxia</u>

* <u>Hypothermia</u>

* <u>Hypothyroidism</u>

* See "<u>Etiology of sinus node dysfunction</u>" and "<u>Reversible causes of sinus node dysfunction</u>" for details.

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntY7VI)




* All patients: Obtain <u>ECG</u> to confirm sinus <u>bradycardia</u>.

* Likely <u>physiological sinus bradycardia</u>: No further workup is required in healthy individuals (e.g., athletes, healthy young adults, or occurrence during <u>sleep</u>) with mild, asymptomatic sinus <u>bradycardia</u>.

* <u>Symptomatic bradycardia</u> or <u>heart rate</u> < 50/minute: Initiate <u>diagnostics for sinus node dysfunction</u>.





> [!WARNING]
> Do not delay <u>initial management of bradycardia</u> for detailed diagnostics in patients with <u>clinical features of unstable bradycardia</u>.




### <u>ECG</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/AN1Rdh0)




* Rate < 60/minute [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ntY7VI)

* <u>P wave</u> in all leads, with normal polarity

* Regular P–P intervals
