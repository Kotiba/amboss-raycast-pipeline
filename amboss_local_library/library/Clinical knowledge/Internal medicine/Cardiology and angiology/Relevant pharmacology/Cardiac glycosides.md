## Summary

Cardiac glycosides are drugs that inhibit the Na<sup>+</sup>/<u>K<sup>+</sup></u>- ATPase found on the outer cell surface. Digoxin is the only drug of this class that is commonly used in clinical settings. The main indications for digoxin treatment are <u>atrial fibrillation</u> and <u>heart failure</u> in treatment-resistant cases. Because cardiac glycosides have a <u>narrow therapeutic index</u>, close monitoring of serum concentrations is necessary. Typical symptoms of <u>cardiac glycoside poisoning</u> are <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, blurry <u>vision</u>, and <u>cardiac arrhythmias</u>. Overdose can quickly become life-threatening and swift treatment is vital. The first-line treatment for <u>cardiac glycoside poisoning</u> is administration of digoxin-specific <u>antibodies</u>.




See also “<u>Digoxin poisoning</u>.”

## Overview

* Drugs: digoxin, ouabain

* Mechanism: inhibition of the cardiac and neuronal Na<sup>+</sup>/<u>K<sup>+</sup></u>- ATPase

* Onset of effect

* Oral: 0.5–2 h

* IV: 15–30 min

* <u>Half-life</u>: 36–40 hours

* Protein binding: 20–40%

* Elimination: renal

## Pharmacodynamics

* Inhibition of <u>Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase</u> → higher intracellular Na<sup>+</sup>concentration → reduced <u>efficacy</u> of <u>Na<sup>+</sup>/Ca<sup>2+</sup> exchangers</u> → higher intracellular <u>Ca<sup>2+</sup></u> concentration

* In <u>cardiomyocytes</u>, this leads to increased contractility (positive <u>inotropic</u> effect), reduced <u>velocity</u> of electric conduction (negative <u>dromotropic</u> effect) via <u>AV node</u> <u>depression</u>,;  and a reduction of the <u>heart rate</u> (negative <u>chronotropic</u> effect) via <u>SA node</u> <u>depression</u>.

* In <u>neurons</u> of the <u>vagal nerve</u>, this leads to increased <u>velocity</u> of electric conduction, which causes reduced <u>heart rate</u> (via increased <u>vagal</u> tone and a reflexive reduction of <u>sympathetic</u> transmission).




> [!NOTE]
> Cardiac glycosides inhibit <u>Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase</u>, increasing cardiac contractility and decreasing AV conduction and <u>heart rate</u>!

## Cardiac glycoside poisoning

* <u>Poisoning</u> can occur from the ingestion of medications and/or plants containing cardiac glycosides.

* Medications primarily consist of digoxin and its derivatives.

* Naturally occurring cardiac glycosides can be found in:

* Plants of the genus Digitalis (also known as foxglove), lily of the valley, oleander, and pheasant's <u>eye</u> (Adonis vernalis)

* Plants that cause <u>ouabain</u> <u>poisoning</u>

* Patients can present with life-threatening <u>bradyarrhythmias</u> or <u>tachyarrhythmias</u>;  and <u>electrolyte</u> disturbances (e.g., <u>hyperkalemia</u>).

* Other symptoms include <u>disorientation</u>, <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>,;  and visual disturbances (e.g., blurry <u>vision</u> with a yellow tint and halos).

* <u>ECG</u> most often shows <u>premature ventricular beats</u>; the <u>digitalis effect</u> (e.g., ↑ <u>PR interval</u>, <u>T-wave inversion</u> or flattening) may be present even at therapeutic levels. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Y11n2f0)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XNW9-l0)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/KmWUgN0)

* Treatment typically consists of <u>immediate hemodynamic support</u>, Digoxin immune fab administration, and correction of <u>electrolyte</u> abnormalities.

* See “<u>Digoxin poisoning</u>” for details on the diagnosis and management of all forms of <u>cardiac glycoside poisoning</u>.

## Indications

* <u>Congestive heart failure</u> (symptomatic patients with <u>NYHA</u> ≥ II despite pharmacotherapy)

* <u>Atrial fibrillation</u>

* <u>Supraventricular tachycardia</u>

## Contraindications

* <u>Ventricular fibrillation</u>

* Use with caution in pregnant women and in patients with:

* <u>Electrolyte</u> and fluid disorders (e.g., <u>volume depletion</u>, <u>hypokalemia</u>, <u>hypomagnesemia</u>, and/or <u>hypercalcemia</u> )

* Cardiovascular disorders (e.g., <u>acute coronary syndrome</u>, <u>AV blocks</u>, <u>Wolff-Parkinson-White</u> syndrome, <u>hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy</u>, <u>sick sinus syndrome</u>)

* <u>Renal failure</u> (can lead to <u>digoxin overdose</u> and, vice versa, digoxin can also cause/worsen <u>renal failure</u>)

* Certain medications (see “Interactions” below)

## Interactions

* <u>K<sup>+</sup></u>-depleting <u>diuretics</u> → <u>hypokalemia</u> → <u>arrhythmias</u>

* <u>Verapamil</u>, <u>diltiazem</u>, <u>amiodarone</u>, <u>quinidine</u> → possible overdose (reduce digoxin dose to avoid overdose)
