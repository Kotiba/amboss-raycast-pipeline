## Summary

Parenteral <u>anticoagulants</u> are routinely indicated for the prevention and treatment of <u>venous thromboembolism</u> (<u>VTE</u>). <u>Heparin</u> is typically the preferred agent for inpatient parenteral anticoagulation. Serious side effects include bleeding complications and <u>heparin-induced thrombocytopenia</u> (<u>HIT</u>), which causes arterial and <u>venous thromboembolism</u> due to an <u>antibody</u>-mediated aggregation of <u>platelets</u>. A drop in <u>platelet count</u> (< 100,000 /Î¼L or decrease of > 50% compared to baseline) may indicate <u>HIT</u>; therefore, the <u>platelet count</u> must be closely monitored in patients on parenteral <u>anticoagulants</u>, especially <u>heparin</u>. If <u>HIT</u> is suspected, treatment involves discontinuing all heparins and beginning <u>nonheparin anticoagulation</u> (usually <u>argatroban</u>).




See also “<u>Anticoagulation reversal</u>.”

## Overview

### Unfractionated heparin (<u>UFH</u>)




* Drug: heparin

* Administration

* Prophylaxis: subcutaneous

* Therapeutic: continuous intravenous infusion

* Monitoring during therapy: : <u>activated partial thromboplastin time</u> (<u>aPTT</u>);  , <u>platelet count</u> (including baseline before treatment is started)

* Clearance: hepatic (preferred agent for patients with <u>renal insufficiency</u>)

* <u>Antidote</u>: : protamine sulfate (a positively-charged protein that can neutralize negatively-charged <u>heparin</u> by forming inactive complexes)





> [!NOTE]
> In order to detect <u>heparin-induced thrombocytopenia</u>, <u>platelets</u> must be continuously monitored during <u>heparin</u> therapy and a baseline should be established before commencing treatment.




### Low molecular weight heparin (<u>LMWH</u>)




* Drugs: enoxaparin, dalteparin, tinzaparin, nadroparin, certoparin

* Administration: subcutaneous

* Monitoring during therapy: <u>anti-factor Xa activity</u>;  can be assessed in specific cases; not generally recommended

* Clearance: renal (contraindicated for patients with <u>renal insufficiency</u>)

* <u>Antidote</u>: : <u>protamine sulfate</u> (partial reversal)





### Synthetic heparin




* Drugs: fondaparinux

* Administration: subcutaneous

* Monitoring during therapy

* Not generally recommended

* <u>Anti-factor Xa activity</u> can be assessed in specific cases

* <u>Antidote</u>: : possibly activated <u>prothrombin complex concentrates</u> (<u>aPCC</u>)





### Heparinoid (<u>glycosaminoglycan</u>)




* Drugs: danaparoid

* Administration

* Prophylaxis: subcutaneous

* Therapeutic: continuous intravenous infusion

* Monitoring during therapy: anti-factor Xa activity

* <u>Antidote</u>: <u>protamine sulfate</u> (partial reversal)





### Direct thrombin inhibitors




* Drugs: argatroban, bivalirudin, desirudin; , <u>dabigatran</u>

* Administration

* Intravenous: <u>argatroban</u>, <u>bivalirudin</u>, <u>desirudin</u>

* Oral: <u>dabigatran</u> (see <u>direct oral thrombin inhibitors</u>)

* Monitoring during therapy: not generally recommended

* <u>Antidote</u>

* Possibly <u>aPCC</u> and/or <u>antifibrinolytics</u> (e.g., <u>tranexamic acid</u>)

* <u>Dabigatran</u>: <u>idarucizumab</u> (<u>monoclonal antibody</u>)

## Pharmacodynamics

### <u>Unfractionated heparin</u> (<u>UFH</u>)




* Enhances the activity of <u>antithrombin</u>

* Indirect inhibitor of

* <u>Factor Xa</u>: <u>antithrombin III</u> potentiation → inhibition of <u>factor Xa</u> → decreased activation of <u>prothrombin</u> → ↓ <u>thrombin</u>→ ↓ <u>fibrinogen</u> activation → ↓ <u>fibrin</u>

* <u>Thrombin</u> (<u>factor IIa</u>); : <u>UFH</u> binds <u>antithrombin III</u> and <u>thrombin</u> simultaneously at two distinct binding sites → <u>antithrombin III</u> and <u>thrombin</u> held by <u>heparin</u> in close proximity (complex formation) → ↑ <u>thrombin</u> inhibition → ↓ <u>fibrinogen</u> activation → ↓ <u>fibrin</u>

* Short <u>half-life</u>: <u>anticoagulant</u> effect quickly ceases once administration is stopped





### <u>Low molecular weight heparin</u> (<u>LMWH</u>) and <u>synthetic heparin</u> (<u>fondaparinux</u>)




* Mechanism of action [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/XC09qR)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Z2bZTG)

* <u>LMWH</u>: binds to <u>antithrombin III</u> ;   → inhibition of <u>factor Xa</u> → decreased conversion of <u>prothrombin</u> → ↓ <u>thrombin</u>

* <u>Synthetic heparin</u>: only binds to <u>antithrombin III</u> → selective inhibition of <u>factor Xa</u>

* Higher <u>bioavailability</u> than <u>unfractionated heparin</u>

* Long <u>half-life</u>: 2–4 times longer than <u>unfractionated heparin</u>





### <u>Direct thrombin inhibitors</u>




* Directly inhibit <u>thrombin</u> (freely circulating and in association with clots)

* Act independently from <u>antithrombin</u>





> [!WARNING]
> The effect of most parenteral <u>anticoagulants</u> (except for <u>direct thrombin inhibitors</u>) depends on native <u>antithrombin</u>. In patients with <u>antithrombin III deficiency</u> (e.g., due to <u>nephrotic syndrome</u>), this effect is reduced.

## Adverse effects

### General side effects




* Bleeding

* Drug-drug interactions





> [!NOTE]
> The significantly increased risk of bleeding is the main side effect of all <u>anticoagulants</u>.




### Specific side effects




| Overview |  |
| --- | --- |
| Agents | Side effects |
| <u>UFH</u> and <u>LMWH</u> | <br><br>* <u>Allergic reactions</u><br><br>* Risk of <u>heparin-induced thrombocytopenia</u>: <u>LMWH</u>:<u>UFH</u> ≈ 1:10<br><br>* <u>Osteoporosis</u><br><br>* <u>Drug interactions</u> (e.g., <u>ASA</u>, <u>tetracycline</u>)<br><br><br> |

| <u>Heparinoid</u> (<u>glycosaminoglycan</u>) | <br>* <u>Asthma exacerbation</u> (rarely)<br><br> |
| <u>Direct thrombin inhibitors</u> | <br>* <u>Allergic reactions</u>, <u>fever</u><br><br> |





### <u>Thrombocytopenia</u>




* Treatment with <u>heparin</u>, especially <u>UFH</u>, can cause <u>thrombocytopenia</u>.

* <u>Thrombocytopenia</u> may be immune-mediated <u>heparin-induced thrombocytopenia</u> (formerly known as <u>type 2 HIT</u>) or non-immune <u>heparin</u> associated <u>thrombocytopenia</u> (formerly known as <u>type 1 HIT</u>): See “<u>Alternative diagnoses of HIT</u>” for distinguishing features.





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Heparin</u> treatment requires regular monitoring of the <u>platelet count</u>, especially for <u>UFH</u>, even before starting treatment.

## Indications

### <u>Unfractionated heparin</u>




#### Low-dose therapy



* <u>VTE prophylaxis</u>




#### High-dose therapy




* Treatment of:

* Acute <u>DVT</u>

* Acute <u>pulmonary embolism</u>

* <u>Acute coronary syndrome</u>

* Acute arterial <u>thrombosis</u>

* <u>Prophylaxis of VTE</u> for patients during <u>hemodialysis</u>, <u>ECMO</u>, mechanical <u>heart valve</u> replacement

* <u>Atrial fibrillation</u>





### <u>Low molecular weight heparin</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sUbteG)




* Treatment of:

* Acute <u>DVT</u>

* Acute <u>pulmonary embolism</u>

* <u>Unstable angina</u>

* <u>Non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome</u>

* <u>STEMI</u> in patients undergoing <u>fibrinolysis</u>

* Prophylaxis of:

* <u>VTE</u>

* <u>Stent thrombosis</u> in patients undergoing <u>percutaneous coronary intervention</u>

* <u>Stroke</u> in patients with <u>Afib</u> undergoing <u>cardioversion</u>





### <u>Direct thrombin inhibitors</u>




* Treatment of <u>heparin-induced thrombocytopenia</u>

* Prophylaxis of:

* <u>VTE</u>

* <u>Stroke</u> and systemic embolism in patients with <u>nonvalvular Afib</u>





> [!NOTE]
> Use <u>direct thrombin inhibitors</u> (Bivalirudin, Argatroban, Dabigatran) to treat the BAD <u>HIT</u> (Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia), which has a worse prognosis than <u>non-immune heparin-associated thrombocytopenia</u>.

## Contraindications

* Recent <u>stroke</u>

* Uncontrolled <u>hypertension</u>

* Active bleeding

* <u>HIT</u> (except for <u>direct thrombin inhibitors</u> which can be used for anticoagulation in <u>HIT</u>)




We list the most important contraindications. The selection is not exhaustive.

## Special patient groups

### <u>Pregnancy</u>




* Physiological <u>hypercoagulability</u> (e.g., due to increased levels of <u>clotting factors</u>) leads to increased risk of <u>thrombosis</u>.

* <u>UFH</u> and <u>LMWH</u> can be used during <u>pregnancy</u>: neither cross the <u>placenta</u> nor are they transferred through <u>breast milk</u>

* <u>LMWH</u> has fewer side effects.





### Patients with decreased renal function



* In severe <u>renal failure</u>: accumulation of <u>LMWH</u> → increased bleeding risk → adjust dose or switch to <u>UFH</u>
