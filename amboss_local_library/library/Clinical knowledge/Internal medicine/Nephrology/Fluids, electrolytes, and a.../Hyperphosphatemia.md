## Summary

Hyperphosphatemia is the abnormal elevation of serum <u>phosphate</u>. The majority of the body's <u>phosphate</u> is stored in bone, while the remainder plays a critical role in the <u>intracellular space</u> as part of multiple <u>proteins</u> and, in the form of <u>adenosine triphosphate</u> (<u>ATP</u>), as part of energy stores. Serum <u>phosphate</u> levels are regulated by <u>parathyroid hormone</u> (<u>PTH</u>), <u>fibroblast growth factor 23</u> (<u>FGF23</u>), <u>vitamin D</u>, and <u>calcium</u>. The most common <u>cause of hyperphosphatemia</u> is <u>chronic kidney disease</u> (<u>CKD</u>). In <u>end-stage renal disease</u>, <u>CKD-mineral and bone disorder</u> (<u>CKD-MBD</u>) leads to dysregulation of <u>phosphate</u>, <u>calcium</u>, and <u>PTH</u>, resulting in <u>secondary hyperparathyroidism</u>. Hyperphosphatemia is often asymptomatic and diagnosed serologically. Clinical features of severe hyperphosphatemia are often related to <u>hypocalcemia</u>. In addition, insoluble <u>calcium</u> <u>phosphate</u> formation can lead to <u>nephrocalcinosis</u> and <u>calcinosis cutis</u>. Treatment of hyperphosphatemia centers around treating the underlying cause and may also include <u>IV fluids</u>, <u>hemodialysis</u>, and, in selected cases, pharmacotherapy (e.g., <u>phosphate binders</u>).

## Clinical features

* Often asymptomatic

* Symptoms are often related to concomitant: [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* <u>Hypocalcemia</u>: e.g., <u>paresthesias</u>, muscle spasms, <u>cardiac arrhythmias</u>

* <u>Nephrolithiasis</u>: e.g., <u>renal colic</u>, <u>hematuria</u>, <u>dysuria</u>

* Calcifications under the <u>skin</u> (i.e., <u>calcinosis cutis</u>) and in other tissues: e.g., localized <u>pruritus</u> and <u>pain</u> [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/35YSPp)





> [!TIP]
> High <u>phosphate</u> levels cause the formation of insoluble <u>calcium</u> <u>phosphate</u>, which can lead to <u>hypocalcemia</u>, <u>nephrolithiasis</u>, and tissue calcifications.

## Treatment

### General principles [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kr1mhR0)




Consult nephrology to guide management.




* All patients

* Identify and treat the underlying cause.

* Counsel patients to limit their dietary intake of <u>phosphate</u>.

* Discontinue medications that can lead to elevated <u>phosphate</u> levels.

* Patients with chronic hyperphosphatemia:  (e.g., resulting from <u>CKD-MBD</u>): Consider pharmacotherapy, including <u>phosphate binders</u> (e.g., <u>sevelamer</u>, <u>calcium</u> acetate).

* Patients with severe hyperphosphatemia (i.e., serum <u>phosphate</u> > 6.25 mg/dL)

* Consider <u>IV fluids</u> to increase renal <u>phosphate</u> clearance and excretion.

* Evaluate for <u>indications for renal replacement therapy</u> (<u>RRT</u>), e.g.:

* Severely elevated <u>phosphate</u>

* Severe <u>AKI</u> or advanced <u>CKD</u>





### Pharmacotherapy [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/taWXmP0)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MRcMnX0)




Pharmacotherapy is usually reserved for patients with chronic hyperphosphatemia (e.g., patients with <u>CKD-MBD</u>) if dietary changes and/or <u>hemodialysis</u> do not maintain normal <u>phosphate</u> levels.




#### Oral phosphate binders




* Overview

* Indicated for patients with <u>CKD-MBD</u> who are on <u>RRT</u>

* The dose is adjusted to achieve target serum <u>phosphate</u> levels.

* Mechanism of action: bind <u>phosphate</u> in the gut (e.g., <u>sevelamer</u> is nonabsorbable) → ↓ <u>phosphate</u> absorption → ↓ serum <u>phosphate</u> → ↓ <u>PTH</u>

* Options

* Sevelamer DOSAGE

* <u>Calcium</u> acetate DOSAGE  [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MRcMnX0)

* Aluminum hydroxide (not commonly used because of the potential for aluminum toxicity)

* Adverse effects include: <u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>, <u>diarrhea</u>, <u>constipation</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>, and <u>hypophosphatemia</u>





#### Additional agents




Additional agents are used in the management of <u>hyperparathyroidism</u>.




* <u>Vitamin D</u>: e.g., <u>calcitriol</u>, paricalcitol, doxercalciferol

* <u>Calcimimetics</u>: e.g., <u>cinacalcet</u>, etelcalcetide
