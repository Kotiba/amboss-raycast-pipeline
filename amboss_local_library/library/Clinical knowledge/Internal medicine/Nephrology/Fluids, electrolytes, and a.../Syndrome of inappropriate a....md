## Summary

Syndrome of inappropriate <u>antidiuretic hormone</u> secretion (SIADH) is the most common cause of <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u>. SIADH is an endocrine disorder caused by increased <u>antidiuretic hormone</u> (<u>ADH</u>) secretion in the <u>pituitary gland</u> (e.g., due to infection, drugs), <u>ectopic</u> production of <u>ADH</u> (e.g., in <u>small cell lung carcinoma</u>), or enhanced <u>ADH</u> <u>receptor</u> activation in the <u>kidneys</u> as a result of a genetic mutation (i.e., nephrogenic SIADH). Patients with SIADH present with <u>hyponatremia</u> caused by increased renal water retention. Diagnosis requires confirmation of serum <u>hyponatremia</u> and <u>hypoosmolality</u>, increased urine salt excretion and <u>urine osmolality</u>, clinical <u>euvolemia</u>, and exclusion of other causes of <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u> (e.g., <u>hypothyroidism</u>, <u>adrenal insufficiency</u>, <u>diuretic</u> use). Treatment involves managing symptoms of <u>acute hyponatremia</u> with <u>hypertonic saline</u>, addressing underlying causes (e.g., infection, cancer, medications), reducing free water intake with fluid restriction, and, if needed, increasing free water excretion with pharmacological treatments.




For detailed information on <u>hyponatremia management</u>, see “<u>Treatment of hyponatremia</u>.”

## Pathophysiology

* ↑ <u>ADH</u> secretion → <u>receptor</u>-mediated <u>signaling cascade</u> in the <u>distal convoluted tubules</u> and the <u>collecting ducts</u> of the <u>kidneys</u> → build-up of additional water canals (aquaporin-2) in the luminal <u>cell membrane</u>

* Water is drawn out of the urine and into the hyperosmolar <u>kidney</u> tissue → <u>concentration of urine</u> and ↑ <u>urine osmolality</u> (becomes higher than <u>serum osmolality</u>)

* Water retention → ↓ <u>serum osmolality</u> with transient volume expansion → ↑ <u>ANP</u>, ↑ <u>BNP</u>, and ↓ <u>aldosterone</u> → ↑ urinary <u>sodium</u> and water excretion → <u>euvolemic hyponatremia</u>

* Osmotic fluid shifts → <u>cerebral edema</u> and ↑ <u>intracranial pressure</u> (may occur in patients with extremely low Na<sup>+</sup> levels)

* For more information regarding <u>ADH</u> secretion and regulation, see “<u>Antidiuretic hormone</u>.”

## Clinical features

### Symptoms of <u>hyponatremia</u>




<u>Chronic hyponatremia</u> may be asymptomatic and <u>CNS</u> symptoms are less common.




* Mild

* <u>Anorexia</u>

* <u>Nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* Muscle cramps

* Moderate

* <u>Muscle weakness</u>

* <u>Lethargy</u>

* Confusion

* Severe 

* <u>Seizures</u>

* Altered consciousness





### Other clinical features




* <u>Normotension</u>

* Symptoms of the underlying condition





> [!TIP]
> SIADH patients are usually <u>euvolemic</u>, <u>normotensive</u>, and have no <u>edema</u>. A <u>hyponatremic</u> patient with <u>edema</u> should raise suspicion for other conditions (e.g. <u>congestive heart failure</u>).

## Diagnosis

### Approach [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)




* Follow the <u>diagnostic approach to hyponatremia</u> to confirm <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u>. 

* Order initial blood studies (e.g., <u>CMP</u>, <u>serum osmolality</u>) and urine studies (e.g., <u>sodium</u>, <u>creatinine</u>, <u>osmolality</u>).

* Order additional studies to exclude other causes of <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u> (e.g., <u>TFTs</u>, morning <u>cortisol</u> levels).

* Confirm the patient meets the <u>diagnostic criteria for SIADH</u>.

* Consider additional tests for possible underlying <u>causes of SIADH</u>, e.g.:

* <u>X-ray</u> and/or CT chest

* CT or <u>MRI</u> head





> [!TIP]
> SIADH is a diagnosis of exclusion. Rule out other causes of <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u> before making a diagnosis of SIADH.

### Additional findings [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-7YDoq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)




These findings are not required to make a diagnosis, but may further support SIADH as the cause of <u>euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia</u>.




* Blood studies

* Plasma <u>ADH</u> (inappropriately) normal to elevated

* <u>BUN</u> < 10 mg/dL

* Serum <u>uric acid</u> < 4 mg/dL  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)

* <u>Fractional excretion</u> values

* <u>FEUA</u> > 12%

* <u>FEUrea</u> > 55%

* <u>FENa</u> > 1%

* Response to initial therapeutic measures, e.g.: [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)

* ↑ Serum <u>sodium</u> and ↓ urine <u>sodium</u> excretion after fluid restriction

* ↓ Serum <u>sodium</u> (or no change) after a <u>trial of volume expansion</u>





> [!TIP]
> <u>FEUA</u> and <u>FEUrea</u> values are not affected by <u>diuretic</u> use. [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/dLco910)[[11]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U_bbnw)

## Treatment

For patients with <u>acute hyponatremia</u> and/or who are severely symptomatic; , initiate immediate 3% <u>hypertonic saline</u> administration;  and start <u>ICU</u> monitoring; a <u>loop diuretic</u> (e.g., <u>furosemide</u>) may be added in severe cases. See also “<u>Treatment of hyponatremia</u>.” [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)




> [!WARNING]
> In patients with acute and <u>severely symptomatic hyponatremia</u>, immediate use of <u>hypertonic saline</u> can treat and/or prevent serious neurologic complications (e.g., <u>cerebral edema</u>, <u>brain herniation</u>, <u>seizures</u>, <u>altered mental status</u>). [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)




### Approach [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)




The following management applies to patients with nonacute, nonsevere <u>hyponatremia</u> associated with SIADH and after the initial stabilization of patients with acute and/or <u>severely symptomatic hyponatremia</u>.




* Management

* Restrict all fluids; do not limit salt or protein intake.

* If initial measures fail, consider pharmacotherapy.

* Manage the underlying cause (e.g., <u>nausea</u>, infection, cancer, offending medications).

* Monitoring

* Measure serum <u>sodium</u> frequently.

* Follow the recommended <u>sodium</u> correction rates for <u>chronic hyponatremia</u> to prevent <u>sodium</u> overcorrection.

* If recommended limits are exceeded, provide <u>management of sodium overcorrection</u>.





> [!TIP]
> For patients with <u>chronic hyponatremia</u>, the maximum limit (not goal) for serum <u>sodium</u> level increase is 10–12 mEq/L per 24 hours. Use a lower maximum limit of 8 mEq/L per 24 hours for patients with <u>high-risk factors for osmotic demyelination syndrome</u>. [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)




> [!WARNING]
> In patients with <u>chronic hyponatremia</u>, <u>sodium</u> overcorrection can lead to <u>osmotic demyelination syndrome</u>. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)




### Fluid restriction [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)




Restriction of all fluids (e.g., PO intake, <u>IV fluids</u>, medications, IV flushes) is the first-line treatment for SIADH.




* Recommend < 1000 mL/day for most patients.  [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)

* Adjust based on the patient's response (i.e., serum <u>sodium</u> levels and urine output).





> [!TIP]
> Ideally, daily fluid intake should be 500 mL less than daily urine output. [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)




### Pharmacotherapy [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-7YDoq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)[[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/U6bb4u)




These agents are used to increase free water excretion. Medications should be ordered in consultation with a specialist.




* Indications

* Fluid restriction fails to increase serum <u>sodium</u>.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)

* Consider early pharmacotherapy if: [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)

* <u>Urine osmolality</u> is ≥ 500 mOsm/kg

* Urinary <u>sodium</u> is â¥ 130 mEq/L

* Important considerations

* The optimal pharmacotherapy for SIADH is controversial; recommendations vary among guidelines and experts.

* <u>Vaptans</u>, <u>urea</u>, and <u>demeclocycline</u> should not be used in combination.

* If <u>vaptans</u> are used, avoid fluid restriction during the first 24–48 hours of treatment.  [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)

* Agents   [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MXYMAn)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)

* <u>Vaptans</u>, e.g., <u>conivaptan</u>, <u>tolvaptan</u>

* <u>Urea</u>   [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-7YDoq)

* <u>Demeclocycline</u>

* Low-dose <u>loop diuretics</u> (e.g., <u>furosemide</u>) combined with oral salt tablets (<u>off-label</u>)  [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/-7YDoq)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/PMbWK8)
