## Summary

Hospital-acquired infections (HAIs), also called <u>health care-associated infections</u> (<u>HCAIs</u>) and nosocomial infections, are infections contracted in a hospital or other <u>health care facility</u> that were not present or incubating at the time of admission. Symptoms and/or signs of an HAI typically manifest 48 hours or more after admission. HAIs are transmitted through patient exposure to health care workers, other patients, hospital equipment, or interventional procedures. The most common types of HAIs include <u>intravascular catheter-related bloodstream infection</u> (<u>CRBSI</u>), <u>catheter-related urinary tract infection</u> (<u>CAUTI</u>), <u>hospital-acquired pneumonia</u>, <u>ventilator-associated pneumonia</u>, <u>surgical site infection</u> (<u>SSI</u>), and <u>Clostridioides difficile infection</u> (<u>CDI</u>). The most common causative pathogens differ depending on the site of infection (e.g., <u>gastrointestinal tract</u>, <u>urinary tract</u>, <u>lungs</u>, <u>skin</u>). An increasing number of HAIs are caused by <u>multidrug-resistant organisms</u> (<u>MDROs</u>). Common <u>MDROs</u> include <u>methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus</u> (<u>MRSA</u>) and <u>extended-spectrum beta-lactamase-producing bacteria</u> (<u>ESBL</u>).




This article provides an overview of the diagnosis and management of common HAIs. <u>Prevention of nosocomial infections</u> is covered separately.




See also “<u>Intravascular catheter-related bloodstream infections</u>,” “<u>Device-related infections</u>,” and “<u>Bacteremia</u>.”

## Definitions

The following terms are often used interchangeably:




* Hospital-acquired infection (nosocomial infection): an infection acquired in a hospital or another inpatient <u>health care facility</u> that was not present or incubating at the time of admission [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/C11qQf0)

* Health care-associated infection (<u>HCAI</u>): an infection acquired after receiving health care in any setting (including a hospital, long-term care facility, <u>nursing home</u>, ambulatory care clinic, home care, or surgical intervention) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/x11EQf0)

## Etiology

### Risk factors for HAIs [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Wp0PoS)[[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Qp0u6S)[[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z2Xr4x)[[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/sgct9b0)




* Age > 70 years

* Lengthy hospital stays; <u>pathogen</u> transmission can occur via:

* Medical staff (e.g., insufficient <u>disinfection</u> of hands, clothing)

* Contact surfaces (e.g., equipment, furniture)

* Contaminated indoor air (e.g., via droplets)

* <u>Iatrogenic</u> causes

* Foreign bodies (e.g., catheters, intravenous catheters, <u>endotracheal tubes</u>) and invasive instruments

* Multiple interventional procedures (e.g., in patients with <u>shock</u>, major trauma, <u>acute renal failure</u>, <u>coma</u>)

* <u>Mechanical ventilation</u>

* <u>Hemodialysis</u>

* Recent <u>antibiotic</u> use

* Metabolic diseases (especially <u>diabetes mellitus</u>)

* <u>Immunosuppression</u>





> [!TIP]
> Admission through the emergency department is associated with an increased risk of <u>hospital-acquired pneumonia</u>, as airborne pathogens are easily transmitted in crowded health care settings.
