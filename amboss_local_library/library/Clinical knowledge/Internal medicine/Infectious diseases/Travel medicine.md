# Travel medicine

## Summary

Travel medicine is a discipline focused on the prevention and management of health risks associated with travel, especially infectious diseases. Travel medicine is primarily concerned with international travel, particularly to tropical and subtropical regions. Before departure, travelers should have a <u>pretravel consultation</u> with their primary care provider or a travel medicine specialist to assess health risks based on the traveler’s <u>medical history</u>, destination, and itinerary. Risk-reduction strategies, including risk communication and preventive measures (e.g., <u>vaccinations</u> before travel, <u>malaria prophylaxis</u>, self-treatment strategies), should be addressed during this consultation. After travel, patients may present with infections or complications related to exposures during their trip and require a thorough clinical assessment and management.




<u>Migrant and refugee health</u> is discussed separately.

---

## Pretravel management

### General principles




* Discuss findings of risk assessment and offer appropriate management, e.g.:

* <u>Immunizations before travel</u>

* Specific prevention strategies (e.g., <u>malaria prophylaxis</u>)

* Consult a specialist for management strategies (e.g., travel deferment) in individuals with acute events (e.g., recent <u>surgery</u>, exacerbation of chronic conditions, <u>DVT</u>).

* Pretravel risk management helps prevent the international spread of infectious diseases.





### Immunizations before travel [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Bs1zCR0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dvd1XH0)




* Ensure individuals are up to date on <u>age-appropriate vaccines</u>.

* Provide a record of all administered <u>vaccines</u>.





#### General pretravel <u>vaccines</u>




The following are recommended for individuals from the United States who are planning international travel.




* <u>HepA vaccine</u> and <u>HepB vaccine</u>

* <u>Infants</u> 6–11 months of age: 1 dose of <u>HepA vaccine</u> before international travel (does not count toward <u>routine immunization</u>)

* Adults: accelerated 3-dose series of <u>HepA-HepB</u> <u>vaccine</u>, followed by a booster at 12 months

* <u>MMR vaccine</u>

* <u>Infants</u> 6–11 months of age: 1 dose of <u>MMR</u> (does not count toward <u>routine immunization</u>)

* Children ≥ 12 months of age: 2-dose series of <u>MMR</u> or <u>MMRV</u> (counts toward <u>routine immunization</u>) [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/981NKi0)

* <u>Influenza vaccine</u>: ≥ 2 weeks before travel if traveling to areas with <u>influenza</u> activity  [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z7Wro50)[[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/FAdgls0)

### Additional prevention strategies [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dvd1XH0)




Consider based on the traveler's risk assessment.




* Infectious diseases

* <u>Traveler's diarrhea prophylaxis</u>: if traveling to high-risk areas  [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dvd1XH0)

* <u>Malaria prophylaxis</u>: if traveling to <u>endemic</u> regions (e.g., West and Central Africa); see “Tips and Links” for current <u>malaria</u> recommendations depending on travel destination.

* Other recommendations

* <u>Tick bite prevention</u>

* Avoid swimming in freshwater in high-risk areas for <u>leptospirosis</u>  or <u>schistosomiasis</u>.

* Chronic medical conditions

* Bring a list of medications and medical conditions.

* Bring an adequate medication supply in original containers in carry-on luggage.

* Ensure availability of <u>oxygen therapy</u> during travel if appropriate.

* <u>VTE prophylaxis</u>

* Indications: > 4 hours travel periods in individuals with an additional <u>risk factor for DVT</u>  [[21]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/z8Yr6I)

* First-line: <u>graduated compression stockings</u>

* Other

* Consider a dental assessment for longer trips (≥ 6 months).

* Counsel on <u>altitude-related disorders</u> for destinations 6,600–8,200 ft above sea level. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WDdPWH0)

* Counsel on <u>diving-related illnesses</u> prevention.

* Consider <u>anticholinergic agents</u> for <u>motion sickness</u> prophylaxis.





### Self-treatment recommendations for specific conditions [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WDdPWH0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dvd1XH0)




* <u>Traveler's diarrhea</u>

* Supportive therapy: oral hydration, <u>antidiarrheal agents</u>

* Empiric <u>antibiotics for traveler's diarrhea</u>: only indicated for moderate to severe symptoms that affect normal activities  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/WDdPWH0)

* Advise patients to seek medical care if symptoms are severe (e.g., high <u>fever</u>, bloody <u>diarrhea</u>, <u>dehydration</u>) or persist for 24–36 hours.

* <u>Urinary tract infection</u> (<u>UTI</u>): <u>antibiotics</u> based on previous causative pathogens for the <u>treatment of UTIs</u> in case of recurrent infections

* <u>Vaginal yeast infection</u>: patient-preferred <u>antifungal</u> if <u>prone</u> to or at risk of <u>vulvovaginal candidiasis</u> (e.g., prior infections, sexually active, on <u>antibiotics</u>)

* <u>Malaria</u>: <u>standby emergency treatment</u>





> [!TIP]
> Consider prescriptions for self-treatment depending on the risk, type of travel, availability of medical care, and the patient's ability to self-treat. [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/Dvd1XH0)
