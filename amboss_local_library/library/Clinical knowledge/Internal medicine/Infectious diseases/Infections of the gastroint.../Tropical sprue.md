# Tropical sprue

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Infectious diseases > Infections of the gastrointestinal tract > Tropical sprue*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/1902mR)

---

## Summary

Tropical sprue is a <u>malabsorption syndrome</u> that primarily occurs in tropical regions such as South Asia and the Caribbean. The exact cause is unknown. Tropical sprue typically manifests with <u>chronic diarrhea</u>, progressive weight loss, <u>anorexia</u>, and features of nutritional deficiencies such as <u>anemia</u>, neuropathy, and bone disease. Diagnosis is based on the clinical presentation, evidence of <u>malabsorption</u>, and characteristic histological findings on a <u>small bowel biopsy</u> (e.g., villous <u>atrophy</u>), after other causes have been excluded. Management focuses on correcting fluid, <u>electrolyte</u>, and nutritional deficits, along with a prolonged course of <u>antibiotics</u> (e.g., <u>tetracycline</u>) and supplementation with <u>folic acid</u> and <u>vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>.

---

## Definitions

Tropical sprue is a disease characterized by <u>chronic diarrhea</u> with subsequent <u>malabsorption</u> in association with a stay in the tropics or subtropics.

---

## Etiology

* The exact cause is unknown, but it is believed to be multifactorial. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)

* Potential associations [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* May occur after an episode of acute <u>bacterial gastroenteritis</u> that leads to structural damage of the intestinal <u>mucosa</u>

* Gut dysbiosis, including <u>small intestinal bacterial overgrowth</u>

* <u>Protozoal</u>, fungal, or viral exposures

---

## Clinical features

* <u>Chronic diarrhea</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)

* Progressive weight loss [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)

* Abdominal cramps

* <u>Anorexia</u> [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* Fatigue [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* Features of <u>malabsorption</u> and nutritional deficiencies [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* <u>Anemia</u>

* Neuropathy

* Bone disease

* <u>Proximal</u> <u>muscle weakness</u> (due to <u>potassium</u> deficiency)





> [!NOTE]
> <u>Celiac disease</u> and tropical sprue have similar features (e.g., <u>steatorrhea</u>, abdominal <u>pain</u>, weight loss), but only tropical sprue responds to <u>antibiotics</u>.

---

## Diagnosis

The diagnosis is established based on characteristic clinical features, laboratory evidence of <u>malabsorption</u>, characteristic histological findings, and the exclusion of other causes. [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)




### <u>Laboratory studies</u>




* Blood tests: to assess for <u>malabsorption</u> and nutritional deficiencies and rule out differential diagnoses [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)

* <u>CBC</u>: <u>megaloblastic anemia</u>

* ↓ <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>

* ↓ <u>Folate</u>

* <u>Hypoalbuminemia</u>

* <u>Hypocalcemia</u>

* <u>Vitamin D deficiency</u>

* <u>Celiac disease serology</u>

* Stool analysis

* Fecal fat 10–40 g/day

* <u>Diagnostics for infectious gastroenteritis</u>: <u>stool culture</u> and/or <u>PCR</u> for pathogens such as <u>Giardia lamblia</u> and <u>Entamoeba histolytica</u>





### <u>Small bowel</u> endoscopy with <u>biopsies</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0)




* Histological findings: villous <u>atrophy</u>, elongated crypts, inflammatory cells (<u>plasma cells</u>, <u>lymphocytes</u>, <u>eosinophils</u>)

* Mainly affects the <u>duodenum</u> and <u>jejunum</u> but may progress to the <u>ileum</u>

---

## Management

* Fluid and <u>electrolyte repletion</u> as needed [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* <u>Tetracycline</u> for 2 months [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)

* <u>Vitamin</u> supplementation [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0)[[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/d0VoUG0) 

* <u>Folic acid</u>

* <u>Vitamin B<sub>12</sub></u>

* <u>Nutritional support</u> to treat <u>malnutrition</u>, e.g.: [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/W0VPUG0) 

* Restriction of <u>long-chain fatty acids</u> (may reduce <u>steatorrhea</u>)

* Supplementation with medium-chain <u>triglycerides</u>
