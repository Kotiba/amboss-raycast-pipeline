# Miscellaneous mosquito-borne diseases

*Categories: Clinical knowledge > Internal medicine > Infectious diseases > Zoonoses and vector-borne diseases > Miscellaneous mosquito-borne diseases*

[Original Article Link](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/7p04IS)

---

## Summary

Mosquito-borne diseases are a group of viral infections transmitted by the bite of an infected mosquito, typically from the Culex, Anopheles, or Aedes species. Vertical and human-to-human transmission (e.g., via <u>blood transfusions</u>, organ, and tissue transplants) are also possible. These viral diseases belong to three main families: <u>Togaviridae</u> (<u>Chikungunya virus</u>, <u>Eastern equine encephalitis</u> (EEEV), <u>Western equine encephalitis</u> (<u>WEEV</u>), <u>Flaviviridae</u> (<u>West Nile virus</u>, <u>Dengue virus</u>, <u>Zika virus</u>, <u>Yellow fever virus</u>, <u>Japanese encephalitis virus</u>), and <u>Bunyaviridae</u> (<u>Rift Valley fever virus</u>, <u>La Crosse encephalitis</u> <u>virus</u>). Clinical features of these diseases vary but often include an initial systemic <u>febrile</u> illness (i.e., <u>fever</u>, <u>headache</u>, <u>myalgias</u>, gastrointestinal symptoms) that progresses to a neuroinvasive disease (e.g., <u>meningitis</u>, <u>encephalitis</u>). Diagnosis is made via <u>antibody</u> detection (e.g., <u>IgM</u>, <u>IgG</u>), <u>ELISA</u>, FIA, or <u>PCR</u>. Treatment is typically supportive. Prevention primarily consists of mosquito control measures (i.e., adequate clothing, insect repellents, mosquito nets, minimizing mosquito breeding).

### Chikungunya fever [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Found primarily in tropical and subtropical regions (e.g., Central and South America, Caribbean, sub-Saharan Africa, Southern and Western Asia, and Europe)[[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Periodic <u>outbreaks</u> with high <u>attack rates</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Groups at risk for severe disease [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Individuals with underlying chronic medical diseases  (e.g., <u>diabetes</u>, <u>heart</u> disease)

* Adults > 65 years of age

* <u>Neonates</u> with <u>intrapartum</u> exposure

* Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>Chikungunya virus</u>  (belongs to the alphavirus genus of the family <u>Togaviridae</u>)

* <u>Vectors</u>: mosquitoes (<u>Aedes aegypti</u> and Aedes albopictus), possibly leading to <u>coinfection</u> with <u>dengue</u>

* Reservoirs: nonhuman or  human primates, bats  [[1]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/kIYm1q)

* Route of transmission: mosquito bite (most common)  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Clinical features  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* <u>Incubation period</u>: usually 3–7 days

* Sudden onset <u>fever</u> (typically > 39Â°C)

* <u>Malaise</u>

* Severe bilateral <u>polyarthralgia</u>

* <u>Joint</u> swelling (effusion, <u>synovitis</u>, periarticular <u>edema</u>)

* <u>Headache</u>

* <u>Maculopapular rash</u>

* Other (less common): e.g.,  <u>lymphadenopathy</u>, bleeding (rare) , <u>pruritus</u>, <u>encephalopathy</u>, GI symptoms, <u>conjunctivitis</u>

* Diagnosis [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fwWkRn0)

* <u>Clinical diagnosis</u> based on: [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* History of recent travel to <u>endemic</u> regions

* Typical clinical features  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* Exclusion of <u>dengue virus infection</u> with <u>dengue diagnostics</u>  [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Routine <u>laboratory studies</u> [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0) 

* <u>CBC</u>: may reveal  <u>lymphopenia</u> < 1,000/mmÂ³ (most common laboratory finding), and/or <u>thrombocytopenia</u> [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* <u>CMP</u>: may reveal↑ <u>AST</u>, ↑ <u>ALT</u>, <u>hypocalcemia</u>, and/or ↑ <u>creatinine</u>

* Diagnostic confirmation

* <u>RT-PCR</u>: during the first week of illness (<u>viremic</u> phase) [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* <u>Serology</u>: ↑ <u>IgM</u>  [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* Viral culture: not routinely performed [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)

* Nationally <u>notifiable disease</u> [[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fwWkRn0)

* Differential diagnosis [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* <u>Dengue fever</u> and other <u>viral hemorrhagic fevers</u>

* <u>Malaria</u>

* <u>Zika virus infection</u>

* Other alphaviruses (e.g., Barmah Forest, Ross River)

* <u>Oropouche virus disease</u>

* Rheumatologic conditions

* Treatment [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)[[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* <u>Supportive care</u>

* <u>Acute pain</u> and <u>fever</u>: <u>Acetaminophen</u> is preferred until <u>dengue</u> has been ruled out.

* Persistent <u>joint</u> <u>pain</u>: Consider <u>NSAIDs</u>, <u>corticosteroids</u>, and <u>physical therapy</u>.

* No specific <u>antiviral therapy</u> is available.

* <u>Mosquito bite prevention</u> to reduce the risk of local transmission

* Prevention [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)[[4]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/fwWkRn0)

* <u>Mosquito bite prevention</u> e.g., insect repellents, protective clothing

* A <u>live attenuated vaccine</u> is available for individuals aged ≥ 18 years who are at increased risk of exposure.

* Prognosis [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Most individuals fully recover within a week.

* Some may experience a return of rheumatologic symptoms (e.g., <u>polyarthralgia</u>, <u>polyarthritis</u> in the months after acute illness.

* Complications [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Chronic <u>arthralgia</u> (persistent or relapsing <u>arthralgia</u> of the <u>distal</u> <u>joints</u>) [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)

* Most common and severe complication

* May be associated with <u>arthritis</u>

* May last for months to years and lead to long-term disability

* Treatment: <u>steroids</u> (e.g., <u>prednisone</u>), <u>DMARDs</u> (e.g., <u>methotrexate</u>, <u>sulfasalazine</u>)

* Neurologic: <u>Guillain-Barré syndrome</u>, <u>cranial nerve palsies</u>, <u>meningoencephalitis</u>, <u>myelitis</u>

* Ocular: <u>uveitis</u>, retinitis

* Other: <u>acute kidney injury</u>, <u>myocarditis</u>, <u>hepatitis</u>, <u>blistering skin diseases</u>, <u>multiorgan failure</u>





> [!TIP]
> Consider <u>chikungunya virus</u> infection  in patients with sudden onset of <u>fever</u> and <u>polyarthralgia</u> who have recently traveled to regions where the <u>virus</u> is present. [[2]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/ewWx3n0)




> [!TIP]
> Hemorrhagic complications are uncommon and should prompt consideration of  alternative diagnosis, e.g., <u>dengue</u>.




> [!WARNING]
> Avoid <u>NSAIDs</u> (e.g., <u>aspirin</u>) until <u>dengue</u> has been ruled out to prevent bleeding. [[3]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/gwWFRn0)




### Eastern equine encephalitis (<u>EEE</u>)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Found primarily in the Americas

* In the US, occurs east of the Mississippi River (e.g., New York, New Jersey, Michigan)

* <u>Bimodal distribution</u>

* ≤ 5 years of age

* ≥ 60 years of age

* <u>Outbreaks</u> occur from late spring to early fall.

* Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>Eastern equine encephalitis</u> <u>virus</u> (EEEV)

* <u>Vectors</u>: mosquitoes (Culiseta melanura, Aedes, Coquillettidia, and Culex spp.)

* Reservoirs: horses, birds

* Route of transmission

* Mosquito bite

* Human-to-human: <u>organ transplantation</u>

* Clinical features

* <u>Incubation period</u>: 4–10 days [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q4XC4y)

* Systemic <u>febrile</u> illness (acute <u>EEE</u>)

* Lasts up to 14 days [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q4XC4y)

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* Gastrointestinal symptoms (<u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>)

* <u>EEE</u>

* A life-threatening inflammation of the <u>CNS</u>

* Occurs in ∼ 5% of patients [[5]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/q4XC4y)

* Seen shortly after systemic <u>febrile</u> illness

* Neurological symptoms include <u>seizures</u>, confusion, focal disabilities (<u>cranial nerve palsies</u>), and <u>coma</u>.

* Diagnostics

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>: ↑ <u>WBCs</u>

* <u>Serology</u> (<u>gold standard</u>)

* <u>IgM</u> and/or rising levels of <u>IgG antibodies</u> detected using <u>ELISA</u>, <u>IFA</u>, or <u>hemagglutination</u> assays

* Confirmation: ≥ 4-fold <u>antibody</u> <u>titer</u> increase in serum samples collected after the first week of symptoms

* <u>Reverse transcriptase PCR</u>

* <u>PRNT</u>

* <u>CSF analysis</u>

* ↑ <u>Neutrophils</u> and <u>proteins</u>

* Normal or decreased <u>glucose</u>

* Analyzed using <u>ELISA</u> test for EEV-specific <u>antibodies</u>

* <u>Brain</u> <u>MRI</u>

* Viral culture

* Differential diagnosis

* <u>Aseptic meningitis</u>

* Viral <u>encephalitis</u> (e.g., <u>measles</u>, <u>echovirus</u>)

* Treatment: mainly supportive

* <u>Analgesics</u> for <u>pain</u> and <u>fever</u>

* Management of fluids and <u>electrolyte</u> balance

* Maintenance of blood pressure and oxygenation

* Anticonvulsive treatment for <u>seizures</u>

* Complications

* Severe neurological sequelae [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_zb5vw)

* <u>Epilepsy</u>

* Cognitive disabilities

* Behavioral changes

* <u>Death</u>: occurs within 5 days of diagnosis [[6]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/_zb5vw)





### Western equine encephalitis (WEE)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Occurs on the West Coast of the United States and Canada

* <u>Outbreaks</u> during summer

* Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>Western equine encephalitis</u> <u>virus</u> (<u>WEEV</u>)

* <u>Vectors</u>: mosquitoes (Culex tarsalis, Aedes spp.)

* Reservoirs: horses, mules, birds

* Route of transmission

* Mosquito bite

* Human-to-human

* <u>Blood transfusions</u> from an infected host

* Transplacental

* Clinical features

* <u>Incubation period</u>: 2–7 days [[7]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/MNXMbA)

* Most affected individuals are asymptomatic.

* Systemic <u>febrile</u> illness (acute WEE)

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* <u>Myalgias</u>

* <u>Malaise</u>, fatigue

* Gastrointestinal symptoms (<u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>)

* WEE

* Adults have more mild symptoms than children and elderly individuals.

* Severe forms of <u>encephalitis</u> are more common in children.

* Neurological symptoms include <u>seizures</u>, confusion, <u>somnolence</u>, <u>spasticity</u>, cognitive impairment, and <u>coma</u>.

* Diagnostics

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>: ↑ <u>WBCs</u>

* <u>Serology</u>

* <u>IgM</u> and/or rising levels of <u>IgG antibodies</u> detected using <u>ELISA</u>, <u>IFA</u>, or <u>hemagglutination</u> assays

* Confirmation: ≥ 4-fold <u>antibody</u> <u>titer</u> increase in serum samples collected after the first week of symptoms

* <u>Reverse transcriptase PCR</u>

* <u>PRNT</u>

* <u>CSF analysis</u>

* ↑ <u>Opening pressure</u>, <u>lymphocytes</u>, and <u>proteins</u>

* Normal <u>glucose</u>

* Analyzed using <u>ELISA</u> test for <u>WEEV</u>-specific <u>antibodies</u>

* <u>Brain</u> <u>MRI</u>

* Viral culture

* Differential diagnosis

* <u>Murray Valley encephalitis</u>

* <u>West Nile virus</u> <u>encephalitis</u>

* <u>Japanese encephalitis</u>

* <u>HSV encephalitis</u>

* <u>EEE</u>

* <u>VEE</u>

* Treatment: mainly supportive

* <u>Analgesics</u> for <u>pain</u> and <u>fever</u>

* Management of fluids and <u>electrolyte</u> balance

* Maintenance of blood pressure and oxygenation

* Anticonvulsive treatment of <u>seizures</u>

* Complications

* Neurological sequelae

* <u>Epilepsy</u>

* <u>Spasticity</u>

* Behavioral changes

* <u>Death</u>





### Venezuelan equine encephalitis (<u>VEE</u>)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Found predominantly in the Americas (Colombia, Venezuela, Panama, Guatemala, Mexico, the United States, Ecuador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Peru)

* Sporadic <u>outbreaks</u>

* Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>

* <u>Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus</u> (VEEV)

* Subtypes IAB and IC are responsible for <u>VEE</u> in humans.

* <u>Vectors</u>: mosquitoes (Ochlerotatus taeniorhynchus, Ae. sollicitans, and Ae. taeniorhynchus transmit epizootic strains )

* Reservoirs: horses, mules, donkeys, rodents

* Route of transmission

* Mosquito bite

* Inhalation of aerosols

* Human-to-human: <u>vertical transmission</u>

* Clinical features

* <u>Incubation period</u>: 2–5 days [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mNXVbA)

* Systemic <u>febrile</u> illness (acute <u>VEE</u>)

* Lasts 3–4 days [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mNXVbA)

* <u>Fever</u>

* <u>Headache</u>

* <u>Myalgias</u>, <u>arthralgias</u>

* <u>Sore throat</u>

* Retroorbital <u>pain</u>

* Gastrointestinal symptoms (<u>nausea</u>, <u>vomiting</u>)

* <u>Venezuelan equine encephalitis</u>

* Manifests in ≤ 15% of patients [[8]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/mNXVbA)

* Severe forms of <u>encephalitis</u> are more common in children.

* Neurological symptoms include <u>seizures</u>, confusion, motor and cognitive impairment, and focal disabilities.

* Diagnostics

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>

* <u>CBC</u>: <u>leukopenia</u>, <u>lymphopenia</u>

* <u>LFTs</u>: ↑ <u>transaminases</u>

* <u>Serology</u> (<u>gold standard</u>)

* <u>IgM</u> and/or rising levels of <u>IgG antibodies</u> detected using <u>ELISA</u>, <u>IFA</u>, or <u>hemagglutination</u> assays

* Confirmation: ≥ 4-fold <u>antibody</u> <u>titer</u> increase in serum samples collected

* <u>Reverse transcriptase PCR</u>

* Blood or pharyngeal sample

* Can be performed on the 8<sup>th</sup> day after symptom onset

* <u>PRNT</u>

* <u>CSF analysis</u>: ↑ <u>lymphocytes</u>, <u>proteins</u>, and <u>glucose</u>

* Analyzed using <u>ELISA</u> test for VEEV-specific <u>antibodies</u>

* <u>EEG</u>: diffuse slowing activity

* Viral culture

* Differential diagnosis

* <u>Dengue fever</u>

* <u>Chikungunya fever</u>

* <u>EEE</u>

* <u>Zika</u>

* <u>HSV encephalitis</u>

* Treatment: mainly supportive

* <u>Analgesics</u> for <u>pain</u> and <u>fever</u>

* Management of fluids and <u>electrolyte</u> balance

* Maintenance of blood pressure and oxygenation

* Anticonvulsive treatment of <u>seizures</u>

* Complications

* Neurological sequelae

* Cognitive disabilities

* Behavioral changes

* Pregnant women

* <u>Preterm delivery</u>

* <u>Spontaneous abortion</u>

* <u>Stillbirth</u>

* <u>Death</u>





### Ross River fever (<u>RRF</u>)




* <u>Epidemiology</u>

* Found predominantly in Australia, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands, and other parts of Southeast Asia

* <u>Outbreaks</u> during summer and early autumn

* Etiology

* <u>Pathogen</u>: <u>Ross River virus</u> (<u>RRV</u>)

* <u>Vectors</u>: mosquitoes (Aedes vigilax, Aedes camptorhynchus, Culex annulirostris, and <u>Aedes aegypti</u>)

* Reservoirs: wallabies, kangaroos, possums, horses

* Route of transmission

* Mosquito bite

* Human-mosquito-human transmission

* Human-to-human: <u>blood transfusions</u> from an infected host

* Clinical features

* <u>Incubation period</u>: 3–9 days [[9]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/lNXvYA)

* <u>Febrile</u> illness

* Usually <u>self-limited</u>

* Classic triad: <u>fever</u>, <u>rash</u>, and <u>arthritis</u> 

* <u>Maculopapular rash</u>, affecting the trunk, arms, palms, soles, and face

* <u>Arthritis</u> is symmetrical, mainly involving the knees, ankles, wrists, and fingers.

* Generalized fatigue, <u>myalgias</u>, and lymphadenopathies are also common.

* Other clinical manifestations

* <u>Hematuria</u> and/or <u>glomerulonephritis</u>

* <u>Splenomegaly</u>

* <u>Meningoencephalitis</u>

* Diagnostics

* <u>Laboratory studies</u>: ↑ <u>ESR</u>

* <u>Serology</u> (<u>gold standard</u>): <u>IgM</u> and/or rising levels of <u>IgG antibodies</u> detected using <u>ELISA</u>

* <u>PCR</u>

* Viral culture

* Differential diagnosis

* <u>Barmah Forest virus</u>

* <u>Chikungunya virus</u>

* Treatment: mainly supportive

* <u>Analgesics</u> for <u>pain</u> and <u>fever</u>

* Management of fluids and <u>electrolyte</u> balance

* Maintenance of blood pressure and oxygenation

* Anticonvulsive treatment of <u>seizures</u>

* Complications

* Persistent symptoms (<u>arthritis</u>, fatigue)  [[10]](https://coursology-qbank.com/amboss/article/NNX-YA)

* <u>Septic arthritis</u>
